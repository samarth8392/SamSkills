# Gender Dysphoria in Adolescents and Adults

> **Spark note:** Marked incongruence between experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender for ≥6 months, shown by ≥2 of 6 indicators, with clinically significant distress or impairment. Experienced gender may be binary or nonbinary/alternative. The diagnosis captures the dysphoria, not the identity, and not every transgender person meets it. The source describes elevated suicide risk and associated factors including victimization and depression; individual assessment remains necessary. Gender identity held without other psychotic features is **not** a delusion.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Marked incongruence between experienced/expressed and assigned gender, **≥6 months**, with **≥2 of 6**:
  1. Marked incongruence between experienced/expressed gender and primary and/or secondary sex characteristics (young adolescents: **anticipated** secondary sex characteristics).
  2. Strong desire to be rid of primary/secondary sex characteristics because of that incongruence (young adolescents: desire to **prevent** anticipated development).
  3. Strong desire for the primary/secondary sex characteristics of another gender.
  4. Strong desire to be another gender (or an alternative gender different from assigned).
  5. Strong desire to be treated as another gender (or alternative gender).
  6. Strong conviction of having the typical feelings and reactions of another gender (or alternative gender).
- **B.** Clinically significant distress or impairment in social, **occupational** or other important functioning.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With a disorder/difference of sex development** (e.g., E25.0 congenital adrenal hyperplasia; E34.50 androgen insensitivity syndrome) — only with a specific, codable DSD documented in the medical record; code both.
- **Posttransition:** living full-time in experienced gender (legal change not required) **and** has undergone or is preparing for ≥1 gender-affirming medical procedure/regimen — regular gender-affirming hormones or gender-affirming surgery (e.g., breast augmentation and/or vulvovaginoplasty for someone AMAB; transmasculine chest surgery and/or phalloplasty or metoidioplasty for someone AFAB). Used to support continuing treatment for the new gender assignment.
- No severity specifier.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Core: incongruence + distress about it. Distress may involve being a binary gender other than assigned, or an intermediate/alternative gender.
- **Younger adolescents:** secondary sex characteristics not yet developed; may not voice dislike but be markedly distressed by **imminent** changes. Puberty onset AFAB ~9–13, AMAB ~11–14.
- Often (not always) a desire to be rid of one's sex characteristics and/or acquire those of another gender.
- To varying degrees, adopt behavior, clothing and mannerisms of experienced gender; discomfort at being regarded as, or functioning in society as, assigned gender.
- Some have a strong desire to be and be treated as another gender, with inner certainty, **without seeking medical treatment**; some partially live in the role or adopt a role neither conventionally male nor female.

## Associated features
- At early puberty: individuals AMAB may shave facial/body/leg hair and bind genitals to make erections less visible; individuals AFAB may bind breasts, stoop, wear loose clothing.
- Adolescents increasingly request (or obtain **without prescription/supervision**) GnRH agonists or androgen blockers (e.g., spironolactone). Clinically referred adolescents often want hormones; many want gender-affirming surgery.
- In accepting environments: openly express experienced gender, dress, hairstyle, friendships, new first name.
- Sexuality: older adolescents/adults with genital aversion often don't let partners see/touch genitals.
- Adults may seek hormones (sometimes unsupervised) and surgery; some are satisfied with hormones only, surgery only, or no medical treatment.
- **Autism spectrum traits** overrepresented; autistic individuals more often gender diverse.
- Before gender-affirming treatment and legal gender change: increased mental health problems including suicidal ideation, attempts and suicide. After reassignment, adjustment varies; risks may persist.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Treatment-seeking-based prevalence **<0.1%** for both AMAB and AFAB (likely underestimate).
- General population surveys (US/Europe): self-identify as transgender 0.5–0.6%; incongruent gender identity 0.6–1.1%; feeling like a person of a different sex 2.1–2.6%; desire for medical treatment 0.2–0.6%.
- Incongruence is the central feature in adolescents/adults; distress may be eased by supportive environments and knowing treatments exist. Impairment may include school refusal, depression, anxiety, peer/behavioral problems, substance use.
- **Early/prepubertal-onset:** met child criteria; may persist, desist or be denied (sometimes identifying as gay/lesbian or heterosexual cisgender), with possible later recurrence. Often present for care in childhood through young adulthood; dysphoria may be more intense.
- **Late/pubertal-postpubertal onset:** around puberty or much later. Some recall an unexpressed childhood wish or subthreshold nonconformity; others recall no childhood signs; parents are often surprised. Distress may be more variable and less intense.
- Puberty itself, or anticipating it, can intensify dysphoria regardless of trajectory.
- **With DSD:** early medical attention, gonadectomy and induced puberty, infertility, early genital surgery common; many experience gender **uncertainty** rather than firm conviction; rates of dysphoria/transition vary by syndrome and assignment.
- **Temperamental:** greater childhood nonconformity intensity and older age at presentation → persistence. For postpubertal onset, a factor "under consideration" is a history of transvestism that may develop into autogynephilia (sexual arousal at the thought/image of oneself as a woman) — TR presents this as a hypothesis.
- **Environmental:** AMAB without DSD more often have older brothers.
- **Genetic/physiological:** weak familiality; higher MZ vs DZ concordance; polygenic basis suggested. No systemic sex-hormone abnormalities in 46,XY individuals; mildly elevated androgens (hirsute-female range) in 46,XX individuals. Evidence insufficient to call it a CNS-limited intersex condition. DSD-related risk increases with grossly atypical prenatal androgen exposure; prenatal androgens relate more to behavior than identity; more patient-initiated female→male change with full prenatal masculinizing exposure.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Reported worldwide; equivalents in cultures with institutionalized nonbinary gender categories (India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Oman, Samoa, Thailand, Indigenous Peoples of North America); unclear if criteria are met there.
- Coexisting problems vary with attitudes toward nonconformity; anxiety relatively common even in accepting cultures.
- Adolescents/young adults: shift toward **more AFAB** than AMAB presenting. Adults seeking gender-affirming treatment: more AMAB, ratios 1:1 to 6.1:1 (US/Europe).

## Suicide risk
- Transgender individuals: suicidality/attempt rates of **30–80%**; risk factors: past maltreatment, gender-based victimization, depression, substance use, younger age.
- Gender-clinic-referred transgender adolescents have much higher rates than nonreferred adolescents.
- Increased risk before gender-affirming treatment and legal reassignment; afterward, coexisting symptoms often improve but some remain anxious/depressed and at increased suicide risk.
- Among adolescents, highest attempt rate in **transgender young men**, then those identifying as **neither male nor female**.

## Functional consequences
- Distress from incongruence often interferes with daily life; relationship (including sexual) difficulties; school/work impairment.
- High stigmatization, discrimination and victimization → negative self-concept, depression, suicidality, other disorders, school dropout, unemployment and economic marginalization, especially without family/social support.
- Structural barriers to health/mental health care (institutional discomfort, inexperience, hostility).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Nonconformity to gender roles:** no strong desire to be another gender, limited extent; e.g., occasional cross-dressing in adult men → no diagnosis. Limit diagnosis to those meeting distress/impairment criteria.
- **Transvestic disorder:** heterosexual or bisexual adolescent/adult males (rarely females) aroused by cross-dressing, with distress/impairment, **without** questioning assigned gender. Both diagnoses may be given; in some postpubertal-onset AMAB individuals attracted to women, arousal with cross-dressing precedes gender dysphoria.
- **Body dysmorphic disorder:** wants a body part altered because it looks abnormal, not because it signals repudiated gender; both may be diagnosed. Wish for healthy limb amputation (body integrity identity disorder) usually involves no desire to change gender.
- **Autism spectrum disorder:** rigid thinking about gender roles or poor social understanding vs co-occurring dysphoria can be hard to tell apart.
- **Schizophrenia/psychotic disorders:** gender-themed delusions (up to 20% of schizophrenia) are bizarre and wax and wane with psychotic episodes; **without psychosis, insisting on being another gender is not a delusion**; the two can co-occur.
- **Other presentations:** some with emasculinization desire who develop a nonmale/nonfemale identity do meet criteria; males seeking genital surgery for aesthetics or to remove androgen effects **without** changing male identity do not.

## Comorbidity
- Anxiety and depressive disorders most common in clinically referred adolescents/adults.
- ASD more prevalent than in general population.
- PTSD after harassment and violence. Substance use noted as an impairment correlate and suicide risk factor.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** record which ≥2 indicators are present, in client's words where helpful (e.g., "describes chest as 'not mine' and binds daily [A1/A2]; wants to be seen and addressed as a man [A4/A5]"); duration ≥6 months.
- Note identity (trans woman, trans man, nonbinary, etc.), name and pronouns; assigned gender as AFAB/AMAB.
- **B:** distress (body-related distress, social discomfort) or impairment (school/work, relationships, avoidance); separate dysphoria-related distress from **stigma/minority stress** (both may be present).
- **Specifiers:** DSD only with documented, codable condition; posttransition = full-time social transition + ≥1 gender-affirming medical treatment received or planned.
- **Transition context:** social, legal and medical steps; unsupervised hormone or blocker use (safety/medical referral).
- **Rule-outs / co-occurring:** psychosis screen if beliefs are bizarre or fluctuate; BDD features; ASD traits; transvestic arousal pattern; depression, anxiety, PTSD, substance use.
- **Safety:** structured suicide risk assessment each episode of care; record victimization, family rejection, supports, and protective factors.
- MSE: describe presentation neutrally (e.g., "presents in clothing consistent with stated gender; affect anxious when discussing puberty-related changes; thought process linear; no delusions").
