# Gender Dysphoria — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** One overarching diagnosis, **gender dysphoria**, with two developmentally appropriate criteria sets: children (F64.2) and adolescents/adults (F64.0). The core is a marked incongruence between experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender lasting ≥6 months, **plus** clinically significant distress or impairment. The diagnosis targets the **dysphoria** (the clinical problem), not gender identity itself. Being transgender or gender nonconforming is not a disorder. Distress that comes only from stigma is a distinct (though often co-occurring) source and is not what the diagnosis captures.

## Key terminology (as the TR uses it, p. 713–714)
- **Sex/sexual:** biological indicators of male and female (chromosomes, gonads, sex hormones, nonambiguous genitalia).
- **Disorders/differences of sex development (DSDs):** intersex conditions, e.g., ambiguous genitalia (clitoromegaly, micropenis), complete androgen insensitivity syndrome, gonadal agenesis, Turner or Klinefelter syndrome, ovotestes. Historical terms (hermaphroditism) are outdated.
- **Gender:** the public, sociocultural (usually legally recognized) lived role as boy/girl, man/woman, or **other gender**.
- **Gender assignment:** usually at birth → **birth-assigned gender/sex** (older terms "biological sex," "natal gender"). **Assigned gender** also includes reassignments in infancy/early childhood (usually intersex conditions).
- **Gender-atypical:** statistically atypical features/behaviors; **gender-nonconforming, gender variant, gender diverse** are nondiagnostic alternatives.
- **Gender reassignment:** official (sometimes legal) change of gender.
- **Gender-affirming treatments:** hormones and/or surgeries aligning physical characteristics with experienced gender.
- **Gender identity:** social identity as male, female, in between (e.g., gender fluid) or other (e.g., gender neutral).
- **Gender dysphoria:** descriptively, distress that may accompany incongruence; diagnostically, defined by criteria.
- **Transgender:** gender identity differs from birth-assigned gender. **Cisgender:** congruent (nontransgender). **Transsexual:** historical term for someone seeking or having undergone social (often also medical) transition.
- Replaces DSM-IV **gender identity disorder**; reframed to focus on dysphoria rather than identity.

## Language guidance for notes
- Use the client's name and pronouns; describe as "assigned female/male at birth (AFAB/AMAB)" rather than "biological male/female."
- Use "gender-affirming" (hormones, surgery, care) and "transition" (social, legal, medical).
- Recognize nonbinary and alternative identities: the criteria explicitly include "some alternative gender different from one's assigned gender."
- Frame distress and risk in context (minority stress, stigma, discrimination, family support) without pathologizing identity.

## Child vs adolescent/adult criteria
| Feature | Children (F64.2) | Adolescents & adults (F64.0) |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | ≥6 months | ≥6 months |
| Threshold | ≥6 of 8, **A1 required** | ≥2 of 6 |
| Style | Concrete, behavioral (play, clothing, toys, playmates, anatomy) | Incongruence with sex characteristics, desire to be/be treated as another gender, conviction of having that gender's feelings |
| Criterion B | Distress or impairment in social, **school**, or other areas | Distress or impairment in social, **occupational**, or other areas |
| Specifiers | With a disorder/difference of sex development | With a DSD; **Posttransition** |

## TR changes / notable updates
- Terminology updated: "experienced gender," "birth-assigned gender," "gender-affirming" treatment/surgery (replacing "cross-sex"/"sex reassignment").
- Specifier is now **with a disorder/difference of sex development**; DSD coding examples **E25.0** congenital adrenal hyperplasia and **E34.50** androgen insensitivity syndrome.
- Posttransition specifier updated to mention transmasculine chest surgery, phalloplasty/metoidioplasty, breast augmentation/vulvovaginoplasty.
- New TR material: autism spectrum traits overrepresented; increasing early social transition; shift toward more AFAB adolescents presenting; suicide data section (incl. Canadian 572-child study); structural barriers to care.

## Shared specifiers & coding conventions
- **With a disorder/difference of sex development** — only when a specific, codable DSD is documented in the medical record; code the DSD **and** gender dysphoria.
- **Posttransition** (adolescents/adults only) — full-time living in experienced gender (with or without legal change) plus ≥1 gender-affirming medical procedure or regimen undergone or being prepared; used for continuing treatment that supports the new gender assignment.

## Chapter-level differential logic
- **Gender nonconformity** (e.g., "tomboyism," occasional cross-dressing) without strong desire to be another gender and without distress/impairment → no diagnosis.
- **Transvestic disorder:** sexual excitement from cross-dressing without questioning assigned gender; both diagnoses possible.
- **Body dysmorphic disorder:** body part seen as abnormally formed, not as a marker of repudiated gender; both diagnoses possible. Body integrity identity concerns (wish for amputation) differ.
- **Autism spectrum disorder:** co-occurrence is common; rigid thinking about gender roles can be hard to tell apart.
- **Schizophrenia/psychosis:** gender-themed delusions (up to 20% in schizophrenia) are bizarre and fluctuate with psychosis. **Without psychosis, a person's insistence on their gender is not a delusion.**
- **Other presentations:** males seeking genital surgery for aesthetic reasons or to remove androgen effects without changing male identity → criteria not met. A nonmale/nonfemale identity with emasculinization desire can meet criteria.

## Pitfalls
- Diagnosing gender diversity itself; the diagnosis requires criteria-level incongruence **and** distress/impairment.
- Forgetting A1 is mandatory in the child criteria.
- Treating gender-nonconforming behavior (including in DSDs) as proof of current or future dysphoria.
- Mislabeling distress from rejection/stigma as the dysphoria; document both.
- Treating gender identity as delusional without other psychotic features.

## Documentation cues (chapter-wide)
- Use client's identity terms, name and pronouns; record assigned gender as AFAB/AMAB.
- Record duration (≥6 months), the specific criteria met, and the nature of distress or impairment (separate from stigma-related distress).
- Record family/social support, school/work functioning, victimization, minority stress, and access barriers.
- Always assess suicide risk; rates are high and risk factors include maltreatment, victimization, depression, substance use and younger age.
- Screen for co-occurring anxiety, depression, ASD, PTSD and substance use; rule out psychosis-related gender beliefs when relevant.
