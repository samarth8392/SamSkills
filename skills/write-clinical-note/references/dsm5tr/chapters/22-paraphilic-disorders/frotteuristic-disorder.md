# Frotteuristic Disorder

> **Spark note:** For ≥6 months, recurrent, intense sexual arousal from **touching or rubbing against a nonconsenting person** (the classic example is a crowded subway), which is **acted on** or causes distress or impairment. The behavior is common (up to 30% of adult men report frotteuristic acts), but the disorder is much rarer. In young people, separate sexually motivated touching from nonsexual conduct-disordered behavior.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** **≥6 months** of recurrent, intense sexual arousal from **touching or rubbing against a nonconsenting person**, shown in fantasies, urges or behaviors (p. 1044).
- **B.** **Acted on** the urges with a **nonconsenting person**, **or** the urges or fantasies cause **clinically significant distress or impairment** (p. 1044).

## Specifiers & severity
- **In a controlled environment:** opportunities to touch or rub against nonconsenting people are restricted, as in institutional settings (p. 1044).
- **In full remission:** **≥5 years uncontrolled** with no acting on urges with a nonconsenting person **and** no distress or impairment (p. 1044).
  - Frotteurism itself may persist after behaviors and distress remit (p. 1044).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Disclosing individuals** (pp. 1044–1045):
  - With psychosocial impairment → disorder.
  - No distress (no anxiety, obsessions, guilt or shame), no impairment, and no acting on it per psychiatric or legal history → **frotteuristic sexual interest only**.
- **Nondisclosing individuals** (p. 1045):
  - Known repeated touching or rubbing on separate occasions, but they deny urges or call it unintentional or nonsexual, or admit episodes but deny persistent interest.
  - **May still be diagnosed**: recurrent behavior fulfils **A** and shows **harm (B)**.
- **"Recurrent"** (p. 1045):
  - Multiple victims on separate occasions; or fewer victims with repeated incidents against the same person; or corroborating preferential interest.
  - Multiple victims are sufficient, not necessary: acknowledged intense interest **with distress or impairment** also qualifies.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Disorder prevalence is unknown** (p. 1045).
- Frotteuristic **acts**, including uninvited sexual touching or rubbing, occur in **up to 30% of adult men** in the US and Canadian general population (p. 1045).
- "Intense desire" **3.8%**, "persistent behavior" **0.7%**, so the disorder is much rarer (pp. 1045).
- **10–14%** of men in outpatient settings for paraphilic disorders and hypersexuality meet criteria (p. 1045).
- **Likely lower in women** (p. 1045).
- **Onset:** men report awareness in **late adolescence or emerging adulthood** (p. 1045).
- **Youth:** children and adolescents may touch or rub against others **without** the disorder. There is no minimum age, but at younger ages the disorder is hard to distinguish from **conduct-disordered behavior without sexual motivation** (p. 1045).
- **Course:** persistence is unclear. Distress and impairment fluctuate. It may **decrease with age** (pp. 1045–1046).
- **Temperamental risk:** **nonsexual antisocial behavior** and **sexual preoccupation/hypersexuality** may be nonspecific risk factors. Causality is uncertain (p. 1046).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Frotteurism (nondisordered):** not acted on and no distress or impairment → no diagnosis (p. 1046).
- **Mania, major NCD, IDD, personality change due to another medical condition, substance intoxication, schizophrenia:** diagnose only if the behavior **also occurs outside** these states (p. 1046).
- **CD (adolescents) / ASPD:** broader norm-breaking, usually **without** the specific sexual interest (p. 1046).

## Comorbidity
- Data come mostly from **men suspected or convicted**. They may not apply to people diagnosed on the basis of subjective distress (pp. 1046).
- **Hypersexuality** and other paraphilic disorders, **especially exhibitionistic and voyeuristic** (p. 1046).
- Also **CD, ASPD, depressive, bipolar, anxiety and substance use disorders** (p. 1046).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** the content (sexually motivated touching or rubbing against a **nonconsenting** person), the setting (e.g. crowded transit), the source, and the **≥6-month** span.
- **B:** the specific incidents, number of separate occasions and victims, **or** the reported distress or impairment.
- **Disclosure status:** for denying clients, the pattern of recurrent incidents. For youth, note the evidence of **sexual motivation** versus nonsexual conduct problems.
- **Rule-outs:** intoxication, mania, psychosis, NCD, IDD, personality change; CD or ASPD.
- **Session wording:**
  - "Describes seeking crowded trains to rub against strangers; reports guilt and attempts to avoid rush hour."
  - "Denies sexual intent; multiple transit-police reports on separate dates."
- **Comorbidity screen:** exhibitionistic and voyeuristic disorders, hypersexuality, mood, anxiety, substance use.
- **Specifier rationale:** controlled environment, or remission (≥5 years uncontrolled with no acts and no distress).
