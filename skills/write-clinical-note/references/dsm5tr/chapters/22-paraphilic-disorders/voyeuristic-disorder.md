# Voyeuristic Disorder

> **Spark note:** For ≥6 months, recurrent, intense sexual arousal from **secretly watching an unsuspecting person** who is naked, undressing or having sex. To be a disorder it must be **acted on with a nonconsenting person** or cause distress or impairment, and the person must be **≥18**. It is the only courtship disorder with an age floor. A **denying** person with repeated spying on separate occasions can still be diagnosed, because the behavior satisfies both A and B.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** For **≥6 months**, repeated strong sexual arousal centers on secretly viewing someone unaware of being watched during nudity, undressing, or sex; fantasy, urges, or enacted behavior may supply evidence (p. 1037).
- **B.** **Acted on** the urges with a **nonconsenting person**, **or** the urges or fantasies cause **clinically significant distress or impairment** (pp. 1037–1038).
- **C.** The person experiencing the arousal and/or acting on it is **≥18 years** old (p. 1038).

## Specifiers & severity
- **In a controlled environment:** mainly for people in institutional or other settings where voyeuristic opportunities are restricted (p. 1038).
- **In full remission:** **≥5 years in an uncontrolled environment** with **no acting on urges** with a nonconsenting person **and no distress or impairment** (p. 1038).
  - Remission says nothing about whether the **voyeuristic interest** persists, and it may continue (p. 1038).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Disclosing individuals** (p. 1038):
  - With distress or psychosocial problems → disorder.
  - With **no distress** (no anxiety, obsessions, guilt or shame), no impairment, and **no history of acting on it** (psychiatric or legal) → **voyeuristic sexual interest only**, not a disorder.
- **Nondisclosing individuals** (p. 1038):
  - Known repeated spying on separate occasions, with denial of urges, or claims it was accidental or nonsexual. Or they admit episodes but deny significant interest.
  - They will also deny distress, yet **may still be diagnosed**: recurrent behavior fulfils **A** and simultaneously shows **harm to others (B)**.
- **"Recurrent"** (pp. 1038–1039):
  - It may be read as **multiple victims on separate occasions**, which raises confidence in the inference.
  - **Fewer victims** can suffice with multiple occasions with the same victim, or corroborating evidence of preferential interest.
  - Multiple victims are **sufficient, not necessary**. Acknowledged intense interest can also meet criteria.
- **Age floor rationale:** puberty increases sexual curiosity, so the **≥18** minimum avoids pathologizing normal adolescents (p. 1039).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Disorder prevalence is unknown**. Voyeuristic **acts** are the **most common potentially law-breaking sexual behaviors** (p. 1039).
- **Quebec survey** (p. 1039):
  - Lifetime voyeuristic behavior **34.5%** (men 50.3%, women 21.2%).
  - "Intense desire" 9.6%; "persistent behavior" 2.1%. The disorder is therefore much rarer.
- **Male:female ratio** for the behavior: **~2:1** (Quebec), **3:1** (Sweden) (p. 1039).
- **Austrian incarcerated sex offenders** (n = 1,346): **3.7%** had voyeuristic disorder (p. 1039).
- **Onset:** men often become aware of the interest in **adolescence** (p. 1039).
- **Course:** persistence is unclear. Distress (guilt, shame, sexual frustration, loneliness) and impairment can change over time, as can psychiatric morbidity, hypersexuality and sexual impulsivity. Preferences and behavior may **decrease with age** (p. 1039).
- **Risk:** voyeurism is the precondition, so its risk factors apply (p. 1039).
- **Environmental risk:** suggested factors are **childhood sexual abuse, substance misuse and sexual preoccupation/hypersexuality**. The causal role and specificity are uncertain (pp. 1039–1040).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- **Very uncommon in women** in clinical settings. For single arousing voyeuristic acts, the male:female ratio is about **2:1–3:1** (p. 1040).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Voyeurism (nondisordered):** arousal without acting on it with an unsuspecting person and without distress or impairment → no diagnosis (p. 1040).
- **Manic episode, major NCD, IDD, personality change due to another medical condition, substance intoxication, schizophrenia:**
  - These can cause disinhibition or impaired judgment that leads to voyeuristic acts.
  - Diagnose voyeuristic disorder **only if the behavior also occurs outside** these contexts (p. 1040).
- **Conduct disorder (adolescents) / ASPD:** broader norm-breaking, with the specific sexual interest in secretly watching **usually lacking** (p. 1040).

## Comorbidity
- Data come mostly from **males suspected or convicted** of the behavior, so they may not generalize (p. 1040).
- **Hypersexuality** and other paraphilic disorders, **especially exhibitionistic** (p. 1040).
- Also **depressive, bipolar, anxiety and substance use disorders**, **ADHD**, **CD and ASPD** (p. 1040).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** the source of the evidence (self-report of fantasies or urges, collateral, legal records), the **≥6-month span**, and the **content** (unsuspecting person; nude, undressing or sexual activity).
- **B:** either the specific **nonconsensual acts** (number of occasions or victims, dates, from records) **or** the reported distress or impairment (guilt, shame, time lost, relationship or occupational effects).
- **C:** document **age ≥18**.
- **Disclosure status:** "disclosing" vs "nondisclosing/denies interest." For denying clients, note the **recurrent separate-occasion behavior** that supports A and B.
- **Rule-outs:** behavior is **not confined** to intoxication, mania, psychosis or neurocognitive or intellectual impairment. Assess for CD or ASPD.
- **Specifier rationale:** for a controlled environment, the setting; for remission, ≥5 years uncontrolled with no acts and no distress.
- **Session wording:**
  - "Acknowledges recurrent urges to watch neighbors undress; reports shame and avoidance of intimacy."
  - "Denies sexual motive; records document repeated surreptitious recording on separate dates."
- **Comorbidity screen:** exhibitionistic and other paraphilic disorders, hypersexuality, mood, substance use, ADHD.
