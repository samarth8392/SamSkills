# Paraphilic Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** A **paraphilia** is an *interest*: an intense and persistent sexual interest in anything other than genital stimulation or preparatory fondling with phenotypically normal, physically mature, consenting human partners. A **paraphilic disorder** is a paraphilia that **currently causes distress or impairment**, **or** whose satisfaction has **harmed or risked harm to others**. Having a paraphilia is **necessary but not sufficient** for a disorder, and a paraphilia alone does not necessarily need treatment. Every criteria set is two-pronged: **Criterion A = the paraphilia** and **Criterion B = its negative consequences**. (pp. 1036–1037)

## Paraphilia vs paraphilic disorder (the key distinction)
- **Paraphilia, defined:**
  - Any **intense and persistent** sexual interest other than interest in genital stimulation or preparatory fondling with phenotypically normal, physically mature, consenting human partners (p. 1036).
  - When "intense" does not fit (e.g. **very old or medically ill** people with low sexual interest overall), a paraphilia can be defined as any sexual interest **≥** the person's nonparaphilic interests (p. 1036).
  - Some paraphilias are better described as **preferential** rather than intense interests (p. 1036).
- **Two kinds of paraphilia** (pp. 1036–1037):
  - **Erotic-activity** paraphilias, e.g. spanking, whipping, cutting, binding, strangulating.
  - **Erotic-target** paraphilias, e.g. children, corpses, amputees as a class, animals, inanimate objects such as shoes or rubber.
  - The person's **choice of pornography** often reflects their paraphilic interests.
- **Paraphilic disorder** = paraphilia + **current** distress or impairment to the person, **or** satisfaction that has entailed personal harm, or risk of harm, to others (p. 1037).
- **Use the term "diagnosis" only when both A and B are met.** If only A is met (e.g. a benign paraphilia found while evaluating something else), the person **has the paraphilia, not a disorder** (p. 1037).
- **Multiple paraphilias** are not rare. The foci may be related (foot and shoe fetishism) or apparently coincidental. **Give separate comorbid diagnoses** when more than one paraphilia causes suffering or harm (p. 1037).
- **Severity and measurement** can target either the **strength of the paraphilia** or the **seriousness of its consequences**. The consequences (reactive depression, anxiety, guilt, poor work history, impaired relationships) can be measured with general psychosocial or quality-of-life tools (p. 1037).

### How Criterion B differs across disorders (high-yield)
| Group | Criterion B can be met by | Disorders |
|---|---|---|
| **Victim-involving** | **Acted on urges with a nonconsenting person** *or* clinically significant distress/impairment | Voyeuristic, exhibitionistic, frotteuristic, sexual sadism |
| **Pedophilic** | **Acted on urges** *or* **marked distress or interpersonal difficulty** | Pedophilic |
| **Distress/impairment only** | Clinically significant distress or impairment **only** (no "acted on nonconsenting person" route) | Sexual masochism, fetishistic, transvestic |

**Nondisclosing (denying) individuals:**
- For the victim-involving disorders and pedophilic disorder, the diagnosis can be made **despite denial**. **Recurrent behavior with separate victims** satisfies **both A** (it evidences the paraphilia) **and B** (it shows harm to others) (pp. 1038, 1042, 1045, 1050, 1053).
- **Multiple victims are sufficient but not necessary**. Fewer victims can satisfy "recurrent" with repeated acts toward the same victim, or with corroborating evidence of preferential interest.
- Masochism criteria are written **only** for people who freely admit the interest (p. 1047).

## Chapter organization (why these 8)
- **Why these eight are listed:** they are relatively **common** compared with other paraphilic disorders, and some involve **noxious or harmful acts that are classed as crimes**. They do **not** exhaust the possibilities. Dozens of paraphilias exist, and almost any can become a disorder if it has negative consequences (p. 1036).
- **Ordering scheme** (p. 1036):
  - **Anomalous activity preferences**
    - *Courtship disorders* (distorted components of courtship): voyeuristic, exhibitionistic, frotteuristic.
    - *Algolagnic disorders* (pain and suffering): sexual masochism, sexual sadism.
  - **Anomalous target preferences**
    - Directed at humans: pedophilic.
    - Directed elsewhere: fetishistic, transvestic.

| Frotteuristic | Touching or rubbing against a nonconsenting person | Nonconsenting act OR distress/impairment | None | — |

| Pedophilic | Sexual activity with prepubescent child(ren), generally ≤13 y | Acted on OR marked distress/interpersonal difficulty | **≥16 y and ≥5 y older** than the child | Exclusive/nonexclusive; attracted to males/females/both; limited to incest |

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- **"In a controlled environment"** is available for the seven named disorders other than pedophilic disorder; other specified also permits it. It applies mainly to people in institutional or other settings where opportunities are restricted.
- **"In full remission"** is available for the seven named disorders other than pedophilic disorder. It requires **≥5 years in an uncontrolled environment** with:
  - **no acting on urges with a nonconsenting person** (voyeuristic, exhibitionistic, frotteuristic, sadism); **and**
  - **no distress or impairment** (all of them).
- Remission is about **behavior and distress, not the interest**. The paraphilia itself may persist after remission (pp. 1038, 1041, 1044).
- **Pedophilic disorder has no remission or controlled-environment specifier** in the text. Pedophilia per se appears **lifelong** (p. 1054).
- Other specified paraphilic disorder **can** be specified as in remission and/or in a controlled environment (p. 1063).
- ICD-10-CM codes are as printed in the TR text: F65.3, F65.2, F65.81, F65.51, F65.52, F65.4, F65.0, F65.1, F65.89, F65.9.

## Edition-specific points visible in the text
- Separate listed codes include F65.51 (masochism), F65.52 (sadism), F65.81 (frotteuristic), and F65.89 (other specified). The passage does not itself establish the revision history of each code.
- Masochism and sadism have suicidality sections: disorder-specific associations are unknown; a BDSM sample associated stigma-related guilt/shame with ideation. (pp. 1048, 1051)
- Pedophilic-disorder discussion includes emotional congruence with children and methodological/legal limits of diagnostic measures. Explicit child material alone does not establish the enacted-urge route to Criterion B without in-person sexual interaction. (pp. 1054–1056)
- Partialism is included in fetishistic disorder; the text specifically contrasts this with its DSM-IV-TR placement under paraphilia NOS. (p. 1058)
- These notes describe supplied wording; they do not assume that all features were newly introduced in TR.

## Chapter-level differential logic and pitfalls
- **Paraphilia without B ≠ disorder.** Consensual BDSM practitioners, untroubled fetishists and nondistressed cross-dressers do not get a diagnosis (pp. 1051–1052, 1058, 1062).
- **Disinhibition from another condition:** major NCD, IDD, personality change due to another medical condition, schizophrenia, mania and substance intoxication can produce voyeuristic, exhibitionistic or frotteuristic acts. Diagnose the paraphilic disorder **only if the behavior also occurs outside** those contexts (pp. 1040, 1043, 1046).
- **CD/ASPD:** broader norm-breaking, usually **without** the specific sexual interest. Coercive sex or cruelty within CD/ASPD is not sadism unless arousal to suffering is shown. If both are met, both can be diagnosed (pp. 1040, 1043, 1046, 1052).
- **Sex offense ≠ paraphilic disorder.** Pain inflicted instrumentally during rape is not sadism without evidence of arousal to suffering. A primarily adult-attracted offender who abuses a child because of access, ASPD or intoxication may not have pedophilic disorder (pp. 1052, 1056).
- **OCD:** ego-dystonic intrusive worries about attraction to children, with no positive feelings and no link to sexual behavior, point to **OCD**, not pedophilic disorder (p. 1056).
- **Overlap rules** (pp. 1056, 1059, 1062):
  - Exhibiting **only** to children → assess both exhibitionistic and pedophilic criteria independently. Indiscriminate exposure across ages can reflect exhibitionism without pedophilic preference.
  - "Forced cross-dressing" aroused mainly by humiliation → **sexual masochism disorder**.
  - Fetish limited to cross-dressing clothing → **transvestic**, not fetishistic.
  - Transvestic disorder + gender dysphoria → **both** can be diagnosed.
- **Comorbidity data are skewed.** Most come from **convicted, almost all male** samples and may not generalize to people diagnosed through distress alone.
- **Age floors protect adolescents:** voyeuristic disorder needs age ≥18; pedophilic disorder needs ≥16 and ≥5 years older, with a note excluding a late adolescent in an ongoing relationship with a 12- or 13-year-old.
