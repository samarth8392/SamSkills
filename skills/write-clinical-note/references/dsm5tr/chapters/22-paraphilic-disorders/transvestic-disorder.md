# Transvestic Disorder

> **Spark note:** Cross-dressing is not itself a disorder or evidence of gender dysphoria. This diagnosis requires a ≥6-month pattern of intense sexual arousal tied to cross-dressing plus significant distress/impairment. (pp. 1059–1062)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** ≥6 months of recurrent, intense sexual arousal associated with cross-dressing, expressed in fantasies, urges, or behaviors.
- **B:** Clinically significant distress or impairment in an important functional domain from these experiences. (p. 1059)

## Specifiers & severity
- **With fetishism:** arousal to fabric, materials, or garments.
- **With autogynephilia:** arousal to images/thoughts of oneself as a woman (source terminology).
- **In a controlled environment:** the setting restricts dressing opportunities.
- **In full remission:** ≥5 years without significant distress/impairment in an uncontrolled environment.
- In the male samples discussed, fetishism is associated with lower likelihood of gender dysphoria, autogynephilia with higher likelihood; neither specifier determines a person's gender or independently diagnoses dysphoria. (p. 1060)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Dressing can involve a few garments or a full presentation. Assess sexual arousal and adverse consequences directly rather than deducing them from appearance.
- Self-report is usually central. Repeated disposal then reacquisition of clothing may reflect efforts to suppress urges and associated distress, but is not mandatory.
- Some experience difficulty sustaining arousal without dressing or related fantasy. (p. 1060)

## Associated features
Autogynephilic themes described by the source can concern feminine anatomy, physiological functions, or culturally stereotyped behavior. These are associated descriptions, not criteria for gender identity. (p. 1061)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Disorder prevalence is unknown and appears higher in men. Fewer than 3% of Swedish men reported ever being aroused by dressing in women's clothing; this is not disorder prevalence.
- Source descriptions largely concern men: childhood fascination can precede pubertal sexual excitement. With aging, arousal may decrease while comfort or desire to dress persists.
- Course may be persistent or episodic; temporary reduction with a new relationship can reverse. Some people later also meet gender dysphoria criteria. Developmental descriptions do not predict any individual's identity/course. (p. 1061)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
The source describes conflict with desired partnered sexual functioning and romantic relationships; determine actual impairment for the individual rather than assuming conventional relationship goals. (pp. 1061–1062)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Transvestism without disorder:** no significant distress/impairment means B is absent.
- **Fetishistic disorder:** clarify whether the rewarding feature is material/object sensation versus thoughts of dressing or presenting as a woman; other non-garment material fetishes may clarify the pattern.
- **Gender dysphoria:** assess incongruence and desire regarding gender separately from sexual arousal. Both diagnoses may be made if each full criteria set is met; gender expression alone establishes neither. (p. 1062)

## Comorbidity
Fetishistic and masochistic interests/behaviors often coexist. Transvestic interests occur in a substantial share of fatal autoerotic asphyxia cases; this association does not imply such behavior in every person with the disorder. (p. 1062)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Assess duration, arousal pattern, and manifestations (A); separately record concrete distress/functional impact (B).
- Ask which aspect is arousing (dressing, material, self-image, humiliation), and distinguish behavior, gender identity, and any separately assessed dysphoria.
- Identify sources of distress, relationship goals, co-occurring interests, and any independently reported unsafe behavior without inferring them from clothing.
- Document setting restrictions and the actual five-year remission interval only if established. Use the individual's stated identity and pronouns.
