# Pedophilic Disorder

> **Spark note:** This diagnosis requires a persistent sexual focus on prepubescent children plus enacted urges or marked distress/interpersonal difficulty. Child sexual offending does not automatically establish the preference, and unwanted obsessional fears about being attracted to children are a different presentation. (pp. 1052–1056)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** ≥6 months of recurrent, intense sexually arousing fantasies, urges, or behaviors involving sexual activity with prepubescent children; age ≤13 is an approximate guide, not a substitute for developmental status.
- **B:** Has acted on these urges, or urges/fantasies produce marked distress or interpersonal difficulty.
- **C:** Individual is ≥16 and ≥5 years older than the child. The text excludes a late adolescent in an ongoing relationship with a 12- or 13-year-old; do not extend this narrow diagnostic note into a legal judgment. (pp. 1052–1053)

## Specifiers & severity
- **Exclusive type** (only attracted to children) or **Nonexclusive type**.
- Specify attraction to **males**, **females**, or **both**.
- **Limited to incest**, when applicable.
- No controlled-environment, remission, or graded severity specifier is provided in this criteria set. (p. 1053)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Pubertal timing varies between individuals and cultural/ethnic groups; establish prepubescence rather than relying solely on chronological age.
- Pedophilic interest alone without B is not the disorder. Multiple victims can support the inference but are unnecessary when the persistent interest is established otherwise.
- Denial does not preclude diagnosis when recurrent behavior over ≥6 months and enacted urges/interpersonal difficulty are evidenced. Sexual interactions can be noncontact, including exposure.
- Sexually explicit material depicting prepubescent children may inform A; use alone, without in-person sexual interaction, does **not** establish the enacted-urge route to B. Separately assess distress/interpersonal difficulty.
- Interest reports, patterns of victims, and relevant material are corroborative information, not stand-alone replacements for full criteria. (pp. 1053–1054)

## Associated features
Emotional identification or affinity with children can include preferring their company or seeking roles with child access. Research links this feature to interest and reoffending among offenders, but ordinary work with children is not diagnostic evidence by itself. (p. 1054)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Population prevalence is uncertain, probably <3% among men internationally; prevalence among women is poorly known and likely substantially lower.
- Interest may first be recognized near puberty and appears enduring; disorder status varies with distress, impairment, and propensity to act. Sexual behavior can diminish with age.
- Antisocial traits may increase the likelihood of acting on an existing interest. Childhood abuse associations do not establish causation. Some evidence suggests prenatal neurodevelopmental disruption as a correlate of interest. (pp. 1054–1055)

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
Developmental assessment must account for variation in puberty. Physiological assessment methods studied in men have very limited validation in women. (pp. 1053, 1055)

## Diagnostic markers
Specialist psychophysiological and viewing-time measures may supplement evaluation when reports conflict with history. Procedures and diagnostic accuracy vary; relative response to child versus adult stimuli matters more than absolute response. The text raises legal concerns about diagnostic sexual images of children; no single laboratory measure replaces clinical criteria. (pp. 1055–1056)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Pedophilia without disorder:** A without enacted urges or marked distress/interpersonal difficulty fails B.
- **Other paraphilic disorders:** exposure to children can reflect exhibitionism without a child-specific preference; both diagnoses require independent evidence.
- **Antisocial personality disorder:** opportunistic abuse by an adult primarily attracted to mature people does not establish the ≥6-month pedophilic pattern.
- **Substance intoxication:** disinhibition may lead to offending without the underlying preference.
- **OCD:** intrusive, feared sexual thoughts generally lack positive sexual appraisal or related sexual behavior; assess obsessional avoidance/checking and broader intrusive thoughts rather than diagnosing from thought content alone. (p. 1056)

## Comorbidity
Substance use, depressive, bipolar, anxiety, antisocial personality, and other paraphilic disorders occur in predominantly male offender samples. Generalization to nonoffending distressed individuals is uncertain. (p. 1056)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Establish duration and nature of arousal (A), developmental status of targets, the individual's age and age gap (C); identify source and uncertainty.
- Record B as actual reported/corroborated interaction versus marked distress/interpersonal difficulty. Do not treat use of explicit material alone as proof of enacted B.
- Assess OCD-type feared intrusions versus sexually rewarding thoughts, intoxication, opportunistic behavior, and other paraphilic motivations.
- Describe reported behavior and risk/access facts objectively, distinguishing allegation, report, and corroborated evidence. Do not invent a victim history, disclosure, examination finding, or legal conclusion.
