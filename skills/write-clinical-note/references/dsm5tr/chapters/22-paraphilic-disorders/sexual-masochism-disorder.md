# Sexual Masochism Disorder

> **Spark note:** Sexual arousal involving one's own suffering becomes this disorder only when it produces clinically significant distress or impairment. Consensual BDSM participation alone does not establish the diagnosis; breathing restriction also raises a distinct accidental-death concern. (pp. 1047–1049)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** ≥6 months of repeated, intense sexual arousal centered on humiliation, beating, restraint, or other suffering experienced by the individual; evidenced through fantasy, urges, or behavior.
- **B:** Those experiences produce clinically significant distress or impaired functioning in an important domain. There is no separate nonconsenting-act alternative in this criteria set. (p. 1047)

## Specifiers & severity
- **With asphyxiophilia:** arousal involves restricting breathing.
- **In a controlled environment:** opportunities for these behaviors are restricted by the setting.
- **In full remission:** ≥5 years without significant distress or impairment while living outside a controlled setting. It does not require disappearance of the interest. No graded severity scale is given. (p. 1047)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The criteria are framed for individuals who acknowledge the sexual interest. Establish both the arousal pattern and its adverse consequences rather than treating a sexual preference as sufficient.
- BDSM describes a broad range of practices, including consensual restraint, sensory restriction, and dominance/submission; it is broader than this diagnosis. (p. 1047)

## Associated features
- Relevant pornography may be prominent. Reduced pain sensitivity has been described in people engaging in sadomasochistic practices, but applicability to the disorder is unknown.
- Evidence does not support assuming a childhood sexual-abuse history from the interest itself. (pp. 1047–1048)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Disorder prevalence is unknown. Australian past-year BDSM participation estimates (men 2.2%, women 1.3%) are behavior estimates, not diagnostic prevalence.
- A community paraphilia sample reported mean masochism onset at 19.3 years; childhood/puberty onset also occurs. Persistence is poorly studied.
- Distress, impairment, associated psychiatric conditions, and impulsivity may fluctuate; preferences and behavior may decline with age. (p. 1048)

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Distinguish culturally sanctioned painful religious/spiritual practices from behavior motivated by sexual arousal; the sexual role of such practices cannot be presumed. (p. 1048)

## Suicide risk
The disorder's association with suicidality is unknown. In a study of 321 BDSM-involved adults, stigma-related guilt/shame correlated with suicidal ideation; this does not establish a disorder-specific causal relationship. (p. 1048)

## Functional consequences
General functional consequences are poorly characterized. Asphyxiophilia may accompany greater distress/maladjustment and can cause accidental death; accidental autoerotic fatalities must not automatically be characterized as suicide or as proof of this disorder. (pp. 1048–1049)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Sexual masochism without disorder:** interest without significant distress/impairment fails B.
- **Religious/spiritual practices:** assess sexual motivation and cultural context rather than inferring it from pain alone. (pp. 1048–1049)

## Comorbidity
Other paraphilic disorders, including transvestic presentations, occur in treatment samples. A small study suggests association with borderline personality disorder; generalizability is limited. (p. 1049)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record the reported arousal focus, fantasy/urge/behavior evidence, frequency, and ≥6-month timeline (A).
- Specify concrete distress and functional limitations attributable to the pattern (B); do not infer them from BDSM participation.
- Assess and describe any breathing restriction and accidental-injury risk without describing dangerous procedures.
- Record cultural meaning, relevant comorbidity, and actual setting/remission duration. Attribute statements to interview, collateral, or records; these are assessment prompts, not client facts.
