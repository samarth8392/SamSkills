# Exhibitionistic Disorder

> **Spark note:** For ≥6 months, recurrent, intense sexual arousal from **exposing one's genitals to an unsuspecting person**, which is **acted on with a nonconsenting person** or causes distress or impairment. The **victim-age subtype** (prepubertal children, physically mature people or both) exists so that co-occurring **pedophilic disorder is not overlooked**. Unlike voyeuristic disorder, there is **no minimum age**.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** **≥6 months** of recurrent, intense sexual arousal from **exposing one's genitals to an unsuspecting person**, shown in fantasies, urges or behaviors (p. 1041).
- **B.** **Acted on** the urges with a **nonconsenting person**, **or** the urges or fantasies cause **clinically significant distress or impairment** (p. 1041).

## Specifiers & severity
**Specify whether** (subtype by victims' age or maturity) (p. 1041):
- Sexually aroused by exposing genitals to **prepubertal children**.
- Sexually aroused by exposing genitals to **physically mature individuals**.
- Sexually aroused by exposing genitals to **prepubertal children and to physically mature individuals**.
- Purpose: draws attention to victim characteristics so co-occurring **pedophilic disorder** is not missed. Arousal to exposing to children **does not preclude** a pedophilic disorder diagnosis (p. 1041).

**Specify if:**
- **In a controlled environment:** opportunities to expose are restricted, as in institutional settings (p. 1041).
- **In full remission:** **≥5 years uncontrolled** with no acting on urges with a nonconsenting person and no distress or impairment. Exhibitionism itself may persist (p. 1041).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Disclosing individuals** (pp. 1041–1042):
  - With psychosocial difficulties → disorder.
  - No distress (no anxiety, obsessions, guilt or shame), no impairment, and self-reported, psychiatric or legal histories showing they do **not act on it** → **exhibitionistic sexual interest only**.
- **Nondisclosing individuals** (p. 1042):
  - Repeated exposure on separate occasions, with denial of urges, or claims it was accidental or nonsexual. Or they admit episodes but deny sustained interest.
  - **May still be diagnosed**: the recurrent behavior satisfies **A** and demonstrates **harm (B)**.
- **"Recurrent"** (p. 1042):
  - Multiple victims on separate occasions increases confidence.
  - Fewer victims can suffice with repeated exposure to the same person, or corroborating evidence of strong or preferential interest.
  - Multiple victims are sufficient, not necessary: acknowledged intense interest **with distress or impairment** also meets criteria.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Disorder prevalence is unknown**; the disorder is **highly unusual in women** (p. 1042).
- Exhibitionistic **acts** are not uncommon. Single sexually arousing acts occur up to **half as often in women** as in men (p. 1042).
- **Quebec survey** (p. 1042):
  - Lifetime exhibitionistic behavior **30.9%** (men 32.6%, women 29.4%).
  - "Intense desire" **4.8%**; "persistent behavior" **0.8%**.
- **Swedish study:** estimated lifetime **disorder** prevalence of **4.1% in men, 2.1% in women** (p. 1042).
- **Onset:** men report awareness in **adolescence**, somewhat **later** than typical normative sexual interest (p. 1042).
- **Age:** no minimum age, but the disorder is hard to separate from adolescent curiosity (pp. 1042–1043).
- **Course:** little is known about persistence. Distress, impairment, psychiatric morbidity, hypersexuality and sexual impulsivity can all change. Behavior may **decrease with age** (p. 1043).
- **Temperamental risk:**
  - **Antisocial history, ASPD, alcohol misuse and pedophilic sexual preference** may increase **sexual recidivism** in exhibitionistic offenders.
  - Hence ASPD, AUD and pedophilic interest may count as risk factors for the disorder in men with exhibitionistic preferences.
  - (p. 1043)
- **Environmental risk:** suggested factors are **childhood sexual and emotional abuse** and **sexual preoccupation/hypersexuality**. Causality is uncertain (p. 1043).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- The disorder is highly unusual in women, although single exhibitionistic acts are relatively common in women (p. 1042).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Exhibitionism (nondisordered):** no acting on it with an unsuspecting person and no distress or impairment → no diagnosis (p. 1043).
- **Mania, major NCD, IDD, personality change due to another medical condition, substance intoxication, schizophrenia:** diagnose exhibitionistic disorder only if the exposure **also occurs outside** these disinhibited states (p. 1043).
- **CD (adolescents) / ASPD:** broader norm-breaking, usually **without** the specific interest in exposing (p. 1043).
- **Pedophilic disorder** (p. 1056):
  - Exposing **exclusively to prepubescent children** → may warrant **both** diagnoses.
  - Exposing to victims **regardless of age** → exhibitionistic disorder alone may suffice.

## Comorbidity
- Data come from **mostly male convicted** samples (pp. 1043–1044).
- High rates of **depressive, bipolar, anxiety and substance use disorders**; **hypersexuality**; **ADHD**; **other paraphilic disorders**; **ASPD** (p. 1044).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** the content (exposing genitals to an **unsuspecting** person), the source (self-report, records), and the **≥6-month** span.
- **B:** the number of separate exposure incidents and victims (from police or court records), **or** the reported distress or impairment.
- **Subtype:** state the **age or maturity of the targets**. If children were involved, document the pedophilic disorder assessment.
- **Disclosure status:** for denying clients, document the pattern of recurrent separate-occasion exposures.
- **Rule-outs:** exposure not limited to intoxication, mania, psychosis, NCD or IDD. Assess for CD or ASPD.
- **Recidivism-relevant factors** (per text): antisocial history, ASPD, alcohol misuse, pedophilic preference.
- **Session wording:**
  - "Reports urges to expose to strangers on public transit, followed by shame."
  - "Minimizes incidents as 'accidental'; three separate charges on record."
- **Specifier rationale:** controlled environment, or remission (≥5 years uncontrolled with no acts and no distress).
