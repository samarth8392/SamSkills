# Somatic Symptom and Related Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Every disorder here centers on prominent somatic symptoms and/or illness anxiety with significant distress or impairment, usually seen first in primary care/medical settings rather than mental health (p. 516). DSM-5 replaced DSM-IV "somatoform disorders" and dropped "medically unexplained" as the organizing principle: diagnoses rest on **positive features** (excessive thoughts/feelings/behaviors; clinical evidence of incompatibility), and a real medical condition **does not exclude** them. The classic error is making a mental disorder diagnosis just because no medical cause was found (p. 516).

## What unites the chapter and how the disorders differ
| Disorder | Core | Somatic sx | Key discriminator | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Somatic symptom disorder (F45.1) | Distressing somatic sx + excessive thoughts/feelings/behaviors | Present, distressing | Criterion B excess response | Typically >6 months symptomatic |
| Illness anxiety disorder (F45.21) | Preoccupation with having/getting serious illness | Absent or mild | Health anxiety without significant sx | ≥6 months |
| Functional neurological symptom disorder (F44.4–F44.7) | Altered voluntary motor/sensory function | Neurological | **Positive exam evidence of incompatibility** with neuro/medical disease | Acute <6 mo / persistent ≥6 mo |
| Psychological factors affecting other medical conditions (F54) | Psych/behavioral factors worsen a medical condition | Medical condition present | Direction: psych → medical | None |
| Factitious disorder (F68.10 self / F68.A another) | Falsification/induction of illness with identified deception | Falsified/induced | **Deception without obvious external reward** | Single or recurrent episodes |
| Other specified (F45.8) / unspecified (F45.9) | Subthreshold or pseudocyesis | Variable | — | e.g., brief <6 months |

- Contributing factors across the class: genetic/biological vulnerability (e.g., pain sensitivity), early trauma (violence, abuse, deprivation), **medical iatrogenesis** (sick-role reinforcement, excessive referrals/testing), learning (no reinforcement for nonsomatic expressions of distress), sociocultural stigma of psychological suffering (p. 517).
- ~⅔–¾ of former DSM-IV hypochondriasis → somatic symptom disorder; remaining ~¼–⅓ (high health anxiety, no somatic sx) → illness anxiety disorder (p. 517).
- Illness anxiety disorder could sit in anxiety disorders; it is placed here for utility (medical settings) (p. 518).
- Factitious disorder is here because most cases present with somatic symptoms and expressed disease conviction (pp. 518).

## TR changes / notable updates in the text
- **Factitious disorder imposed on another = F68.A** (new TR code); imposed on self = F68.10 (p. 538).
- **FND is a "rule-in" diagnosis**, not one of exclusion; it can coexist with recognized neurological disease (epilepsy, MS) and **no longer requires a psychological stressor** — instead specify with/without psychological stressor (pp. 518, 530–531). TR text lists specific positive signs (Hoover's, hip abductor, tremor entrainment, functional dystonia, functional seizure features, fogging test) (pp. 531–532).
- FND symptom-type codes: F44.4 (weakness/paralysis, abnormal movement, swallowing, speech), F44.5 (attacks/seizures), F44.6 (anesthesia/sensory loss, special sensory), F44.7 (mixed) (p. 530).
- SSD prevalence updated: questionnaire studies 6.7–17.4%; general adult population ~4–6% (p. 520).
- Factitious disorder text notes falsification may target community members **online or in person** without engaging clinicians (p. 539).

## Chapter-level pitfalls & differential logic
- **Somatic symptoms significant? → SSD. Minimal/absent symptoms but health preoccupation? → IAD** (pp. 524, 528).
- **Neurological loss of function with positive incompatibility signs → FND**; SSD focuses on the distress/response to symptoms; both can be diagnosed together (pp. 524, 533).
- **Psychological factor → harms medical condition = PFAOMC**; medical condition → causes mental sx physiologically = mental disorder due to another medical condition; mental sx as a reaction to a medical condition = adjustment disorder (p. 536–537).
- **Deception?** None → SSD/IAD/FND. Deception for external reward → malingering. Deception without obvious external reward → factitious disorder (pp. 540–541). FND is not feigned; definite evidence of feigning moves toward malingering/factitious (pp. 533–534).
- **Psychiatric mimics**: panic (acute, episodic) vs SSD/IAD (persistent); GAD (health is one of many worries); depression (core mood/anhedonia); OCD (intrusive, future-oriented fears + other obsessions, rituals); BDD (appearance, not illness); delusional disorder, somatic type (delusional intensity, bizarre ideas) (pp. 524, 528–529).
- **Culture**: somatic "idioms of distress" (heaviness, "gas," heat in body, burning in head, semen loss, shenjing shuairuo); culturally sanctioned rituals, healers and illness beliefs should not be pathologized (pp. 522, 528, 533, 536).
- Never say symptoms aren't "real": SSD and FND suffering is authentic (p. 519).

## Shared specifiers & coding conventions
- **Severity**: SSD (mild/moderate/severe by number of B criteria); PFAOMC (mild/moderate/severe/extreme).
- **Course/duration**: SSD "persistent"; FND "acute episode" (<6 mo) vs "persistent" (≥6 mo); factitious "single episode" vs "recurrent episodes."
- **Type**: SSD "with predominant pain"; IAD care-seeking vs care-avoidant; FND symptom type (drives the code) and with/without psychological stressor.
- **Factitious imposed on another**: diagnosis goes to the **perpetrator**; victim may get an abuse code (e.g., T74.12X); both self and another may be diagnosed (pp. 538–539).
- PFAOMC (F54): requires a medical condition by definition; used when the psychological traits/behaviors do **not** meet criteria for another mental disorder (p. 537). This chapter's text gives no explicit recording-order note; naming the affected medical condition in the record is good practice.
