# Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Every disorder here involves poor self-control of emotion and/or behavior that shows up as **violating others' rights** (aggression, property destruction) or **clashing with norms and authority**. The disorders differ in how much of the problem is *emotional* (IED: anger out of proportion), *behavioral* (CD: rule/rights violations) or a mix (ODD). Pyromania and kleptomania are narrow impulse-control problems in which a specific act relieves internal tension. (pp. 727–728)

## What unites the chapter and how the disorders differ
- **Common thread:** dysregulated emotion and behavior that harms others or brings the person into conflict with authority. Other DSM disorders involve dysregulation too; what is unique here is the **externalized, rights- or norm-violating** expression (p. 727).
- **Emotion vs behavior gradient** (p. 727):
  - **Conduct disorder (CD):** mostly *behavioral*, i.e. violating rights or major norms. It may or may not come from poor emotional control, and some aggression reflects *constricted* emotion (callous-unemotional).
  - **Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD):** *balanced* between emotion (anger and irritability) and behavior (arguing and defiance), plus vindictiveness.
  - **Intermittent explosive disorder (IED):** mostly *emotional*, with anger outbursts far out of proportion to the provocation.
  - **Pyromania / kleptomania:** poor impulse control over **one specific act** (fire setting or stealing) that **relieves tension**.
- **Etiology** varies widely across disorders and across people within a diagnosis (p. 727).
- **Sex:** all are more common in males, but the size of the male excess varies by disorder and by age (kleptomania is the exception, with a 3:1 **female** excess) (pp. 727, 750).
- **Onset:** usually childhood or adolescence. **First onset of CD or ODD in adulthood is very rare** (p. 727).
- **Developmental link ODD → CD:** most childhood-onset CD was preceded by ODD, but **most children with ODD never develop CD**. ODD also raises the risk of anxiety and depressive disorders (pp. 727–728).
- **Externalizing spectrum:** the disorders are linked to disinhibition and some facets of negative emotionality, and inversely to constraint and agreeableness. This shared spectrum may explain their high mutual comorbidity and their comorbidity with SUDs and ASPD. The shared diathesis is still unknown (p. 728).
- **Normativity rule:** many of these behaviors occur in typically developing people. Judge their **frequency, persistence, pervasiveness across settings and impairment** against what is normative for the person's **age, gender and culture** (p. 728).

### Antisocial personality disorder (cross-reference, p. 747)
This chapter gives **no criteria** for ASPD; it only lists it. The criteria and text are in "Personality Disorders." ASPD is **dual-listed** because it sits on the same externalizing spectrum as CD and is closely tied to the Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders chapter. Key links back to this chapter:
- **CD Criterion C:** if the person is **≥18 years**, CD is diagnosed only if **ASPD criteria are not met**.
- Pyromania (Crit F) and kleptomania (Crit E) are excluded when the behavior is **better explained by ASPD**.
- In IED, the impulsive aggression of ASPD (and BPD) is **lower in level** than in IED, and IED Criterion F lists ASPD among the exclusions.

## TR-era notes visible in the text
The chapter text does not explicitly label TR changes. What appears in this TR text:
- **"Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior"** sections for IED (IED + PTSD → 41% lifetime attempts), CD, pyromania (data on fire setters) and kleptomania. ODD's raised suicide-attempt risk is listed under Associated Features.
- **Expanded culture sections:** possible misdiagnosis or overdiagnosis of disruptive disorders in some cultural groups. In racialized youth, anger and resistance-based coping in response to **racism** may be misread as CD. Migrants and refugees may have **lower** ODD/CD risk. Lower IED prevalence in Asia and the Middle East may reflect elicitation or cultural factors.
- **Terminology:** "intellectual developmental disorder (intellectual disability)."
- **ICD-10-CM codes as printed:** ODD F91.3; IED F63.81; CD F91.1 (childhood-onset) / F91.2 (adolescent-onset) / F91.9 (unspecified onset); pyromania F63.1; kleptomania F63.2; other specified F91.8; unspecified F91.9.
- **Coding quirk:** **F91.9** is used both for *CD, unspecified onset* and for *unspecified disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorder*. Record the name in words so the two can be told apart.

## Chapter-level differential logic
| Question | Points toward |
|---|---|
| Angry/irritable mood + arguing/defiance, **no** serious aggression, property destruction, theft or deceit | **ODD** |
| Aggression to people or animals, property destruction, theft/deceit, serious rule violations (≥3 of 15 in 12 mo) | **CD** |
| Impulsive, **unplanned** outbursts far out of proportion to the trigger, with **no** instrumental goal; age ≥6 | **IED** |
| Persistent irritable mood **between** outbursts most days; onset <10 y; first diagnosis not after 18 | **DMDD** (excludes ODD and IED) |
| Planned, **instrumental/predatory** aggression for gain | CD / ASPD, **not** IED |
| Behavior only during a mood or psychotic episode | Mood/psychotic disorder, **not** ODD/IED/CD |
| Onset tied to a stressor, resolving within 6 mo after the stressor ends | **Adjustment disorder** (with disturbance of conduct) |
| Fire setting driven by tension or fascination and relieved by pleasure, with no motive (money, revenge, ideology, concealment) | **Pyromania** |
| Stealing items not needed or wanted, with tension → relief, unplanned and done alone | **Kleptomania** |
| Age ≥18 with a pervasive antisocial pattern | **ASPD** (CD not diagnosed if ASPD criteria are met) |

**Allowed co-diagnoses:**
- ODD + CD can both be given.
- ADHD + CD, and ADHD + ODD, can both be given. For ODD, check that noncompliance is not limited to sustained-effort or sit-still demands.
- IED can be added to ADHD, CD, ODD or ASD when the outbursts exceed what is usual for those disorders **and warrant independent clinical attention**.
- **ODD is never diagnosed alongside DMDD**: DMDD trumps it.

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- **Severity by pervasiveness** (ODD): mild = 1 setting; moderate = ≥2 settings; severe = ≥3 settings.
- **Severity by number and harm** (CD): mild / moderate / severe.
- **CD onset subtype** sets the ICD code: before age 10 → F91.1; no symptom before 10 → F91.2; unknown → F91.9.
- **CD "with limited prosocial emotions"** (callous-unemotional traits): ≥2 of 4 traits, persistent ≥12 months, across multiple relationships and settings, and requires **multiple informants**.
- IED, pyromania and kleptomania have **no** severity or course specifiers in the text.
- Other specified (F91.8): record the reason, e.g. "recurrent behavioral outbursts of insufficient frequency."

## Chapter pitfalls
- Diagnosing ODD from a single clinic visit. Symptoms are most visible with familiar adults and peers and may not appear in the exam room, so use collateral.
- Counting interactions with **siblings** toward ODD. At least one non-sibling must be involved.
- Calling preschool weekly tantrums ODD. Weekly tantrums are common at that age; *daily* tantrums occur in only about 10% of preschoolers.
- Treating trauma reenactment, or anger in response to racism, as conduct problems.
- Diagnosing IED when the outbursts occur **only** during intoxication or withdrawal, or only in a mood or psychotic episode.
- Labeling ordinary shoplifting or arson-for-motive as kleptomania or pyromania. Both are **rare** and require tension → relief and no external motive.
