# Pyromania

> **Spark note:** **Repeated, deliberate fire setting** with **tension before** and **pleasure or relief during and after** the act. It is driven by **fascination with fire** and its context, and **not** by money, revenge, ideology, concealing a crime, delusions or impaired judgment. It is **very rare** as a primary diagnosis, even among repeat arsonists (~3%). Most fire setting, especially in youth, belongs to CD, ADHD, adjustment disorder or a motive-driven act.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Deliberate, purposeful fire setting on **>1 occasion**.
- **B.** **Tension or affective arousal** before the act.
- **C.** **Fascination with**, interest in, curiosity about or attraction to fire and its situational context (paraphernalia, uses, consequences).
- **D.** **Pleasure, gratification or relief** when setting fires or when witnessing or joining in the aftermath.
- **E.** Not done:
  - for money;
  - for sociopolitical ideology;
  - to conceal crime;
  - out of anger or vengeance;
  - to improve living circumstances;
  - in response to a delusion or hallucination;
  - from impaired judgment (major NCD, IDD, substance intoxication).
- **F.** Not better explained by **CD, a manic episode or ASPD**.
(p. 747)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- People with pyromania often **watch fires** in their neighborhood, set off **false alarms**, and enjoy fire-related institutions, equipment and personnel (p. 748).
- They may **hang around fire departments**, set fires to be affiliated with them, or **become firefighters** (p. 748).

## Associated features
- May **prepare considerably** in advance (p. 748).
- May be **indifferent** to harm to life or property, or satisfied by the destruction (p. 748).
- **Consequences:** property damage, legal trouble, injury or death (p. 748).
- **Impulsive fire setters** (with or without pyromania) often have current or past **alcohol use disorder** (p. 748).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Population prevalence is unknown** (p. 748).
- **Fire-setting behavior** (not sufficient for diagnosis): **1.0–1.1%** lifetime; men **1.7%** vs women **0.4%**. Common comorbidities of fire setting are ASPD, SUD, bipolar disorder and gambling disorder (p. 748).
- **Pyromania is rare** (p. 748):
  - **3.3%** of Finnish repeat fire setters in the criminal system met full criteria.
  - **3.4%** of US psychiatric inpatients met criteria for current pyromania.
- **Onset:** possibly **late adolescence** (p. 748).
- **Childhood link:** no documented link between childhood fire setting and adult pyromania (p. 748).
- **Course:** episodic, waxing and waning; longitudinal course unknown (p. 748).
- **Juvenile fire setting:** **>40%** of US arson arrestees are under 18, but childhood pyromania appears rare. Juvenile fire setting is usually tied to **CD, ADHD or adjustment disorder** (p. 748).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Fire setting is associated with antisocial behavior in both sexes, but the accompanying antisocial behaviors differ. It is unknown whether this applies to pyromania (p. 749).

## Suicide risk
- A matched-control study of male forensic fire setters found **higher suicide and suicide-attempt rates** at follow-up. It is unknown whether this applies to pyromania itself (p. 749).

## Functional consequences
- Not a separate section. See Associated features: property damage, legal consequences, injury or death (p. 748).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Other causes of intentional fire setting** (p. 749):
  - Profit, sabotage, revenge, concealing a crime.
  - Political statement (terrorism, protest).
  - **Attention or recognition**, e.g. setting a fire in order to "discover" it and be the hero.
  - **Developmental experimentation** in childhood (playing with matches or lighters).
- **Other mental disorders** (p. 749):
  - No separate diagnosis if the fire setting is part of **CD, a manic episode or ASPD**.
  - None if it responds to **delusions or hallucinations** (e.g. schizophrenia) or results from **another medical condition** (e.g. **epilepsy**).
  - None if it stems from impaired judgment in **major NCD, IDD or substance intoxication**.

## Comorbidity
- High co-occurrence with SUDs, **gambling disorder**, depressive and bipolar disorders, and other disruptive, impulse-control and conduct disorders (p. 749).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** list the fire-setting episodes (dates, targets, method) and show they were deliberate.
- **B and D:** document the **subjective sequence** in the client's words: rising tension or arousal → setting or watching the fire → pleasure, gratification or relief.
- **C:** evidence of fascination, e.g. collects fire paraphernalia, watches local fires, false alarms, interest in firefighting.
- **E:** explicitly document the **absence of motive**: no insurance or money gain, revenge, ideology, crime concealment, or housing gain. Also document **no psychosis**, no intoxication, and no cognitive or intellectual impairment at the time.
- **F:** document the screen for a broader antisocial pattern (CD or ASPD) and mania.
- Rule out epilepsy or another medical cause where indicated.
- **Session wording:**
  - "Describes building tension relieved only by lighting fires."
  - "Expresses excitement when discussing fire trucks and response."
  - "Denies financial or retaliatory motive; collateral consistent."
- **Risk:** document risk to others and property, means (lighters, accelerants), alcohol use, suicide screen, and legal status.
