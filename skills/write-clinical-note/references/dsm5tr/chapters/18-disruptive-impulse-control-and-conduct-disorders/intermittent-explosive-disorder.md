# Intermittent Explosive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Recurrent **impulsive, anger-based** aggressive outbursts that are far out of proportion to the trigger. The outbursts have a rapid onset, usually last **under 30 minutes**, and are often triggered by small provocations from someone close. There are two routes to Criterion A: **frequent low-level** outbursts (twice weekly for 3 months) **or** **3 damaging or injurious** outbursts in 12 months. The key discriminator is that the aggression is **not premeditated and not instrumental**. Planned aggression for money, power or intimidation points to CD or ASPD.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recurrent behavioral outbursts that reflect failure to control aggressive impulses, via **either** route (p. 734):
  1. **Verbal aggression** (tantrums, tirades, arguments, fights) **or physical aggression** toward property, animals or people, averaging **twice weekly for 3 months**. This physical aggression causes **no damage** to property and **no injury** to animals or people.
  2. **Three outbursts** involving property damage or destruction and/or physical assault **with injury** to animals or people, within **12 months**.
- **B.** The aggression is **grossly out of proportion** to the provocation or any psychosocial stressor (p. 734).
- **C.** The outbursts are **not premeditated** (impulsive and/or anger-based) and **not for a tangible goal** such as money, power or intimidation (p. 734).
- **D.** Causes marked distress, **or** occupational or interpersonal impairment, **or** financial or legal consequences (p. 734).
- **E.** **Age ≥6 years**, or the equivalent developmental level (p. 734).
- **F.** Not better explained by another mental disorder, e.g. MDD, bipolar disorder, DMDD, psychotic disorder, **ASPD** or **BPD** (pp. 734–735).
  - Not due to another medical condition (e.g. head trauma, Alzheimer's) or to a substance or medication.
  - **Ages 6–18:** aggression that is part of an **adjustment disorder** does not count.
- **Note:** can be diagnosed **in addition to ADHD, CD, ODD or ASD** when the impulsive outbursts **exceed** what is usual in those disorders **and warrant independent clinical attention** (p. 735).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Rapid onset**, little or no prodrome, typically **<30 min**. Often triggered by a **minor provocation from a close intimate or associate** (p. 735).
- Commonly, less severe A1-type episodes (verbal or nondamaging) occur **between** more severe A2-type destructive or assaultive episodes (p. 735).
- A2 damage counts **whatever the object's tangible value** (p. 735).
- **Core feature:** failure to control impulsive aggression in response to a subjectively experienced provocation that would not normally cause an outburst (p. 735).
- **Do not diagnose:** age <6; DMDD; outbursts due to a medical condition or substance; or ages 6–18 when the outbursts occur within an adjustment disorder (p. 735).
- **Neurological findings** (p. 738):
  - Nonspecific soft signs and nonspecific EEG changes are **compatible** with IED.
  - The exception is when a diagnosable medical condition better explains the outbursts.

## Associated features
- Depressive, anxiety and substance use disorders are associated with IED. They usually start **later** than IED (p. 735).
- **Neurobiology:**
  - Serotonergic abnormalities, globally and in the anterior cingulate and orbitofrontal cortex.
  - Greater amygdala response to anger stimuli on fMRI.
  - Reduced frontolimbic gray matter, inversely related to aggression. This finding is not always seen.
  - (p. 736)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **US prevalence:** **2.6%** over 1 year; **4.0%** lifetime (p. 736).
- **Higher 1-year rates in some US groups** (p. 736):
  - **3.9%** in African American adolescents and **6.9%** (narrow definition) in Caribbean Black adolescents, especially males.
  - The text links this possibly to downward social mobility and the effects of racism.
  - It also cautions that misdiagnosis or overdiagnosis can affect the reported prevalence of disruptive disorders in some cultural groups.
- More common in people **under ~35–40** than in those over 50, and in those with **high school education or less**. Sex differences are inconsistent (p. 736).
- **Onset:** most often late childhood or adolescence; **rarely first after age 40** (p. 736).
- **Course:** may be episodic, but is generally **chronic and persistent over many years**. Common whether or not ADHD or other disruptive disorders are present (p. 736).
- **Environmental risk:** physical or emotional **trauma in the first 20 years** of life. Among some refugees, long-term displacement and separation from family (p. 736).
- **Genetic risk:** first-degree relatives are at increased risk. Twin studies show **substantial genetic influence** on impulsive aggression (p. 736).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Lower prevalence in some regions (Asia, Middle East) and countries (Romania, Nigeria) than in the US. This may be because the behavior is **not elicited** on questioning or is truly less frequent for cultural reasons (p. 737).
- Sex differences are inconsistent across studies (p. 736).

## Suicide risk
- In a sample of 1,460 volunteers, **IED + PTSD** had a **41%** lifetime suicide-attempt rate (p. 737).
- Among soldiers with suicidal ideation, PTSD and IED were the only disorders associated with suicide attempts. IED's role was less clear in multivariate analysis (p. 737).

## Functional consequences
- **Social:** lost friends and relatives, marital instability.
- **Occupational:** demotion, job loss.
- **Financial:** the value of destroyed objects.
- **Legal:** civil suits, assault charges.
- (p. 737)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Episode-bound outbursts:** do not diagnose IED if Criteria A1/A2 are met **only during an episode** of MDD, bipolar disorder or a psychotic disorder. The same applies to outbursts from a medical condition or substance, and especially, for ages 6–18, outbursts within an **adjustment disorder** (p. 737).
- **DMDD:** **persistent negative mood** (irritability, anger) most of the day, nearly every day, **between** outbursts. Requires onset **<10 y**, and is never first diagnosed after **18 y**. Otherwise DMDD and IED are **mutually exclusive** (p. 737).
- **ASPD / BPD:** recurrent impulsive aggression occurs, but at a **lower level** than in IED (pp. 737–738).
- **Delirium, major NCD, personality change due to another medical condition (aggressive type):** do not diagnose IED if the outbursts result from a diagnosable medical condition, e.g. brain injury with personality change, or complex partial epilepsy (p. 738).
- **Substance intoxication or withdrawal:** do not diagnose IED if the outbursts are **nearly always** tied to intoxication or withdrawal (alcohol, PCP, cocaine and other stimulants, barbiturates, inhalants). IED can be given if **enough outbursts also occur while sober** and they warrant attention (p. 738).
- **ADHD, CD, ODD, ASD** (p. 738):
  - ADHD involves general impulsivity.
  - CD aggression is **proactive and predatory**.
  - ODD aggression is mostly tantrums and verbal arguments **with authority figures**.
  - IED outbursts respond to a **broader range of provocations** and may include physical assault. Physical assault is **not mandatory** when the verbal-aggression route in A1 is satisfied.
  - If Criteria A–E are met and the outbursts warrant independent attention, add IED.

## Comorbidity
- **Community samples:** depressive disorders, anxiety disorders, PTSD, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder, SUDs (p. 738).
- **Higher risk of comorbid IED:** ASPD, BPD, and a history of ADHD, CD or ODD (p. 738).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Outburst log (Criterion A):**
  - For each outburst, record the date, trigger, form (verbal, nondamaging physical, property damage, injury), duration and target.
  - State the route: "≈2 verbal or nondamaging outbursts/week × 3 months" (A1) or "3 damaging or injurious outbursts in past 12 months," with a list (A2).
- **Proportionality (B):** describe the trigger next to the response, e.g. "punched wall and broke door after partner commented on lateness."
- **Impulsivity (C):** "outbursts are unplanned, rapid onset, followed by regret or relief." Document that no goal was sought, i.e. **no** money, control or intimidation.
- **Consequences (D):** relationship breakdown, job discipline, police contact, repair costs.
- **Age/development (E):** state chronological or developmental age ≥6.
- **Rule-outs to document (F):**
  - Outbursts occurring outside mood or psychotic episodes.
  - Sobriety status during outbursts (BAL/UDS history if available).
  - Head injury, seizures, dementia.
  - DMDD (inter-episode mood).
  - ASPD and BPD patterns.
  - Adjustment disorder for ages 6–18.
- **MSE/session wording:**
  - "Calm and euthymic between episodes."
  - "Describes sudden, 'red-out' anger lasting minutes, out of proportion to the provocation."
  - "Expresses remorse and embarrassment after episodes."
- **Risk:** screen for **suicide** (especially with comorbid PTSD) and for **violence toward others**. Document access to weapons and the safety plan.
