# Other Specified and Unspecified Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorder

> **Spark note:** Use these residual categories when disruptive, impulse-control or conduct symptoms **predominate** and cause clinically significant distress or impairment, but full criteria for ODD, IED, CD, pyromania or kleptomania are not met. "Other specified" **states the reason**. "Unspecified" does not state it, or there is too little information (e.g. in the ER).

## Criteria at a glance
**Both categories** (pp. 751–752):
- Symptoms characteristic of a disruptive, impulse-control and conduct disorder **predominate**.
- They cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational or other important functioning.
- They do **not** meet full criteria for any disorder in this class.

**Other specified (F91.8)** (pp. 751–752):
- The clinician **chooses to communicate the specific reason** criteria are not met.
- Record "other specified disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorder" plus the reason.
- **Example given in the text:** "recurrent behavioral outbursts of insufficient frequency" (e.g. an IED-like picture below the A1/A2 thresholds).
- The text gives **no other enumerated examples**.

**Unspecified (F91.9)** (p. 752):
- The clinician **chooses not to specify** the reason, **or** there is **insufficient information** for a more specific diagnosis (e.g. **emergency room** settings).

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| F91.8 | Other specified disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorder (+ reason) |
| F91.9 | Unspecified disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorder |

**Caution:** F91.9 is **also** the code for *conduct disorder, unspecified onset*. Always write out the full diagnosis name.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The chapter introduction (p. 727) describes the other specified category as covering symptoms of CD, ODD or other disorders in the class whose **number or type** falls below threshold despite clinically significant impairment.
- **Illustrative subthreshold uses** (clinical extrapolation; only the first is the text's own example):
  - IED-type outbursts below the frequency threshold (text example).
  - Conduct problems with only 2 of 15 CD criteria but clear impairment.
  - ODD symptoms present for less than 6 months but not tied to a stressor.
- Before using these codes, check **adjustment disorder** (stressor-linked) and **DMDD**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Specific disorders in the class:** re-check thresholds, durations and the non-sibling requirement (ODD).
- **Adjustment disorder** (with disturbance of conduct): onset linked to a stressor.
- **DMDD, mood or psychotic disorders, substance or medical causes:** these exclude several disorders in the class.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Other specified:** state the **exact unmet element**, e.g. "verbal outbursts ~1×/week for 4 months (below IED A1 frequency); marked occupational impairment."
- **Unspecified:** note **why** a more specific diagnosis cannot be made yet (e.g. "single ED contact; collateral unavailable; reassess").
- Document the impairment and distress, the behaviors observed or reported, and the rule-outs considered (adjustment disorder, mood, substances, medical).
- Plan to **re-evaluate** and convert to a specific diagnosis once enough information is available.
