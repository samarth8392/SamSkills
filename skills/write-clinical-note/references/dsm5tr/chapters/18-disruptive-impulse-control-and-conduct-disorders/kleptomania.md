# Kleptomania

> **Spark note:** Recurrent failure to resist impulses to steal objects the person **does not need or want for their value**. The theft is preceded by **rising tension** and brings **pleasure or relief**. The items are often given away, thrown out, hoarded or secretly returned. The thefts are **unplanned, done alone**, and followed by **guilt**. It is **rare** (0.3–0.6%) and **female-predominant (3:1)**. Ordinary shoplifting is common and motivated by usefulness or value, so it does not count.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recurrent failure to resist impulses to steal objects **not needed for personal use or for their monetary value**.
- **B.** **Increasing tension immediately before** the theft.
- **C.** **Pleasure, gratification or relief** at the time of the theft.
- **D.** Not done to express **anger or vengeance**; not a response to a **delusion or hallucination**.
- **E.** Not better explained by **CD, a manic episode or ASPD**.
(pp. 749–750)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The objects are usually of **little value** to the person, who **could have paid** for them. They are often **given away or discarded**, occasionally **hoarded** or **secretly returned** (p. 750).
- The person usually avoids stealing when arrest is **immediately likely** (e.g. a police officer in view). Thefts are **usually not preplanned**, and the risk of being caught may not be fully weighed; these are descriptive tendencies, not additional lettered criteria (p. 750).
- Always done **without help from, or collaboration with, others** (p. 750).

## Associated features
- The person typically **tries to resist** and knows the act is **wrong and senseless** (p. 750).
- Frequently **fears apprehension** and feels **depressed or guilty** about the thefts (p. 750).
- **Neurobiology:** serotonin, dopamine and opioid pathways (behavioral-addiction circuitry) appear to be involved (p. 750).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **4–24%** of people arrested for shoplifting in the US and Canada (p. 750).
- **General population:** **0.3–0.6%**. **Women:men = 3:1** (p. 750).
- **Onset:** often **adolescence**, but can begin in childhood, adulthood or, rarely, late adulthood (p. 750).
- **Three described courses** (p. 750):
  1. **Sporadic:** brief episodes, long remissions.
  2. **Episodic:** protracted stealing periods plus remissions.
  3. **Chronic:** with fluctuation.
- Can persist for years **despite multiple shoplifting convictions** (pp. 750–751).
- **Genetic risk:** higher rate of **alcohol use disorder in first-degree relatives** (p. 751).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Women outnumber men 3:1 (p. 750). No separate culture section.

## Suicide risk
- Associated with **increased risk of suicide attempts** (p. 751).

## Functional consequences
- Legal, family, career and personal difficulties (p. 751).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Ordinary theft:**
  - Ordinary theft, planned or impulsive, is **deliberate** and motivated by the item's **use or value**.
  - Adolescents may steal on a **dare**, as **rebellion** or as a **rite of passage**.
  - Diagnose kleptomania only if its other characteristic features are present. Kleptomania is rare; shoplifting is common.
  - (p. 751)
- **Malingering:** symptoms may be **simulated to avoid prosecution** (p. 751).
- **ASPD / CD:** distinguished by a **general pattern of antisocial behavior** (p. 751).
- **Manic episodes, psychotic episodes, major NCD:** intentional or inadvertent stealing during mania, in response to delusions or hallucinations (e.g. schizophrenia), or from major NCD is not kleptomania (pp. 751).

## Comorbidity
- **Compulsive buying** (p. 751).
- Depressive and bipolar disorders, **especially MDD** (p. 751).
- Anxiety disorders (p. 751).
- Eating disorders, **especially bulimia nervosa** (p. 751).
- Personality disorders (p. 751).
- SUDs, **especially alcohol use disorder** (p. 751).
- Other disruptive, impulse-control and conduct disorders (p. 751).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** describe the stolen items (low value, not needed) and what happened to them: discarded, given away, hoarded, returned. Note **ability to pay**.
- **B and C:** record the client's description of **mounting tension → relief or gratification** at the moment of theft.
- **D:** document **no anger or revenge motive** and **no psychotic content**.
- **E:** document the absence of a broader antisocial pattern (CD or ASPD) and of mania.
- **Pattern features:** unplanned, **alone**, resists urges unsuccessfully, ego-dystonic **guilt or shame** afterward.
- **Malingering screen:** note any pending charges or legal context and consistency over time. Obtain collateral or records where possible.
- **Session wording:**
  - "Reports urges to take small items she does not want; attempts to resist."
  - "Describes relief followed by guilt; disposes of items."
  - "Insight intact; recognizes behavior as senseless."
- **Comorbidity and risk:** screen for depression, bulimia, alcohol use and compulsive buying. Complete a **suicide-attempt risk screen**.
