# Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder

> **Spark note:** A disorder of **chronic, severe, nonepisodic irritability** in children aged 6–18,
> with onset before age 10. It shows up two ways: frequent explosive outbursts (≥3/wk) **and** an angry or irritable
> mood *between* outbursts, for ≥12 months, across settings. It was added to curb the overdiagnosis of pediatric
> bipolar disorder. These children tend to develop unipolar depression and anxiety, **not** bipolar disorder.
> Common error: diagnosing DMDD alongside ODD, IED or bipolar disorder. It **trumps** ODD and IED and is
> **excluded** by any history of mania or hypomania.

## Criteria at a glance
(pp. 303–304)
- **A.** Severe, recurrent temper outbursts, verbal (rages) and/or behavioral (aggression toward people/property), **grossly out of proportion** in intensity or duration to the provocation.
- **B.** Outbursts are inconsistent with developmental level.
- **C.** Outbursts occur on average **≥3 times per week**.
- **D.** Mood between outbursts is **persistently irritable or angry**, most of the day, nearly every day, and **observable by others** (parents, teachers, peers).
- **E.** A–D present for **≥12 months**, with **no period of ≥3 consecutive months** free of all A–D symptoms.
- **F.** A and D present in **≥2 of 3 settings** (home, school, peers) and **severe in ≥1**. The differential text adds: severe in one and at least mild–moderate in a second.
- **G.** Do not make the first diagnosis **before age 6 or after age 18**.
- **H.** Onset of A–E **before age 10** (by history or observation).
- **I.** **Never** a distinct period lasting **>1 day** in which full manic/hypomanic symptom criteria (except duration) were met. Developmentally appropriate elation (e.g., about a very positive event) does not count.
- **J.** Not exclusively during an MDE, and not better explained by another disorder (ASD, PTSD, separation anxiety disorder, PDD).
  - **Cannot coexist with ODD, IED or bipolar disorder.** If criteria for both DMDD and ODD are met, give **only DMDD**. Any lifetime manic/hypomanic episode rules DMDD out.
  - **Can coexist with** MDD, ADHD, conduct disorder and SUDs.
- **K.** Not due to a substance or another medical/neurological condition.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The core feature is **chronic, severe, persistent irritability**, with two manifestations:
  1. **Frequent temper outbursts.** Usually triggered by frustration; can be verbal or behavioral (aggression against property, self or others).
  2. **Chronic irritable/angry mood** between outbursts. This is characteristic of the child and noticeable to others.
- **Historical context:** In the late 20th century, some researchers framed severe nonepisodic irritability as pediatric mania, and pediatric bipolar diagnoses rose sharply as a result. DSM-5 reserves "bipolar" for **episodic** presentations, and DMDD captures the nonepisodic irritable group.
- Irritability may wax and wane, but severe irritability is the child's baseline. **There is no distinct "different from usual" period**, unlike mania.

## Associated features
- Information-processing deficits (face-emotion labeling, decision-making, cognitive control) overlap with pediatric bipolar disorder, but may rest on different neural dysfunction.
- Some deficits are disorder-specific, e.g., attention deployment to emotional stimuli.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** common in pediatric mental health clinics; community rates unclear. **2.5%** in Brazilian 11-year-olds; **8.2%** in a US community sample of 6-year-olds, so it may be more common at younger ages.
- **Sex:** no consistent difference in population samples. Clinic samples are mostly male (up to 80% boys in one Turkish chart review).
- **Course:** about **half still meet criteria 1 year later**. Those who drop below threshold often keep clinically impairing irritability.
- **Conversion to bipolar disorder is very low.** Risk is instead for **unipolar depressive and/or anxiety disorders** in adulthood.
- *Temperamental:* a long history of chronic irritability often precedes full criteria and may earlier have qualified as **ODD**. ADHD and anxiety disorders are often present early. MDD criteria are sometimes also met.
- *Environmental:* disrupted family life: psychological abuse/neglect, parental psychiatric disorder, low parental education, single-parent household, early trauma, parental death, parental grief, divorce, malnutrition (e.g., vitamin deficiency).
- *Genetic/physiological:* family history of depression may be a risk factor. Twin data suggest the link from early irritability to later depression and anxiety is partly genetic.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Data are limited. Sociocultural adversity (post-conflict zones, long-standing racism and discrimination) affects impulsivity and emotion, reward, threat and behavior regulation. **Distinguish DMDD from transient, context-dependent adaptive responses to adversity.**
- Twin studies: irritability is strongly genetic in both sexes. In boys, genetic variance increases through childhood. In girls, genetic influence is high at school age but falls in adolescence as environmental influence grows.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Marked disruption of family and peer relationships and of school performance. Very low frustration tolerance makes it hard to succeed in school, join typical activities or keep friends.
- Impairment is generally **comparable to pediatric bipolar disorder**. **Aggression and psychiatric hospitalization are common.**

## Differential diagnosis
- **Bipolar disorders:** the key is **longitudinal course**. Bipolar disorder is **episodic**, with a distinct period of mood change, clearly different from baseline, plus associated symptoms (distractibility, increased goal-directed activity). DMDD is persistent over months. **Elevated/expansive mood and grandiosity** point to mania. Any full-duration manic or hypomanic episode, or any >1-day manic/hypomanic period, excludes DMDD.
- **ODD:** ODD symptoms are usually present in DMDD, but DMDD mood symptoms are uncommon in ODD (**only ~15% of ODD** meets DMDD criteria). DMDD requires severe, frequent outbursts **plus** persistent mood disruption, severe impairment in one setting and mild–moderate in another. If both are met, **diagnose DMDD only**.
- **IED:** also severe outbursts, but **no persistent irritable mood between outbursts**. IED needs only **3 months** of symptoms (verbal or non-damaging physical aggression ≥2×/wk) versus 12 months for DMDD. **Never diagnose both.** Outbursts plus persistent irritability = DMDD.
- **ADHD, MDD, anxiety disorders, ASD:**
  - ADHD, MDD and anxiety disorders **can** be comorbid with DMDD.
  - If irritability occurs **only** during an MDE or PDD, diagnose that disorder instead.
  - If irritability occurs **only** during exacerbations of an anxiety disorder, diagnose the anxiety disorder instead.
  - Outbursts in **ASD** when routines are disrupted are secondary to ASD, so no DMDD. The same applies to disrupted routines in OCD.

## Comorbidity
- **Extremely high**. DMDD alone is rare. The strongest overlap is with **ODD**, and the range is broad: disruptive behavior, mood, anxiety and ASD symptoms.
- Not with bipolar disorder (diagnose bipolar only), and not with ODD or IED (diagnose DMDD only).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Outburst log:** frequency per week (average ≥3), form (verbal rage, property destruction, aggression toward people), duration and intensity compared with the trigger ("30-min screaming episode after being asked to turn off tablet"), and developmental inappropriateness.
- **Interepisode mood:** document irritability/anger **most of the day, nearly every day**, with reports from ≥2 informants (parent, teacher), e.g. "described by teacher as 'grumpy all day' even on outburst-free days."
- **Settings:** list home, school and peers, and rate severity in each (severe in ≥1).
- **Timeline:** onset before age 10, current age 6–18, total ≥12 months, longest symptom-free stretch <3 months.
- **Bipolar rule-out:** document absence of any distinct period (>1 day) of elevated/expansive mood, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep or increased goal-directed activity that differed from baseline. Ask parents to identify any "different from usual" period.
- **Other rule-outs:** irritability not confined to an MDE/PDD or anxiety flare; outbursts not explained by ASD or OCD routine disruption; no substance or medical/neurological cause. Note any ODD/IED symptoms and that DMDD supersedes them.
- **Context:** family adversity and trauma exposure. Distinguish from transient adaptive responses in highly adverse environments.
- **MSE phrases:** "irritable, sullen affect throughout interview"; "low frustration tolerance; became verbally aggressive when task was difficult"; "no pressured speech, grandiosity or flight of ideas."
