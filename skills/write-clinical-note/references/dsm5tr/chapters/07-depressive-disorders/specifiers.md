# Specifiers for Depressive Disorders

> **Spark note:** These specifiers describe the **current MDE** (or the **most recent** one if MDD is in partial or full remission).
> Only **anxious distress** and **atypical features** also apply to **PDD**. **Seasonal pattern** describes the *pattern* of recurrent MDD, not a
> single episode. Only **psychotic features** (and remission/severity) change the MDD code. **Catatonia** adds **F06.1**. Common
> confusion: melancholic and atypical are mutually exclusive in the same episode, and mixed features means subthreshold mania *within* an MDE
> (full mania/hypomania = bipolar disorder).

## With anxious distress (pp. 342–343)
- **≥2 of 5**, present on the **majority of days** of the current MDE (or most recent MDE if in remission) or current PDD:
  1. Feeling keyed up or tense
  2. Unusually restless
  3. Trouble concentrating because of worry
  4. Fear something awful may happen
  5. Feeling one might lose control of oneself
- **Severity:** **Mild** = 2 symptoms · **Moderate** = 3 · **Moderate-severe** = 4–5 · **Severe** = 4–5 **plus motor agitation**.
- **Why it matters:** prominent in both bipolar and MDD, in primary care and specialty settings. High anxiety is linked to **higher suicide risk, longer illness and more treatment nonresponse**. Specify it for treatment planning and monitoring.

## With mixed features (p. 343)
- **A.** **≥3 of 7** manic/hypomanic symptoms on the **majority of days** of the current (or most recent) MDE:
  1. Elevated, expansive mood
  2. Inflated self-esteem/grandiosity
  3. More talkative or pressured speech
  4. Flight of ideas or racing thoughts
  5. More energy or goal-directed activity (social, work/school, sexual)
  6. Excessive involvement in risky activities (spending sprees, sexual indiscretions, foolish investments)
  7. **Decreased need for sleep** (feels rested on less sleep; contrast with insomnia)
- **B.** **Observable by others** and a change from usual behavior.
- **C.** If full mania or hypomania criteria are met, diagnose **bipolar I or II** instead.
- **D.** Not due to a substance (drug, medication, other treatment).
- **Note:** mixed features in an MDE are a **significant risk factor for later bipolar I/II**. Record them for treatment planning.
- Note the list **omits distractibility, irritability and psychomotor agitation**, which overlap with depression.

## With melancholic features (pp. 343–344)
- **A.** At the **most severe period** of the current (or most recent) episode, **≥1**:
  1. Loss of pleasure in all or almost all activities
  2. **Lack of reactivity** to usually pleasurable stimuli (doesn't feel much better even briefly)
- **B.** **≥3 of 6**:
  1. **Distinct quality** of depressed mood (profound despondency, despair, moroseness, or "empty" mood)
  2. Depression **regularly worse in the morning**
  3. **Early-morning awakening** (≥2 hours before usual)
  4. **Marked** psychomotor agitation or retardation
  5. Significant anorexia or weight loss
  6. Excessive or inappropriate guilt
- **Pearls:**
  - Near-**complete absence** of pleasure capacity, not just a reduction.
  - Reactivity guideline: even highly desired events don't markedly brighten mood. Either no brightening, or only partial (~**20–40% of normal for minutes**).
  - "Distinct quality" means **qualitatively different** from nonmelancholic depression. Mood that is just more severe, longer lasting or without reason does **not** qualify.
  - Psychomotor changes are **nearly always present and observable**.
  - Only a modest tendency to recur across episodes. More common in **inpatients**, in more severe episodes, and **with psychotic features**. Linked to HPA-axis hyperactivity (MDD associated features).

## With atypical features (pp. 344–345)
Applies when these **predominate on the majority of days** of the current (or most recent) MDE or current PDD.
- **A.** **Mood reactivity:** mood brightens with actual or potential positive events.
- **B.** **≥2 of 4**:
  1. Significant weight gain or increased appetite
  2. **Hypersomnia** (≥10 h/day including naps, or ≥2 h more than when not depressed)
  3. **Leaden paralysis** (heavy, weighted-down arms/legs; usually ≥1 h/day, often many hours)
  4. **Long-standing interpersonal rejection sensitivity** (trait, not limited to episodes) causing significant social or occupational impairment
- **C.** Not met for "with melancholic features" or "with catatonia" in the same episode.
- **Pearls:**
  - "Atypical" is **historical**: it contrasts with classic agitated or "endogenous" depression. It does **not** mean uncommon.
  - With reactivity, mood may be euthymic for long periods if circumstances stay favorable.
  - Rejection sensitivity is an **early-onset trait** present whether or not the person is depressed, though it may worsen during episodes.
  - More common in women and younger people (MDD text).

## With psychotic features (p. 345)
- Delusions and/or hallucinations at **any time** in the current (or most recent) MDE. **Code it regardless of severity** (F32.3 / F33.3).
- **With mood-congruent psychotic features:** **all** content fits depressive themes: personal inadequacy, guilt, disease, death, nihilism, deserved punishment.
- **With mood-incongruent psychotic features:** content does **not** involve those themes, **or is a mix** of congruent and incongruent themes.
- Linked to lower recovery rates, later conversion to bipolar disorder or schizophrenia, and melancholia (MDD course section).

## With catatonia (p. 345)
- Applies to the current (or most recent) MDE if catatonic features are present **during most of the episode**.
- Use the criteria for **catatonia associated with another mental disorder** (Schizophrenia Spectrum chapter).
- **Coding:** add **F06.1** after the MDD code.

## With peripartum onset (pp. 345–346)
- Applies to the current (or most recent) MDE if mood symptoms begin **during pregnancy or within 4 weeks after delivery**.
- **~50% of "postpartum" MDEs start before delivery**, hence "peripartum."
- **Prevalence:** ~**9%** have an MDE between conception and birth. Just under **7%** between birth and 12 months postpartum.
- **With or without psychosis.** **Infanticide** is rare and most often tied to postpartum psychosis with **command hallucinations** to kill the infant or **delusions that the infant is possessed**. Psychosis can also occur without such content.
- **Postpartum mood episodes with psychotic features:** **1 in 500 to 1 in 1,000 deliveries**, possibly more common in **primiparous** women. Risk is highest with prior postpartum psychotic episodes. It is also raised by a prior depressive or bipolar disorder (**especially bipolar I**) and by family history of bipolar disorder. **Recurrence with each later delivery: 30–50%.**
- Distinguish from **postpartum delirium** (fluctuating awareness or attention).
- Distinguish from **"maternity blues"/"baby blues"**: **not a disorder**. Sudden mood shifts (tearfulness without depression), sleep disturbance, even brief confusion soon after delivery, **without functional impairment**. Self-limited, usually improves **within a week** without treatment.
- **Thyroid and other perinatal medical causes:** if depression is due to a perinatal medical condition, diagnose **depressive disorder due to another medical condition**, not MDE with peripartum onset.

## With seasonal pattern (pp. 346–347)
Applies to **recurrent MDD** only and describes the pattern of episodes.
- **A.** Regular temporal relationship between MDE **onset** and a particular season (e.g., fall/winter). Exclude cases with obvious **seasonal psychosocial stressors** (e.g., unemployed every winter, school schedule).
- **B.** **Full remissions** also occur at a characteristic time (e.g., spring).
- **C.** In the **last 2 years**, **2 MDEs** showing this seasonal relationship and **no nonseasonal MDEs** in that period.
- **D.** Over the lifetime, seasonal MDEs **substantially outnumber** nonseasonal MDEs.
- **Pearls:**
  - Usually fall/winter onset with spring remission. **Summer** episodes are less common.
  - Typical symptoms: loss of energy, **hypersomnia, overeating, weight gain, carbohydrate craving** (atypical-like).
  - Winter-type prevalence **rises with higher latitude**, varies with sex, and is higher in **younger** people.

## Remission (p. 347)
- **In partial remission:** some symptoms of the last MDE remain but full criteria aren't met, **or** there has been <2 months without significant MDE symptoms since the episode ended.
- **In full remission:** **no significant signs or symptoms for the past 2 months**.
- For MDD, remission specifiers are used **only when full MDE criteria are not currently met**. Severity and psychotic specifiers are used **only when they are**.

## Severity (pp. 347–348)
Based on **symptom count, symptom intensity and functional disability**.
- **Mild:** few or no symptoms beyond the minimum; distressing but **manageable**; **minor** impairment.
- **Moderate:** between mild and severe.
- **Severe:** symptom count **substantially exceeds** the minimum; **seriously distressing and unmanageable**; **marked** interference with social and occupational function.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- For every specifier, **list the qualifying criteria observed** and the time frame ("majority of days" vs "most severe period" vs "any time").
- **Anxious distress:** count the symptoms (2/3/4–5) and note motor agitation, which determines the severity grade.
- **Mixed features:** name the ≥3 manic symptoms, who observed them, and that full hypomania/mania criteria are **not** met. Flag the bipolar risk in the plan.
- **Melancholic:** document non-reactivity (e.g., "no brightening when daughter visited"), diurnal variation (worse AM), early-morning awakening ≥2 h, and observed psychomotor change.
- **Atypical:** document mood reactivity plus ≥2 features with numbers (sleep hours, weight change, leaden-paralysis duration). Note rejection sensitivity as a lifelong trait. Confirm melancholic/catatonic features are absent.
- **Psychotic:** quote the theme in paraphrase (e.g., guilt delusion about causing family's ruin = congruent) and classify it.
- **Peripartum:** date of delivery vs symptom onset (pregnancy or ≤4 wk postpartum); infant-related thoughts (intrusive vs delusional, any command hallucinations); infant safety plan; thyroid screen; distinguish from blues (impairment? >1–2 weeks?).
- **Seasonal:** list episode onset/offset months over ≥2 years; confirm no nonseasonal episodes in that span; exclude seasonal stressors.
- **Remission:** date the last full-criteria MDE and the length of the near-symptom-free interval (≥2 months = full).
