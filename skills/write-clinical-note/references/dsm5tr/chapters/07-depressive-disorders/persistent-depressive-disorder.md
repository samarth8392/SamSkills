# Persistent Depressive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Chronic depression: depressed mood **more days than not for ≥2 years** (≥1 yr in youth)
> plus ≥2 of 6 symptoms, with no gap longer than 2 months. It merges DSM-IV **dysthymia** and **chronic MDD**. MDEs may occur within it.
> When they do, **diagnose both PDD and MDD** and use the PDD course specifier. Common error: missing it because
> patients see the symptoms as their personality ("I've always been this way") and don't report them unless asked directly.

## Criteria at a glance
(pp. 320–321)
- **A.** Depressed mood most of the day, **more days than not**, by self-report or others' observation, for **≥2 years**. *Kids/teens: may be irritable; duration ≥1 year.*
- **B.** While depressed, **≥2 of 6**:
  1. Poor appetite or overeating
  2. Insomnia or hypersomnia
  3. Low energy/fatigue
  4. Low self-esteem
  5. Poor concentration or indecisiveness
  6. Hopelessness
- **C.** During the 2-year (1-year youth) period, never without A and B symptoms for **>2 months at a time**.
- **D.** MDD criteria may be continuously present for the 2 years.
- **E.** **Never** a manic or hypomanic episode.
- **F.** Not better explained by persistent schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia, delusional disorder or other specified/unspecified psychotic disorder.
- **G.** Not due to a substance (drug, medication) or another medical condition (e.g., hypothyroidism).
- **H.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note:** if full MDE criteria are met at any time in the period, **add a separate MDD diagnosis** plus the relevant PDD specifier.

Note that the PDD symptom list **omits** anhedonia, psychomotor change and death/suicidal thoughts, which are in the MDE list.

## Specifiers & severity
See [specifiers.md](specifiers.md) for full definitions.
- **Features:** with anxious distress; with atypical features (the only feature specifiers allowed for PDD).
- **Remission:** in partial remission; in full remission.
- **Onset:** **early onset** (<21 y) vs **late onset** (≥21 y).
- **Course pattern over the most recent 2 years:**
  | Specifier | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | With pure dysthymic syndrome | Full MDE criteria **not met** in at least the preceding 2 yr |
  | With persistent major depressive episode | Full MDE criteria met **throughout** the preceding 2 yr |
  | With intermittent MDEs, **with current episode** | Full MDE now, but there have been periods of **≥8 weeks** below MDE threshold in at least the preceding 2 yr |
  | With intermittent MDEs, **without current episode** | MDE not currently met, but **≥1 MDE** in at least the preceding 2 yr |
- **Current severity:** mild / moderate / severe (same anchors as MDD).

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| F34.1 | Persistent depressive disorder (all specifiers are uncoded) |
| + F32.x / F33.x | Add a separate MDD code when MDE criteria have been met (e.g., with persistent MDE or intermittent MDEs) |

Recording example: "F34.1 Persistent depressive disorder, early onset, with intermittent major depressive episodes, with current episode, moderate, with anxious distress, moderate" plus "F33.1 Major depressive disorder, recurrent episode, moderate."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Mood is described as sad or "down in the dumps". ≥2 of the 6 B symptoms are present during depressed periods.
- Symptoms are **ego-syntonic and long-standing**, especially with early onset, so they **may not be reported unless directly asked**.
- MDD can precede PDD, and MDEs can occur during PDD. If MDD criteria have been met for 2 years, give **both** PDD and MDD.
- **Ask about symptom-free intervals.** Any such interval must have been ≤2 months.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **US 12-month prevalence:** **~0.5% dysthymic disorder**, **~1.5% chronic MDD**. Women are ~1.5× and ~2× higher, respectively.
- Lifetime and 12-month dysthymia may be higher in high-income countries, but suicide risk and disability are elevated everywhere.
- **Onset:** often early and insidious (childhood, adolescence, early adulthood); chronic by definition.
- **Borderline PD** is a particularly robust risk factor. When the two coexist, their features move together over time, suggesting a shared mechanism.
- **Early onset (<21)** is linked to more comorbid personality disorders and SUDs.
- When symptoms rise to MDE level they tend to fall back, but they are **much less likely to resolve fully** than in nonchronic MDE.
- *Temperamental:* poorer outcome is predicted by high neuroticism, greater severity, poorer global functioning, and comorbid anxiety or conduct disorder.
- *Environmental:* childhood parental loss or separation; childhood adversity.
- *Genetic/physiological:* no clear differences between dysthymia and chronic MDD. First-degree relatives are likely to have more PDD and more depressive disorders than relatives of people with nonchronic MDD. Implicated regions: prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate, amygdala, hippocampus. Possible polysomnographic abnormalities.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Cultures and age cohorts differ in how much chronic depressive symptoms are seen as abnormal. Some see long-standing symptoms as a **normal response to adversity**, which affects detection and willingness to accept treatment.

## Suicide risk
- Elevated risk of suicidal outcomes, with disability comparable across high-, middle- and low-income countries.

## Functional consequences
- Varies widely but **can be as great as or greater than MDD**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **MDD:** if depressed mood more days than not + ≥2 B symptoms for ≥2 years = PDD. Add MDD if MDE criteria were met at any point, and pick the course specifier.
- **Other specified / unspecified depressive disorder:** a few people have >2 years of depressive symptoms that don't meet PDD criteria, because the MDE list contains items (anhedonia, psychomotor change, SI) that are not in the PDD list. If full MDE criteria were met during the current illness episode, diagnose MDD; otherwise use OSDD or UDD.
- **Bipolar I/II:** any manic or hypomanic episode **precludes** PDD. Mania means bipolar I. Hypomania with past MDE (no mania) means bipolar II. Hypomanic episodes with a chronic depressive presentation that has **never met MDE criteria** = **other specified bipolar and related disorder**.
- **Cyclothymic disorder precludes PDD.** If during ≥2 years of depressed mood there were **numerous subthreshold hypomanic periods**, no symptom-free gap >2 months, and **never** a full MDE, manic or hypomanic episode, the diagnosis is cyclothymic disorder.
- **Psychotic disorders:** depressive symptoms are common associated features of chronic psychosis. No PDD if symptoms occur only during the psychotic disorder (including residual phases).
- **Due to another medical condition:** if the mood disturbance is attributed to the direct pathophysiological effect of a usually chronic medical condition (e.g., MS), use the AMC diagnosis. Otherwise, record PDD and list the medical condition (e.g., diabetes) as concomitant.
- **Substance/medication-induced:** a substance is judged etiologically related.
- **Personality disorders:** commonly co-occur. **Diagnose both** if both are met.

## Comorbidity
- Compared with MDD, PDD carries **higher risk of psychiatric comorbidity overall**, especially **anxiety disorders, SUDs and personality disorders**.
- **Early-onset PDD is strongly associated with Cluster B and C personality disorders.**

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Timeline:** onset date or age (for early vs late onset), total duration ≥2 years (≥1 yr youth), and the **longest symptom-free period**, which must be ≤2 months, e.g. "Patient cannot recall more than a few weeks without low mood since high school."
- **Criterion B:** list the ≥2 symptoms present with examples (chronic low self-worth, pervasive hopelessness, low energy, overeating).
- **Ask directly**, because symptoms feel normal to the patient. Document that they were elicited through direct inquiry.
- **MDE history over the last 2 years**, to choose the course specifier (pure dysthymic / persistent MDE / intermittent with or without current episode). If an MDE was met, code MDD as well.
- **Rule-outs:** lifetime mania/hypomania screen; cyclothymia (subthreshold hypomanic periods); psychotic disorder; substances/medications; medical conditions (thyroid, chronic neurological disease) with labs. Note concomitant medical conditions.
- **Personality assessment:** screen for borderline and Cluster B/C traits. Diagnose comorbidly if met.
- **MSE phrases:** "chronically dysthymic affect, mildly constricted"; "self-description dominated by long-standing low self-esteem"; "endorses pervasive hopelessness about future; denies anhedonia" (the absence of anhedonia supports a pure dysthymic presentation).
- Document functional impact, which may be large despite milder symptom intensity.
