# Substance/Medication-Induced Depressive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Prominent, persistent depressed mood or anhedonia that **predominates** and arises during or soon after
> intoxication or withdrawal, or exposure to or withdrawal from a medication. The agent must be capable of causing it, and no independent
> depressive disorder better explains it. Use it **instead of** plain intoxication/withdrawal only when the mood picture
> is severe enough to need its own clinical attention. Key judgment: **did depression precede use, or
> persist ~1 month after cessation?** If so, think independent depression.

## Criteria at a glance
(pp. 330–331)
- **A.** Prominent, persistent mood disturbance that **predominates** in the clinical picture: depressed mood **or** markedly reduced interest/pleasure in all or almost all activities.
- **B.** Evidence from history, exam or labs of **both**:
  1. Symptoms developed **during or soon after** substance intoxication or withdrawal, or after exposure to or withdrawal from a medication; **and**
  2. The substance/medication is **capable** of producing such symptoms.
- **C.** Not better explained by an independent (non-induced) depressive disorder. Evidence of independence:
  - symptoms **preceded** substance/medication use; or
  - symptoms **persist for a substantial period (~1 month)** after acute withdrawal or severe intoxication ends; or
  - other evidence, e.g., **history of recurrent non-substance-related episodes**.
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note:** diagnose this **instead of** substance intoxication/withdrawal **only** when A symptoms predominate and are severe enough to warrant clinical attention.

## Specifiers & severity
Specify onset (see Table 1 in the Substance-Related chapter for which onset types apply to each class):
- **With onset during intoxication:** intoxication criteria met and symptoms develop during intoxication.
- **With onset during withdrawal:** withdrawal criteria met and symptoms develop during or shortly after withdrawal.
- **With onset after medication use:** symptoms began at medication initiation, with a change in use, or during medication withdrawal.

**Recording procedure** (p. 332):
- Name the **specific substance** (e.g., cocaine, dexamethasone).
- Medications outside the listed classes (e.g., dexamethasone), and cases where the class is unknown, use **"other (or unknown) substance"** (F19.x4).
- Order: [comorbid SUD severity + substance] use disorder **with** [substance]-induced depressive disorder, **with onset during [intoxication/withdrawal]**.
  - "F14.24 severe cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced depressive disorder, with onset during withdrawal" (no separate SUD code).
  - No SUD: "F16.94 phencyclidine-induced depressive disorder, with onset during intoxication."
  - Mild SUD: "mild cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced depressive disorder" (F14.14).
- **Multiple substances:** list each separately, e.g., "F15.24 severe methylphenidate use disorder with methylphenidate-induced depressive disorder, with onset during withdrawal; F19.94 dexamethasone-induced depressive disorder, with onset during intoxication."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Drugs of abuse most likely to cause it:** **depressants** (*intoxication* with alcohol, benzodiazepines and other sedative-hypnotics) and **stimulants** (*withdrawal* from amphetamine-type substances and cocaine).
- **Medications especially likely to cause it:** steroids; antihypertensives (clonidine, guanethidine, methyldopa, reserpine); interferon; L-dopa.
- **Medications implicated with varying evidence:** antibiotics; antivirals (efavirenz); cardiovascular agents (beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers); retinoids (isotretinoin); antidepressants; anticonvulsants; triptans; antipsychotics; hormonal agents (corticosteroids, oral contraceptives, GnRH agonists, tamoxifen); chemotherapy; varenicline. The list keeps growing.
- **Most likely induced when:** high doses of a relevant agent and **no past independent depressive episodes** (e.g., depression during heavy use, or within the first weeks of alpha-methyldopa in someone with no MDD history).
- **Caveat:** an established disorder (e.g., recurrent MDD) can recur while the person happens to be taking a depressogenic agent. The clinician must judge causation in each case.
- Some substances cause **protracted** neurochemical changes after intoxication or withdrawal, so intense symptoms can persist for a while after stopping and still count as induced.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Lifetime alcohol- and stimulant-induced depressive episodes: ≥40%** of people with the relevant SUDs.
- **US general population:** lifetime prevalence of induced depressive disorder without any independent depressive disorder is only **0.26%**. Search actively for induced conditions in alcohol and stimulant users.
- **Onset:** must begin during use or withdrawal; most often within the **first few weeks to 1 month of heavy use**.
- **Offset:** usually remits **within days to several weeks** of stopping, depending on half-life and withdrawal. **If symptoms last 4 weeks beyond the expected withdrawal course, consider other causes.**
- Most evidence suggests these conditions fade quickly with abstinence. **Residual depressive symptoms after SUD treatment predict relapse into use.**
- **Risk factors:** history of antisocial PD, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder; stressful events in the past 12 months; prior drug-induced depressions; family history of SUD. Withdrawal-related depression and anxiety perpetuate use and lower SUD remission. Social-structural adversity (poverty, racism, marginalization) can worsen the course.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Among people with an SUD, risk appears **similar in men and women**.

## Diagnostic markers
- Blood/urine assays have **limited value**. Results are often negative by the time of evaluation because induced depression can last **up to 4 weeks** after use stops. A positive test shows only recent use, not the time course. **History and MSE are the most important data.**

## Suicide risk
- Risk of suicide attempts is higher in people with possible alcohol use disorder who have depressive episodes, **whether induced or independent**, than in controls.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Substance intoxication/withdrawal:** depressive symptoms are common in both (e.g., dysphoria is a feature of cocaine withdrawal). Diagnose induced depressive disorder only if mood symptoms **predominate and are severe enough for separate attention and treatment**.
- **Independent depressive disorder:** if the depressive syndrome appears **at times other than during use**, diagnose an independent disorder even if the substance dose could be relevant.
- **Depressive disorder due to another medical condition:** people with medical illness take medications. Decide whether the illness or the drug is causal, mainly from history. A trial of substituting or stopping the medication may be needed. If **both** contribute, **both diagnoses** may be given.
- **Insufficient evidence** to decide among substance, medical condition or independent depression: use **other specified or unspecified depressive disorder**.
- **Delirium:** exclude if symptoms occur only during delirium.

## Comorbidity
- Compared with independent MDD without SUD: more comorbidity overall, more **tobacco use disorder, gambling disorder and antisocial PD**, and **less PDD**.
- Compared with MDD with comorbid SUD: more likely to have alcohol or other SUD, less likely to have PDD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Temporal sequence** (Criterion B1/C): date mood symptoms started relative to the start of use or dose change, last use, and withdrawal onset, e.g. "low mood and anhedonia began on day 2 of cocaine abstinence after 3-week binge; no depressive episodes during prior 2-year abstinence."
- **Capability** (B2): name the agent, dose and duration, and note it is known to be depressogenic (e.g., interferon, isotretinoin, corticosteroids, varenicline).
- **Independence check** (C): any depression before use; any depressive episodes during sustained abstinence; whether symptoms persist ≥~1 month after acute withdrawal. Plan to **reassess after 4 weeks of abstinence** or after a medication change.
- **Predominance:** explain why the mood picture exceeds usual intoxication/withdrawal and warrants independent attention.
- **SUD severity** for the same class, to select the 4th character. Do not list the SUD separately.
- **Delirium screen:** document orientation, attention and fluctuation.
- **Suicide risk**, especially with alcohol involvement.
- **Labs:** tox screen results, read cautiously; a negative result does not rule out the diagnosis.
- **MSE phrases:** "dysphoric, anhedonic, psychomotor slowed in context of early stimulant withdrawal"; "alert and oriented, attention intact (no delirium)."
