# Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** Depressed mood or anhedonia that predominates and is the **direct pathophysiological
> consequence** of a medical illness (e.g., stroke, Parkinson's, Huntington's, hypothyroidism, Cushing's, pancreatic cancer).
> The key variable is **etiology through a physiological mechanism**, not the psychological reaction to being ill.
> A reaction to illness = adjustment disorder or demoralization. Common error: mixing these up, or forgetting that the
> illness's *treatment* (steroids, interferon) may be the true cause.

## Criteria at a glance
(p. 336)
- **A.** Prominent, persistent mood disturbance that **predominates**: depressed mood or markedly reduced interest/pleasure in all or almost all activities.
- **B.** Evidence from history, exam or labs that it is the **direct pathophysiological consequence** of another medical condition.
- **C.** Not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., **adjustment disorder with depressed mood** where the stressor is the illness).
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.

## Specifiers & severity
The specifier determines the code:
- **With depressive features:** full MDE criteria **not** met.
- **With major depressive–like episode:** full MDE criteria met **except Criterion C** (the medical-cause exclusion).
- **With mixed features:** manic/hypomanic symptoms also present but **not predominant**.

- **Include the medical condition's name** in the diagnosis, e.g., "F06.31 depressive disorder due to hypothyroidism, with depressive features."
- **Code the medical condition separately and list it immediately before**: "E03.9 hypothyroidism; F06.31 depressive disorder due to hypothyroidism, with depressive features."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- First **establish that a medical condition is present**, then establish a **physiological** etiological link. There are no infallible rules. Guiding considerations:
  1. **Temporal association** between onset, exacerbation or remission of the medical condition and the mood disturbance.
  2. **Features atypical for independent depression**: unusual age of onset or course, no family history.
  3. **Literature support** for a direct link between that condition and mood symptoms.
- The list of causative conditions is never complete; **clinical judgment is the essence** of this diagnosis.

## Associated features
- **Clear associations, with neuroanatomical correlates:** stroke (CVA), **Huntington's**, **Parkinson's**, **TBI**.
- **Neuroendocrine:** **Cushing's syndrome**, **hypothyroidism**.
- **Also linked:** autoimmune disease (SLE), vitamin deficiencies (B12), MS, among others. Evidence strength varies.
- **Strongest support for a direct mechanism:** focal lesions (CVA, TBI, neoplasm) in certain brain regions, Parkinson's, Huntington's, hypothyroidism, Cushing's, **pancreatic cancer**.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** sex differences follow those of the medical condition (SLE more common in women; stroke somewhat more common in middle-aged men).
- **Stroke:** onset is usually **acute, within days**, sometimes weeks to months later. The MDE lasts **9–11 months** on average in the largest series.
- **Parkinson's / Huntington's:** depression often **precedes** motor and cognitive impairment. In Huntington's it is considered the **first neuropsychiatric symptom** and may become less common as the neurocognitive disorder progresses.
- Static brain injury and other CNS disease: mood symptoms may be **episodic/recurring**.
- **Cushing's, hypothyroidism:** depression can be an **early manifestation**. **Pancreatic cancer:** depression often **precedes** other features.
- **Risk:**
  - **Stroke:** acute-onset depression (1 day to 1 week) correlates with lesion location. Highest risk with **left frontal** lesions, lowest with **right frontal** lesions. This laterality effect is absent for depression **2–6 months** after stroke, which may instead be MDD, adjustment disorder or demoralization.
  - **Parkinson's:** early onset, heavier motor burden, longer illness.
  - **TBI:** female gender, prior depression, early post-injury psychiatric symptoms, lower brain volume, unemployment.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Women may be at differentially higher risk of depression with cardiovascular disease, **especially after stroke**.

## Diagnostic markers
- Markers belong to the medical condition, e.g., blood/urine steroid levels to support Cushing's, which can cause manic or depressive syndromes.

## Suicide risk
- No epidemiological evidence separates suicide risk in medically caused MDEs from MDEs in general. Case reports of suicide exist.
- There is a **clear link between serious medical illness and suicide, especially soon after onset or diagnosis**. **Assume risk is not lower than, and may be greater than**, in other MDEs.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Depressive disorders not due to a medical condition:** causation is supported by (a) **no depressive episodes before** the medical condition, (b) the condition's **known potential** to cause depression, and (c) depressive symptoms **beginning shortly after onset or worsening** of the condition, especially if they **remit when it is treated**.
- **Medication-induced depressive disorder:** steroids, alpha-interferon and similar treatments can cause depressive or manic symptoms. Use clinical judgment to decide which factor is more likely or more important. Both diagnoses can be given if both contribute.
- **Delirium / major or mild NCD:** no separate diagnosis if the mood disturbance occurs **only during delirium**. It **can be added** to an NCD if depression is a physiological result of the same pathology **and** a prominent part of the picture.
- **Adjustment disorders:** the illness itself is a stressor. Distinguish by **pervasiveness** of the depressive picture and the **number and quality** of depressive symptoms reported or seen on MSE.
- **Demoralization:** a common reaction to chronic illness. Subjective incompetence, helplessness, hopelessness, wanting to give up, often low mood and fatigue. It **typically lacks anhedonia**: the person can still enjoy meaningful activities and have moments of happiness.

## Comorbidity
- Comorbidities follow the etiological medical condition.
- Delirium can come before or alongside depressive symptoms (e.g., Cushing's).
- Anxiety, usually generalized, is common in depressive disorders whatever the cause.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Name and code the medical condition first** (with its ICD-10-CM code), then the F06.3x diagnosis naming that condition.
- **Etiology evidence** (Criterion B): timeline linking mood onset to diagnosis or flare of the condition (e.g., "low mood began 4 days post left frontal CVA"); relevant labs or imaging (TSH/free T4, cortisol, B12, neuroimaging); literature support; atypical features (late first onset, no prior episodes, no family history).
- **Specifier rationale:** list MDE symptoms to justify "with major depressive–like episode" versus "with depressive features." Note any subthreshold manic/hypomanic symptoms ("with mixed features").
- **Rule-outs:** adjustment reaction or demoralization (document whether **anhedonia** is present; preserved capacity for pleasure argues against this diagnosis); medication causes (steroids, interferon, beta-blockers) with start dates; delirium (attention, orientation, fluctuation); prior independent depressive episodes.
- **Response to treatment** of the medical condition (mood improving with thyroid replacement supports the etiological link).
- **Suicide screen**, especially soon after a serious diagnosis.
- **MSE phrases:** "pervasively depressed, non-reactive affect; reports inability to enjoy visits from family" (vs demoralization: "hopeless about prognosis but brightens with grandchildren, enjoys TV").
