# Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder

> **Spark note:** A cyclic, treatment-responsive depressive disorder. ≥5 symptoms (≥1 core
> affective) appear in the **final week before menses**, improve within a few days of menses onset, and are
> **minimal or absent the week after**, in most cycles of the past year. The pitfall: relying on retrospective recall.
> Many people with MDD, PDD or bipolar disorder believe their symptoms are premenstrual until they chart them. **Prospective
> daily ratings over ≥2 cycles** confirm the diagnosis; without them it is "provisional."

## Criteria at a glance
(pp. 325–326)
- **A.** In **most menstrual cycles**, **≥5 symptoms** are present in the **final week before menses**, **start improving within a few days after menses begins**, and become **minimal or absent in the week postmenses**.
- **B.** **≥1** of these core affective symptoms:
  1. Marked affective lability (mood swings, sudden sadness or tearfulness, rejection sensitivity)
  2. Marked irritability/anger or more interpersonal conflict
  3. Marked depressed mood, hopelessness or self-deprecating thoughts
  4. Marked anxiety, tension, feeling keyed up or on edge
- **C.** **≥1** of these, for a **total of ≥5** combined with B:
  1. Less interest in usual activities
  2. Subjective difficulty concentrating
  3. Lethargy, easy fatigue, marked lack of energy
  4. Marked appetite change, overeating or food cravings
  5. Hypersomnia or insomnia
  6. Feeling overwhelmed or out of control
  7. Physical symptoms: breast tenderness/swelling, joint/muscle pain, bloating, weight gain
  - Note: A–C must have been met in **most cycles of the preceding year**.
- **D.** Clinically significant distress or interference with work, school, social activities or relationships (avoiding social activities; lower productivity).
- **E.** Not merely an exacerbation of another disorder (MDD, panic disorder, PDD, a personality disorder), **though it may co-occur** with them.
- **F.** Criterion A should be **confirmed by prospective daily ratings over ≥2 symptomatic cycles**. A provisional diagnosis is allowed before then.
- **G.** Not due to a substance (drug, medication, **other treatment**) or another medical condition (e.g., hyperthyroidism).

## Specifiers & severity
- No formal specifiers. Add **"provisional"** if prospective ratings over ≥2 cycles have not yet been done.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The core is **mood lability, irritability, dysphoria and anxiety** recurring in the premenstrual (luteal) phase and remitting around or soon after menses. Behavioral and physical symptoms often come with them.
- **Somatic/behavioral symptoms without mood/anxiety symptoms are not enough** (that picture suggests PMS).
- Symptoms usually **peak around menses onset** and may linger into the first few days, but there **must be a symptom-free follicular period**.
- Severity is comparable to an MDE or GAD; **duration** is what differs.
- Impairment is concentrated in the week before menses. How symptoms are expressed is shaped by culture, religion, social tolerance, attitudes toward menstruation and gender roles.

## Associated features
- Delusions and hallucinations in the late luteal phase have been described but are **rare**.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **12-month prevalence 5.8%** (German community study). The most rigorous US estimate, using prospective ratings over 2 consecutive cycles with impairment and no comorbid disorder, is **1.3%**.
- Retrospective estimates run higher than prospective ones. Prospective studies may undercount the most severe cases, who may be unable to keep up the ratings.
- Adolescent symptom prevalence may be higher than adult.
- **Course:** onset any time after menarche. **Incidence 2.5% over 40 months** (Germany). Symptoms **stop after menopause**, but cyclic HRT can bring them back.
- *Environmental:* stress, interpersonal trauma, seasonal change, sociocultural aspects of female sexuality and gender roles.
- *Genetic:* no PMDD-specific heritability studies. Heritability of premenstrual dysphoric symptoms is estimated at **30–80%**; whether the symptoms themselves are heritable is unclear.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Observed in the US, Europe, India, Nigeria, Brazil and Asia, with a broad range of prevalence.
- Expression, perceived consequences, help-seeking and management are shaped by sexual abuse or domestic violence history, social support and cultural attitudes toward menstruation.

## Diagnostic markers
- Confirm with **2 months of prospective ratings**. Validated tools:
  - **Daily Rating of Severity of Problems (DRSP)**
  - **Visual Analogue Scales for Premenstrual Mood Symptoms**
  - **Premenstrual Tension Syndrome Rating Scale** (self-report and observer versions)

## Suicide risk
- Some consider the premenstrual phase a **risk period for suicide**.

## Functional consequences
- Partner discord and problems with children, family or friends that occur **only** in the PMDD context (as opposed to chronic interpersonal problems).
- Work and health-related QoL impairment can be **on par with MDD and PDD**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Premenstrual syndrome (PMS):** does not require ≥5 symptoms or mood symptoms; generally milder; **~20%** prevalence. Somatic or behavioral symptoms without the required affective ones = PMS, not PMDD.
- **Dysmenorrhea:** painful menses, not an affective syndrome. Starts **with** menses, while PMDD starts **before** menses.
- **Bipolar disorder, MDD, PDD** (natural or substance-induced): many patients believe they have PMDD, but charting shows no premenstrual pattern. Menses is a memorable event that biases recall, which is why prospective ratings are required. Symptom overlap is greatest with MDE, PDD, bipolar disorder and **borderline PD**.
- **Hormonal treatments** (including contraceptives): if moderate–severe premenstrual symptoms began after starting exogenous hormones and **resolve when hormones stop**, this fits **substance/medication-induced depressive disorder**.
- **Other medical conditions:** rule out conditions that better explain the symptoms, e.g., **thyroid deficiency and anemia**.

## Comorbidity
- **A prior MDE is the most commonly reported earlier disorder.**
- Many conditions worsen premenstrually: migraine, asthma, allergies, seizures, depressive and bipolar disorders, anxiety disorders, bulimia, SUDs. **No symptom-free postmenstrual interval means no PMDD.** Call it a *premenstrual exacerbation* of that condition.
- PMDD **can be added** to another disorder if the premenstrual symptoms and functional changes are characteristic of PMDD and **markedly different** from the ongoing disorder's symptoms.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Cycle linkage:** describe the timing pattern. Symptoms begin about X days before menses, peak at onset, improve within a few days and are **absent in the post-menses week**. Record cycle length and last menstrual period (LMP).
- **Prospective rating status:** which instrument (DRSP), how many cycles completed, and results. If <2 cycles, record **"provisional"**.
- **Symptom count:** list which B (core affective) and C symptoms are present, totaling ≥5, and state that this pattern holds in most cycles over the past year.
- **Impairment:** premenstrual-week-specific examples (missed work days, partner conflicts confined to the luteal week, social withdrawal).
- **Rule-outs:** underlying MDD, PDD, bipolar disorder, panic disorder or borderline PD with premenstrual worsening (document whether a symptom-free follicular phase exists); hormonal contraceptive or HRT start dates; thyroid and anemia labs.
- **Safety:** screen for SI with attention to the premenstrual phase.
- **MSE phrases** (if seen in the luteal phase): "labile, tearful affect; reports feeling 'out of control' and keyed up"; (follicular phase) "euthymic, full-range affect; denies current mood symptoms."
