# Depressive Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Sad, empty or irritable mood and associated functional change unite this chapter. Duration, temporal pattern and etiology distinguish diagnoses; a depressive episode also occurs in bipolar illness, so lifetime mania/hypomania history matters (pp. 302–303).

## How the disorders differ
| Disorder | Main timing/phenotype anchor |
|---|---|
| DMDD | Chronic childhood irritability + severe recurrent outbursts, ≥12 months |
| MDD | Discrete ≥2-week episodes with ≥5 symptoms, including depressed mood/anhedonia |
| PDD | Depressed/irritable mood more days than not ≥2 years (≥1 year in youth), with ≥2 associated symptoms; no gap >2 months |
| PMDD | ≥5 cyclical symptoms including ≥1 core affective symptom, premenstrual onset with postmenses relief; prospective confirmation |
| Substance/medication-induced | Causal exposure-related depressive syndrome |
| Due to another medical condition | Direct physiological cause established |
| Other specified/unspecified | Significant depressive symptoms without a full specific diagnosis and without the relevant adjustment diagnoses |
| Unspecified mood | Cannot resolve bipolar versus depressive residual classification |

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **MDE versus MDD:** ≥5 symptoms/2 weeks is the episode syndrome; MDD additionally requires the disorder-level psychotic exclusions and no lifetime mania/hypomania (except exclusively induced/medical elevations). Assess impairment, medical/substance attribution and developmental context (pp. 309–310).
- **Grief:** culturally understandable loss responses do not automatically qualify or exclude MDE. Judge the full syndrome, course and individual context; bereavement can coexist with depression (pp. 302, 310).
- **DMDD versus bipolar:** chronic irritable baseline differs from distinct elevation episodes. Outbursts average ≥3/week, baseline anger persists most days, ≥2 settings are involved, onset <10 and first diagnosis ages 6–18; no symptom-free interval ≥3 months during the ≥12-month course. Do not co-diagnose with ODD, IED or bipolar disorder (pp. 303–304).
- **PDD versus MDD:** chronicity does not eliminate major episodes. The TR source explicitly directs an additional MDD diagnosis if a full MDE occurs during PDD, with the relevant PDD course specifier (pp. 321–322).
- **PMDD versus exacerbation:** most cycles in the preceding year show the phase-specific pattern; a postmenses low-symptom interval is central. Confirm prospectively in ≥2 symptomatic cycles; use provisional status beforehand. Existing depression worsening premenstrually is insufficient by itself (pp. 325–327).
- **Mixed features versus bipolar:** ≥3 opposite-pole symptoms can specify MDD, but full mania/hypomania changes classification. Insomnia differs from decreased sleep need; irritability alone does not prove mixed features (pp. 342–343).
- **Residual depression:** both specified and unspecified categories exclude adjustment disorder with depressed mood and mixed anxiety/depressed mood. F39 is for uncertainty across bipolar/depressive classes, not simply missing depressive severity (pp. 340–342).

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- MDD: single versus recurrent, current severity/psychosis or remission, then applicable remaining specifiers. Recurrent episodes need ≥2 consecutive months between episodes below full MDE criteria (pp. 310–311).
- Psychotic features select **F32.3/F33.3** regardless of severity. Remission codes apply when full current episode criteria are absent; do not code an active episode as in remission (pp. 310–311).
- Anxious distress and atypical features also apply to PDD; mixed, melancholic, psychotic, catatonic, peripartum and seasonal options have their own applicability. Catatonia adds **F06.1**. Seasonal pattern requires recurrent MDD and a qualifying longitudinal pattern (pp. 342–348).
- PDD is **F34.1**, PMDD **F32.81**, DMDD **F34.81**. Other specified depression is **F32.89**, unspecified depression **F32.A**, unspecified mood **F39**. Medical-related and substance-induced diagnoses follow their own cause/coding conventions; see the separate notes.

## TR changes and notable updates visible in the source
The source uses **F32.A** for unspecified depressive disorder and includes **F39 unspecified mood disorder** in both mood chapters. PDD instructions explicitly call for separate MDD coding when full major episodes occur; the MDD psychotic-disorder exclusion addresses superimposed episodes, also represented in other-specified depression examples (pp. 310, 321–322, 340–342). Avoid presenting DSM-5 introductions such as DMDD or the consolidation of dysthymia/chronic depression as newly created by TR.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
Record a longitudinal mood/episode timeline, exact symptom frequency and duration, observable psychomotor change, concrete impairment, and the source of each report. Assess lifetime elevation, medical/substance timing, psychotic symptoms and current suicidal thoughts/behavior as clinically indicated. Use daily cycle ratings for PMDD, setting/onset history for DMDD and sustained-course evidence for PDD; do not infer these from a single visit.
