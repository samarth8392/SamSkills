# Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome

> **Spark note:** Vague, variable somatic and anxiety symptoms (dizziness, "brain zaps," insomnia, anxiety) begin **2-4 days** after stopping or sharply cutting an antidepressant. It happens most with **short half-life** agents (paroxetine, venlafaxine) and can occur with **intermittent missed doses**. It is purely pharmacological, with **no craving or drug-seeking**. The key error is mistaking it for **relapse** (p. 1082-1083).

## Criteria at a glance (structured from text)
- Follows abrupt discontinuation or significant dose reduction of **any** antidepressant (risk depends on dose, half-life, taper rate).
- Onset typically **2-4 days** after the last dose.
- **SSRIs:** dizziness, tinnitus, "electric shock"-like sensations, insomnia, acute anxiety.
- **TCAs:** GI cramping (cholinergic rebound) and **rebound hypomania**.
- Prior antidepressant use must **not** have caused hypomania or a mixed state, so that the symptoms are not mood-instability fluctuations.
- No pathognomonic sx; no craving; not a reinforcement phenomenon.
- If a stimulant used for augmentation is stopped abruptly, symptoms **may** reflect stimulant withdrawal. Assess the stimulant-withdrawal criteria rather than automatically assigning antidepressant discontinuation syndrome or stimulant withdrawal from cessation alone.

## Course · Risk
- Prevalence unknown. Varies with pre-discontinuation dose, **half-life** (short → more), receptor affinity (more with serotonin reuptake inhibitors), and possibly genetics (rapid/ultrarapid CYP metabolizers).
- **Fluoxetine** (long half-life) rarely causes significant effects. **Venlafaxine** can cause it with even intermittent nonadherence.
- Usually **≤2 wk**, seldom present **>3 wk** after stopping. Very gradual tapers reduce symptoms.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Relapse** of the original depressive or anxiety disorder (e.g., panic): discontinuation sx can mimic anxiety or return of somatic depressive sx. Timing (days) and the somatic/sensory pattern (dizziness, shocks) favor discontinuation.
- **Somatic symptom disorder**, **bipolar I/II with mixed features**, **migraine**, **CVA**.
- **Substance use disorder/withdrawal:** antidepressants aren't reinforcing or euphoric. No self-escalation, no drug-seeking; SUD criteria not met.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Agent, dose, date of last dose or reduction, taper schedule (or lack of one), missed-dose pattern.
- Symptom onset in days after stopping; specific sx (dizziness, "zaps," tinnitus, insomnia, anxiety, GI cramping).
- Pertinent negatives: no prior AD-induced hypomania/mixed state; no craving or drug-seeking.
- Relapse vs. discontinuation reasoning: "sx began 3 days after abrupt stop of paroxetine, predominantly dizziness and electric-shock sensations; mood otherwise stable; consistent with discontinuation rather than relapse."
- Encounter character: A while actively being evaluated/treated; D for follow-up/recovery; S for sequelae.
- Plan: prescriber coordination, psychoeducation on tapering and adherence.
