# Medication-Induced Postural Tremor

> **Spark note:** A **fine, fast (8-12 Hz) tremor** that shows when a posture is held (arms out, mouth open) and arises with a medication, most often **lithium**. It's usually benign, but it can be embarrassing and lead to nonadherence. A tremor that turns **coarse** with twitching or ataxia may signal **lithium toxicity** (p. 1080-1081).

## Criteria at a glance (structured from text)
- Fine tremor on trying to hold a posture, developing with medication use.
- Regular rhythmic oscillation of hands/fingers (most common), head, mouth or tongue at **8-12 Hz**.
- Seen best with sustained posture. May worsen with intentional movement (kinetic/action tremor). **Usually absent at rest.**
- If reported but not seen, recreate the trigger (e.g., drinking from a cup and saucer).
- Not diagnosed when better accounted for by MIP or a preexisting tremor.

**Agents:** lithium, β-adrenergic drugs (isoproterenol), stimulants (amphetamine), dopaminergic drugs, anticonvulsants (valproic acid), antidepressants, methylxanthines (caffeine, theophylline).

## Lithium specifics
- Common and generally well tolerated at therapeutic levels. Complaints decline over time on treatment. May improve spontaneously.
- **Toxicity signs:** coarser tremor + muscle twitching, fasciculations, ataxia as levels near toxicity.
- Risk ↑: older age, high serum level, concurrent antidepressant or antipsychotic/DRBA, excess caffeine, personal or family hx of tremor, AUD, anxiety.
- Exacerbated by: anxiety, stress, fatigue, hypoglycemia, thyrotoxicosis, pheochromocytoma, hypothermia, alcohol withdrawal. Tremor can be an early sign of **serotonin syndrome**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Preexisting tremor (e.g., essential tremor):** predates the medication, doesn't track serum levels, persists after stopping. A preexisting tremor that **worsens** on medication is not coded as medication-induced.
- **Physiological causes** listed above can cause tremor independently.
- **MIP tremor:** 3-6 Hz, worse **at rest**, suppressed by action, with akinesia/rigidity.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Observed tremor: body part, fine vs. coarse, present at rest / with posture / with action.
- Med and level: lithium dose, most recent serum level and date; caffeine intake.
- Toxicity screen if coarse: GI sx, ataxia, confusion, twitching → urgent medical eval.
- Impact: handwriting, eating/drinking, embarrassment, work tasks, adherence.
