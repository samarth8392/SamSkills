# Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome

> **Spark note:** NMS is a **medical emergency**: DRBA exposure, then **fever with sweating, lead-pipe rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability and high CK**. Mental status and neuro changes usually come **before** the systemic signs. Mortality is 10-20% when it is missed. It can be indistinguishable from **malignant catatonia**, and some consider NMS a drug-induced form of it (p. 1072-1074).

## Criteria at a glance (structured from text)
- **Exposure:** a dopamine antagonist generally given within **72 h** before symptoms.
- **Hyperthermia:** >100.4°F / >38.0°C on **≥2 occasions** (oral), with profuse diaphoresis. This sets NMS apart from other antipsychotic neuro side effects. Extreme elevations support the dx.
- **Rigidity:** generalized, lead-pipe when severe, usually **unresponsive to antiparkinsonian agents**. May come with tremor, sialorrhea, akinesia, dystonia, trismus, myoclonus, dysarthria, dysphagia, rhabdomyolysis.
- **CK:** ≥**4× ULN** is common.
- **Mental status:** delirium or altered consciousness ranging from stupor to coma, often **early**. Can look alert but dazed and unresponsive, like catatonic stupor.
- **Autonomic instability** (early clue): HR >25% above baseline; diaphoresis; BP ≥25% above baseline (systolic or diastolic) or fluctuation (≥20 mmHg diastolic or ≥25 mmHg systolic within 24 h); urinary incontinence; pallor.
- **Tachypnea** >50% above baseline is common. Respiratory distress (acidosis, hypermetabolism, chest-wall restriction, aspiration, PE) can lead to sudden arrest.
- Labs are nonspecific: leukocytosis, metabolic acidosis, hypoxia, **low serum iron**, high muscle enzymes and catecholamines. CSF and imaging usually normal; EEG shows generalized slowing.

## Prevalence · Course · Risk
- Incidence: **0.01%-0.02%** of antipsychotic-treated individuals (database studies); 0.11% in a Hong Kong population study.
- Onset: hours to days; some within 24 h, **most within week 1**, virtually all **within 30 days**.
- Recovery after stopping the drug: mean **7-10 days**, most ≤1 wk, nearly all ≤30 days. Longer with **long-acting injectables**. Residual neuro signs can persist for weeks.
- Fatality: **10%-20%** if unrecognized. Most cases resolve fully with recognition.
- Rechallenge: many don't recur, but some do, especially if the antipsychotic is restarted **soon** after the episode.
- Risk: agitation, exhaustion, **dehydration, iron deficiency**; prior episode (15%-20% of index cases); high-potency agents > low-potency/atypicals; parenteral route, rapid titration, high total dose. Usually occurs **within the therapeutic range**. Antiemetic DRBAs (metoclopramide, prochlorperazine) also implicated. Partial or milder forms may occur with newer agents.
- Can occur in people **without** a mental disorder who receive DRBAs.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Medical:** CNS infection, inflammatory/autoimmune conditions, status epilepticus, subcortical lesions, pheochromocytoma, thyrotoxicosis, tetanus, heat stroke.
- **Drug-related:** serotonin syndrome; parkinsonian hyperthermia after abrupt dopamine agonist withdrawal; alcohol or sedative withdrawal; malignant hyperthermia (anesthesia); stimulant/hallucinogen hyperthermia; atropine/anticholinergic poisoning.
- **Malignant catatonia** in schizophrenia or mood disorders can be indistinguishable.
- **MIP:** rigidity/akinesia without fever or high CK. **Acute dystonia:** no fever or generalized rigidity.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Emergency language:** document vitals (temp ×2, HR, BP vs. baseline, RR), rigidity, mental status, and immediate medical referral/transfer.
- Exposure: DRBA name, dose, route (LAI?), timing relative to onset, recent titration or parenteral dosing.
- Labs if available: CK, WBC, iron, electrolytes.
- Rule-outs considered: serotonergic agents on board, withdrawal, infection, catatonia.
- Afterward: note the history of NMS prominently (risk on rechallenge), any rechallenge plan, and LAI-prolonged course.
- Example: "Day 5 after haloperidol increase: T 39.1°C ×2, diaphoretic, lead-pipe rigidity, confused/stuporous, HR 128 (baseline 80), CK 4,200; NMS suspected; antipsychotic held; sent to ED."
