# Tardive Dystonia (G24.09) / Tardive Akathisia (G25.71)

> **Spark note:** These are tardive syndromes whose movements are **dystonia or akathisia** rather than dyskinesia. They emerge **late in treatment** and **may persist for months to years**, even after the antipsychotic/DRBA is stopped or reduced (p. 1080). That potential persistence is a course feature, not a requirement to wait months or years before recognizing the syndrome. Tardive akathisia shares **G25.71** with acute akathisia; the written name carries the difference.

## Criteria at a glance
- Movement type: dystonia (sustained contractions/postures) or akathisia (subjective + observed restlessness).
- **Late** emergence in the course of DRBA treatment, unlike acute forms (hours to weeks).
- Can **persist** for months to years despite discontinuation or dose reduction.
- Tardive dystonia may become evident when the antipsychotic dose is **lowered** (p. 1076).

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| G24.09 | Tardive Dystonia |
| G25.71 | Tardive Akathisia (write "tardive akathisia" to separate it from acute) |

## Differential
- **Acute dystonia / acute akathisia:** early onset tied to start or increase; tend to resolve with dose change or corrective agents.
- **TD:** choreiform/athetoid movements rather than sustained postures or restlessness. These syndromes can coexist.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Total exposure duration and when the movements first appeared relative to treatment start.
- Persistence after dose reduction or discontinuation (dates).
- Phenomenology (posture/region for dystonia; subjective + observed restlessness for akathisia).
- Functional/psychological impact and suicidality screen (especially with akathisia).
