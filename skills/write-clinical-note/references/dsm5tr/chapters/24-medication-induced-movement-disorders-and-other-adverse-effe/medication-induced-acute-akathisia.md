# Medication-Induced Acute Akathisia

> **Spark note:** Akathisia is a **subjective inner restlessness** (mostly in the legs) **plus** observable restless movement, beginning within weeks of starting or raising a drug. It is commonly mistaken for anxiety, agitation or psychotic worsening, and the dose increase that follows makes it worse. It is associated with **dysphoria, aggression and suicide attempts**, and it drives nonadherence (p. 1076-1078).

## Criteria at a glance (structured from text)
- **Subjective** restlessness, **and**
- **≥1 observed** movement:
  - fidgeting or leg swinging while seated
  - rocking foot to foot / "walking on the spot" while standing
  - pacing to relieve restlessness
  - can't sit or stand still for at least several minutes (most severe: can't hold any position for more than a few seconds)
- Subjective features: inner restlessness (legs), compulsion to move the legs, distress if asked to stop, dysphoria, anxiety.
- **Timing:** usually **within 4 wk** of starting or raising the causative drug. Occasionally follows **reduction** of an anticholinergic/EPS-preventing drug.
- **Causative agents:** antipsychotics/DRBAs, **TCAs, SSRIs**, dopamine agonists, CCBs.
- Not better explained by a mental disorder (schizophrenia, substance withdrawal, manic/MDE agitation, ADHD) or a medical condition (PD, iron-deficiency anemia).

## Course · Prevalence
- Can develop very fast. **Dose-dependent**; more common with high-potency agents and high D2 affinity.
- Persists as long as the drug continues; intensity fluctuates.
- Prevalence on antipsychotics/DRBAs: **20%-75%** (varies by definition, prescribing, design, population).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Neuro/medical restlessness:** PD and iron-deficiency anemia can look identical. Abrupt onset right after a med start or increase favors medication.
- **SSRI-induced akathisia** has the same phenomenology and treatment response as DRBA-induced akathisia.
- **TD:** may coexist (generalized restlessness). Differences: type of movement and timing. **Raising the antipsychotic worsens akathisia but temporarily relieves TD.**
- **Mental disorders** with agitation: depressive/manic episodes, GAD, psychosis, ADHD, major NCD, delirium, cocaine intoxication, opioid withdrawal. Favoring the mental disorder: agitation **before** drug exposure; no increase with dose increases; no relief from dose reduction or anti-akathisia treatment. Some patients can tell akathisia apart because it **feels different** from their usual anxiety.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Subjective report in the client's words (e.g., "feels like I have to keep moving my legs," "can't stay in my skin").
- Observed: shifting in chair, leg crossing/uncrossing, rocking, pacing, standing up mid-session; duration tolerated seated. Name the scale if used (e.g., Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale).
- Med timeline: agent, recent start or increase, any anticholinergic taper.
- **Safety:** screen and document SI, irritability, aggression. Akathisia is linked to suicide attempts.
- Differentiation: onset before or after the med change, relation to dose, response to dose reduction, client's sense that it differs from usual anxiety.
- Adherence risk, prescriber notified, plan.
