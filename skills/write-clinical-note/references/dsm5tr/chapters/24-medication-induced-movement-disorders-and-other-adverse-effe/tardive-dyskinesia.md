# Tardive Dyskinesia

> **Spark note:** TD is a set of involuntary **choreiform, athetoid or semirhythmic** movements (usually orofacial) appearing after **months** of dopamine-blocker exposure, lasting ≥4 wk, and sometimes emerging **after** the drug is stopped. It must be **told apart from MIP** because their treatments make each other worse. Not every dyskinesia in someone on an antipsychotic is TD (p. 1078-1080).

## Criteria at a glance (structured from text)
- Abnormal involuntary movements of tongue, jaw, trunk or extremities.
- Linked to drugs blocking postsynaptic dopamine receptors: FGAs, SGAs, **metoclopramide**, etc.
- Movements present for **≥4 wk**. Choreiform (rapid, jerky, nonrepetitive), athetoid (slow, sinuous, continual), or semirhythmic (e.g., stereotypies). **Not** the rhythmic 3-6 Hz tremor of MIP.
- Onset **during** exposure, or within **4 wk of stopping an oral** agent / **8 wk of stopping a long-acting injectable**.
- Exposure history **≥3 mo** (≥**1 mo if age ≥60**).

## Clinical features
- **Orofacial** movements are most obvious (most patients). ~½ have limb involvement; up to ¼ axial (neck, shoulders, trunk). Pharyngeal, diaphragmatic and abdominal involvement is uncommon without the others.
- Younger patients: more limb/truncal without orofacial. Older patients: orofacial typical.
- **Worsened by:** stimulants, antipsychotic withdrawal, **anticholinergics (e.g., benztropine)**; transiently by emotional arousal, stress, and voluntary movement of unaffected parts.
- **Reduced by:** relaxation, voluntary movement of affected parts. Absent in sleep. **Masked temporarily by raising the antipsychotic.**
- Insidious onset, usually minimal or mild and noticed only by a keen observer. Even mild TD can cause **distress and social avoidance**. Severe: oral ulcers, tooth loss, macroglossia; trouble walking, swallowing or breathing; muffled speech; weight loss; depression; **suicidal ideation**.

## Prevalence · Course · Risk
- Long-term antipsychotic use: **20%-30%** prevalence. Younger patients: **3%-5%/yr** incidence. Middle-aged/elderly: prevalence up to **50%**, incidence **25%-30%** after ~1 yr cumulative exposure. More common in chronically institutionalized patients.
- No clear sex difference; possibly higher in **postmenopausal women**.
- Most consistent risks: **cumulative antipsychotic exposure** and **early acute EPS** (e.g., MIP). Also mood disorders (esp. MDD), neuro conditions, AUD. **Age + cumulative exposure** matter most.
- SGAs carry somewhat lower risk than FGAs, but the gap is smaller than once thought once dose is accounted for.
- Older adults: more likely to worsen or generalize with continued use. After stopping, some improve while others have persistent TD.

## Differential diagnosis
- **MIP:** must be separated. Anticholinergics for MIP can **worsen TD**; **VMAT2 inhibitors** for TD can **worsen MIP**.
- **Withdrawal-emergent dyskinesia:** may remit as withdrawal continues. If it persists **≥4 wk**, TD may be diagnosed.
- **Other dyskinesias:** Huntington's, Wilson's, Sydenham's chorea, SLE, thyrotoxicosis, heavy metals, **ill-fitting dentures**, L-dopa/bromocriptine, **spontaneous dyskinesia** (>5%, more in elderly, so causality can be hard to prove). Clues: movements before exposure, focal neuro signs. Other movement disorders can coexist.
- **Acute disorders:** acute dystonia and akathisia arise within hours to days; MIP within weeks. TD needs **months to years** of exposure.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe the movements: body region, type (lip smacking, tongue darting/protrusion, chewing, grimacing, finger piano-playing, truncal rocking), and whether they're present at rest or with distraction. Give AIMS score and date if done.
- Exposure: agent(s), cumulative duration (≥3 mo / ≥1 mo if ≥60), oral vs. LAI, date stopped or reduced.
- Duration of movements ≥4 wk.
- Distinguish from MIP (rhythmic 3-6 Hz rest tremor, rigidity) and from dentures or neuro disease.
- Psychosocial impact: embarrassment, social avoidance, mood, SI screen.
- Prescriber coordination; baseline and periodic movement monitoring.
