# Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention

> **Spark note:** This chapter documents clinically relevant behavior, health concerns, circumstances, and encounters that are not mental disorders. A condition may be coded as the visit reason, to explain testing/treatment, as a contributor to onset/exacerbation, or because it matters to overall management. (pp. 1085–1086)

## Shared usage and coding conventions
- Write the actual condition and its clinical relevance. Codes can accompany a mental disorder; context alone does not establish one. Code forms reproduce this DSM-5-TR extract rather than asserting current billing validity. (p. 1085)
- **Abuse/neglect:** distinguish suspected T76 versus confirmed T74, and active treatment A versus routine recovery care D (seventh character in this subsection). Victim/perpetrator mental health encounters and personal history have separate Z codes. (pp. 1087–1094)
- **Suicidal behavior:** preserve the source's T14.91A/D forms and its stated sixth-character convention; the apparent formatting discrepancy is unresolved by the extract. NSSI has separate current/history codes. (pp. 1086–1087)
- **Wandering:** code the associated mental disorder first, followed by Z91.83 with the association specified. Exclude purposeful escape and medication-induced akathisia. (p. 1101)

These repetitions are in the source, not silently resolved into newly invented codes. (pp. 1088–1099)

## Differential logic and clinical pitfalls
- Separate a stressor from the syndrome it may cause. Psychological reactions to deployment or institutional relocation are assessed as adjustment disorder or another disorder rather than encoded by a circumstance label alone. (pp. 1096–1097)
- Bereavement requires cultural context and differentiation from major depressive episodes and prolonged grief disorder. (p. 1101)
- Malingering requires intentional falsification plus external incentive; medicolegal context or inconsistent presentation alone is not proof. (p. 1102)
- Nonadherence must merit independent attention and not meet psychological-factors-affecting-other-medical-conditions criteria. Assess cost, beliefs, side effects, and disability. (p. 1102)
- Age-related cognitive decline requires objective, age-consistent change after excluding mental/neurological explanations; borderline intellectual functioning requires attention to both intellectual and adaptive ability. (pp. 1102–1103)

## Edition-specific features visible in this extract
The source includes distinct suicidal-behavior/NSSI current and history categories, sheltered/unsheltered homelessness, food insecurity and safe-water codes, and a prolonged-grief differential reference. It does not itself document the publication history of every code; these notes do not infer that every listed feature was newly introduced in TR. (pp. 1086–1087, 1097–1098, 1101)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
Record the fact/pattern, source, timing, relevant encounter/history distinction, clinical effect, and actual assessment/management needs. Distinguish reported, suspected, and corroborated information. Never turn examples in these notes into client facts.
