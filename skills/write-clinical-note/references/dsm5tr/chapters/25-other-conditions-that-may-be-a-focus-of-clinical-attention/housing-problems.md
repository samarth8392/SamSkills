# Housing Problems

> **Spark note:** Describe the actual living arrangement and care impact. Sheltered and unsheltered homelessness are distinct, and an emotional reaction to a move requires its own diagnostic assessment. (p. 1097)

## Clinical use and distinctions
- **Sheltered:** primary sleeping residence is a shelter, temporary/transitional arrangement, or motel as described in the chapter; includes domestic-violence and warming shelters.
- **Unsheltered:** residence is not intended for human habitation, such as a vehicle, public station, or abandoned nonresidential structure.
- **Inadequate housing:** utilities, sanitation, infestation, crowding, sleeping space, or noise compromise adequacy; consider cultural norms.
- **Residential institution:** problems of institutional living, not the psychological reaction to relocation, which may meet adjustment-disorder criteria.
- **Discord/other:** select according to the specific housing problem. (p. 1097)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe nighttime location, stability and material conditions based on available report, and effect on treatment or prognosis.
- Distinguish resource conditions from symptoms of an independently diagnosed disorder.
- Write the housing label because Z59.9 is also used for other economic problems in this source; record assessed barriers and care needs without inventing housing facts.
