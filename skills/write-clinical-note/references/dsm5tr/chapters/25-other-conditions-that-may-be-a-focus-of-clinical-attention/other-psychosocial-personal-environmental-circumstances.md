# Other Psychosocial, Personal, and Environmental Circumstances

> **Spark note:** Code the relevant circumstance and assess its psychological consequences separately. The same Z65.4 code covers crime victimization and terrorism/torture in this source, so the written description matters. (pp. 1099–1100)

## Clinical use and distinctions
Lifestyle problems include limited exercise, diet, risky sexual behavior, and sleep hygiene. If a behavior is already a mental-disorder symptom, add Z72.9 only when it is a specific treatment focus or directly influences the condition's course/prognosis/treatment; then both may be coded. (p. 1099)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Identify the reported event, lifestyle pattern, or service conflict and its concrete clinical relevance.
- For lifestyle coding, state why separate attention is warranted rather than merely duplicating a symptom.
- For victimization/exposure, separate event history from assessed trauma-related symptoms and diagnosis; do not invent the event or its verification.
