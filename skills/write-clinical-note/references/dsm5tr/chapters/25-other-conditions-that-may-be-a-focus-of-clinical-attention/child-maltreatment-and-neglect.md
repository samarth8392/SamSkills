# Child Maltreatment and Neglect Problems

> **Spark note:** Distinguish the type of maltreatment, confirmed versus suspected status, treatment phase, and whether this encounter concerns a victim, perpetrator, or past history. These are clinical-attention conditions rather than mental disorders. (pp. 1087–1090)

## Scope and clinical distinctions
- **Physical abuse:** nonaccidental injury inflicted by a parent/caregiver or responsible individual; the caregiver need not intend the injury. The text excludes reasonable discipline that produces no bodily injury. Document actions and injury rather than assuming all discipline is equivalent.
- **Sexual abuse:** sexual acts or noncontact exploitation involving a child for the gratification of a parent, caregiver, or responsible individual/others. Lack of penetration or direct contact does not rule out the category.
- **Neglect:** egregious caregiver action/omission depriving basic developmentally appropriate needs and causing or reasonably threatening physical/psychological harm; includes supervision, emotional care, education, medical needs, nourishment, shelter, and clothing.
- **Psychological abuse:** nonaccidental verbal/symbolic caregiver behavior causing or reasonably threatening significant psychological harm; examples include humiliation, threats, confinement, scapegoating, and extreme disciplinary frequency/duration. Physical and sexual acts are classified separately. (pp. 1087–1090)

## Coding conventions and pearls
- T74 = confirmed and T76 = suspected in this source. Do not silently upgrade a reported suspicion to confirmed status.
- The **seventh** character of abuse/neglect T codes marks phase: **A** for active treatment (including evaluation by a new clinician), **D** for routine healing/recovery care after active treatment. It does not simply mean first-ever versus repeat office visit.
- Z69 encounter codes identify victim/perpetrator and parental/nonparental relation; they are shared across the four types, so write the specific maltreatment label.
- Childhood physical and sexual abuse share history code Z62.810; psychological abuse and neglect have distinct history codes. (pp. 1087–1090)

## Documentation cues (for client notes) and differential considerations
- Describe alleged/reported acts, developmental needs, actual/potential injury, responsible relationship, timing, and source (child, caregiver, collateral, records).
- State whether evidence supports suspected or confirmed status and why; preserve uncertainty and differing accounts.
- Identify the purpose of this encounter (event care, victim/perpetrator mental health service, history) and actual treatment phase.
- Record current clinical impact and management needs without fabricating disclosures, injuries, reports, or legal findings. Evaluate any associated trauma-related or other mental disorder separately.
