# Borderline Intellectual Functioning

> **Spark note:** Differentiate borderline intellectual functioning from mild intellectual developmental disorder by carefully assessing both intellectual and adaptive functioning. This paragraph does not provide a diagnostic IQ cutoff. (p. 1103)

## Clinical use and differential
Use when functioning is the clinical focus or influences treatment/prognosis. Examine intellectual–adaptive discrepancies and test validity, especially when co-occurring illness (such as schizophrenia or highly impulsive ADHD) can affect engagement with standardized testing. (p. 1103)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record available cognitive testing, adaptive strengths/limitations across contexts, developmental information, and why the finding affects care.
- Document testing conditions, language/cultural considerations when assessed, and limitations from co-occurring symptoms; avoid classifying from a single uncontextualized score.
- Explain the differential with mild intellectual developmental disorder without inventing test results or adaptive deficits.
