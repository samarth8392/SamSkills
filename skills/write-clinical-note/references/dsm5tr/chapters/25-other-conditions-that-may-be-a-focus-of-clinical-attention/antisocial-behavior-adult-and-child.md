# Adult and Child or Adolescent Antisocial Behavior

> **Spark note:** These categories address antisocial acts that are the clinical focus but are not attributable to a mental disorder. An isolated act in a young person does not by itself establish conduct disorder. (pp. 1101–1102)

## Clinical use and differential
Adult examples include some patterns of professional theft, racketeering, or illegal substance dealing without a relevant mental disorder. Child/adolescent examples emphasize isolated acts rather than a pervasive antisocial pattern. Assess conduct disorder, antisocial personality disorder, intermittent explosive disorder, and other relevant conditions independently; do not substitute a behavior code when a disorder explains the acts. (pp. 1101–1102)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record the specific behavior, age/development, context, frequency/course, and reason for attention; distinguish report from verified events.
- Explain the assessed absence of a disorder accounting for the behavior without asserting that all rule-breaking implies a personality disorder.
