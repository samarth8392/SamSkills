# Suicidal Behavior and Nonsuicidal Self-Injury

> **Spark note:** These clinical-attention categories distinguish behavior with at least some intent to die from intentional bodily injury without suicidal intent. Actual injury is not necessary for suicidal behavior; low injury severity does not establish nonsuicidal intent. They are not mental-disorder diagnoses. (pp. 1085–1087)

## Definitions and boundaries
- **Suicidal behavior:** potentially self-injurious action with some intent to die from the act. Intent can be explicitly reported or inferred from circumstances. If another person stops the individual, or the individual changes their mind, **before the action begins**, this category does not apply.
- **Nonsuicidal self-injury (NSSI):** deliberate bodily damage likely to produce pain, bruising, or bleeding, without suicidal intent. Examples include cutting, burning, striking, stabbing, or excessive rubbing.
- Ideation alone does not satisfy these behavior definitions. Assess intent for each event; one individual may report different kinds of self-injury at different times. (pp. 1086–1087)

## Coding
| Source code | Use |
|---|---|
| T14.91A | Current suicidal behavior, initial encounter: active treatment, including emergency care or evaluation/treatment by a new clinician. |
| T14.91D | Current suicidal behavior, subsequent encounter: routine healing/recovery care after active treatment, such as aftercare, follow-up, or medication adjustment. |
| Z91.51 | History of suicidal behavior at any point in life. |
| R45.88 | Current NSSI forming part of the clinical presentation. |
| Z91.52 | History of NSSI at any point in life. (pp. 1086–1087) |

## Coding distinctions and source limitation
- Initial/subsequent describes **treatment phase**, not simply whether the appointment is the first visit in this practice.
- The supplied source explicitly describes the suicidal-behavior T-code A/D marker as its “6th character” and prints T14.91A/D. These notes preserve that source wording/code form; this extract does not resolve its relationship to other ICD-10-CM formatting conventions.
- Current/history categories are based on the presentation versus lifetime occurrence. The text gives no fixed number of days for when a behavior stops being current. Do not invent a remission interval or automatic time-based conversion.
- The chapter permits clinically relevant coding regardless of whether a mental disorder is also diagnosed; it does not make every lifetime-history code mandatory at every visit. (pp. 1085–1087)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record event date/sequence, behavior, injury or absence of injury, treatment, and evidence for intent. Attribute direct report, collateral, records, and clinical inference separately.
- Specify whether behavior actually began, since actions stopped before initiation are excluded from this category while remaining relevant to assessment.
- State current-presentation versus historical relevance and the actual treatment phase supporting A/D.
- If intent is uncertain, document the uncertainty and supporting evidence rather than presuming NSSI from low lethality.
- Assess current risk, function, and management needs separately from selecting a code. These cues are prompts for assessment, not client facts or source-supplied criteria for a risk instrument.
