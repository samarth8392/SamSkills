# Relational Problems and Family Environment

> **Spark note:** Code a relationship or family-context problem when it is the clinical focus or materially affects another condition's course or care. Identify whose relationship is affected and how; the code does not establish a disorder in either participant. (pp. 1094–1096)

## Section coverage and clinical distinctions
- **Parent–child:** includes primary caregiving by biological, adoptive, foster, or other caregivers. Assess behavior (supervision, overprotection, pressure, escalation, avoidance), interpretations (hostility, blame, estrangement), and affect (anger, sadness, disengagement), in developmental/cultural context.
- **Sibling:** impairing patterns affecting individual/family function or symptoms/care; applies in childhood or adulthood and includes half-, step-, foster, and adopted siblings.
- **Partner distress:** clinical focus on relationship quality or its effect on illness/care, across same- or different-gender relationships. Conflict, withdrawal, overinvolvement, hostile interpretations, and persistent negative emotion can be relevant.
- **Upbringing away from parents:** kin/foster care, informal nonparental homes, group homes, and orphanages; excludes the separate Z59.3 residential-institution problem.
- **Child affected by parental distress:** code the child's adverse effects from parental conflict/disparagement, rather than assuming the child is the source of the couple's problem.
- **Separation/divorce:** intimate partners live apart because of relationship problems or are divorcing.
- **High expressed emotion:** hostility, criticism, or emotional overinvolvement directed at an identified patient affects family or clinical care. (pp. 1094–1096)

## Documentation cues (for client notes) and differential considerations
- Record relationship participants, concrete interaction pattern, impairment, and how it affects treatment/prognosis or is itself the clinical focus.
- Assess maltreatment separately when actions exceed relationship discord; do not dilute violence/coercion into a generic couple-conflict label.
- Distinguish caregiver relationship quality from the circumstance of separate upbringing and from the child's response to parental conflict.
- Document cultural/developmental context and source of each account; do not assign psychiatric diagnoses to absent family members.
