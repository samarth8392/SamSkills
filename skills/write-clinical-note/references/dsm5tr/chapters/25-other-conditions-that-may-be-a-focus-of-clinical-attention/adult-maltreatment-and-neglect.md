# Adult Maltreatment and Neglect Problems

> **Spark note:** Adult maltreatment can occur within or outside an intimate partnership. Shared T codes do not encode the perpetrator's relationship; describe the relationship, subtype, certainty, and treatment phase explicitly. (pp. 1090–1094)

## Scope and clinical distinctions
- **Partner physical violence:** deliberate force causing or reasonably threatening physical harm, or provoking significant fear. Injury is not required. Acts used to protect oneself or one's partner are excluded.
- **Partner sexual violence:** force or psychological coercion to compel unwanted sexual acts, including uncompleted acts and sexual acts when a partner cannot consent.
- **Partner neglect:** egregious deprivation of a dependent partner's basic needs with actual or reasonably potential harm. Establish the dependency, including physical, psychological/intellectual, or substantial cultural/communication limitations.
- **Partner psychological abuse:** verbal/symbolic acts with actual or reasonably potential significant harm; includes humiliation, threats, restriction of movement/resources/support, obstructed help, stalking, and manipulation of reality perception.
- **Adult abuse by nonspouse/nonpartner:** physical, sexual, or psychological acts by another adult outside an intimate partnership. The same physical/sexual/psychological T-code families apply; the source excludes protective physical acts. It does not give a separate nonpartner-neglect subsection or dedicated nonpartner-history code here. (pp. 1090–1094)

## Coding conventions and pearls
- **T74** indicates confirmed and **T76** suspected maltreatment. For these T codes, seventh-character **A** means active treatment; **D** means routine healing/recovery care after active treatment.
- Nonpartner abuse uses the physical (11), sexual (21), and psychological (31) rows below, not the partner-neglect (01) rows.
- Victim partner sexual-violence services use **Z69.81**, whereas physical/neglect/psychological victim services use **Z69.11**. Partner-perpetrator mental health services use **Z69.12** across types.
- **Z69.81** also covers nonpartner adult-abuse victim mental health services; **Z69.82** is nonpartner perpetrator services. Shared codes require clear written labels.
- **Z91.410** covers history of both physical and sexual partner violence. (pp. 1090–1094)

## Documentation cues (for client notes) and differential considerations
- Separate abusive acts/coercion from general relationship distress and distinguish defensive protection from the pattern described.
- Document behavior, fear/harm, consent/capacity, dependency if neglect is considered, relationship, and date/current versus past status.
- Attribute reports; specify suspected/confirmed basis, encounter purpose, and phase of treatment without making unsupported legal conclusions.
- Describe clinical effect and assessed management/safety needs; diagnose any separate mental disorder using its own criteria.
