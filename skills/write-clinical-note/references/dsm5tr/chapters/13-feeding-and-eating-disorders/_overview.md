# Feeding and Eating Disorders — Overview

> **Spark note:** Six named disorders united by a persistent eating/feeding disturbance that changes what is consumed or absorbed and damages physical health or psychosocial functioning (p. 544). The big three (AN, BN, BED) form a **mutually exclusive hierarchy** — only one per episode — because course, outcome and treatment differ. The two questions that sort almost every case: *Is weight significantly low?* and *Is there weight/shape overvaluation?*

## What unites the chapter / how disorders differ
- **Common thread:** eating-related behavior disturbance → altered intake/absorption → clinically significant physical or psychosocial harm (p. 544).
- **Body-image-driven disorders:** anorexia nervosa (AN), bulimia nervosa (BN). Self-evaluation unduly tied to shape/weight. BED usually has distress about binges; overvaluation is not a criterion but is higher in obese BED vs obese non-BED (p. 574).
- **Pica, rumination and ARFID:** their core presentations are not defined by weight/shape overvaluation. ARFID explicitly excludes a weight/shape-experience disturbance; rumination excludes symptoms occurring exclusively within the named eating disorders. Pica can coexist with other disorders when it warrants additional attention.
- **Durations:** pica ≥1 mo; rumination ≥1 mo; ARFID — no duration; AN — no duration (subtype uses last 3 mo); BN and BED ≥1×/wk for 3 mo.
- **Age:** pica suggested minimum age 2 yr; rumination onset in infants usually 3–12 mo; ARFID typically infancy/early childhood but any age; AN/BN onset adolescence/young adulthood (rare before puberty or after 40); BED typically adolescence/young adulthood, can be later.
- **Substance-use resemblance:** some report craving/compulsive-use-like features, possibly shared reward/self-control circuitry; relative contributions unclear (p. 544).
- **Obesity is NOT a mental disorder** in DSM-5 (energy imbalance with multifactorial causes), though robustly associated with BED, depressive/bipolar disorders, schizophrenia; psychotropic side effects contribute (p. 544).

## TR changes / notable updates in the text
- **ICD-10-CM codes as printed in the TR:** pica F98.3 (child) / F50.89 (adult); rumination F98.21; ARFID F50.82; AN restricting F50.01, binge-eating/purging F50.02; BN F50.2; BED F50.81; OSFED F50.89; UFED F50.9.
  - Codes reflect the 2022 source volume; verify current official coding before billing.
- **Pica coding by age** (child vs adult) is a coding convention; note that adult pica F50.89 is the same code as OSFED.
- TR text is updated on culture (e.g., absence of "fat phobia" more common in Asian populations; lower service use among underserved ethnic/racialized groups) and suicide sections ("Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior") for AN, BN and BED.
- Elevated risk of AN after **bariatric surgery** (rare), and the **PANS** rule-out for ARFID, are mentioned.
- ARFID replaces and extends DSM-IV feeding disorder of infancy or early childhood (p. 550).

## Hierarchy and exclusion rules
- **AN > BN:** binge/purge only during AN episodes → AN, binge-eating/purging type. Do not add BN (p. 569).
- **AN > ARFID:** never diagnose both together. AN requires fear of weight gain or behavior that interferes with weight gain, plus body-image disturbance (p. 554).
- **BN > BED:** BED excluded if there is recurrent compensatory behavior or if binges occur only during BN/AN.
- **Rumination** excluded if it occurs only during AN, BN, BED or ARFID.
- **Pica in AN:** if nonfood (e.g., tissues) is eaten mainly for weight control, AN is primary.
- **Remission and crossover:** diagnose on the **current (past 3 months)** presentation. BN→AN crossover happens in 10–15%; AN subtypes describe current symptoms, not lifetime course.
- **Co-diagnosis allowed when full criteria are met for both:** BN plus MDD with atypical features; BN or BED plus borderline PD; BED plus a bipolar or depressive disorder; ARFID plus ASD, OCD, MDD, psychosis, specific phobia or RAD (only when the eating disturbance needs specific attention).

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **Low weight with weight-gain fear/preventing behavior and the Criterion C disturbance** prompts assessment of the full AN criteria. Low weight alone does not establish AN. Without those features, assess ARFID, medical conditions, MDD or psychosis as appropriate.
- **"Non–fat phobic" AN** denies fear of fat but still persistently prevents weight gain. It is hard to tell from ARFID, so use course, family history and observation over time (p. 555).
- In youth or anyone who denies fear, Criterion B of AN can be **inferred** from collateral history, observed behavior, labs or longitudinal course (p. 558).
- Adult BMI **≥19** rules out AN. A youth above the **median BMI-for-age** also rules out AN. Use OSFED (atypical AN) instead (p. 558).
- BN weight is usually in the normal-to-overweight range (BMI 18.5 to <30). With significantly low weight, assess AN-BP; assign it only if all AN criteria are met.
- Binge = objectively large amount in a discrete period (usually <2 hr) + loss of control. Continuous grazing ≠ binge.
- BED vs obesity: most obese people do not binge. BED brings more psychopathology and impairment.
- BED vs BN: BED lacks recurrent inappropriate compensatory behavior. Marked sustained dietary restriction between binges is typically absent in BED, but this is a descriptive distinction rather than a separate exclusion criterion. Dieting usually comes **after** binge onset in BED and **before** it in BN.
- Pica, rumination and ARFID that occur in the context of another disorder (IDD, ASD, medical) need **severity beyond the usual** for that condition and a **separate clinical focus**.
- Factitious disorder (and factitious disorder imposed on another) can mimic ARFID or pica.

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- **"In remission"** applies to pica, rumination and ARFID: full criteria were met before and have not been met for a sustained period.
- **Partial/full remission** applies to AN, BN and BED, each with its own definition (see files).
- **Severity:** AN by BMI (adults) or BMI percentile (youth): mild ≥17, moderate 16–16.99, severe 15–15.99, extreme <15. BN by compensatory episodes per week and BED by binge episodes per week: mild 1–3, moderate 4–7, severe 8–13, extreme ≥14. The clinician may raise severity for symptoms, disability or need for supervision.
- **AN subtype** (restricting vs binge-eating/purging) is based on the **last 3 months** and sets the code.
- **OSFED:** record "other specified feeding or eating disorder" + the reason (e.g., "atypical anorexia nervosa").
