# Bulimia Nervosa

> **Spark note:** Recurrent objective binges with loss of control, plus recurrent compensatory behavior (vomiting, laxatives or diuretics, fasting, driven exercise), both averaging **≥1×/week for 3 months**, with self-worth tied to shape and weight, and weight **not** significantly low. If weight is significantly low, assess all AN criteria before assigning its binge-eating/purging subtype. **Severity counts compensatory episodes, not binges.**

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recurrent binge episodes, each with **both**:
  1. In a **discrete period** (e.g., any 2-hour window), eating an amount **definitely larger** than most people would in similar time and circumstances.
  2. A sense of **lack of control** during the episode.
- **B.** Recurrent **inappropriate compensatory behaviors** to prevent weight gain: self-induced vomiting; misuse of laxatives, diuretics or other medications; fasting; excessive exercise.
- **C.** Binges **and** compensatory behaviors both occur on average **≥1×/week for 3 months**.
- **D.** Self-evaluation is **unduly influenced by body shape and weight**.
- **E.** Not exclusively during episodes of AN.

## Specifiers & severity
- **In partial remission:** some but not all criteria met for a sustained period.
- **In full remission:** no criteria met for a sustained period.
- **Severity** = average number of **inappropriate compensatory behavior** episodes per week. It may be raised for other symptoms or disability.

| Level | Compensatory episodes per week |
|---|---|
| Mild | 1–3 |
| Moderate | 4–7 |
| Severe | 8–13 |
| Extreme | ≥ 14 |

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Large amount** is judged in context: a holiday meal can be normal. **Discrete period** usually means <2 hours. A binge can **span settings** (it starts at a restaurant and continues at home). **Continual grazing on small amounts is not a binge** (p. 565).
- **Loss of control:** inability to refrain or stop. May have a **dissociative** quality. Not absolute: a person may keep eating while the phone rings but stop if a roommate walks in. Some describe a generalized uncontrolled pattern rather than acute loss. If they have **abandoned efforts to control eating, count LOC as present**. Binges can be **planned**.
- Binges are defined more by **amount** than by craving for particular nutrients, but people tend to eat foods they otherwise avoid.
- Shame and **secrecy**. Eating usually continues until the person is uncomfortably or painfully full.
- **Triggers:** negative affect (most common), interpersonal stress, dietary restraint, negative feelings about body or food, boredom. Short-term relief is followed by self-criticism and dysphoria.
- **Compensatory behaviors:** self-induced vomiting is most common. It relieves discomfort and fear of gain, and can become a **goal in itself** (bingeing in order to vomit, or vomiting after small amounts). Patients learn to vomit at will. Rarely syrup of ipecac. Laxatives, diuretics, rarely enemas (seldom the only method). Thyroid hormone. **Insulin omission** in diabetes. Fasting ≥1 day. **Excessive exercise** = interferes with important activities, occurs at inappropriate times or places, or continues despite injury (p. 566).
- Fear of gain, desire to lose weight and body dissatisfaction can look like AN, but weight is not significantly low.

## Associated features
- Weight is typically in the **normal-to-overweight** range (adult BMI ≥18.5 and <30). BN is uncommon in obesity.
- Restriction **between** binges, choosing "diet" foods, avoiding trigger foods.
- Menstrual irregularity or amenorrhea (cause uncertain).
- **Medical:** fluid and electrolyte disturbances. Rare but potentially fatal: **esophageal tears, gastric rupture, cardiac arrhythmias**. Ipecac → cardiac and skeletal myopathy. Laxative dependence. GI symptoms. Rectal prolapse (p. 566–567).

## Diagnostic markers
- There is no specific test. Purging-related labs: **hypokalemia** (arrhythmia risk), hypochloremia, hyponatremia. Vomiting → **metabolic alkalosis**. Laxatives or diuretics → **metabolic acidosis**. Mildly elevated serum amylase (salivary isoenzyme).
- Exam is often normal. Possible findings: **lingual enamel erosion** of the front teeth ("moth-eaten," chipped), caries, **parotid enlargement**, **dorsal hand calluses or scars** (p. 568).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- 12-month (US adults): **0.14–0.3%** (women 0.22–0.5%; men 0.05–0.1%). Lifetime **0.28–1.0%** (women 0.46–1.5%; men 0.05–0.08%). Adolescents aged 13–18: lifetime 1.3% (girls) and 0.5% (boys) (p. 567).
- Similar across US ethnoracial groups. Highest in high-income industrialized countries. Some Latin American and Middle Eastern regions are similar. Gradually rising in low- and middle-income countries.
- Onset in **adolescence or young adulthood**. Uncommon before puberty or after 40. Binges often begin **during or after dieting**. Multiple stressors can precipitate onset.
- Course: persists for years in many clinical samples. Chronic or intermittent. Many improve over the long term with or without treatment (treatment clearly helps). **Remission >1 year** predicts better outcome.
- **Mortality:** all-cause and suicide mortality are elevated. Crude mortality is **nearly 2% per decade**.
- **Crossover:** BN → AN in **10–15%**, often reverting or switching repeatedly. Some keep bingeing but stop compensating → BED or OSFED. **Diagnose on the past 3 months.**
- **Temperamental:** weight concerns, low self-esteem, depressive symptoms, social anxiety disorder, childhood GAD.
- **Environmental:** thin-ideal internalization; childhood **sexual or physical abuse**.
- **Genetic/physiological:** **childhood obesity**, **early puberty**, familial and genetic transmission.
- **Course modifier:** more severe psychiatric comorbidity predicts a worse long-term outcome (p. 568).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Community prevalence does not differ across US ethnoracial groups, but **treatment use is lower** among underserved ethnic and racialized groups.
- Much more common in girls and women. Males are especially **under-represented in treatment** samples.

## Suicide risk
- Elevated. About **one-quarter to one-third** have had SI, and a **similar proportion have attempted** suicide (p. 569). Suicide mortality is elevated.

## Functional consequences
- Reduced health-related quality of life. A minority have severe role impairment, with the **social domain** most affected.

## Differential diagnosis
- **AN, binge-eating/purging type:** binges only during AN episodes → AN-BP, not BN. If AN later no longer fits (e.g., weight normalizes), diagnose BN only after **full BN criteria have been met for ≥3 months** (p. 569).
- **Binge-eating disorder:** binges **without regular compensatory behavior**.
- **Kleine-Levin syndrome** and other neurological or medical conditions: disturbed eating without overconcern about shape or weight.
- **MDD with atypical features:** overeating, but no compensation and no shape/weight overconcern. **Give both** if both are met.
- **Borderline PD:** binge eating is one of its impulsivity examples. **Give both** if both are met.

## Comorbidity
- Most patients have ≥1 other mental disorder, often several, across a broad range.
- **Depressive disorders** especially (also bipolar). Mood disturbance often begins with or after BN, though sometimes before. Anxiety symptoms and disorders. These often **remit with effective BN treatment**.
- Lifetime substance use disorder is **≥30%**, especially alcohol and **stimulants** (often started to control appetite) (p. 570).
- Personality disorders, most often **borderline**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion A1:** a concrete binge example (foods, amount, timeframe <2 hr, setting). Distinguish it from grazing and from subjective binges.
- **Criterion A2:** LOC language ("couldn't stop," "zoned out," "gave up trying to control it").
- **Criterion B:** each compensatory method with **frequency per week** (vomiting, laxative or diuretic doses, fasting days, exercise hours, insulin omission).
- **Criterion C:** a log or estimate showing **≥1×/week over ≥3 months** for both binges and compensation.
- **Criterion D:** overvaluation statements; mood or self-worth that tracks weight.
- **Criterion E / AN rule-out:** current height, weight, BMI. Record that weight is **not** significantly low.
- **Severity:** weekly compensatory count → band, plus any upgrade rationale.
- **Medical:** K+, Cl, Na, HCO3, amylase, ECG if hypokalemic; dental, parotid and hand exam; GI symptoms; ipecac use.
- **Safety:** SI or attempts, NSSI, substance use (especially stimulants and alcohol).
- **MSE phrases:** "Reports binge ~3,000 kcal in 1 hr, then self-induced vomiting; ~5 vomiting episodes per week × 6 months." "Self-worth 'depends entirely on the number on the scale.'" "Bilateral parotid swelling; calluses on dorsal right knuckles."
