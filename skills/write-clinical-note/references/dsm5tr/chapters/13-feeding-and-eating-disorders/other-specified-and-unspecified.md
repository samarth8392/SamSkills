# Other Specified and Unspecified Feeding or Eating Disorder

> **Spark note:** Use these when clinically significant feeding/eating symptoms with distress or impairment **predominate** but no specific disorder's criteria are met. **Other specified (F50.89)** = state the reason (e.g., "atypical anorexia nervosa"). **Unspecified (F50.9)** = choose not to specify, or there is not enough information (e.g., an ED setting). Atypical AN carries **many of AN's medical complications** despite normal weight. Do not dismiss it.

## Criteria at a glance
- Symptoms characteristic of a feeding or eating disorder cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational or other important areas, and **predominate**.
- **Full criteria are not met** for any disorder in this class.
- **Other specified:** the clinician communicates the specific reason.
- **Unspecified:** the clinician does not specify, or information is insufficient.

## Coding
| Code | When | Recording |
|---|---|---|
| F50.89 | Other specified feeding or eating disorder | "Other specified feeding or eating disorder, [reason]" (e.g., "…, bulimia nervosa of low frequency") |
| F50.9 | Unspecified feeding or eating disorder | "Unspecified feeding or eating disorder" |

Note that F50.89 is also the code for **pica in adults**.

## Example "other specified" presentations (p. 574–575)
1. **Atypical anorexia nervosa:** all AN criteria are met, but despite **significant weight loss**, weight is **within or above normal**. Patients can have many of AN's physiological complications. The AN text advises considering this category at adult BMI ≥19 or youth BMI above the median for age; that weight finding alone is not diagnostic (p. 558).
2. **Bulimia nervosa (of low frequency and/or limited duration):** all BN criteria are met, but binges and compensatory behavior average **<1×/week and/or last <3 months**.
3. **Binge-eating disorder (of low frequency and/or limited duration):** all BED criteria are met, but binges average **<1×/week and/or last <3 months**.
4. **Purging disorder:** recurrent purging to influence weight or shape (vomiting; misuse of laxatives, diuretics or other medications) **without binge eating** (and, by implication, without the significantly low weight of AN).
5. **Night eating syndrome:** recurrent night eating, either **eating after waking from sleep** or **excessive intake after the evening meal**.
   - The person is **aware of and recalls** the eating, which separates it from sleep-related eating parasomnias.
   - Not better explained by external influences (changes in the sleep-wake cycle, local social norms).
   - Causes significant distress or impairment.
   - Not better explained by BED, another mental disorder or substance use, and not due to a medical condition or medication.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- These are **examples**, not an exhaustive list. Any clinically significant subthreshold presentation can be specified.
- The past-3-months rule and crossover still matter: e.g., BN patients who stop compensating may fall into BED or OSFED (p. 567).
- Prefer "other specified" whenever the reason is known. It communicates more to the next clinician.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed for these categories.

## Differential diagnosis
- **AN vs atypical AN:** the difference is the weight threshold, not seriousness. Document weight-loss magnitude and medical signs.
- **BN or BED vs low-frequency/limited-duration variants:** a frequency and duration count settles it.
- **Purging disorder vs AN-BP:** purging without binge eating can occur in AN-BP. Significantly low weight warrants assessment of all AN criteria; weight plus purging alone does not establish AN-BP.
- **Purging disorder vs rumination:** rumination is effortless regurgitation without weight/shape motive.
- **Night eating vs BED:** if night episodes meet binge criteria (large amount + LOC + ≥3 features + distress, ≥1×/week for 3 mo), diagnose BED. **Night eating vs parasomnia or medication (e.g., hypnotic-related) eating:** night eating syndrome requires awareness and recall.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Name the **specific reason** in the diagnosis line (e.g., "OSFED – atypical anorexia nervosa").
- **Atypical AN:** amount and time course of weight loss (e.g., from BMI 27 to 21 in 6 months), fear of gain, body-image disturbance, and medical findings (bradycardia, orthostasis, electrolytes, amenorrhea).
- **Subthreshold BN or BED:** document the actual frequency and duration that fall short. Record all other criteria that are met.
- **Purging disorder:** methods and frequency of purging, absence of objective binges, weight/shape motive, current BMI (not significantly low).
- **Night eating:** timing (after the evening meal vs on waking), awareness and recall, sleep schedule, medications (sedative-hypnotics), distress and impairment.
- **Unspecified:** state what information is missing (e.g., "insufficient history in ED setting; re-evaluate after collateral").
- Always include safety screening (SI, NSSI) and medical risk indicators.
