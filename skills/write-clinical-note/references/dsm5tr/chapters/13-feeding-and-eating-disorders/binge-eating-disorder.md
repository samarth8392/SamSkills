# Binge-Eating Disorder

> **Spark note:** Recurrent objective binges with loss of control, **≥1×/week for 3 months**, with **≥3 of 5** associated features and **marked distress**, but **without** the regular compensatory behavior of BN. Common mistakes: equating BED with obesity (most obese people do not binge) and missing that BED often involves frequent dieting attempts, but not the sustained restriction between binges that marks BN.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recurrent binges, each with **both**: (1) a definitely larger than normal amount in a discrete period (e.g., any 2 hr), and (2) a sense of **lack of control**.
- **B.** Binges are associated with **≥3 of 5**:
  1. Eating much more **rapidly** than normal.
  2. Eating until **uncomfortably full**.
  3. Eating large amounts when **not physically hungry**.
  4. Eating **alone because of embarrassment** about the amount.
  5. Feeling **disgusted, depressed or very guilty** afterward.
- **C.** **Marked distress** about the binge eating.
- **D.** On average **≥1×/week for 3 months**.
- **E.** No recurrent inappropriate compensatory behavior (as in BN), and not exclusively during BN or AN.

## Specifiers & severity
- **In partial remission:** binge frequency is on average **<1×/week** for a sustained period (a different definition from BN and AN).
- **In full remission:** no criteria met for a sustained period.
- **Severity** = binge episodes per week. It may be raised for other symptoms or disability.

| Level | Binges per week |
|---|---|
| Mild | 1–3 |
| Moderate | 4–7 |
| Severe | 8–13 |
| Extreme | ≥ 14 |

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The binge definition matches BN: context matters (holiday meals), "discrete" usually means <2 hr, a binge can span settings, and **grazing does not count**.
- LOC may be dissociative and is not absolute (stops if someone walks in). It can be a generalized uncontrolled pattern. If the person has **given up trying to control eating, LOC may still count as present**. Binges can be planned (p. 571).
- Defined by **amount**, not by craving a particular nutrient.
- Shame, **secrecy**, eating inconspicuously. Triggers: negative affect (most common), interpersonal stress, dietary restraint, negative body or food feelings, boredom. Brief relief is followed by self-criticism and dysphoria (p. 572).

## Associated features
- Occurs at normal, overweight and obese weights. Reliably associated with overweight and obesity in **treatment-seeking** samples, but **distinct from obesity**.
- Compared with weight-matched obese people without BED, people with BED eat more calories in lab studies and have more impairment, lower QoL, more distress and more psychiatric comorbidity (p. 572).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- 12-month (US): **0.44–1.2%**, with women **2–3×** men (women 0.6–1.6%; men 0.26–0.8%). Lifetime **0.85–2.8%** (women 1.25–3.5%; men 0.42–2.0%).
- Comparable across US ethnoracial groups. In high-income countries the 12-month rate is 0.1–1.2%. Some Latin American regions have rates at least as high. **Mexican Americans** have a higher rate than people in Mexico.
- Binge eating and **loss-of-control eating without objectively large amounts** occur in **children** and are linked to fat gain and psychological symptoms. LOC or episodic binge eating may be a **prodrome** of eating disorders.
- **Dieting follows binge onset** in many with BED (the reverse of BN, where dieting precedes binge onset) (p. 572–573).
- Typical onset is adolescence or young adulthood, but it can start later. Treatment seekers are **older** than those with AN or BN.
- **Remission rates are higher** than in BN or AN, in both natural-course and treatment studies. Some have a persistent relapsing course similar to BN. Crossover to other eating disorders is **uncommon**.
- **Genetic/physiological:** runs in families (additive genetic influence).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- **Black** individuals may report **less distress** about binge eating and present with **higher binge frequency** than White individuals (p. 573). This is relevant to judging Criterion C.
- Prevalence is 2–3× higher in women.

## Suicide risk
- SI is reported in about **25%** of people with BED (p. 573).

## Functional consequences
- Social role adjustment problems, impaired health-related QoL and life satisfaction, **increased medical morbidity and mortality**, and higher health care use than BMI-matched controls. Increased risk of weight gain and obesity.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Bulimia nervosa:** BED has **no recurrent inappropriate compensatory behavior**. Marked sustained restriction between binges is typically absent, although frequent dieting attempts may be reported; restriction alone is not an additional BED exclusion criterion. BED also improves more often with treatment (p. 573–574).
- **Obesity:** in obese people, BED brings higher overvaluation of shape/weight, higher psychiatric comorbidity, and better response to evidence-based psychological treatment than obesity treatment.
- **Bipolar and depressive disorders:** increased appetite or weight is part of MDE and the atypical features specifier. Overeating in MDE may or may not involve LOC. Binge eating also occurs in bipolar disorder. **Give both** if both are met.
- **Borderline PD:** binge eating is one of its impulsivity examples. **Give both** if both are met.
- **AN / BN:** BED is excluded if binges occur only during their course.

## Comorbidity
- Psychiatric comorbidity is comparable to BN and AN. Most common: **MDD** and **alcohol use disorder**. Comorbidity tracks **binge severity, not degree of obesity** (p. 574).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion A:** a concrete binge example (amount, duration <2 hr, setting) and LOC language. Separate it from grazing and subjective binges.
- **Criterion B:** check off which of the 5 features apply (≥3 needed), ideally with patient examples: "eats in car so no one sees," "keeps eating until it hurts," "eats when not hungry."
- **Criterion C:** document the **marked distress** explicitly (shame, guilt, worry about health or control). Probe carefully where distress may be under-reported.
- **Criterion D:** weekly frequency over ≥3 months (a food or binge log helps).
- **Criterion E:** **record that there is no** vomiting, laxative or diuretic misuse, fasting, or driven exercise used to compensate. If present → evaluate for BN.
- **Severity:** binges per week → band.
- **Context:** weight history, dieting history and its timing relative to binge onset, medical comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension), mood and alcohol screening, SI screening.
- **MSE phrases:** "Describes eating rapidly to the point of physical discomfort, alone at night, ~4×/week for 8 months; feels 'disgusted' afterward." "Denies purging, laxatives or compensatory exercise."
