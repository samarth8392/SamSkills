# Other Specified and Unspecified Sleep-Wake Disorders

> **Spark note:** Residual sleep diagnoses still require clinically significant symptoms and distress/impairment. Choose the insomnia or hypersomnolence residual family before a general sleep-wake residual category; other specified records the known reason criteria are unmet, whereas unspecified leaves that reason unstated or reflects missing information (pp. 668–670).

## Criteria at a glance
Across all six categories: characteristic symptoms predominate and impair function or cause significant distress, without meeting full criteria for the named disorder or another specific chapter diagnosis.
- **Other specified insomnia:** clinician identifies the unmet criterion/reason.
- **Unspecified insomnia:** reason not specified or insufficient information.
- **Other specified hypersomnolence:** clinician states reason for not meeting full criteria.
- **Unspecified hypersomnolence:** reason not stated or information insufficient.
- **Other specified sleep-wake:** does not qualify for a specific disorder **or** other specified insomnia/hypersomnolence; state the reason.
- **Unspecified sleep-wake:** does not qualify for a specific disorder **or** unspecified insomnia/hypersomnolence; reason withheld/unknown (pp. 668–670).

## Specifiers & severity
Examples provided:
- **Short-term insomnia:** <3 months.
- **Restricted to nonrestorative sleep:** unrefreshing sleep without other sleep symptoms such as initiation/maintenance difficulty.
- **Brief-duration hypersomnolence:** example context Kleine-Levin syndrome.
- **Repeated REM arousals without PSG or history of Parkinson's/other synucleinopathy:** other specified sleep-wake example; does not establish confirmed RBD.
No separate formal severity or remission specifiers (pp. 668–670).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
The general residual categories are not automatic substitutes for more descriptive phenotype-specific residual diagnoses. Source discussion also permits G47.9 when causal attribution to medication, medical illness or independent sleep disorder cannot be established (p. 668).

A source tension should remain visible: the insomnia narrative earlier refers isolated nonrestorative sleep to other specified sleep-wake disorder (p. 592), while the later formal other specified insomnia section explicitly includes it (p. 668). Record the actual presentation and acknowledge this internal discrepancy instead of asserting identical instructions.

## Suicide risk
No category-specific discussion; the chapter introduction links insomnia/nightmares with suicidal thoughts/behavior (pp. 589–590).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Full insomnia/hypersomnolence:** assess adequate sleep opportunity, frequency, duration and explanatory conditions before assigning residual status.
- **RBD:** repeated REM-like behavior without requisite physiological/history evidence belongs to a specified residual example; do not imply confirmation.
- **Substance/medication or medical-condition association:** investigate exposure chronology and known illness; unspecified may reflect unresolved cause rather than demonstrated absence of a cause.
- **Normal variation/insufficient opportunity:** residual diagnosis is not justified simply because sleep differs from preference; distress/impairment and characteristic disturbance remain necessary (pp. 597, 603, 668–670).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Identify predominant phenotype, sleep opportunity, frequency/duration and functional effect.
- For specified categories, state exactly why the full syndrome is unmet; avoid generic subthreshold wording alone.
- For unspecified categories, state information limits without inventing absent findings and record assessment/follow-up needs.
- Explain why phenotype-specific residual categories do not fit before choosing G47.8/G47.9.
- For REM-like behaviors, document available collateral and missing PSG/synucleinopathy evidence; for nonrestorative-only sleep, note the source discrepancy above.
