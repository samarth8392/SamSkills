# Central Sleep Apnea

> **Spark note:** A disorder of **ventilatory control** — breathing stops because the brain doesn't drive it (no respiratory effort), not because the airway closes. Needs **≥5 central apneas/hour** on polysomnography. Three coded subtypes: idiopathic, **Cheyne-Stokes breathing** (heart failure, stroke, renal failure), and **comorbid with opioid use** (≈24% of chronic opioid users, including methadone maintenance). For behavioral-health clinicians the opioid/methadone link is the big one: sleepiness, confusion, and depression in a methadone patient may partly reflect CSA.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** PSG evidence of **≥5 central apneas per hour of sleep**.
- **B.** Not better explained by another current sleep disorder.
- When central and obstructive events coexist, CSA is diagnosed/considered predominant when **central events >50%** of all respiratory events.

## Specifiers & severity
**Subtypes (specify whether):**
- **Idiopathic CSA** — repeated apneas/hypopneas from variable respiratory effort, no airway obstruction.
- **Cheyne-Stokes breathing** — periodic crescendo-decrescendo tidal volume causing central apneas/hypopneas at **≥5/hr**, with frequent arousal (text later notes arousals not required).
- **CSA comorbid with opioid use** — opioid effects on medullary respiratory rhythm generators and differential effects on hypoxic vs. hypercapnic drive.

**Severity:** graded by frequency of breathing disturbances plus extent of desaturation and sleep fragmentation. Higher central apnea index = more severe. Cheyne-Stokes during resting wakefulness → marker of increased mortality.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Events occur in periodic or intermittent pattern from variable respiratory effort.
- **Idiopathic and Cheyne-Stokes:** high **loop gain** (overly sensitive ventilatory control) → periodic breathing (hyperventilation alternating with hypoventilation); awake pCO2 slightly low or normal.
- **Treatment-emergent CSA:** can appear when starting OSA treatment; CSA occurring with OSA also attributed to high loop gain.
- **Opioid subtype:** may have **elevated awake pCO2**; central apneas, periodic, or **ataxic** breathing; documented with chronic use (several months); severity higher with morphine-equivalent **>200 mg/day**. Methadone maintenance patients show increased somnolence and depression (causal role of breathing disorder unstudied).
- CSA due to a medical disorder without Cheyne-Stokes: brainstem ventilatory control pathology.
- **High-altitude CSA:** after ascent, generally ≥2,500 m.
- Idiopathic CSA: sleepiness, insomnia, awakenings with dyspnea.
- Cheyne-Stokes cycle length ~**60 s**; central apneas are breathing cessations **>10 s**.

## Associated features
- Sleepiness or insomnia; sleep fragmentation; awakening with dyspnea; some asymptomatic.
- OSA may coexist with Cheyne-Stokes → snoring and abruptly ending obstructive events.
- Cheyne-Stokes physical findings reflect heart failure: JVD, S3, crackles, leg edema.
- Reduced slow-wave sleep.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Idiopathic CSA: unknown, thought rare.
- Cheyne-Stokes: **15%–44%** of people with ejection fraction <45%; even more male-skewed than OSA; mostly **>60 yr**; ~**20%** after acute stroke.
- Opioid subtype: ~**24%** of chronic opioid users for nonmalignant pain, similar in methadone maintenance.
- Children: 4%–6% (France, Canada).
- Course: Cheyne-Stokes onset tied to heart failure development; its oscillations in HR, BP, O2 and elevated sympathetic activity may promote heart failure progression. Post-stroke Cheyne-Stokes may be transient.
- Risk: heart failure (+ atrial fibrillation, older age, male sex), acute stroke, possibly renal failure; long-acting opioids. Children: congenital abnormalities (Arnold-Chiari), GERD; rarely congenital causes present in adulthood.

## Diagnostic markers
PSG characterizes breathing; Cheyne-Stokes = crescendo-decrescendo pattern with ≥5 events/hr and central events >50% of all apneas/hypopneas.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
Idiopathic: insomnia and sleepiness. Cheyne-Stokes + heart failure: sleepiness, fatigue, insomnia (many asymptomatic); more arrhythmias, increased mortality or transplantation. Opioid subtype: sleepiness or insomnia.

## Differential diagnosis
- **OSA hypopnea:** CSA has ≥5 central apneas/hr; when both occur, CSA predominates if central events >50%.
- **Other sleep, mental, and medical disorders causing fragmentation/sleepiness/fatigue:** separated by PSG and, for Cheyne-Stokes, a predisposing condition (heart failure, stroke).
- **High-altitude periodic breathing:** resembles Cheyne-Stokes but shorter cycle, only at altitude, no heart failure.
- **Opioid subtype vs. other breathing disorders:** long-acting opioid use plus PSG central apneas/periodic or ataxic breathing. **Vs. substance-induced insomnia:** PSG evidence of CSA.

## Comorbidity
Frequent in long-acting opioid users (methadone) → sleep disturbance, sleepiness, confusion, depression. OSA may coexist. Cheyne-Stokes: heart failure, stroke, renal failure, atrial fibrillation; these patients tend to be older, male, and **leaner** than OSA patients.

## ICSD-3 relationship
ICSD-3 has eight CSA subtypes (Cheyne-Stokes, medical disorder without Cheyne-Stokes, high-altitude periodic breathing, medication/substance, primary CSA, primary CSA of infancy, primary CSA of prematurity, treatment-emergent CSA). ICSD-3 additionally requires signs/symptoms and central events ≥50%.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** cite PSG central apnea index and proportion of central vs. obstructive events.
- **Subtype evidence:** heart failure/stroke/renal failure history (Cheyne-Stokes); opioid type, dose (morphine-equivalent), duration ≥ several months (opioid subtype); altitude exposure.
- **Coding sequence:** if OUD present, OUD code first, then G47.37.
- **Symptoms:** awakenings short of breath, insomnia, daytime sleepiness, confusion, low mood in methadone/chronic opioid patients.
- **Rule-outs:** OSA dominance, hypoventilation, insomnia due to substance use.
- **Coordination:** document communication with prescriber/sleep medicine about opioid dosing and sedative co-prescription.
- MSE phrasing: "drowsy, intermittently confused; on methadone maintenance; reports waking gasping for air."
