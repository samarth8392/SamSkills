# Rapid Eye Movement Sleep Behavior Disorder

> **Spark note:** Recurrent REM-related vocalization or complex movements occur with rapid, clear orientation on awakening. REM sleep without atonia on polysomnography ordinarily supports diagnosis; a suggestive history plus established synucleinopathy is the criteria's alternative route, described in the text as probable/provisional RBD (pp. 652–653).

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** Repeated sleep arousals with vocalization and/or complex motor behavior.
- **B:** Events arise from REM sleep: generally >90 minutes after sleep onset, later-night predominance, uncommon in naps.
- **C:** On waking, fully alert and oriented, without confusion.
- **D:** Either PSG documents REM without atonia **or** suggestive history accompanies an established synucleinopathy (e.g., Parkinson's disease/multiple system atrophy).
- **E:** Significant distress/impairment, including possible injury to self or bed partner.
- **F:** Not attributable to substance/medication physiology or another medical condition.
- **G:** Coexisting medical/mental conditions do not explain episodes (p. 652).

## Specifiers & severity
No formal DSM course/severity specifiers. Judge clinical severity by behavioral consequences/injury potential rather than frequency alone. The diagnostic text describes history plus established synucleinopathy without PSG as probable/provisional diagnosis; distinguish that from PSG confirmation (p. 653).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Dream enactment can include defensive striking, kicking, jumping or falling out of bed, with loud emotional vocalization; subtler movements also occur.
- Eyes usually stay closed; recalled dream activity can correspond closely to witnessed behavior.
- Isolated REM without atonia is insufficient without clinical enactment; injury risk and household distress matter diagnostically (pp. 652–654).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Population studies: approximately 1% in middle/older age in Switzerland, 2% in elderly South Koreans; not all studies show sex differences.
- Onset may be gradual or abrupt. Idiopathic RBD cohorts show approximately 75% develop a defined neurodegenerative disease within 10–15 years, annualized risk approximately 6%–7%; these are cohort estimates, not a prediction for an individual.
- Young patients, particularly women: consider narcolepsy, medication-induced parasomnia, brainstem lesion or autoimmune encephalopathy (p. 653).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
Taiwanese and U.S. clinical samples were broadly similar, with different wandering/injury rates possibly reflecting earlier family detection. More often recognized in men >50, but women and younger people are affected; women tend to present younger (pp. 653–654).

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Diagnostic markers
Video-PSG assesses increased tonic/phasic EMG during REM; broader limb and chin EMG can improve characterization. REM without atonia may occur without symptoms, so combine physiology with behavior. A pilot study found 7%–14% with isolated REM without atonia later developed clinical RBD; meaning remains uncertain (p. 654).

## Functional consequences
Immediate risk is injury to patient/partner; long-term concern is underlying neurodegeneration. Survey estimates: about 55% experience injury and 12% of injuries are serious, including fractures or subdural bleeding (p. 654).

## Differential diagnosis
- **NREM arousals:** earlier sleep, incomplete awakening, confusion and poorer recall; normal REM atonia unless comorbid RBD.
- **Medication-induced parasomnia:** TCAs, SSRIs and SNRIs may cause REM without atonia/enactment; classify as medication-induced when attributable. Whether agents induce or reveal predisposition is uncertain.
- **Asymptomatic REM without atonia:** no clinical enactment, hence not RBD.
- **Nocturnal seizures:** stereotyped episodes; extended EEG montage helps; REM without atonia generally absent.
- **OSA:** similar actions occur on arousal from obstructive events, improving with effective treatment; evaluate PSG timing.
- **Dissociative behaviors:** emerge from defined wakefulness during the sleep period.
- **Malingering:** objective PSG documentation is necessary when fabricated episodes are a concern; no assumption of deception from history alone (pp. 654–655).

## Comorbidity
Approximately 30% of narcolepsy patients have RBD; these cases are younger with similar rates by sex. Most initially idiopathic clinical cases eventually develop a synucleinopathy, sometimes after >10 years (pp. 655–656).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Obtain bed-partner history: action/vocalization, dream correspondence, timing, eye state, frequency and clear orientation after awakening.
- Cite actual PSG REM-atonia findings or established synucleinopathy plus suggestive history; label probable/provisional status accurately.
- Record injuries, near misses and impairment; distinguish observed events from recollection.
- Review medications, substances, OSA, seizure and dissociation evidence; document neurological follow-up needs without declaring future disease inevitable.
