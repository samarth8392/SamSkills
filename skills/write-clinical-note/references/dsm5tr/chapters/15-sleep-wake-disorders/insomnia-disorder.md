# Insomnia Disorder

> **Spark note:** A *subjective* complaint of poor sleep quantity/quality (trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, or early waking) on ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months, despite adequate opportunity to sleep, with daytime distress or impairment. It is diagnosed **alongside** comorbid depression, pain, sleep apnea, etc. — no causal attribution is required; the comorbidity goes in a specifier. Most common error: either dismissing insomnia as "just a symptom" of the comorbid disorder, or diagnosing it when the person simply lacks time in bed or is a short sleeper with no daytime consequences.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Predominant complaint of dissatisfaction with sleep quantity or quality, plus **≥1** of:
  1. Difficulty initiating sleep (children: can't fall asleep without caregiver help).
  2. Difficulty maintaining sleep — frequent awakenings or trouble returning to sleep (children: can't resettle without caregiver).
  3. Early-morning awakening with inability to return to sleep.
- **B.** Clinically significant distress or impairment (social, occupational, educational, academic, behavioral, or other).
- **C.** Frequency: **≥3 nights/week**.
- **D.** Duration: **≥3 months**.
- **E.** Occurs **despite adequate opportunity** for sleep.
- **F.** Not better explained by, and not exclusively during, another sleep-wake disorder (narcolepsy, breathing-related, circadian, parasomnia).
- **G.** Not attributable to a substance (drug of abuse, medication).
- **H.** Coexisting mental disorders/medical conditions do not adequately explain the predominant insomnia complaint.

**Key notes in the criteria set (p. 591):**
- Symptoms <3 months but otherwise meeting criteria → **other specified insomnia disorder** (short-term insomnia).
- Diagnose whether insomnia is independent or comorbid (e.g., with MDD, pain, sleep apnea). Not necessary to decide which caused which.
- Give a concurrent diagnosis only when the insomnia is severe enough to **warrant independent clinical attention**.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Comorbidity specifiers** (any/all that apply): *with mental disorder, including substance use disorders*; *with medical condition*; *with another sleep disorder*.
- **Course specifiers:**
  - **Episodic:** ≥1 month but <3 months. (Note the tension with Criterion D — see overview "ambiguities".)
  - **Persistent:** ≥3 months.
  - **Recurrent:** ≥2 episodes within 1 year.
- No formal severity specifier. Illustrative (not diagnostic) quantitative anchors (p. 592):
  - Sleep-onset difficulty: subjective sleep latency **>20–30 min**.
  - Maintenance difficulty: subjective wake after sleep onset **>20–30 min**.
  - Early-morning awakening: waking **≥1 hr before** scheduled time and before total sleep reaches **6½ hr**; interpret against bedtime (4 A.M. waking after a 9 P.M. bedtime ≠ after an 11 P.M. bedtime).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Subtypes by timing: **sleep-onset (initial)**, **sleep-maintenance (middle)**, **late (early-morning awakening)**. Maintenance difficulty is the single most common symptom (~60%); a combination is the most common presentation; the type of complaint often shifts over time.
- Diagnosis rests on the **person's (or caretaker's) subjective report**. Self-report typically overstates sleep latency and wake time and understates total sleep vs. polysomnography — thought to reflect hyperarousal/cortical activation. Don't discount the complaint because PSG looks better.
- **Nonrestorative sleep** usually accompanies onset/maintenance problems. When it occurs **alone** (no onset/maintenance difficulty, no other sleep disorder), the text points to an "other specified" diagnosis (insomnia text says other specified sleep-wake disorder; the other-specified insomnia listing names "restricted to nonrestorative sleep"). Isolated nonrestorative sleep ~5%, more common in younger people.
- Daytime impairments are required for "disorder": fatigue (sleepiness less often — more in older adults and with pain or apnea comorbidity), attention/concentration/memory problems, trouble with complex manual tasks, irritability/mood lability. Many older adults with fragmented sleep call themselves good sleepers → **no diagnosis** without distress/impairment.

## Associated features
- Physiological and cognitive **arousal** plus **conditioning**: striving to sleep → frustration → worse sleep (vicious cycle). Often sleeps better when not trying, or away from own bedroom.
- Maladaptive habits: excess time in bed, erratic schedule, napping. Maladaptive cognitions: fear of sleeplessness, worry about next-day impairment, **clock-watching**.
- May look fatigued/haggard **or** overaroused/"wired." More stress-related somatic complaints (tension headache, muscle tension/pain, GI symptoms); no characteristic physical exam findings.
- Subthreshold anxiety/depression; excessive focus on perceived daytime effects of sleep loss.
- Inventories: mild depression/anxiety, worrisome cognitive style, emotion-focused/internalizing conflict style, somatic focus. Neurocognitive findings inconsistent; more effort needed to maintain performance on complex tasks.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Prevalence**
- ~1/3 of adults report insomnia symptoms; 10%–15% have daytime impairment; 4%–22% meet disorder criteria (average ~**10%**). **Most prevalent sleep disorder.**
- Primary care: 20%–40% complain of significant insomnia symptoms.
- 40%–50% of those with insomnia disorder have a comorbid mental disorder.
- F:M ≈ **1.3:1**, rising to **1.7:1 after age 45**; Norwegian 16–18-year-olds: nearly double in girls.

**Development & course**
- First episode most often young adulthood; can begin in childhood/adolescence or late life (often with new health conditions). Menopause increases new-onset insomnia that may outlast hot flashes.
- Forms: **situational/acute** (days–weeks; tied to life events or schedule/environment change) → usually resolves; in vulnerable people persists via conditioning and heightened arousal. Precipitating factors ≠ perpetuating factors.
- Insomnia arising during MDD persists after the depressive episode resolves in **≥40%–50%**.
- Chronicity 45%–75% at 1–7 yr follow-up; night-to-night variability even when chronic. Many report lifelong "light" sleep before onset.
- Children: conditioning (needs parent to fall/return to sleep), lack of consistent schedule/routine. Adolescents: irregular schedules, especially **phase delay**.
- Older adults: higher prevalence partly from physical illness; distinguish from normal age-related change. PSG of limited routine value but more useful in older adults (apnea more common).

**Risk & prognostic factors** (predisposing + precipitating + perpetuating model)
- *Temperamental:* anxiety/worry-prone style, arousal predisposition, high stress reactivity, emotion repression.
- *Environmental:* noise, light, temperature extremes; high altitude (periodic breathing).
- *Genetic/physiological:* female sex, older age; familial — **35%–70%** report ≥1 first-degree relative with insomnia (mother most often); MZ > DZ concordance; heritability highest without comorbidity; sleep reactivity to stress plays a role.
- *Course modifiers:* poor sleep hygiene (excess caffeine, irregular schedule).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Universal experience; culture shapes whether it is seen as a problem, explanatory models (humidity, poor circulation, "internal heat"), and help-seeking (may be seen as normal aging/stress; coping via social support or prayer; nonbiomedical treatment).
- First onset in females often tied to **childbirth** or **menopause**. Despite higher prevalence in peri/postmenopausal females, PSG shows better-preserved continuity and slow-wave sleep in older females than older males.

## Diagnostic markers
- PSG: impaired continuity (↑ sleep latency, ↑ WASO, ↓ sleep efficiency); may show ↑ N1, ↓ N3. Severity often mismatches the complaint.
- Quantitative EEG: ↑ high-frequency power around sleep onset and in NREM (cortical arousal). Neuroimaging consistent with hyperarousal.
- Typically **no** increased objective daytime sleepiness (lower sleep propensity) — useful contrast with hypersomnolence/narcolepsy/apnea.
- Inconsistent evidence of HPA activation (↑ cortisol, heart-rate variability, stress reactivity, metabolic rate).

## Suicide risk
- Insomnia is an **independent risk factor** for suicidal thoughts and behavior (p. 597).
- Chapter-level (p. 589): insomnia may raise risk of suicidal thoughts, behavior, and death even after adjusting for depression; in one college sample 31.3% of those with sleep problems had suicidal thoughts and 82.7% of those with suicidal thoughts had sleep problems; teens sleeping <8 hr have higher risk of self-harm and suicidal thoughts/behavior.

## Functional consequences
- Interpersonal, social, occupational problems; irritability; poor concentration; higher accident rates.
- ≥2-fold risk of new-onset MDD, anxiety disorders, substance use disorders; risk factor for MDD relapse.
- With objective short sleep (<6 hr): significant risk for hypertension, CAD/MI, CHF, cerebrovascular disease.
- Absenteeism, reduced productivity, lower quality of life, economic burden.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Normal sleep variation / short sleepers:** no difficulty falling/staying asleep and no daytime symptoms; may create insomnia-like pattern by lengthening time in bed.
- **Normal age-related change:** fragmentation without complaint or daytime effects.
- **Sleep deprivation from inadequate opportunity** (emergencies, work/family duties): fails Criterion E.
- **Situational/acute insomnia:** days to weeks; if meets all but 3-month duration → other specified or unspecified insomnia disorder.
- **Circadian — delayed sleep phase type:** onset insomnia only when trying to sleep at socially normal times; sleeps fine on own delayed schedule; adolescents/young adults. **Shift work type:** recent shift work history.
- **Restless legs syndrome:** urge to move legs and unpleasant leg sensations.
- **Breathing-related sleep disorders:** loud snoring, breathing pauses, daytime sleepiness; but up to **50%** with sleep apnea also report insomnia (more in women and older adults).
- **Narcolepsy:** predominant excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, sleep paralysis, sleep-related hallucinations.
- **Parasomnias:** unusual behaviors/events dominate rather than insomnia.
- **Substance/medication-induced sleep disorder, insomnia type:** substance etiologically linked (e.g., insomnia only with heavy coffee → caffeine-induced sleep disorder, insomnia type, with onset during intoxication).

## Comorbidity
- Medical: cancer, diabetes, coronary heart disease, COPD, arthritis, fibromyalgia, other chronic pain, degenerative brain disease, TBI. Relationship is **bidirectional** → "comorbid insomnia" is the preferred term.
- Sleep: ~**1 in 7** with insomnia disorder has moderate–severe OSA; ~50% of people with narcolepsy report insomnia.
- Mental: especially bipolar, depressive, anxiety disorders; persistent insomnia is a risk factor or early symptom of bipolar, depressive, anxiety, and substance use disorders.
- Self-medication: alcohol/sedatives for sleep, anxiolytics for tension, caffeine/stimulants for daytime fatigue → may worsen insomnia and progress to SUD.

## ICSD-3 relationship
ICSD-3 has chronic, short-term, and other insomnia disorder; DSM-5 insomnia disorder ≈ ICSD-3 chronic insomnia. ICSD-3 has no separate substance-induced insomnia category.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion A:** record the specific pattern — e.g., "reports ~60 min to fall asleep," "wakes 3–4×/night, 45+ min to return to sleep," "wakes at 4 A.M. (bedtime 11 P.M.), unable to return to sleep." Note the client's own dissatisfaction ("feels sleep is inadequate/unrefreshing").
- **C/D:** nights per week (≥3) and onset date/duration (≥3 months); a 2-week sleep diary is good supporting evidence.
- **E:** document adequate opportunity — usual bedtime, rise time, time in bed, no external constraint keeping them awake.
- **B (daytime impact):** fatigue, low energy, concentration/memory problems, irritability, errors or near-misses at work/driving, missed obligations.
- **Associated/perpetuating factors:** conditioned arousal ("wide awake as soon as I get into bed"), clock-watching, worry about sleep, napping, irregular schedule, sleeps better away from home, caffeine/alcohol timing.
- **Rule-outs to document:** snoring/witnessed apneas/gasping (OSA screen); leg urge/discomfort in the evening (RLS); preferred late schedule with normal sleep when unconstrained (DSPT); shift work; excessive daytime sleep attacks/cataplexy (narcolepsy); parasomnia events; substance/medication timing (caffeine, alcohol, stimulants, activating antidepressants, steroids, withdrawal).
- **Comorbidity specifier:** name the comorbid condition(s) and code them after F51.01; state that insomnia warrants independent clinical attention.
- **Suicide:** document SI screen — insomnia is an independent risk factor.
- MSE/phrasing examples: "appears tired, reduced eye contact and slowed responses attributed to poor sleep"; "anxious preoccupation with sleep and next-day functioning"; "describes racing thoughts at bedtime."
