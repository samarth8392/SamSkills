# Inhalant-Induced Mental Disorders

> **Spark note:** Chapter 19 lists these syndromes as routes to the full substance/medication-induced diagnoses in the corresponding symptom chapters. It does not provide new complete disorder criteria for each here. (pp. 831–832)

## Criteria at a glance
The section cross-references psychotic, depressive, anxiety, major or mild neurocognitive presentations. Apply the complete criteria in the relevant phenomenological chapter, including evidence the agent can cause the syndrome, temporal relation and exclusion of a better independent disorder. Use an induced diagnosis in place of ordinary intoxication when the predominant symptoms warrant independent clinical attention. Assess intoxication delirium separately; persistent NCD is described in chapter 20. (pp. 831–832)

## Specifiers & severity
Use the relevant induced diagnosis's onset/type specifiers; do not infer all listed syndromes occur at every exposure phase. The broad substance-induced framework is on pp. 762–765.

## Coding
Select the exact code from the relevant induced disorder criteria. Same-class use-disorder presence/severity is incorporated when that diagnosis's coding table specifies it; avoid adding a duplicate stand-alone SUD code. No generic code for this entire list is supplied. Name the actual agent when known. (pp. 758–765, 831–832)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Predating exposure or persisting well beyond acute recovery may support an independent disorder; the general roughly one-month heuristic does not apply to persisting NCD or HPPD. Symptoms occurring exclusively within delirium are evaluated as part of delirium rather than automatically assigned multiple induced diagnoses. (pp. 762–764)

## Suicide risk
Risk depends on the particular syndrome and agent; this short cross-reference section gives no separate quantitative risk estimate.

## Differential diagnosis
Ordinary intoxication/withdrawal, an independent primary psychiatric disorder, another agent, and delirium or another medical cause must be considered. Establish the sequence and reassess outside acute exposure. (pp. 762–765, 831–832)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record the actual syndrome, severity/functional impact, agent, dose/route and onset relative to use, cessation or prescribed dosing.
- Document baseline psychiatric symptoms, sober course, medical exclusions and attention/awareness.
- Identify the full induced-disorder criteria used and the included same-class SUD status, with uncertainty made explicit.
