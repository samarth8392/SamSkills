# Alcohol-Induced Mental Disorders

> **Spark note:** Alcohol can produce psychotic, bipolar, depressive, anxiety, sleep, sexual, delirium and neurocognitive syndromes that look identical to independent disorders. They are told apart by **timing**: they arise during **severe** intoxication or withdrawal and usually clear within **days to ~1 month** of abstinence, even without medication. In AUD, about 40% have a lifetime major depressive episode, but only **one-third to one-half** of those are independent. Consider the induced diagnosis **before** labeling an independent mood, anxiety or psychotic disorder in a heavy drinker.

**Threshold rule:** diagnose these **instead of** alcohol intoxication or withdrawal **only when symptoms are severe enough to warrant independent clinical attention**.

## Criteria at a glance (shared framework, pp. 782–783)
- Symptoms resemble the corresponding independent disorder.
- They **must develop in the context of severe intoxication and/or withdrawal**.
- **Not better explained by an independent disorder.** An independent disorder is likely if symptoms were present **before** severe intoxication or withdrawal, or **continued >1 month** after it stopped.
- **Not only during delirium.** Mood, anxiety and reality-testing symptoms seen only during a delirium belong to the delirium.
- Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- Use on top of a **pre-existing** disorder tends to **intensify** it.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 782–783)
- Same severe consequences as independent disorders, **including suicide attempts**, but they **improve without formal treatment** in days to weeks.
- People also show the associated features of AUD.
- **Rates:** lifetime MDE risk in AUD ≈ **40%**, with only **one-third to one-half** independent. Induced **sleep** and **anxiety** disorders occur at similar rates. **Induced psychotic episodes occur in <5%** of people with AUD.

## Development & course (p. 783)
- Symptoms stay relevant **as long as severe intoxication or withdrawal continues**.
- All except **alcohol-induced NCD, amnestic-confabulatory type** (formerly alcohol-induced persisting amnestic disorder / Korsakoff) improve quickly and **rarely stay relevant beyond 1 month** after stopping.
- **Independent** schizophrenia, MDD, bipolar disorder and panic disorder have longer episodes and usually need longer-term medication. Induced disorders resolve **within several days to 1 month, even without psychotropics**.
- Like screening for endocrine or medication causes, **consider the alcohol-induced diagnosis before diagnosing an independent disorder**, given how prevalent AUD is worldwide.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Alcohol intoxication or withdrawal:** symptoms are **within the usual range** for those states.
- **Independent mental disorder:** onset **before** heavy use, persistence **>1 month** of abstinence, or a history of episodes during sobriety.
- **Delirium:** disturbed attention and awareness. Psychiatric symptoms seen only during delirium are not coded separately.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Timeline** is the core evidence: age at onset of heavy drinking vs onset of psychiatric symptoms, symptom status during any **past abstinent periods**, and date of last drink.
- **Onset specifier:** during intoxication vs during withdrawal.
- **Severity beyond the expected:** e.g., "depressed mood, hopelessness and SI dominate the presentation beyond typical post-binge dysphoria; requiring clinical attention."
- **Plan reassessment** after ~4 weeks of abstinence. If symptoms persist → reconsider an independent disorder.
- Screen for **delirium** (orientation, attention) and document the result.
- For NCD: document cognitive testing, nutrition and thiamine status, and **amnestic-confabulatory** features (new learning failure, confabulation).
- **Suicide risk** assessment. Induced episodes carry real risk.
- Record the **single combined code** with AUD severity.
