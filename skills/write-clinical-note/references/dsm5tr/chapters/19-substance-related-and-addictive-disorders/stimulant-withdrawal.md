# Stimulant Withdrawal

> **Spark note:** **Dysphoric mood is required**, plus ≥2 of 5 physiological changes (fatigue, vivid unpleasant dreams, insomnia/hypersomnia, increased appetite, psychomotor change). Bradycardia is a reliable sign. Anhedonia and craving are common but **not criteria**. The "crash" after a binge carries the main risk: **depression with suicidal thoughts or behaviour**.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Cessation of or reduction in **prolonged** amphetamine-type, cocaine or other stimulant use.
- **B.** **Dysphoric mood + ≥2** of the following, within a few hours to several days:
  1. Fatigue
  2. Vivid, unpleasant dreams
  3. Insomnia or hypersomnia
  4. Increased appetite
  5. Psychomotor retardation or agitation
- **C.** Clinically significant distress/impairment.
- **D.** Not due to another medical condition or better explained by another mental disorder, including another substance's intoxication or withdrawal.

## Specifiers & severity
- Specify the substance: amphetamine-type, cocaine or other stimulant. No perceptual-disturbance specifier.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Follows prolonged, generally **high-dose** use.
- **Bradycardia** is often present and is a reliable measure.
- Anhedonia and craving are often present but **not** diagnostic criteria.

## Associated features
- **"Crash"** after "runs" or "binges": intense lassitude, depression, increased appetite; several days of rest needed.
- Depression with suicidal thoughts or behaviour is the most serious problem.
- Many people with stimulant use disorder have withdrawal at some point.
- From the use-disorder section: withdrawal depression can meet MDE symptom and duration criteria but usually resolves **within ~1 week**. Cocaine-related disturbances can persist up to 1 month.

## Suicide risk
- Depressive symptoms with suicidal thoughts or behaviour are generally the most serious problem in crashing or withdrawal.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Stimulant-induced mental disorders** (e.g., stimulant-induced depressive disorder with onset during withdrawal): symptoms exceed usual withdrawal, predominate, and warrant independent attention.
- **MDD:** look for a pre-use history or persistence well beyond withdrawal.

## Comorbidity
See Stimulant Use Disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Substance type (F14 versus F15), binge length and dose, time since last use.
- Dysphoria (required) plus each physiological sign: sleep hours, appetite or eating, dream content, psychomotor observations, HR (bradycardia).
- **Suicide risk assessment** (ideation, plan, access, prior attempts during crashes); safety plan.
- Craving and anhedonia: record them, while noting they aren't criteria.
- Prescribed-stimulant cases: state whether taken as prescribed (F15.93) or not.
- Reassess mood after about 1–4 weeks of abstinence to separate induced from independent depression.
