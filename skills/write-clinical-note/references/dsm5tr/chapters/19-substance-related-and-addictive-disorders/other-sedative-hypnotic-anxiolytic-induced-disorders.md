# Sedative-, Hypnotic-, or Anxiolytic-Induced Mental Disorders

> **Spark note:** Pointer entry. Sedatives can induce a broader range of disorders than opioids: **psychotic, bipolar, depressive, anxiety, sleep and sexual disorders, major or mild NCD, and delirium**. Diagnose them instead of intoxication/withdrawal only when symptoms warrant independent clinical attention. Sedative-induced NCD is one of the persisting exceptions to the "resolves within 1 month" rule (p. 764).

## Coding
- Codes are in the target chapters. The 4th character = use-disorder status (F13.1x mild, F13.2x moderate/severe, F13.9x none). E.g., F13.14 / F13.24 for SHA-induced depressive disorder (p. 848).

## Pearls
- General rule (p. 763): sedating drugs tend to cause **depressive** syndromes during intoxication and **anxiety** syndromes during withdrawal.
- Versus plain intoxication/withdrawal: the symptoms are excessive, predominate, and need separate attention.
- Hallucinations without intact reality testing during withdrawal → consider SHA-induced psychotic disorder (p. 858).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Timeline of symptoms against dosing, taper and abstinence; pre-use psychiatric baseline.
- Onset specifier (intoxication versus withdrawal) and use-disorder severity.
- For cognitive complaints: serial cognitive testing after abstinence to judge persistence (NCD).
