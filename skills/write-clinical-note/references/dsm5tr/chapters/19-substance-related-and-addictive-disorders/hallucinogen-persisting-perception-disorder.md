# Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder

> **Spark note:** HPPD means clinically important recurrence of prior hallucinogen-related perceptions after use has stopped. Preserved insight and exclusion of neurological/ocular or primary psychiatric explanations are central; a benign isolated flashback does not automatically qualify. (pp. 820–822)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** After stopping hallucinogens, ≥1 perception previously experienced during intoxication recurs (often visual: trails, halos, color/motion changes, geometric forms, afterimages, altered object size). **B:** Significant distress or functional impairment. **C:** No better medical condition, mental disorder (including delirium, NCD or schizophrenia), or sleep-transition hallucination explanation. (p. 820)

## Specifiers & severity
No formal severity/course specifier or fixed minimum duration is specified. Symptoms may recur episodically or persist nearly continuously for weeks, months or years. (pp. 820–821)

## Coding
F16.983 is the code specified in the source; no severity-based combination-code alternatives are given here. (p. 820)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Usually follows LSD but can follow other hallucinogens, including limited exposure. Frequency of use does not reliably predict occurrence. Other drugs, darkness, exercise or sensory stimulation may trigger symptoms. Reality testing is maintained; absent insight requires reconsidering the diagnosis. (p. 821)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Population prevalence and risk factors are poorly established. The source cites an initial estimate around 4.2% among hallucinogen users, with limited certainty; persistence varies substantially. (p. 821)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
Persistent symptoms may be distressing, although some affected people compensate and continue ordinary functioning. (p. 821)

## Differential diagnosis
Investigate migraine aura, visual seizures, brain lesions/infection, stroke, head injury, neurodegeneration, other drugs and primary psychosis. Imaging is typically negative but a normal scan does not establish HPPD. Determine whether symptoms arise specifically at sleep-wake transitions. (pp. 821–822)

## Comorbidity
Panic, alcohol use disorder, depression, bipolar I and schizophrenia-spectrum disorders are reported; comorbidity does not remove the need for preserved insight and adequate attribution. (p. 822)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe the original hallucinogen exposure/perception and the matching sober recurrences, with dates, frequency, triggers and duration.
- Record insight/reality testing, distress/functional effects and medical, neurological, substance and psychiatric rule-outs.
- Distinguish documented findings from a patient's causal interpretation of the symptoms.
