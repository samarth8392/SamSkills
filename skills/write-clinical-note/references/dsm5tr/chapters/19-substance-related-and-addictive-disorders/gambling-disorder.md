# Gambling Disorder

> **Spark note:** Clinically significant gambling with ≥4 criteria in 12 months qualifies; money wagered or gambling frequency alone does not. Exclude behavior better explained by mania, while medication-triggered gambling can still meet this diagnosis. (pp. 896–902)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** A recurrent/persistent impairing or distressing gambling pattern with ≥4 in a 12-month period:
1. Increasing stakes needed for comparable excitement.
2. Restlessness/irritability with attempted reduction.
3. Repeated failed efforts to control/stop.
4. Gambling preoccupation or persistent planning/fundraising thoughts.
5. Repeated gambling in distress.
6. Repeated return to recover losses.
7. Concealment of gambling extent through lying.
8. Major relationship, job or educational/career opportunity endangered/lost.
9. Reliance on others for rescue from desperate gambling-related finances.
**B:** A manic episode does not better explain the gambling. (pp. 896–897)

## Specifiers & severity
Mild 4–5; moderate 6–7; severe 8–9 criteria. **Episodic:** qualifying periods separated by ≥several months of symptom subsidence. **Persistent:** continuous qualifying symptoms for multiple years. Early remission ≥3–<12 months and sustained remission ≥12 months after a prior full syndrome, with **no criteria** during that interval; unlike SUD remission, no craving exception is supplied. (pp. 897–898)

## Coding
F63.0; record severity, episodic/persistent course and remission in words. The source includes no substance-style combination-code system for this disorder. (pp. 896–897)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Repeated loss-chasing, concealment and financial bailouts are clinically useful clues. Illusions of control, superstition and denial may occur but are not added criteria. Dopaminergic treatment can induce a qualifying syndrome; record that association and diagnose gambling disorder when criteria are met. (p. 898)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Source U.S. past-year estimate is about 0.2%–0.3%; international estimates vary. Onset ranges from adolescence through later life. Courses can fluctuate, remit or persist. Prior gambling problems, impulsivity, early exposure, psychiatric illness and familial vulnerability increase risk. Digital gambling and chance-based game rewards may affect youth exposure. Amount/frequency are interpreted relative to the actual consequences, not a universal spending cutoff. (pp. 898–900)

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
Available gambling forms, socioeconomic context and reporting of criteria vary. Men have higher overall rates; reported sex differences in progression vary between treatment and community samples. Treatment seeking is low across sexes and age groups. (pp. 900–901)

## Suicide risk
High suicidal ideation/attempt rates in treatment samples and elevated mortality in a Swedish registry support individual risk assessment; selected samples are not universal prevalence estimates. (p. 901)

## Functional consequences
Debt, deception and bailout cycles damage relationships; preoccupation/absence affects school and work. General health and healthcare utilization can also be affected. (p. 901)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Social/professional gambling:** disciplined risk or occasional bounded recreational gambling does not establish a disorder; subthreshold problems still merit description.
- **Mania:** diagnose gambling disorder in addition only when gambling is not better accounted for by manic episodes; assess outside-episode behavior.
- **Personality disorders:** both diagnoses may be made if independently met.
- **Dopaminergic medications:** can cause qualifying gambling symptoms; source still directs a gambling-disorder diagnosis. (pp. 901–902)

## Comorbidity
Substance, depressive, bipolar, anxiety and personality disorders are common and often precede gambling problems. Some medical disorders occur at increased rates. (p. 902)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Map dated examples to all nine items, the common 12-month interval and count; document concrete relationship/work/financial consequences.
- Assess mood episodes and gambling outside them; review dopaminergic medication timing.
- Record episodic/persistent pattern, remission dates, actual suicide assessment and collateral limits without inventing debt or behavior.
