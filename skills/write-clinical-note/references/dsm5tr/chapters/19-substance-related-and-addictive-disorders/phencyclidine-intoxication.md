# Phencyclidine Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Rapid dissociative intoxication with nystagmus, reduced pain response and autonomic/coordination changes supports PCP attribution. Use its acute physical/behavioral criteria rather than inferring it from agitation alone. (pp. 815–817)
## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Recent PCP or similar-agent use. **B:** Problematic behavioral change during/soon after use. **C:** Within one hour, ≥2: vertical/horizontal nystagmus; hypertension/tachycardia; numbness/reduced pain response; ataxia; impaired articulation; rigidity; seizure/coma; hyperacusis. **D:** No better medical, psychiatric or other-intoxicant explanation. (pp. 815–817)

## Specifiers & severity
No separate symptom-count severity scale is specified for this acute syndrome. (pp. 815–817)

## Coding
Source-edition codes (pp. 815–817):

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Confusion, disorientation, unusual perceptions, amnesia and agitation can occur; rapid routes may act especially quickly. PCP may remain urine-detectable up to eight days, but concentration correlates poorly with clinical state. Creatine kinase and aminotransferases can rise. (pp. 816–817)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
Cardiovascular and neurological toxicity, seizures, temperature dysregulation and injury can occur. (p. 817)

## Differential diagnosis
Other hallucinogens, stimulants, anticholinergics and benzodiazepine withdrawal can resemble it. Consider hypoglycemia, electrolyte abnormalities, sepsis, CNS tumors, vascular events, seizures and neuroleptic malignant syndrome. Psychotic symptoms without insight warrant induced-psychosis assessment. A predominant induced mood/anxiety syndrome requires separate consideration. (p. 817)

## Comorbidity
Often accompanies PCP use disorder and other substance disorders. (p. 817)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record exposure, behavior change and ≥2 signs within an hour, with vitals/neurological findings actually observed.
- Document reality testing, attention/arousal, other-drug/medical assessment, and toxicology limitations.
- Establish comorbid use-disorder severity independently for code selection.
