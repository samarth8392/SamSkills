# Other (or Unknown) Substance Withdrawal

> **Spark note:** Withdrawal requires a reduction-linked syndrome and a defensible causal attribution. The source explicitly notes how difficult substance-specific confirmation is when the agent is unidentified. (pp. 893–895)
## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Heavy, prolonged use is stopped or reduced. **B:** A characteristic syndrome follows soon afterward. **C:** Clinically significant distress or impairment. **D:** No better medical, psychiatric or other-withdrawal explanation. **E:** The agent is unknown or outside the other recognized withdrawal classes. No universal symptom-count threshold or single dose/duration is specified. (pp. 893–895)

## Specifiers & severity
No separate symptom-count severity scale is specified for this acute syndrome. **With perceptual disturbances** requires preserved reality testing during hallucinations, or illusions, without delirium. Loss of insight calls for a psychotic-disorder assessment; impaired attention/awareness calls for delirium assessment. (pp. 893–895)

## Coding
Source-edition codes (pp. 893–895):

| Comorbid same-class use disorder | Code |
|---|---|
| Mild | F19.130 |
| Moderate/severe | F19.230 |
| None | F19.930 |

With perceptual disturbances, use F19.132 / F19.232 / F19.932 for mild / moderate-severe / no use disorder.

Use the combined code rather than a separate stand-alone use-disorder code; name the specific substance if known.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
When known, record the specific drug. Temporal onset, elimination rate, dose and exposure duration inform judgment. Withdrawal can range from discomfort to fatal illness and last days to months depending on the agent. Routine tests may not identify new/unusual products. (pp. 893–895)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Prevalence is unknown. Dysphoria and withdrawal relief can motivate renewed use; general risk context is covered in [other/unknown use disorder](other-or-unknown-substance-use-disorder.md). (p. 894)

## Suicide risk
The source includes suicide attempts among potential withdrawal-related consequences; assess individual presentation without presuming all agents have identical risk. (p. 894)

## Functional consequences
Mood, cognitive, autonomic and physical effects may disrupt work, relationships and safety or contribute to overdose/renewed substance use. (p. 894)

## Differential diagnosis
Dose reduction without a syndrome, familiar drugs sold under false names, induced psychiatric disorders, intoxication and medical/neurological causes can resemble withdrawal. (p. 895)

## Comorbidity
Other SUDs and externalizing disorders commonly coexist. (p. 895)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record prior dosing pattern, cessation/reduction time and actual onset/course of each symptom.
- State what supports a characteristic syndrome despite agent uncertainty and which alternatives were assessed.
- Record attention/awareness, insight if perceptual symptoms occur, actual impairment and independent SUD evaluation.
