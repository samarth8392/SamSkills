# Stimulant Use Disorder

> **Spark note:** One criteria set covers **amphetamine-type substances, cocaine and other stimulants**, but the **codes split**: **F14 = cocaine**, **F15 = amphetamine-type and other or unspecified stimulants**. Always record the specific drug. Tolerance/withdrawal from stimulants taken as prescribed for ADHD or narcolepsy under supervision **don't count**. Caffeine, nicotine and MDMA are *not* in this class. The most dangerous withdrawal problem is **depression with suicidality during the "crash."**

## Criteria at a glance
**A.** Pattern of amphetamine-type, cocaine or other stimulant use → clinically significant impairment/distress, **≥2 of 11 within 12 months** (pp. 861–862):
1. Larger amounts / longer than intended
2. Persistent desire or failed efforts to cut down/control
3. Lots of time obtaining, using, recovering
4. Craving
5. Recurrent use → failed major role obligations
6. Continued despite persistent social/interpersonal problems
7. Activities given up/reduced
8. Recurrent use when physically hazardous
9. Continued despite known physical/psychological problem caused/worsened by it
10. Tolerance (markedly more needed, or diminished effect). **Not met if taking stimulant medication solely under appropriate medical supervision (e.g., for ADHD, narcolepsy).**
11. Withdrawal (characteristic stimulant withdrawal, or stimulant or a related substance taken to relieve/avoid it). **Same medical-supervision exclusion.**

## Specifiers & severity
- Severity: **Mild 2–3 · Moderate 4–5 · Severe ≥6**.
- **In early remission:** no criteria for ≥3 to <12 mo (craving allowed). **In sustained remission:** ≥12 mo (craving allowed).
- **In a controlled environment:** restricted access. A further remission specifier (supervised jails, therapeutic communities, locked units).
- **No "on maintenance therapy" specifier** for stimulants.
- Specify the substance type: amphetamine-type, cocaine, or other/unspecified stimulant.

The 4th character of the induced code carries severity: e.g., **F15.14 / F15.24** amphetamine-induced depressive disorder with mild / moderate-severe amphetamine use disorder; **F14.14 / F14.24** for cocaine (pp. 863–864). The amphetamine-type instructions also apply to other/unspecified stimulants.
- Record the drug by name: "methylphenidate use disorder", "methamphetamine withdrawal", "cocaine intoxication".

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Amphetamine-type**: substituted phenylethylamines (amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, methamphetamine), plus structurally different drugs with similar effects (**methylphenidate, modafinil, armodafinil**), plant-derived **khât** and synthetic **cathinones**. Taken orally or IV; methamphetamine also intranasally.
- Prescribed for obesity, ADHD and narcolepsy, so diversion occurs.
- **Cocaine** forms: coca leaves, coca paste, hydrochloride powder (snorted or injected), and alkaloids (freebase, crack: vaporized and inhaled, extremely rapid onset).
- Disorder can develop in **as little as 1 week**. Tolerance occurs with repeated use by any route.
- Most have had tolerance or withdrawal. Withdrawal (hypersomnia, increased appetite, dysphoria) heightens craving.
- Amphetamines are longer-acting than cocaine, so they are used fewer times per day. Use is chronic or episodic with binges.
- High doses → aggression/violence; intense panic- or GAD-like anxiety; paranoia and schizophrenia-like psychosis.
- Withdrawal → intense temporary MDE-like depression, usually resolving **within 1 week**.
- Some amphetamine users develop **sensitization** (enhanced effects) instead of tolerance.

## Associated features
- Smoked or IV → instant well-being, confidence, euphoria. Rapid dramatic behavioural change.
- Long-term: chaotic behaviour, isolation, aggression, sexual dysfunction.
- Acute intoxication: rambling speech, headache, transient ideas of reference, tinnitus. Paranoia, auditory hallucinations in a clear sensorium, tactile hallucinations, usually recognised as drug effects. Threats or aggression.
- Withdrawal: depression, suicidal thoughts, irritability, anhedonia, lability, poor attention. Physiology opposite to intoxication (e.g., **bradycardia**).
- Cocaine-related mental disturbances usually resolve in hours to days but can persist **up to 1 month**.
- Common histories: panic attacks, social-anxiety-like and GAD-like syndromes, **eating disorders**.
- Extreme toxicity: stimulant-induced psychotic disorder (schizophrenia-like).
- **Cue-conditioned craving** (e.g., any white powder) persists after detox and drives relapse.
- Depression with suicidality is generally the most serious withdrawal problem.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Prevalence (12-mo, US, age ≥12)**
| | Overall | 12–17 | 18–25 | ≥26 | Men / Women |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amphetamine-type UD | 0.4% | 0.1% | 0.5% | 0.4% | 0.5% / 0.2% |
| Cocaine UD | 0.4% | 0.1% | 0.7% | 0.3% | 0.5% / 0.2% |

- Amphetamine-type UD ≈0.4% Hispanic and non-Hispanic White, 0.1% African American and Asian American; some evidence of higher rates in AI/AN.
- Prescription stimulants (US adults): 6.6% used; 4.5% without misuse; 1.9% misused without UD; **0.2% UD**. Non-Hispanic Whites more often use nonmedically; Hispanics use more frequently and have higher Rx-stimulant UD rates.
- Cocaine UD: 0.4% African American and non-Hispanic White, 0.3% Hispanic, <0.1% Asian American.

**Development & course**
- All levels of society; peak 18–25. First regular use averages ~**23 y** in treated samples. Average treatment-admission age: methamphetamine 34; smoked cocaine 44; other cocaine routes 37.
- Some start for weight control or performance (school, work, athletics), sometimes with others' ADHD medication.
- Amphetamine-type admissions: 61% smoke, 26% inject, 9% snort.
- Patterns: **episodic** (weekend/weekday bursts separated by ≥2 days off), **binges** (continuous high dose over hours/days until supply runs out or exhaustion; associated with physical dependence), or **chronic daily** use with dose escalation.
- **Smoked/IV → severe disorder in weeks to months; intranasal cocaine or oral amphetamines → months to years.**
- Pleasure diminishes with tolerance and dysphoric effects increase.

**Risk & prognostic**
- Temperamental: comorbid bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, ASPD and other SUDs (risk for onset and for cocaine relapse); high stress reactivity; childhood conduct disorder.
- Cocaine use risk: prior substance use, male sex, Cluster B PD, family history of SUD, being separated, divorced or widowed. **MSM at higher risk for methamphetamine.**
- Environmental: prenatal cocaine exposure, parental cocaine use, childhood community violence. IPV and childhood mistreatment (especially women). SES and food insecurity show a dose-dependent effect in women. Youth (especially girls): unstable home, psychiatric condition, criminal behaviour, associating with dealers and users.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- US cocaine **use** rose 2001–02 → 2012–13 among Whites, African Americans and Latinx, but cocaine **use disorder** rose only among Whites.
- The criteria perform equally across gender and ethnoracial groups.
- AI/AN appear at higher risk for methamphetamine UD (less so for cocaine UD). NH/PI similar to non-Hispanic Whites.
- Treatment admissions: meth/amphetamine 64% non-Hispanic White; smoked cocaine 51% non-Hispanic Black; other cocaine routes 47% non-Hispanic White. **Interpret clinical-sample rates cautiously**: they reflect access, criminalization, stigma and racial bias in referral.
- Women with cocaine UD more often have comorbid depression and PTSD.
- Higher **progesterone** in females → lower stress- and cue-induced craving, which may explain lower use in pregnancy. Gonadal hormones affect male responses too.

## Diagnostic markers
- Cocaine metabolite **benzoylecgonine** in urine: **1–3 days** after a single dose; **7–12 days** with repeated high doses.
- Methamphetamine (short half-life): 1–3 days, up to 4. **Hair: up to 90 days.**
- Mildly raised LFTs in cocaine injectors or with concurrent alcohol. No neurobiological diagnostic markers.
- After chronic cocaine: EEG changes, altered prolactin, dopamine receptor downregulation.
- Both classes: weight loss, malnutrition, poor hygiene.

## Suicide risk
- Data limited (most studies examine use, not disorder).
- Regular or problem amphetamine use (especially injecting or treated) → increased suicide mortality.
- Prescription stimulant UD is associated with suicidal thoughts (US general population).
- Cocaine UD → much more suicidal ideation than other SUDs in treatment samples.
- VA: cocaine and amphetamine UDs each associated with higher suicide death rates.
- **Withdrawal depression with suicidal thoughts or behaviour is the most serious withdrawal problem.**

## Functional consequences
- Route-specific: intranasal (sinusitis, mucosal bleeding, **septal perforation**); smoked (cough, bronchitis, pneumonitis; **pneumothorax** from Valsalva); IV ("tracks" on forearms, HIV, HCV, HBV, STDs, TB).
- Weight loss, malnutrition.
- Cardiovascular: chest pain (common in intoxication), MI, arrhythmias, sudden cardiac or respiratory death, stroke, even in young healthy people.
- Pregnancy (cocaine): placental flow problems, **abruptio placentae**, premature labour, very low birth weight.
- Theft, sex work, dealing; violent injuries in trafficking.
- **Neurocognitive deficits** (attention, impulsivity, verbal learning/memory, working memory, executive function) with both meth and cocaine. Transient psychosis and seizures with chronic use.
- Amphetamine: hyperthermia toxicity; possible dopaminergic neurotoxicity/neuroinflammation; **"meth mouth"** (gum disease, decay, sores, bruxism); pulmonary arterial hypertension.
- Frequent ED visits (psychiatric symptoms, injury, skin infections, dental). Stimulant UD = **20% increase in 30-day all-cause readmission**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **PCP or synthetic "designer drug" intoxication** (mephedrone/"bath salts"): similar picture; distinguished only by cocaine/amphetamine metabolites in urine or plasma.
- **Stimulant intoxication / withdrawal / induced disorders:** syndromes of heavy use; diagnose with the use disorder carried in the code.
- **Independent mental disorders** (schizophrenia, MDD): establish whether symptoms **preceded** drug intake.
- (Prescribed ADHD/narcolepsy stimulant use: tolerance/withdrawal alone ≠ disorder.)

## Comorbidity
- Other SUDs, especially sedatives (taken to offset insomnia and nervousness). Cocaine-treated patients also use heroin, PCP, alcohol; amphetamine-treated patients also use cannabis, heroin, alcohol.
- PTSD, ASPD, ADHD, **gambling disorder**.
- Cardiopulmonary problems (chest pain most common) in cocaine treatment-seekers.
- Adulterants: **levamisole**-cut cocaine → agranulocytosis, febrile neutropenia.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Specify the **substance type** (cocaine versus methamphetamine versus prescription stimulant versus cathinone) because it determines F14 versus F15.
- Route and pattern (binge/run length, days of use per month, crash episodes); age of first regular use.
- Criteria count with dated examples within 12 months.
- If an ADHD or narcolepsy prescription exists: prescribed dose versus actual, diversion, non-oral routes, using to get high or stay awake. Exclude tolerance/withdrawal from prescribed use.
- Psychiatric sx timeline: paranoia or hallucinations only while using versus persisting >1 month abstinent; whether pre-use depression or anxiety existed.
- **Suicide risk assessment at every crash or withdrawal contact.**
- Medical: chest pain, weight, dentition, nasal septum, injection sites, BBV testing, pregnancy status.
- MSE: pressured or rambling speech, hypervigilance, paranoia, stereotypies, psychomotor agitation; in withdrawal, dysphoria, psychomotor slowing, hypersomnia.
- Remission start date; controlled-environment status.
