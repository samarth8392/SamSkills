# Caffeine Withdrawal

> **Spark note:** After **prolonged daily** caffeine use, an abrupt stop or cut leads within **24 h** to **≥3 of 5** symptoms: headache, marked fatigue or drowsiness, dysphoric or depressed mood or irritability, poor concentration, and flu-like symptoms. **Headache is the hallmark** but is not required. It is often **misattributed** to flu or migraine, e.g., during pre-procedure fasting, weekends, travel or religious fasts. It can follow stopping as little as **100 mg/day**. A caffeine challenge that relieves symptoms within 30–60 min confirms it. Code **F15.93**.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 787)
- **A.** Prolonged daily caffeine use.
- **B.** Abrupt cessation or reduction, followed **within 24 h** by **≥3** of:
  1. Headache
  2. Marked fatigue or drowsiness
  3. Dysphoric mood, depressed mood or irritability
  4. Difficulty concentrating
  5. Flu-like symptoms (nausea, vomiting, or muscle pain or stiffness)
- **C.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **D.** Not associated with the physiological effects of another medical condition (e.g., **migraine, viral illness**), and not better explained by another mental disorder **including intoxication with or withdrawal from another substance**.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 788)
- People often **don't recognize hidden sources** (OTC analgesics, cold remedies, weight-loss aids, chocolate, mate), so they don't connect symptoms to caffeine.
- **Headache:** diffuse, comes on gradually, throbbing, can be severe, worse with movement. Other symptoms can occur without headache.
- Caffeine is the **world's most widely used behaviorally active drug**. Because it is built into rituals (coffee break, tea time), people may be unaware of physical dependence.
- **Triggers:** fasting before medical procedures, missed doses due to routine changes (**travel, weekends**).
- Probability and severity **rise with usual daily dose**, with large variability between and within people. It can occur after stopping **~100 mg/day**.

## Associated features (p. 788)
- Impaired behavioral and cognitive performance (sustained attention).
- **Increased total sleep time, sleep efficiency and slow-wave sleep.**
- EEG: **increased theta, decreased beta-2** power.
- Decreased motivation to work and decreased sociability. **Increased analgesic use.**

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **>85%** of US adults and children use caffeine regularly. The adult average is **~280 mg/day**.
- Headache occurs in about **50%** of caffeine abstinence cases.
- Among people trying to quit permanently, **>70%** had ≥1 symptom (**47% headache**), and **24%** had headache plus ≥1 other symptom plus impairment. Among people abstaining ≥24 h without trying to quit, **11%** had headache plus another symptom plus impairment.
- Prevention: use **daily or only infrequently** (≤2 consecutive days). **Taper gradually** over days to weeks.
- **Course:** onset **12–24 h** after the last dose. **Peak at 1–2 days.** Lasts **2–9 days**. **Headache can last up to 21 days.** Re-dosing relieves symptoms in **30–60 min**. Much less than the usual dose prevents withdrawal (e.g., **25 mg** for someone usually taking 300 mg).
- Consumption rises with age. Withdrawal is documented in youth but its risk factors are poorly known. Energy drinks may increase risk.
- *Temperamental:* heavy caffeine use in people with **eating disorders**, alcohol and other SUDs, **smokers**, and **incarcerated** people → higher withdrawal risk.
- *Environmental:* **unavailability**, e.g., medical procedures, pregnancy, hospitalization, religious observance, wartime, travel, research participation.
- *Genetic:* raises vulnerability. No specific genes identified.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (pp. 789–790)
- Habitual users who **fast for religious reasons** are at increased risk.
- Metabolism is slower with **oral contraceptives**, in the **luteal phase**, and in the **2nd and 3rd trimesters** of pregnancy. Slower clearance may **diminish withdrawal** but lengthen adverse effects. Doses **<300 mg/day** are unlikely to harm pregnancy outcomes.

## Functional consequences (p. 790)
- Impairment rates **10–55% (median 13%)**, and up to **73%** in people with other problematic caffeine use.
- Examples: unable to work, exercise or care for children; staying in bed all day; missing religious services; ending a vacation early; cancelling social plans. Headaches are described as "the worst ever." Cognitive and motor decrements.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 790)
- **Medical conditions and medication side effects:** migraine and other headache disorders, viral illness, sinus conditions, tension, other drug withdrawal (**amphetamines, cocaine**), medication side effects. Decide on the **pattern and amount consumed**, the **interval from abstinence to onset**, and the clinical features. A **caffeine challenge with remission** can confirm.
- **Caffeine-induced sleep disorder (insomnia type, onset during withdrawal):** sleep symptoms are **in excess** of usual withdrawal, predominate, and warrant attention.

## Comorbidity (p. 790)
- Associated with MDD, GAD, panic disorder, ASPD, moderate to severe AUD, and cannabis and cocaine use.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** usual daily intake (mg, all sources) and duration.
- **B:** **time of last caffeine** vs **time of symptom onset** (≤24 h). List ≥3 symptoms, e.g., "throbbing bifrontal headache worse on bending" (B1); "sleeping 11 h, drowsy" (B2); "irritable with family" (B3); "can't focus on reading" (B4); "nausea, muscle aches" (B5).
- **Context:** NPO before surgery, hospitalization, travel, religious fast, quit attempt.
- **Response to re-dosing** (relief in 30–60 min) supports the diagnosis.
- **Rule-outs:** migraine history, fever or viral exposure, sinus symptoms, other stimulant cessation, depressive episode (duration >2 weeks, symptoms not tied to the caffeine timeline).
- **Impairment:** missed work or childcare, bed-bound.
- Code **F15.93**.
