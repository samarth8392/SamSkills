# Inhalant Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Unlike inhalant use disorder, intoxication can result from unintentional exposure. A negative standard drug screen does not exclude it because routine panels typically miss volatile hydrocarbons. (pp. 829–831)
## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Recent brief high-dose volatile-hydrocarbon exposure, intentional **or accidental**. **B:** Problematic behavioral/psychological change during/soon after exposure. **C:** ≥2: dizziness; nystagmus; incoordination; slurred speech; unsteady gait; lethargy; reduced reflexes; slowed psychomotor activity; tremor; generalized weakness; blurred/double vision; stupor/coma; euphoria. **D:** No better medical, psychiatric or other-intoxicant explanation. (pp. 829–831)

## Specifiers & severity
No separate symptom-count severity scale is specified for this acute syndrome. (pp. 829–831)

## Coding
Source-edition codes (pp. 829–831):

| Comorbid same-class use disorder | Code |
|---|---|
| Mild | F18.120 |
| Moderate/severe | F18.220 |
| None | F18.920 |

Use the combined code rather than a separate stand-alone use-disorder code; name the specific substance if known.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Intoxication usually resolves within minutes to hours after exposure ends, but toxicity can persist. Containers, product odor, rags/bags, nasal/oral rash and collateral reports can support attribution. Name the particular substance if known. (pp. 830–831)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this section; inhalant use-disorder associations are discussed on p. 828.

## Functional consequences
Anoxia, arrhythmia/sudden death, organ injury, impaired judgment and accidents can occur. Intoxication duration does not set the duration of injury. (pp. 830–831)

## Differential diagnosis
Alcohol/other depressants may look similar; toxicology can detect those while missing inhalants. Assess toxic/metabolic, traumatic, neoplastic, infectious and other neurological causes. A predominant independently significant induced psychiatric syndrome differs from ordinary intoxication. (p. 831)

## Comorbidity
See inhalant use disorder for common substance, psychiatric and medical comorbidities. (p. 831)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record product, exposure circumstances (including accidental), timing and behavioral change.
- Document ≥2 signs, objective observations and collateral; specify limitations of testing.
- Separate medical injury from the intoxication syndrome and assess intentional repeated misuse independently.
