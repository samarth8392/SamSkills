# Other Hallucinogen Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Altered perception during clear wakefulness plus behavioral disturbance and physical signs is the defining pattern. Persistent sober perceptual recurrences instead suggest hallucinogen persisting perception disorder. (pp. 818–820)
## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Recent non-PCP hallucinogen use. **B:** Problematic behavioral/psychological change during/soon after use. **C:** Perceptual alteration while awake and alert, such as intensified sensations, derealization, hallucinations, illusions or cross-sensory experiences. **D:** ≥2: dilated pupils; tachycardia; sweating; palpitations; blurred vision; tremor; incoordination. **E:** No better medical, psychiatric or other-intoxicant explanation. (pp. 818–820)

## Specifiers & severity
No separate symptom-count severity scale is specified for this acute syndrome. (pp. 818–820)

## Coding
Source-edition codes (pp. 818–820):

| Comorbid same-class use disorder | Code |
|---|---|

Use the combined code rather than a separate stand-alone use-disorder code; name the specific substance if known.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Duration depends on the agent: minutes for some, many hours or longer for LSD/MDMA. Specify the actual hallucinogen and investigate adulterants or co-use. General hallucinogen information is in [other hallucinogen use disorder](other-hallucinogen-use-disorder.md). (pp. 818–819)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Intoxication prevalence is incompletely known; the source gives use rates as approximations rather than confirmed syndrome rates. (p. 819)

## Suicide risk
The relationship is uncertain: acute intoxication can produce suicidal thoughts/behavior, but large adjusted surveys have mixed/null or inverse associations. Observational inverse findings do not establish protection. (p. 819)

## Functional consequences
Perceptual/judgment changes can lead to crashes, falls, fighting or other injury. Hyperthermia, arrhythmia, seizures and organ toxicity can occur, particularly with MDMA or mixed exposure. (p. 819)

## Differential diagnosis
Distinguish stimulants, anticholinergics, inhalants, PCP, alcohol/sedative withdrawal, schizophrenia/mood illness and metabolic/neurological disease. Persistent symptoms after the exposure period suggest HPPD; predominant clinically important induced psychiatric symptoms warrant the relevant induced diagnosis. (pp. 819–820)

## Comorbidity
Other hallucinogen use disorder and associated substance/psychiatric disorders commonly coexist. (p. 820)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record agent/time/route, change in behavior, perceptual symptoms during wakefulness and ≥2 physical signs.
- Assess attention, insight, medical causes, other drugs and symptoms while sober.
- Document impairment/hazards and independent determination of use-disorder status.
