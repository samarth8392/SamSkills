# Opioid Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Recent opioid use plus problematic behavioural change, **miosis** (or mydriasis from anoxia in severe overdose) and ≥1 of drowsiness/coma, slurred speech, or attention/memory impairment. Naloxone response supports the diagnosis, but **non-response does not exclude it**: co-ingested benzodiazepines/alcohol or high-potency opioids (fentanyl) can blunt reversal.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recent opioid use.
- **B.** Clinically significant problematic behavioural/psychological change during or shortly after use (e.g., initial euphoria then apathy, dysphoria, psychomotor agitation or retardation, impaired judgment).
- **C.** **Pupillary constriction** (or dilation from anoxia in severe overdose) **plus ≥1**:
  1. Drowsiness or coma
  2. Slurred speech
  3. Impaired attention or memory
- **D.** Not due to another medical condition or better explained by another mental disorder, including intoxication with another substance.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With perceptual disturbances:** rare; hallucinations with *intact reality testing*, or auditory/visual/tactile illusions, **without delirium**.

## Coding
| Comorbid OUD | Without perceptual disturbances | With perceptual disturbances |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | F11.120 | F11.122 |
| Moderate/severe | F11.220 | F11.222 |
| None | F11.920 | F11.922 |

- One combined code covers both the intoxication and the OUD. Don't also code F11.10/F11.20.
- Record the specific substance (e.g., "F11.220 fentanyl intoxication").

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Drowsiness = being **"on the nod"**; can progress to coma.
- Inattention to the environment, even ignoring potentially harmful events.
- Overdose trend: Rx-driven until 2009, heroin from 2010, fentanyl-dominated deaths since 2015.

## Associated features
- Decreased respiratory rate and BP, mild hypothermia.
- Duration varies with the opioid's pharmacokinetics.
- Overdose triad: unconsciousness, respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Occurs in opioid-naive, sporadic and dependent users. The intoxicating dose depends on tolerance.
- Users report the pleasurable quality diminishes with repeated use.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this section. See Opioid Use Disorder (overdose versus attempt).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Alcohol / sedative-hypnotic intoxication:** no miosis, no naloxone response. Mixed intoxication gives only partial naloxone reversal.
- **Naloxone non-response** may still be opioid: co-ingestion (benzodiazepine, alcohol) or high dose/high-potency opioid (fentanyl).
- **Opioid-induced mental disorders** (e.g., opioid-induced depressive disorder, with onset during intoxication): symptoms exceed usual intoxication, predominate, and warrant independent clinical attention.

## Comorbidity
See Opioid Use Disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Time and substance of last use (route, amount, suspected fentanyl contamination); collateral; UDS including fentanyl assay.
- Criterion C signs: pupil size, level of consciousness (alert / drowsy / nodding / unresponsive), speech (slurred), attention and recall on MSE.
- Vitals: RR, SpO2, BP, temperature; naloxone given? Dose and response (full/partial/none).
- Note co-ingestants (benzodiazepines, alcohol) that explain partial reversal.
- Perceptual disturbances: specify reality testing intact and no delirium (orientation, fluctuation), or code delirium instead.
- State whether OUD is present and its severity to select the 4th character.
- Safety: overdose risk, intent screen (accidental versus deliberate), naloxone kit provided.
