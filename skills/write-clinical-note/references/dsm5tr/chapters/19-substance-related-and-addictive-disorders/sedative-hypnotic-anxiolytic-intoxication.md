# Sedative, Hypnotic, or Anxiolytic Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Looks like alcohol intoxication (disinhibition, ataxia, slurred speech, nystagmus) **without alcohol on the breath**, often with prominent **anterograde amnesia ("blackouts")**. Confirm recent ingestion by self-report, informant or toxicology. Unlike opioid and stimulant intoxication, **no "with perceptual disturbances" specifier** and only three codes.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recent use of a sedative, hypnotic or anxiolytic.
- **B.** Clinically significant maladaptive behavioural/psychological change during or shortly after use (e.g., inappropriate sexual or aggressive behaviour, mood lability, impaired judgment).
- **C.** **≥1** during or shortly after use:
  1. Slurred speech
  2. Incoordination
  3. Unsteady gait
  4. Nystagmus
  5. Impaired cognition (attention, memory)
  6. Stupor or coma
- **D.** Not due to another medical condition or better explained by another mental disorder, including intoxication with another substance.

## Coding
| Code | Comorbid SHA use disorder |
|---|---|
| F13.120 | Mild |
| F13.220 | Moderate/severe |
| F13.920 | None |

Record the specific agent (e.g., "F13.920 alprazolam intoxication"). For development/course, risk, culture, markers, functional consequences and comorbidity, the text refers to the use disorder.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Incoordination can be enough to impair driving and usual activities → falls, MVAs.
- **Memory impairment is prominent**: anterograde amnesia like alcoholic blackouts, which distresses the individual.
- Occurs with own prescription, borrowed medication, or deliberate intoxication.
- Co-use with other substances makes attribution hard.

## Associated features
- Taking more than prescribed, using multiple medications, or **mixing with alcohol**, which markedly increases effects.

## Prevalence
- Unknown. Likely similar to nonmedical use. 2018 US nonmedical tranquilizer/sedative use: **2.4%** of age ≥12, **4.9%** of ages 18–25.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Alcohol use disorder:** the presentations can be identical; need evidence of recent sedative ingestion (self-report, informant, tox). Multiple intoxication diagnoses are possible.
- **Alcohol intoxication:** smell of alcohol on the breath; otherwise similar.
- **SHA-induced mental disorders:** symptoms (e.g., anxiety) exceed usual intoxication, predominate and warrant attention.
- **NCDs / TBI / delirium from other causes:** sedatives intoxicate at low doses here. Diagnose by the predominant syndrome; an added intoxication diagnosis can still be appropriate even at a low dose.
- **Opioid intoxication** (from the opioid section): opioids give miosis and a naloxone response, which sedatives don't.

## Comorbidity
See the use disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Agent, amount, timing, source (prescribed/borrowed/illicit); co-ingestants (alcohol, opioids). Breath alcohol and tox screen results.
- Criterion C signs observed: dysarthria, ataxic gait, nystagmus, attention/recall deficits, level of consciousness.
- Behavioural change: disinhibited sexual or aggressive acts, lability, poor judgment, with concrete examples.
- Amnestic gaps reported by patient or collateral.
- Vitals (RR, BP) and airway risk, especially with opioid co-use.
- Older or cognitively impaired patients: baseline cognition and whether the dose was low.
- State use-disorder status for the code.
