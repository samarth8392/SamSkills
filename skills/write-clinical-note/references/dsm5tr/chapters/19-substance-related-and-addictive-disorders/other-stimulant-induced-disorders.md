# Stimulant-Induced Mental Disorders

> **Spark note:** Pointer entry. Stimulants (amphetamine-type, cocaine, other) can induce **psychotic, bipolar, depressive, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, sleep and sexual** disorders, **mild NCD**, and **delirium** (intoxication, or taken as prescribed). Diagnose them instead of intoxication/withdrawal only when symptoms warrant independent attention.

## Where the criteria live (p. 876)
| Induced disorder | Chapter |
|---|---|
| Stimulant-induced psychotic disorder | Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced bipolar and related disorder | Bipolar and Related Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced depressive disorder | Depressive Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced anxiety disorder | Anxiety Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced obsessive-compulsive disorder | Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced sleep disorder | Sleep-Wake Disorders |
| Stimulant-induced sexual dysfunction | Sexual Dysfunctions |
| Stimulant-induced **mild** neurocognitive disorder | Neurocognitive Disorders |
| Stimulant intoxication delirium; delirium from stimulants taken as prescribed | Neurocognitive Disorders |

Note there is **no stimulant withdrawal delirium** and **no major NCD** listed.

## Coding
- 4th character carries use-disorder status in the target chapter's code: F15.14/F15.24 (amphetamine-type or other) and F14.14/F14.24 (cocaine) for the induced depressive disorder example (pp. 863–864). F15 instructions also apply to other/unspecified stimulants.

## Pearls
- General pattern (p. 763): stimulants → **psychotic and anxiety** disorders during intoxication; **major depressive episodes** during withdrawal.
- Stimulant psychosis: delusions and hallucinations resembling schizophrenia. Tactile hallucinations and paranoid ideation are common, and insight that they are drug effects is often retained during plain intoxication.
- Stimulant intoxication versus induced disorder: the induced disorder's symptoms exceed usual intoxication, predominate, and meet full criteria for that disorder (p. 874).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Timeline of psychiatric symptoms against binges, crashes and abstinence; family/personal history of psychosis or mood disorder.
- Insight into hallucinations; orientation (to exclude delirium).
- Substance type for F14 versus F15; onset specifier; use-disorder severity.
- Re-evaluate after ~1 month abstinent; persistence suggests an independent disorder.
