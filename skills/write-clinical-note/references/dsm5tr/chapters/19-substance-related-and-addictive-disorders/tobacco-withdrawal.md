# Tobacco Withdrawal

> **Spark note:** Withdrawal is a clinically consequential syndrome after cutting down daily tobacco, not simply craving. Craving is associated but is not one of the seven counted withdrawal symptoms. (pp. 882–884)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Daily tobacco use for ≥several weeks. **B:** Abrupt stopping/reduction followed within 24 hours by ≥4: irritability/frustration/anger; anxiety; concentration difficulty; increased appetite; restlessness; depressed mood; insomnia. **C:** Significant distress/impairment. **D:** No better medical, psychiatric or other substance explanation. (pp. 882–883)

## Specifiers & severity
No formal severity or perceptual specifier is provided. Symptoms typically begin within a day, peak around days 2–3 and last 2–3 weeks; persistence beyond a month is unusual. (p. 883)

## Coding
F17.203 incorporates moderate/severe tobacco use disorder; the source states withdrawal only occurs with that use-disorder level. Do not add a separate stand-alone tobacco use-disorder code. (p. 883)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Craving, anhedonia, reduced positive affect, increased impulsivity and poor vigilance can accompany the syndrome. Lower heart rate and weight gain may follow cessation. Tobacco smoke induces metabolism of some psychiatric medicines; stopping smoking can raise medication levels through reduced exposure to smoke compounds, rather than removal of nicotine alone. (p. 883)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
About half of daily smokers quitting ≥2 days report ≥4 symptoms, which alone does not establish the distress/exclusion criteria. Comorbid psychiatric disorders can increase severity. Biomarkers reflect exposure only weakly in relation to withdrawal. (pp. 883–884)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this withdrawal section.

## Functional consequences
Withdrawal interferes with sustained quitting and may disrupt function; symptoms can complicate smoke-free inpatient admissions. (p. 884)

## Differential diagnosis
Other withdrawals, caffeine intoxication, mood/anxiety/sleep illness and medication-induced akathisia overlap. Check whether simultaneous tobacco cessation or altered medication concentration explains a new inpatient symptom. Improvement after nicotine supports attribution but does not replace full criteria. (p. 884)

## Comorbidity
See tobacco use disorder for common psychiatric and medical conditions. (p. 884)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record baseline products/use, reduction time and ≥4 specific symptoms starting within 24 hours.
- Document impairment, cravings separately, other withdrawals and medication/smoking changes.
- Record course, symptom observations and evidence for the code's included use-disorder level.
