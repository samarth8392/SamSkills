# Stimulant Intoxication

> **Spark note:** Behavioural change plus **≥2 of 9** autonomic, neuromuscular or cardiac signs. Note that the criteria allow **opposite directions** (tachy- or bradycardia, high or low BP, agitation or retardation), because depressant-type effects emerge with chronic high-dose use. Prominent paranoia and auditory hallucinations must be separated from schizophrenia. Severe intoxication can kill (seizures, arrhythmia, hyperpyrexia).

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recent use of an amphetamine-type substance, cocaine or other stimulant.
- **B.** Clinically significant problematic behavioural/psychological change (e.g., euphoria or affective blunting; changed sociability; hypervigilance; interpersonal sensitivity; anxiety, tension, anger; stereotyped behaviours; impaired judgment) during or shortly after use.
- **C.** **≥2 of 9**, during or shortly after use:
  1. Tachycardia or bradycardia
  2. Pupillary dilation
  3. Elevated or lowered BP
  4. Perspiration or chills
  5. Nausea or vomiting
  6. Evidence of weight loss
  7. Psychomotor agitation or retardation
  8. Muscular weakness, respiratory depression, chest pain or cardiac arrhythmias
  9. Confusion, seizures, dyskinesias, dystonias or coma
- **D.** Not due to another medical condition or better explained by another mental disorder, including another substance's intoxication.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Specify the intoxicant**: amphetamine-type substance, cocaine or other stimulant.
- **With perceptual disturbances:** hallucinations with intact reality testing, or auditory/visual/tactile illusions, without delirium.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Starts with a "high": euphoria with vigour, gregariousness, hyperactivity, restlessness, hypervigilance, interpersonal sensitivity, talkativeness, anxiety, tension, alertness, grandiosity, stereotyped/repetitive behaviour, anger, impaired judgment.
- **Chronic intoxication** → affective blunting with fatigue or sadness, and social withdrawal.
- Auditory hallucinations and paranoid ideation may be prominent; distinguish them from independent psychosis.
- Acute or chronic intoxication often impairs social and occupational function.
- Severe intoxication → convulsions, arrhythmias, **hyperpyrexia, death**.
- Intoxication is **not** a use-disorder criterion (the use disorder needs 2 of 11).

## Associated features
- Effects depend on dose, individual, tolerance, absorption rate, chronicity and context.
- Stimulant effects (euphoria, raised pulse/BP, psychomotor activity) are most common. Depressant effects (sadness, bradycardia, low BP, slowing) are less common and generally only with chronic high-dose use.

## Prevalence
Unknown; use is the upper bound. 12-mo US use (age ≥12):
- **Cocaine 2.2%** (12–17: 0.5%; 18–25: 6.2%; ≥26: 1.7%); males 3% / females 1.4%; White 2.3%, Hispanic 2.2%, African American 1.7%, Asian American 1%.
- **Methamphetamine 0.6%** (12–17: 0.2%; 18–25: 1.1%; ≥26: 0.6%); males 0.8% / females 0.4%; White 0.7%, Hispanic 0.6%, African American 0.2%, Asian American 0.1%.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Stimulant-induced mental disorders** (e.g., anxiety disorder with onset during intoxication): symptoms exceed usual intoxication, predominate, and **meet full criteria** for the relevant disorder.
- **Independent disorders**: schizophrenia, bipolar and depressive disorders, GAD, panic disorder.
- **PCP / synthetic cathinones** (from the use-disorder section): distinguished only by urine/plasma metabolites.

## Comorbidity
See Stimulant Use Disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Substance (cocaine versus amphetamine-type versus other), route, last use; UDS (benzoylecgonine, amphetamines). Note that cathinones and PCP need separate testing.
- Record each Criterion C sign with vitals: HR, BP, temperature, pupils, diaphoresis, chest pain/ECG, seizures, dyskinesia/dystonia.
- Behavioural change with examples: hypervigilance, paranoia, aggression or threats, stereotypies (picking, repetitive tasks).
- Hallucinations: modality, insight ("knows it's the drug"), orientation. Choose the perceptual-disturbance code versus induced psychotic disorder versus delirium.
- Use-disorder severity for the 4th character.
- Safety: violence risk, hyperthermia, cardiac risk; medical referral criteria.
