# Unspecified Tobacco-Related Disorder

> **Spark note:** This is a clinically significant tobacco-related presentation that does not fully meet a specific diagnosis in the chapter. The label does not itself establish intoxication, withdrawal or a use disorder. (pp. 884–885)

## Criteria at a glance
Characteristic substance-related symptoms predominate, produce meaningful distress or impairment, and do not satisfy a more specific substance-related/addictive diagnosis. The source supplies no extra symptom count, duration threshold or named example presentations for this category. (pp. 884–885)

## Specifiers & severity
No category-specific severity, remission or onset specifiers are supplied here.

## Coding
F17.209, as listed in this edition. Record the clinical reason a specific presentation is not established; do not manufacture a same-class use disorder from this residual code alone. (pp. 884–885)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this residual section.

## Differential diagnosis
Reassess class-specific use disorder, intoxication or recognized withdrawal, induced psychiatric syndromes, primary psychiatric illness and medical causes. For an unknown agent, distinguish uncertainty about substance identity from failure to establish a particular syndrome. (pp. 756–765, 884–885)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record actual substance-related symptoms, exposure evidence and clinically meaningful functional impact.
- State which specific diagnoses were considered, unmet requirements or information gaps, and medical/psychiatric alternatives.
- Document known identity versus unknown agent and planned reassessment when more information becomes available.
