# Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders

> **Spark note:** Separate a problematic use pattern from acute intoxication, withdrawal, and a predominant induced psychiatric syndrome. The same person may have all of these, but source-edition combination codes incorporate the same-class use disorder rather than requiring duplicate stand-alone SUD codes. Gambling has its own behavioral criteria and coding. (pp. 754–765, 896–902)

## Framework and diagnostic distinctions
Ten classes are covered: alcohol, caffeine, cannabis, PCP/other hallucinogens, hydrocarbons/inhalants, opioids, sedatives/hypnotics/anxiolytics, stimulants, tobacco and other/unknown agents. Their shared reward-system effects do not imply identical syndromes. The chapter recognizes gambling disorder; it does not give established diagnoses for every proposed behavioral addiction. (pp. 754–756)

For the detailed general framework, introductory coding tables and early-class discussion, see [_overview-part1.md](_overview-part1.md), pp. 754–765. This file supplies the complete chapter map.

## Shared thresholds, specifiers and coding conventions
- SUD generally requires ≥2 criteria within 12 months with significant impairment/distress. Mild 2–3, moderate 4–5, severe ≥6. PCP, other hallucinogens and inhalants have 10 countable items because no withdrawal criterion is established. Caffeine has no established use-disorder diagnosis in this chapter. (pp. 756–759)
- Early SUD remission ≥3–<12 months; sustained ≥12 months; only craving may remain after prior full criteria. Document controlled access and, for opioids/tobacco when applicable, maintenance therapy. Expected tolerance/withdrawal during appropriately supervised treatment alone does not establish SUD. (pp. 757–759, 833–834, 878)
- Combination codes typically distinguish mild versus moderate/severe versus no same-class SUD. Name the actual agent, code each distinct class appropriately, and avoid a duplicate stand-alone same-class SUD code when the induced code incorporates it. Tobacco has special Z72.0/F17.2xx rules. Codes in these notes reproduce this source edition, not a current billing-code verification. (pp. 758–765, 878–883)
- Gambling requires ≥4 of 9 in 12 months; severity 4–5/6–7/8–9. Its remission interval permits no criteria, with no SUD-style craving exception. (pp. 896–897)

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
A positive test proves neither disorder nor current intoxication; a negative routine test does not exclude agents the test misses. Establish timing, route, dose, observed syndrome and impairment. Hydrocarbon exposure is inhalant-class; nitrous oxide/nitrites are other-class; synthetic cannabinoids are cannabis-class; cathinones are stimulant-class; MDMA is discussed with other hallucinogens. (pp. 760–761, 812, 825–826, 887–889)

Preserved reality testing and clear attention matter when perceptual symptoms occur. Delirium is more than hallucinations; it centers on attention/awareness and physiological causation. A predominant induced psychiatric syndrome warrants its full phenomenological criteria; symptoms predating use or persisting after acute recovery can support an independent illness. The roughly one-month heuristic has exceptions for persisting NCD and HPPD. (pp. 762–765, 820–822)

Unknown substance identity differs from an unspecified syndrome. F19 diagnoses cover a known unclassified or genuinely unidentified agent; residual unspecified-class diagnoses cover clinically significant symptoms without a fully established specific presentation. Neither is a substitute for investigating medical mimics. (pp. 761, 885–896)

## Notable content in this edition
The text discusses high-potency cannabis, synthetic cannabinoids, vaping/dabbing, cannabinoid hyperemesis, fentanyl-related harms, disparities in exposure/access and suicide-related evidence. Its observations, prevalence figures and regulatory descriptions retain the publication context. Without a separate edition comparison, these notes do not label every observation as newly introduced in TR. (pp. 769–774, 793–800, 835–839, 868–870)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
Record actual agent/product and route, amount/frequency, last exposure/reduction, temporal psychiatric/cognitive course, and dated item-level SUD evidence. State the symptom count and assessment window, functional consequences, medical/psychiatric differential, remission dates/access conditions and evidence source. Separate exposure, acute syndrome, repeated use pattern and persistent impairment; never invent examples or observed signs to support a diagnosis.
