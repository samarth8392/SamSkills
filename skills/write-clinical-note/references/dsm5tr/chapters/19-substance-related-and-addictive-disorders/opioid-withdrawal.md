# Opioid Withdrawal

> **Spark note:** ≥3 of 9 characteristic signs after stopping or cutting down heavy, prolonged (≥ several weeks) opioid use, **or after an antagonist** (precipitated). Withdrawal on its own is **not** OUD. It occurs in pain patients taking opioids as prescribed (code F11.93). Craving and drug-seeking are what suggest comorbid OUD.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Either:
  1. Cessation or reduction of heavy, prolonged opioid use (**several weeks or longer**), or
  2. Opioid **antagonist** given after a period of opioid use.
- **B.** **≥3 of 9**, emerging minutes to several days after A:
  1. Dysphoric mood
  2. Nausea or vomiting
  3. Muscle aches
  4. Lacrimation or rhinorrhea
  5. Pupillary dilation, piloerection, or sweating
  6. Diarrhea
  7. Yawning
  8. Fever
  9. Insomnia
- **C.** Clinically significant distress/impairment.
- **D.** Not due to another medical condition or better explained by another mental disorder, including intoxication/withdrawal from another substance.

## Coding
| Code | When |
|---|---|
| F11.13 | With comorbid mild OUD |
| F11.23 | With comorbid moderate/severe OUD |
| F11.93 | No OUD (e.g., taking opioids solely under appropriate medical supervision) |

Combined code; don't separately code OUD. Record the specific opioid (e.g., "F11.23 heroin withdrawal").

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Opioids may be illicit or licitly prescribed for pain.
- **Precipitated withdrawal**: by antagonists (naloxone, naltrexone, nalmefene), or by a **partial agonist (buprenorphine) given to someone currently on a full agonist**.
- Earliest symptoms are subjective: anxiety, restlessness, an "achy feeling" (back/legs), irritability, increased pain sensitivity.
- **Piloerection and fever = more severe withdrawal**. They are rarely seen in practice because people usually obtain opioids before reaching that stage.
- Concurrent craving and drug-seeking suggest comorbid OUD.

## Associated features
- Occurs after repeated use in pain management, during agonist therapy, in illicit use, or after self-treating mental symptoms.
- Distinct from "addiction"/OUD; does not require drug-seeking.
- Males: piloerection, sweating, **spontaneous ejaculations while awake**.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- 60% of people in US clinical settings who used heroin at least once in the past 12 months had withdrawal.
- Anyone using opioids regularly for a while (Rx or illicit) is at risk of physical dependence.
- **Timing by half-life:**
  - Short-acting (heroin): onset **6–12 h**, peak **1–3 days**, subsides over **5–7 days**.
  - Long-acting (methadone, buprenorphine): onset **2–4 days**.
  - Protracted symptoms (anxiety, dysphoria, anhedonia, craving, insomnia) for **weeks to months**.
- Severity also scales with duration of use. In long-term pain treatment, a slow taper minimises symptoms.
- In OUD, a cycle of using to relieve withdrawal leads to recurrent withdrawal later.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this section (see OUD: repeated withdrawal can produce severe transient depression).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Sedative-hypnotic withdrawal:** shared anxiety/restlessness, but no rhinorrhea, lacrimation or mydriasis.
- **Hallucinogen / stimulant intoxication:** mydriasis, but no nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, cramps, rhinorrhea or lacrimation.
- **Opioid-induced mental disorders** (e.g., depressive disorder with onset during withdrawal): symptoms exceed usual withdrawal, predominate and warrant independent attention.

## Comorbidity
See Opioid Use Disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Last-use time and agent (short- versus long-acting); whether antagonist or buprenorphine induction preceded symptoms (precipitated).
- Duration and heaviness of prior use (≥ several weeks).
- List each Criterion B sign observed or reported. Objective: pupil size, rhinorrhea/tearing, piloerection, sweating, yawning, temperature, vomiting/diarrhea. Subjective: dysphoria, myalgias, insomnia. A structured scale score (e.g., COWS) is useful as supporting data.
- Prescription status: if opioids are taken as prescribed with no other OUD criteria, use F11.93 and say so explicitly.
- Note craving and drug-seeking (supports OUD) separately from physiological signs.
- MSE: anxious/restless, irritable, dysphoric affect; psychomotor agitation.
- Rule out: sedative/alcohol withdrawal (seizure risk differs), stimulant intoxication, medical causes of GI illness or fever.
