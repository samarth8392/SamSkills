# Level of Personality Functioning Scale

> **Spark note:** LPFS severity describes overall self and relationship functioning, not symptom count or moral judgment. Level 2 is the alternative model's minimum dysfunction threshold; a score alone does not establish personality disorder.

## Four areas
- **Identity:** coherent sense of self and boundaries, stable realistic self-appraisal, and emotional range/regulation.
- **Self-direction:** coherent attainable goals, constructive internal standards, and productive self-reflection.
- **Empathy:** understanding others' experience, tolerating perspectives, and appreciating one's interpersonal effects.
- **Intimacy:** enduring closeness, mutuality, and cooperative relationships. (pp. 1147–1148)

## Rating anchors
Select the overall level best matching current personality functioning; this is not a mechanical average of four checklist scores. Use the full source table for a detailed rating. (pp. 1160, 1163–1165)

| Level | Self functioning | Interpersonal functioning |
|---|---|---|
| **0: little/no impairment** | Integrated identity, realistic esteem, regulated affect; workable goals/standards and reflection | Appreciates other viewpoints and own effects; lasting reciprocal, flexible relationships |
| **1: some impairment** | Identity mostly intact but stress affects boundaries/esteem; some goal conflict or rigid standards | Some difficulty considering others and own impact; relationships remain enduring with limits in depth or cooperation |
| **2: moderate impairment** | Excessive reliance on outside appraisal; fragile esteem and self-referential goals; limited reflection | Others understood mainly in relation to self; superficial or esteem-regulating intimacy; limited mutuality |
| **3: severe impairment** | Weak agency/identity, unstable or rigid boundaries and fragile esteem; incoherent standards/goals and poor reflection | Marked limits in alternative perspectives, frequent misattribution of motives; strong dependency/abandonment expectations and unstable connections |
| **4: extreme impairment** | Unique self/agency nearly absent or organized around persecution; confused boundaries and profoundly impaired goals/reflection | Profound inability to understand others; disconnected, disorganized, or negative relationships focused on need fulfillment or avoiding harm |

## Clinical use
Severity is associated with disability, treatment intensity needs, and prognosis. LPFS may be useful independently of assigning a personality diagnosis and may be repeated to characterize change. The chapter stresses that self and interpersonal representations affect alliance and treatment outcome. (pp. 1159–1163)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- State assessment date, overall rating, rationale, and evidence within all four areas.
- Distinguish current overall functioning from temporary decompensation, longstanding pattern, and strengths.
- Describe observable relationship patterns and the person's reported internal experience without assuming motives.
- If evidence supports only level 0 or 1, do not label alternative-model personality disorder merely because traits are elevated.
