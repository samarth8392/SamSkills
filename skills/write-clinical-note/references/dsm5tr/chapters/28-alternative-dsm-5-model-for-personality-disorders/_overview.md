# Alternative DSM-5 Model for Personality Disorders

The Section III alternative model combines personality-functioning severity with pathological trait style. Section II retains the official categorical system. Six alternative profiles and PD-TS reduce reliance on one categorical label while retaining explicit proposed thresholds.

## Chapter-level distinctions and pitfalls
- General criteria A–G apply to every alternative profile; trait elevation alone is not personality disorder.
- All six specific profiles require ≥moderate impairment and characteristic difficulty in ≥2 of four functioning areas. Each has a different facet-count rule; required facets cannot be replaced by extra optional ones.
- LPFS 0–4 grades overall functioning; the five domains/25 facets describe style. They answer different questions.
- Do not infer enduring traits from an isolated behavior or episode. Review developmental, cultural, substance/medical, and psychiatric explanations.
- PD-TS retains general disorder thresholds even when a named profile is subthreshold. Paranoid, schizoid, histrionic, and dependent Section II presentations are represented descriptively through PD-TS here.

## Shared specifiers and coding
Record LPFS severity and additional traits. Antisocial profile adds a psychopathic-features specifier. No separate ICD coding system is supplied for these proposed profiles; official diagnostic coding requires the Section II framework. The chapter offers no explicit TR-specific change log.
