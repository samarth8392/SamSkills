# Alternative Model: General Criteria and Assessment

> **Spark note:** The Section III alternative model combines severity of personality dysfunction (A) with a trait profile (B). The Section II personality disorder system remains the official diagnostic framework; criteria and thresholds from the two systems must not be mixed.

## General criteria A–G
- **A:** at least moderate impairment in self/interpersonal personality functioning.
- **B:** at least one pathological personality trait.
- **C:** dysfunction and traits are relatively inflexible and evident across a broad range of personal/social contexts.
- **D:** relatively enduring over time, with onset traceable to adolescence or early adulthood.
- **E:** not explained better by another mental disorder.
- **F:** not solely due to substances or another medical condition, such as major head injury.
- **G:** not normative for developmental stage or sociocultural circumstances. (pp. 1146–1149)

## How the model works
Assess identity, self-direction, empathy, and intimacy; use the LPFS 0–4 scale and then review all five trait domains. Each of six named personality profiles requires characteristic dysfunction in at least two of the four areas plus its own trait algorithm. General C–G apply to every profile and PD-TS. General A alone does not mean that every personality area must be moderately impaired. (pp. 1147–1149)

Traits are tendencies across situations and time, not single acts. Traits may change, and symptoms fluctuate more readily. A personality assessment can be informative even below the disorder threshold or in people with another psychiatric disorder. Comorbidity is possible if dysfunction is not simply an expression of that disorder. (pp. 1148–1149, 1159–1163)

## Coding and specifiers
This chapter presents an alternative model and proposed criteria, without a separate set of ICD-10-CM codes. Document the model explicitly. Use Section II criteria/coding for official diagnoses rather than assigning an alternative profile solely from this chapter. Specify LPFS severity and additional clinically relevant traits when using the model descriptively.

## Differential logic
- Compare functioning before, during, and after mood/psychotic/substance episodes; episodic symptoms alone do not establish pervasive personality pathology.
- Exclude acquired personality changes from medical illness or substances.
- Developmental change and sociocultural norms can resemble pathological traits. Culture-incongruent behavior requires contextual assessment, not clinician unfamiliarity.
- Enduring impairments can coexist with depression or other disorders; comorbidity does not automatically invalidate a personality formulation.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Label “Section III alternative-model formulation” and distinguish it from any established Section II diagnosis.
- Give concrete, longitudinal examples from multiple settings for each impaired functioning area and elevated trait.
- Record collateral sources, baseline, developmental onset, persistence, flexibility, impairment, and strengths.
- Document exclusions E–G, relevant episodes, medical/substance timelines, and cultural/developmental context. Do not infer a personality pattern from one difficult encounter.
