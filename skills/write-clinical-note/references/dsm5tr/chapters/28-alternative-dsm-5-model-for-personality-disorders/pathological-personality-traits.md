# Pathological Personality Trait Domains and Facets

> **Spark note:** Review all five domains, then relevant facets; a single behavior does not establish a trait. The 25 facets overlap across domains, so cross-listing does not create additional distinct traits.

## Assessment and interpretation
Traits are dimensional dispositions in thinking, feeling, perception, and behavior; they are relatively enduring but changeable. High trait anxiousness does not mean constant anxiety. Traits have opposing poles, and an apparently adaptive opposite can be problematic at an extreme. Interview, clinical judgment, normative comparison, and PID-5 self/informant reports may support elevation; this chapter supplies no universal numeric cutoff. Domain-only review can provide a broad portrait, but facet assessment matters when personality is a treatment focus. (pp. 1160–1163)

## Domains and all 25 distinct facets
| Domain / opposite pole | Facets and concise meanings |
|---|---|
| **Negative Affectivity / Emotional Stability** | **Emotional lability:** easily activated, intense/shifting affect. **Anxiousness:** ready fear, worry, expectation of threat. **Separation insecurity:** fear of rejection/being alone with low confidence in self-care. **Submissiveness:** yielding even against one's needs. **Hostility:** recurrent anger, irritability, vindictiveness. **Perseveration:** persisting after a strategy fails or loses purpose. |
| **Detachment / Extraversion** | **Withdrawal:** reduced approach to social contact. **Intimacy avoidance:** avoiding attachments and close/sexual relationships. **Anhedonia:** diminished engagement or pleasure. **Depressivity:** persistent miserable/hopeless affect, shame/guilt, low worth, potentially suicidal thinking/behavior. **Restricted affectivity:** limited emotional experience/expression or engagement. **Suspiciousness:** expectation of interpersonal harm, disloyalty, or exploitation. |
| **Antagonism / Agreeableness** | **Manipulativeness:** indirect/covert control for one's ends. **Deceitfulness:** dishonest or fabricated self/event presentation. **Grandiosity:** superiority and entitlement. **Attention seeking:** striving for notice/admiration. **Callousness:** limited concern or remorse for harm to others. |
| **Disinhibition / Conscientiousness** | **Irresponsibility:** failing obligations, commitments, or care for property. **Impulsivity:** action without planning or considering effects, often urgent under distress. **Distractibility:** easily diverted attention and impaired goal focus. **Risk taking:** unnecessary dangerous pursuit despite consequences. **Rigid perfectionism:** inflexible correctness/detail demands at the opposing pole; its absence characterizes low conscientious control. |
| **Psychoticism / Lucidity** | **Unusual beliefs and experiences:** atypical abilities/reality experiences. **Eccentricity:** conspicuously unusual behavior, appearance, or speech. **Cognitive and perceptual dysregulation:** unusual thought/perception, dissociation, sleep–wake or thought-control experiences. |

Source taxonomy: pp. 1165–1166. The table also cross-lists depressivity/suspiciousness under Negative Affectivity and hostility under Antagonism; lack of restricted affectivity relates to low Negative Affectivity. Rigid perfectionism is included as a clinically relevant opposing-pole facet, not a sign of high disinhibition.

## Differential and clinical utility
Trait depressivity is not equivalent to a major depressive episode; momentary impulsivity is not necessarily trait impulsivity; Psychoticism domain elevation is not automatically a psychotic disorder. Evaluate baseline, context, and syndrome-level explanations. Functioning severity and trait style contribute distinct information about disability, self-harm/violence concerns, treatment needs, and prognosis; no profile deterministically predicts an individual's behavior. (pp. 1161–1163)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- List domains reviewed, facets elevated, assessment methods, sources, and supporting examples across time/settings.
- Keep observed acts, inferred disposition, and measured score distinct. Include uncertainty and discrepant informants.
- Record relevant adaptive traits, culture, developmental context, and episode/substance effects.
- Directly assess safety when suicidal thoughts, self-injury, aggression, or dangerous acts are reported; a trait label cannot replace a current assessment.
