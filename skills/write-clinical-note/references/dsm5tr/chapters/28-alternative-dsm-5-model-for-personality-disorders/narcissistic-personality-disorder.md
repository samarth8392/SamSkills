# Narcissistic Personality Disorder — Alternative Model

> **Spark note:** Vulnerable or fluctuating self-esteem regulated by admiration, attention, and overt/covert grandiosity. This is the Section III alternative-model profile, not the Section II criteria set.

## Proposed criteria at a glance
**A:** ≥moderate personality-functioning impairment, with characteristic difficulty in ≥2 of these four areas:

- Identity: others' responses organize self-definition and unstable inflated/deflated self-appraisal.
- Self-direction: approval/exceptionality/entitlement organize goals and standards; motivation awareness is limited.
- Empathy: others' reactions matter chiefly for self-relevance; own impact is misjudged.
- Intimacy: superficial, self-esteem-serving relationships with limited mutuality.

**B:** Both grandiosity and attention seeking are required.

**C–G:** All [general alternative-model criteria](general-model-and-assessment.md) also apply: pervasiveness/inflexibility, relative stability with adolescent/early-adult onset, and exclusion of other mental disorders, substance/medical causes, and normative developmental/sociocultural explanations.

## Specifiers & severity
Additional antagonistic facets (manipulativeness, deceitfulness, callousness) may characterize more broadly antagonistic presentations; anxiousness/depressivity may describe vulnerability. Specify LPFS severity.

## Coding and model boundaries
No distinct ICD code is supplied for this proposed alternative profile. Establish and code an official Section II diagnosis using its own criteria; do not combine trait thresholds here with Section II counts.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Document esteem regulation, relationship reciprocity, and concrete impact rather than equating confidence or ambition with pathology. Separate episodic grandiosity during mania from enduring patterns.
- Record specific examples for ≥2 functioning areas and each counted trait; identify duration, settings, baseline, impact, sources, and contrary evidence.
- Document general exclusions and the model used. Additional traits are descriptive specifiers and cannot substitute for a required facet.
- This chapter does not provide separate profile-specific epidemiology, course, or comprehensive comorbidity sections.
