# Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder

> **Spark note:** Persistent or recurrent feelings of being detached from or unreal about oneself (DP) and/or one's surroundings (DR), with **reality testing intact**: the person knows it's "as if," not real. Transient DP/DR is very common (~half of adults have had an episode), so the disorder needs persistent **or recurrent** episodes, distress/impairment and exclusion of panic-only, psychotic, depressive, trauma, substance and medical causes. People are often misread as blunted or psychotic; the robotic, flat demeanor hides marked suffering.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Persistent or recurrent DP, DR or both (p. 508):
  1. **Depersonalization**: unreality, detachment or being an outside observer of one's thoughts, feelings, sensations, body or actions (e.g., perceptual changes, distorted time sense, unreal/absent self, emotional and/or physical numbing).
  2. **Derealization**: unreality or detachment from surroundings (people/objects seem unreal, dreamlike, foggy, lifeless or visually distorted).
- **B.** Reality testing stays intact during the experiences.
- **C.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **D.** Not attributable to a substance (drug, medication) or another medical condition (e.g., seizures).
- **E.** Not better explained by another mental disorder — e.g., **schizophrenia, panic disorder, MDD, acute stress disorder, PTSD, or another dissociative disorder**.

## Specifiers & severity
- None. DP-predominant vs DR-predominant has no evidence of being distinct (p. 491).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Depersonalization** (pp. 508–509): detached from whole self ("I am no one") or parts — feelings (hypoemotionality: "I know I have feelings but can't feel them"), thoughts (not my own; "head full of cotton"), body/body parts, sensations (touch, proprioception, hunger, thirst, libido). Reduced agency (robotic, automaton, not controlling speech/movement). Split self (observer vs participant); extreme = out-of-body experience.
  - Factor structure: anomalous body experience (self-unreality, perceptual alterations); emotional/physical numbing; temporal distortion with anomalous subjective recall (pp. 508–509).
- **Derealization** (p. 509): fog, dream, bubble, veil or glass wall between self and world; surroundings artificial, colorless, lifeless; visual distortions (blurriness, heightened acuity, widened/narrowed field, 2-D flatness, exaggerated 3-D, macropsia/micropsia); auditory muting/heightening.

## Associated features
- Difficulty describing symptoms; fear of "going crazy"; fear of irreversible brain damage (p. 509).
- Altered time sense (too fast/slow); difficulty vividly recalling and "owning" past memories as personal/emotional (p. 509).
- Vague somatic sx: head fullness, tingling, lightheadedness (p. 509).
- Rumination/obsessional preoccupation (Do I exist? checking whether perceptions look real) (p. 509).
- Anxiety and depression common; physiological hyporeactivity to emotional stimuli; HPA axis, inferior parietal lobule, prefrontal-limbic circuits implicated (p. 509).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Transient DP/DR (hours–days) common; ~**50% of adults** report ≥1 lifetime episode; full disorder much rarer; **1-month prevalence ~1–2% (UK)** (p. 509).
- **Mean onset age 16**; can start in childhood; some can't remember being without it; <20% onset after 20, 5% after 25; onset ≥40 → look hard for medical causes (brain lesions, seizures, sleep apnea) (pp. 509–510).
- Onset sudden to gradual; episodes last hours to years (p. 510).
- **Course often persistent**: ~⅓ discrete episodes, ~⅓ continuous from the start, ~⅓ episodic → continuous (p. 510).
- Intensity may wax/wane or be constant for years/decades. Exacerbants: stress, worse mood/anxiety, novel/overstimulating settings, lighting, sleep loss (p. 510).
- **Temperamental**: harm avoidance, immature defenses (idealization/devaluation, projection, acting out), disconnection schemas (defectiveness, emotional inhibition) and overconnection schemas (dependency, vulnerability, incompetence) (p. 510).
- **Environmental**: childhood interpersonal trauma in a substantial portion, but less prevalent/extreme than in other dissociative disorders; **emotional abuse and emotional neglect** most strongly linked; also physical abuse, witnessing DV, mentally ill parent, unexpected death/suicide of close person; sexual abuse less common (p. 510).
- **Proximal precipitants**: severe stress, depression, anxiety (esp. **panic attacks**), illicit drugs — THC, hallucinogens, ketamine, MDMA, salvia; marijuana can trigger panic and DP/DR together (p. 510).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Volitional DP/DR in meditative/spiritual practice is **not** a disorder; some who induce it lose control and become averse to the practice (p. 510).
- Cultural explanations (spiritual/supernatural) may lessen fear of "losing one's mind" (pp. 510–511).
- Sex/gender: not specifically addressed.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Highly distressing, major morbidity; flat, robotic demeanor seems incongruent with intense reported pain (p. 511).
- Interpersonal and occupational impairment from hypoemotionality, subjective trouble focusing/retaining information, general disconnection from life (p. 511).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Illness anxiety disorder**: DPDR may involve vague somatic complaints and fear of brain damage, but shows the typical DP/DR constellation without other illness-anxiety features (p. 511).
- **MDD**: numbness, deadness, dreaminess occur in MDE; diagnose DPDR if DP/DR **clearly precedes** the MDE or **clearly persists after** it resolves (p. 511).
- **OCD**: DPDR may involve obsessive preoccupation/checking about the symptoms, but no unrelated OCD symptoms (p. 511).
- **Other dissociative disorders**: don't diagnose DPDR when DP/DR occurs in the context of DID; amnesia and FND don't overlap symptomatically (p. 511).
- **Panic attacks**: not diagnosed if DP/DR occurs **only during panic attacks** (panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, specific phobia). Can diagnose when DP/DR began with panic if 1) DP/DR is prominent from the start and clearly exceeds panic attacks in duration/intensity, or 2) persists after panic disorder remits/is treated (pp. 511–512).
- **Psychotic disorders**: intact reality testing about DP/DR is the key; nihilistic delusions ("I am dead," "the world isn't real") — distinguish "as if" experience from conviction (p. 512).
- **Substance/medication-induced**: DP/DR only during intoxication/withdrawal is not DPDR; **~15% of DPDR cases are substance-precipitated** (marijuana, hallucinogens, ketamine, ecstasy, salvia); if symptoms persist without further use, DPDR applies — most become phobic of the drug and stop using (p. 512).
- **TBI**: DP/DR typical after TBI; distinguished by onset after injury and lack of the other DPDR symptoms (p. 512).
- **Dissociative sx due to another medical condition**: onset after 40 or atypical sx/course → thorough medical/neurological work-up (labs, viral titers, EEG, vestibular, visual, sleep studies, imaging); ambulatory EEG if seizure suspected — temporal lobe most common, also parietal/frontal epilepsy (p. 512).

## Comorbidity
- High lifetime unipolar depression and anxiety disorders, often both; **low PTSD comorbidity** (p. 512).
- Most common PDs: avoidant, borderline, obsessive-compulsive (p. 512).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **A1/A2 evidence**: record client's own descriptions paraphrased by clinician — e.g., feels like watching self from outside, body feels not their own, emotions muted though present, world looks flat/foggy/behind glass, time distortion. Note whether DP, DR or both.
- **B (reality testing)**: explicitly document the "as if" quality — "Client recognizes these experiences are subjective and the world is real; no delusional elaboration." This is the single most important line in the note.
- **Course**: onset age, episodic vs continuous, triggers (stress, sleep loss, lighting, overstimulation, drug use).
- **Rule-outs to document**: substance history and timing (esp. cannabis, hallucinogens, ketamine, MDMA, salvia) and whether sx persist during abstinence; panic attacks — are DP/DR confined to attacks?; MDE timing; psychosis screen; trauma/dissociative screen (amnesia, identity states); head injury; seizure/neurological history; medical work-up if onset ≥40 or atypical.
- **MSE language**: "Affect constricted/flat, incongruent with reported severe distress"; "Describes feeling robotic and detached; thought process linear; no hallucinations or delusions; insight intact regarding unreality experiences"; "Expresses fear of 'going crazy' or permanent brain damage."
- Note functional impact: trouble concentrating/retaining information at work/school, emotional disconnection in relationships.
