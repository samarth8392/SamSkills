# Dissociative Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Dissociation = a break in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, emotion, perception, body representation, motor control and behavior (p. 491). The chapter sits *next to* (not inside) trauma- and stressor-related disorders because most presentations follow psychological trauma; ASD and PTSD themselves contain dissociative symptoms (amnesia, flashbacks, numbing, DP/DR). The commonest error is missing these disorders: patients typically hide, minimize or are unaware of amnesia and identity shifts.

## What unites the chapter
- **Positive dissociative symptoms**: unbidden intrusions with loss of continuity in experience — division of identity, depersonalization, derealization (p. 491).
- **Negative dissociative symptoms**: inability to access information or control mental functions that are normally accessible — e.g., amnesia (p. 491).
- **"Traumatic experiences"** in this chapter = psychological sequelae, *not* physical impact causing TBI (p. 491).
- **Cross-cultural risk factors** (p. 491): earlier trauma onset; parental neglect and sexual/physical/emotional abuse; cumulative early adversity; repeated sustained trauma/torture in captivity (POWs, trafficking).
- Most people are initially **unaware of their amnesia** or minimize/rationalize it; awareness often comes when they find evidence of or are told about events they can't recall (pp. 491–492).

## How the disorders differ
| Disorder | Core feature | Amnesia? | Identity disruption? | Duration/timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dissociative identity disorder (F44.81) | ≥2 distinct personality states or possession experience + recurrent amnesia | Yes — recurrent, incl. everyday events | Yes (required) | No minimum; chronic, onset any age |
| Dissociative amnesia (F44.0 / F44.1 with fugue) | Inability to recall important autobiographical info, inconsistent with ordinary forgetting | Yes (the core) | No | No minimum; episodic or chronic |
| Depersonalization/derealization disorder (F48.1) | Persistent/recurrent DP and/or DR with intact reality testing | Typically no | No | Persistent or recurrent; mean onset 16 y |
| Other specified (F44.89) / unspecified (F44.9) | Dissociative sx predominate, full criteria not met | Variable | Variable | e.g., acute reactions <1 month |

- Text: no evidence for a meaningful distinction between predominantly-depersonalization vs predominantly-derealization presentations (p. 491).
- Amnesia types: **localized** (event/period), **selective** (some aspects of a period), **generalized** (identity + life history; rare), plus **systematized** (category of info) and **continuous** (anterograde; forgets each new event) (pp. 491, 502).

## TR changes / notable updates in the text
- **DID code is F44.81** (p. 492); **other specified dissociative disorder is F44.89** (p. 513); **DPDR remains F48.1** (p. 508).
- **Dissociative fugue** is not a separate disorder: it is a specifier of dissociative amnesia and carries its own code, **F44.1** (vs F44.0 without fugue) (p. 501).
- DID Criterion A explicitly allows **possession-form** presentations; Criterion D excludes culturally/religiously accepted practice; note on children's imaginary playmates (pp. 492–493).
- Dissociative amnesia Criterion C names **transient global amnesia** and TBI sequelae among medical exclusions; Criterion D lists DID, PTSD, ASD, somatic symptom disorder and major/mild NCD as "better explained by" exclusions (p. 501).
- Functional neurological symptom disorder (conversion disorder) is used throughout as the term for conversion phenomena.
- Expanded suicide sections: >70% of DID outpatients have attempted suicide; dissociation is an independent risk factor for multiple attempts (p. 497).

## Chapter-level pitfalls & differential logic
- **Marked identity disruption + recurrent amnesia suggests DID; autobiographical amnesia without it suggests dissociative amnesia; DP/DR with intact reality testing suggests DPDR. Each requires distress/impairment and its exclusions** (pp. 498, 505, 511).
- **PTSD vs dissociative disorder:** PTSD amnesia is limited to the trauma or parts of it; amnesia that extends beyond the immediate trauma period may warrant a comorbid dissociative amnesia diagnosis if its full criteria are met (p. 505). DP/DR in the dissociative subtype of PTSD is tied to trauma reminders; in DID it also occurs in daily life and interpersonal stress (p. 499).
- **Psychosis vs DID:** DID voices/passivity experiences are ego-alien and frightening without delusional explanation; multimodal hallucinations linked to flashbacks; DID = highest hypnotizability, schizophrenia = low (p. 499). For DPDR, intact reality testing is the discriminator; watch for nihilistic delusions (p. 512).
- **Bipolar vs DID:** DID state shifts last minutes–hours, tied to identity states; sleep problem is nightmares/nocturnal flashbacks, not reduced need for sleep. DID commonly misdiagnosed as bipolar II with mixed features (p. 498).
- **Panic vs DPDR:** don't diagnose DPDR if DP/DR occurs only within panic attacks (p. 511).
- **Substance blackouts vs amnesia:** longitudinal history showing amnesia only during intoxication argues for substance (p. 505).
- **Medical/neurological rule-outs:** seizures (serial/telemetric EEG), TBI (LOC, neuro signs, NCD pattern), ECT, catatonic stupor, neurocognitive disorders; DPDR with onset after age 40 or atypical course → medical work-up (pp. 506, 510, 512).
- **Feigning:** no test reliably separates dissociative from feigned amnesia; feigners overreport dramatic media-type symptoms, seem untroubled, and show symptoms only when observed (pp. 500, 507).
- **Culture:** possession states that are part of broadly accepted cultural or religious practice are not disorders; pathological possession is involuntary, distressing, uncontrollable, conflictual and violates cultural norms of time/place (pp. 494, 497). Volitional meditative DP/DR is not a disorder (p. 510).

## Shared specifiers & coding conventions
- Only formal specifier in the chapter: **with dissociative fugue** (dissociative amnesia) → changes code F44.0 → F44.1 (p. 501).
- No severity or course specifiers for DID or DPDR.
- Other specified: record "other specified dissociative disorder" + reason (e.g., "dissociative trance") — F44.89. Unspecified: F44.9, incl. insufficient information (e.g., ER) (pp. 513–514).
- Functional neurological symptom disorder and a dissociative disorder can both be diagnosed when both are present (cross-ref Ch. 12).
