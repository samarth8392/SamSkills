# Other Specified Dissociative Disorder & Unspecified Dissociative Disorder

> **Spark note:** Residual categories for clinically significant dissociative presentations that fall short of DID, dissociative amnesia or DPDR. Use **other specified (F44.89)** when you state the reason; use **unspecified (F44.9)** when you choose not to, or lack the information (e.g., in the ER). One source example is identity disturbance without dissociative amnesia.

## Criteria at a glance
- Both: symptoms characteristic of a dissociative disorder **predominate**, cause clinically significant distress/impairment, but don't meet full criteria for any disorder in the class (pp. 513–514).
- **Other specified**: clinician records the specific reason, e.g., "other specified dissociative disorder, dissociative trance" (p. 513).
- **Unspecified**: clinician chooses not to specify, including insufficient information (e.g., emergency settings) (p. 514).

## Example presentations (other specified) (pp. 513)
1. **Chronic and recurrent syndromes of mixed dissociative symptoms**: identity disturbance with *less-than-marked* discontinuities in self and agency; **or** identity alterations/possession episodes in someone reporting **no dissociative amnesia** (i.e., DID Criterion A-ish without B).
2. **Identity disturbance due to prolonged and intense coercive persuasion**: brainwashing, thought reform, indoctrination in captivity, torture, long-term political imprisonment, recruitment by sects/cults or terror organizations → prolonged identity changes or conscious questioning of identity.
3. **Acute dissociative reactions to stressful events**: acute, transient, typically **<1 month** (sometimes hours–days); constriction of consciousness, DP, DR, perceptual disturbance (time slowing, macropsia), **micro-amnesias**, transient stupor, sensory-motor changes (analgesia, paralysis).
4. **Dissociative trance**: acute narrowing or complete loss of awareness of immediate surroundings → profound unresponsiveness/insensitivity to stimuli; may include minor stereotyped behaviors (e.g., finger movements) the person is unaware of/can't control, transient paralysis or loss of consciousness; **not** part of a broadly accepted collective cultural or religious practice.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Example 1 is the home for possession-type or identity-alteration presentations that lack the recurrent amnesia required for DID (p. 513).
- Example 3 differs from dissociative amnesia: amnesic episodes are brief (hours/days), circumscribed micro-amnesias accompanied by other prominent dissociative symptoms (p. 507). Consider ASD if trauma-exposure and ASD symptom criteria are met.
- Example 4 requires that the trance is **not** culturally sanctioned; culturally normative trance/possession is not a disorder.

## Differential diagnosis
- **DID**: requires both marked identity disruption (A) and recurrent amnesia (B).
- **Dissociative amnesia**: autobiographical retrieval loss without the mixed acute picture; no duration cap.
- **DPDR**: persistent/recurrent DP/DR as the predominant picture.
- **ASD/PTSD**: assess whether the dissociative presentation is fully explained by the trauma disorder; trauma exposure alone does not settle the diagnosis.
- **Functional neurological symptom disorder**: if paralysis/unresponsiveness meets FND criteria (positive incompatibility findings), consider FND (both can be diagnosed).
- **Cultural/religious practice**: voluntary, context-appropriate, non-distressing trance/possession → no diagnosis.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- State **which full criteria are not met and why** (e.g., "identity alterations reported; denies amnesia; does not meet DID Criterion B → OSDD, chronic and recurrent mixed dissociative symptoms").
- For coercive persuasion: document the context (cult, captivity, indoctrination), duration, and nature of identity change/questioning.
- For acute reactions: document stressor, onset, duration (<1 month), specific dissociative features observed (unresponsiveness, micro-amnesias, perceptual changes, analgesia/paralysis).
- For trance: describe level of responsiveness, stereotyped movements, cultural context and why it falls outside accepted practice.
- Rule-outs: substances, seizures/medical causes, psychosis, ASD/PTSD.
- Use F44.9 in acute/ER notes when information is insufficient; plan to revise once assessment is complete.
