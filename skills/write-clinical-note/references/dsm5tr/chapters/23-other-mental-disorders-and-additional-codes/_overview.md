# Other Mental Disorders and Additional Codes — Overview

> **Spark note:** This is the last-resort chapter. It holds four residual categories for presentations that *are* mental disorders (clinically significant distress or impairment) but fit nowhere else in Section II, not even in the other specified/unspecified slots of a specific chapter, plus one "additional code" (Z03.89) to record that an evaluation found **no** disorder or condition. The common error is using F99 when a chapter-specific residual category (e.g., unspecified anxiety disorder, F41.9) would fit.

## What unites the chapter
- Every category except Z03.89 needs: (1) a real mental disorder (distress or impairment), (2) full criteria not met for any specific Section II disorder, (3) no fit with any *chapter-level* other specified/unspecified category, and (4) no other mental disorder dx that applies (p. 1065).
- Split along two axes:
  - **Etiology:** due to another medical condition (F06.8 / F09) vs. no medical etiology established (F99).
  - **Other specified vs. unspecified:** other specified = clinician *states the reason* criteria aren't met; unspecified = clinician chooses not to, or info is insufficient (e.g., ED).
- Z03.89 is not a disorder: evaluated, and nothing found.

## TR changes / notable points in the text
- "No Diagnosis or Condition" (Z03.89) is listed under a separate **Additional Codes** heading in this chapter (p. 1067).
- Other specified and unspecified mental disorder share the **same code, F99**; the difference lives only in the written diagnosis (whether a reason is recorded).
- Medical-etiology variants require the **medical condition coded and listed first** (e.g., B20 HIV disease, G40.209 complex partial seizures) (p. 1065-1066).

## Chapter-level differential logic
1. Is the presentation clinically significant (distress/impairment)? No -> not a disorder; consider a Z code (ch. 25) or Z03.89.
2. Does it meet full criteria for a specific disorder? Yes -> use it.
3. Does a chapter-specific other specified/unspecified category fit (e.g., other specified depressive disorder, unspecified psychotic disorder)? Yes -> use that, **not** this chapter.
4. Is the symptom picture a direct physiological consequence of a medical condition?
   - Yes, and a specific "due to AMC" disorder fits (e.g., depressive disorder due to AMC, F06.3x) -> use that.
   - Yes, but no specific due-to-AMC category fits -> F06.8 (with reason) or F09 (without).
   - No / not established -> F99.
5. Substance/medication cause? Use the substance/medication-induced categories in the relevant chapter, not this one.

## Coding conventions
- Due-to-AMC: list the medical code **immediately before** F06.8/F09; insert the actual medical condition name in place of "another medical condition."
- Other specified: write the reason after the name (e.g., "other specified mental disorder due to complex partial seizures, dissociative symptoms").
- Unspecified: no reason recorded; appropriate in emergency or time-limited settings.

## Documentation cues (chapter-wide)
- State why no specific disorder or chapter-level residual category fits.
- Document distress/impairment explicitly; without it, these codes are not justified.
- For F06.8/F09, document the medical evidence (history, exam, labs) and the physiological link.
- Plan to revisit when the evidence is incomplete: note what records, collateral or workup would clarify the diagnosis. Do not automatically add the formal "(provisional)" modifier to a residual diagnosis.
