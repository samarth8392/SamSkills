# Other Specified and Unspecified Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorder

> **Spark note:** Residual psychotic diagnoses require a clinically significant schizophrenia-spectrum/psychotic presentation without a fully met specific disorder. F28 names the reason; F29 omits the reason or reflects inadequate information (pp. 252–253).

## Criteria at a glance
- Characteristic symptoms predominate and cause clinically significant distress or impairment.
- Full criteria for a specific disorder in this class are not met.
- **Other specified:** record the specific reason after the diagnostic name.
- **Unspecified:** choose not to give that reason or lack enough information, such as at emergency presentation (pp. 252–253).

## Example presentations (other specified)
1. **Persistent auditory hallucinations:** ongoing voices without other accompanying features.
2. **Delusions with significant overlapping mood episodes:** mood episodes occupy a substantial part of the delusional illness, exceeding the brief overlap allowed in delusional disorder.
3. **Attenuated psychosis syndrome:** psychosis-like experiences below full intensity/threshold, with more transience and relatively retained insight. The Section II example is not a claim that the Section III research condition is a separate established diagnosis.
4. **Delusional symptoms in a relationship with a delusional person:** another person's delusional material shapes similar beliefs in someone who otherwise lacks enough symptoms for a full psychotic disorder (pp. 252–253).

## Specifiers & severity
The example reason is part of the recording description, not an additional coded disorder or a complete substitute criteria set.

## Coding
| Code | When to use |
|---|---|
| F28 | Other specified schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder; append reason |
| F29 | Unspecified schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder; reason omitted or information insufficient |

## Differential diagnosis
- **Full psychotic disorder:** apply the actual symptom, duration, mood-timing and functional criteria rather than choosing a residual category for convenience.
- **Mood disorder with psychotic features/schizoaffective disorder:** construct the longitudinal mood/psychosis timeline, especially for overlapping delusions and mood episodes.
- **Substance/medical causes or delirium:** determine attribution and whether a more specific induced/medical diagnosis applies.
- **Culturally accepted experiences or nonpsychotic explanations:** assess language, context, reality testing and functional consequences before labeling an experience psychotic (pp. 208–212, 252–253).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe the phenomena, degree of conviction/insight, course, distress and functional impact using actual evidence.
- For F28 state the particular unmet threshold or example presentation; distinguish attenuated symptoms from full psychosis.
- For F29 specify assessment limitations without inventing negative history.
- Record available mood, substance, medical and cultural assessment and the information needed to refine the formulation.
