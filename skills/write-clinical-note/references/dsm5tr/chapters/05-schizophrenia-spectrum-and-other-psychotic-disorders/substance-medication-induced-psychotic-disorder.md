# Substance/Medication-Induced Psychotic Disorder

> **Spark note:** Delusions and/or hallucinations judged to be a **direct physiological effect** of
> a drug, medication or toxin, starting during or soon after intoxication, withdrawal or exposure.
> Two key points: (1) if the person **knows** the perceptions are drug effects (reality testing
> intact), the diagnosis is intoxication or withdrawal **with perceptual disturbances**; (2) only
> delusions and hallucinations count. Substance-induced disorganization or catatonia goes to
> other specified/unspecified. About ⅓ later convert to schizophrenia spectrum or bipolar disorder (44% for cannabis). (pp. 238–244)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Delusions and/or hallucinations.
- **B.** History, exam or labs show **both**:
  1. Symptoms arose **during or soon after** intoxication or withdrawal, or after exposure to or withdrawal from a medication.
  2. The substance or medication **can produce** such symptoms.
- **C.** Not better explained by an independent (non-induced) psychotic disorder. Evidence for an independent disorder: symptoms **preceded** use; symptoms **persist a substantial time (about 1 month)** after acute withdrawal or severe intoxication ends; or other evidence such as a history of recurrent non-substance episodes.
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note:** diagnose this **instead of** intoxication or withdrawal only when psychotic symptoms **predominate** and are severe enough to need clinical attention.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Onset** (check substance Table 1 in Substance-Related chapter for which applies):
  - **With onset during intoxication:** criteria for intoxication met and symptoms began during it.
  - **With onset during withdrawal:** criteria for withdrawal met and symptoms began during or shortly after it.
  - **With onset after medication use:** began at initiation, a change in use, or withdrawal of a medication.
- **Current severity (optional):** Clinician-Rated Dimensions. **Delusions, hallucinations, abnormal psychomotor behavior and negative symptoms** (disorganized speech is **not** listed), each 0–4, most severe in the past 7 days.

**Recording procedure:**
- Name begins with the **specific substance** (e.g., cocaine, dexamethasone).
- Substances outside the classes (e.g., dexamethasone), or an unknown class judged etiologic → **F19.x59 other (or unknown)**.
- Order: [severity] [substance] use disorder **with** [substance]-induced psychotic disorder, **with onset during [intoxication/withdrawal]**.
  - e.g., *F14.259 severe cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced psychotic disorder, with onset during intoxication*.
  - e.g., *F16.959 phencyclidine-induced psychotic disorder, with onset during intoxication* (no use disorder).
- **Multiple substances** → list each separately (e.g., F12.259 … cannabis … ; F16.159 … phencyclidine …).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Hallucinations the person **recognizes** as substance-induced → intoxication or withdrawal **with perceptual disturbances**. This applies to alcohol withdrawal; cannabis intoxication; sedative/hypnotic/anxiolytic withdrawal; and stimulant intoxication.
- **Distinguish from independent psychosis** by onset, course and atypical features:
  - Induced: arises during or soon after use or withdrawal, may persist **weeks**, and continues as long as use continues.
  - Independent: precedes use, occurs during sustained abstinence, persists ≥1 month after intoxication or withdrawal ends, or prior recurrent independent episodes.
  - **Red flag:** de novo delusions in a **male >35** without a psychotic history suggest a substance or medication cause.
  - A prior independent psychotic disorder does **not** rule out a substance-induced episode. Conversely, substance use is common in primary psychosis, so consider other causes even during intoxication.
- Assess cognition, depression and mania domains.

## Associated features
- **Intoxication-linked classes:** alcohol; cannabis; hallucinogens (incl. PCP); inhalants; sedatives/hypnotics/anxiolytics; stimulants (incl. cocaine); other/unknown.
- **Withdrawal-linked classes:** alcohol; sedatives/hypnotics/anxiolytics; other/unknown.
- **Medications reported:** anesthetics and analgesics, **anticholinergics**, anticonvulsants, antihistamines, antihypertensives and cardiovascular drugs, antimicrobials, **antiparkinsonian** agents, chemotherapy (cyclosporine, procarbazine), **corticosteroids**, GI medications, muscle relaxants, NSAIDs, OTC drugs (phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine), antidepressants, disulfiram.
- **Toxins:** anticholinesterases, organophosphate insecticides, sarin and other nerve gases, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, volatile fuels and paints.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- General-population prevalence unknown. **7%–25%** of first-episode psychosis presentations, depending on setting.
- **Onset speed varies:** high-dose smoked cocaine → minutes; alcohol or sedatives → days to weeks of heavy use.
- **Alcohol-induced, with hallucinations:** usually only after prolonged heavy drinking in moderate/severe AUD. Hallucinations are mostly **auditory**.
- **Amphetamine/cocaine:** similar features. Rapid **persecutory delusions**. **Formication** (bugs crawling on or under the skin) → scratching and excoriations.
- **Cannabis:** after high doses. Persecutory delusions, marked anxiety, emotional lability, depersonalization. Usually remits **within a day**, sometimes longer.
- **Amphetamines, PCP and cocaine** can cause psychosis lasting **weeks or longer** despite removal of the agent and neuroleptic treatment.
- **Later life:** polypharmacy and drugs for parkinsonism, cardiovascular and other conditions make **prescription-induced** psychosis more likely than drug-of-abuse psychosis.
- **Danish 20-year registry:** **32%** later diagnosed with schizophrenia spectrum (26%) or bipolar (8%) disorder. **Highest for cannabis (44%)**.

## Diagnostic markers
- Blood levels consistent with toxicity (e.g., BAL, **digoxin**) increase diagnostic certainty.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Typically **severely disabling** in the acute phase and most often seen in **emergency departments**. Usually **self-limited**, resolving after the agent is removed.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Substance intoxication/withdrawal:** if reality testing is intact (the person knows it is the drug and neither believes nor acts on it) → intoxication or withdrawal **with perceptual disturbances**. This includes stimulants, cannabis, **meperidine**, PCP, and alcohol/sedative withdrawal.
- **Hallucinogen persisting perception disorder:** "flashback" perceptions long after hallucinogen use stops.
- **Delirium:** psychosis occurring only during delirium (e.g., severe alcohol withdrawal) is an associated feature and is not coded separately.
- **NCD:** delusions in major or mild NCD → NCD **with behavioral disturbance**.
- **Independent psychotic disorder** (schizophrenia, schizoaffective, delusional, brief psychotic, other specified/unspecified): no substance is judged etiologic.
- **Psychotic disorder due to AMC:** with prescribed medication, onset must be **while taking** the drug (or during its withdrawal). Consider whether the **medical condition itself** is the cause. A trial of medication substitution or discontinuation may be needed. If **both** contribute, **give both diagnoses**.
- **Other specified/unspecified schizophrenia spectrum:** substance-induced **disorganized or catatonic behavior, disorganized speech, incoherence or irrational content** without delusions or hallucinations goes here.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** describe delusions or hallucinations and whether **reality testing is impaired** ("believes the shadow people are real and barricaded the door" vs "knows the visuals are from the drug").
- **Crit B:** substance, route, dose, last use, and timeline of use vs symptom onset. UDS, BAL, serum levels. For medications, document start, dose-change or stop dates.
- **Crit C:** document no psychosis before use and no history of independent episodes. Plan to **re-evaluate about 1 month** after abstinence or withdrawal ends. Persistence beyond that suggests an independent disorder.
- **Crit D:** delirium screen (orientation, attention, fluctuating consciousness), vitals.
- **Crit E:** distress or impairment, and need for clinical attention.
- **Coding support:** document use-disorder severity (number of SUD criteria met) to choose the 4th character (1/2/9).
- **Safety:** agitation, violence risk, excoriations (stimulants), withdrawal risk (alcohol/sedatives), medical monitoring.
- MSE phrasing: "diaphoretic, pupils dilated, psychomotor agitation"; "endorses persecutory delusions beginning hours after methamphetamine use"; "tactile hallucinations of insects with excoriations on forearms"; "oriented ×4, attention intact (no delirium)."
