# Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** The same **≥3 of 12** catatonic features, judged to be a **direct physiological
> consequence** of a medical condition, usually neurological or metabolic. Name the condition in the
> diagnosis and code it **first**. Not given if catatonia occurs only during delirium or NMS. (pp. 250–251)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Clinical picture dominated by **≥3 of 12**: stupor, catalepsy, waxy flexibility, mutism (not applicable with an established aphasia), negativism, posturing, mannerism, stereotypy, agitation not influenced by external stimuli, grimacing, echolalia, echopraxia. Definitions are as in the [catatonia specifier file](catatonia-associated-with-another-mental-disorder.md).
- **B.** History, exam or labs show a **direct pathophysiological consequence** of another medical condition.
- **C.** Not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., a **manic episode**).
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.

## Coding
| Code | When |
|---|---|
| [medical condition code], then F06.1 | "F06.1 catatonic disorder due to [condition]". Code and list the medical condition **immediately before**. |

- Example: *K72.90 hepatic encephalopathy; F06.1 catatonic disorder due to hepatic encephalopathy*.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Diagnosed by ≥3 of the 12 features plus medical attribution (Crit B). Excluded if better explained by a mental disorder (Crit C) or occurring only during delirium (Crit D).

## Associated features
- **Neurological causes:** neoplasms, head trauma, cerebrovascular disease, encephalitis.
- **Metabolic causes:** hypercalcemia, **hepatic encephalopathy**, homocystinuria, **diabetic ketoacidosis**.
- Exam findings, labs and patterns of prevalence and onset follow the underlying condition.
- Also mentioned in the chapter overview: cerebral folate deficiency and rare autoimmune and paraneoplastic disorders (p. 248).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Delirium or NMS:** not diagnosed separately if catatonia occurs only during either. Catatonic symptoms are common in a significant proportion of delirium cases.
- **On neuroleptics:** consider **medication-induced movement disorders** (e.g., acute dystonia causing abnormal positioning) and **NMS** (catatonic-like features plus vital-sign or lab abnormalities).
- **Psychiatric catatonia:** catatonia can occur in brief psychotic, schizophreniform, schizophrenia, schizoaffective and substance/medication-induced psychotic disorders; some neurodevelopmental disorders; **all** bipolar and depressive disorders; and other mental disorders. Use the catatonia specifier when no medical cause is found.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Itemize ≥3 catatonic signs with the exam maneuver and response (see the specifier file for phrasing).
- **Crit B:** state the medical condition and the linking evidence (labs such as calcium, ammonia, glucose/ketones; imaging; EEG; LP), and the timing of catatonia relative to the medical course.
- **Crit C/D:** document no mood or psychotic disorder that better explains it, and a delirium screen (attention, level of consciousness).
- **NMS check:** temperature, BP/HR variability, CK, recent antipsychotic exposure.
- **Coding:** medical condition code listed first, then F06.1 with the condition's name.
- **Medical risks:** intake, hydration, immobility (DVT, pressure ulcers), hyperthermia.
