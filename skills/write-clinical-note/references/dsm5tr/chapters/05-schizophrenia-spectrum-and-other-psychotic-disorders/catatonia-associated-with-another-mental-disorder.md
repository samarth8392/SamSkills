# Catatonia Associated With Another Mental Disorder (Catatonia Specifier)

> **Spark note:** **≥3 of 12** psychomotor features occurring with a neurodevelopmental, psychotic,
> bipolar, depressive or other mental disorder. It is coded **F06.1 after** the primary disorder.
> Most catatonia occurs in **mood disorders**, not schizophrenia. It is under-recognized because
> presentations range from stupor to excitement and teaching overemphasizes rare signs such as
> waxy flexibility. **Rule out medical causes and NMS (G21.0) first.** (pp. 248–250)

## Catatonia overview (shared for all three diagnoses, pp. 248–249)
- The manual does **not** treat catatonia as an independent class. It recognizes three diagnoses: (a) associated with another mental disorder, (b) due to another medical condition, (c) unspecified.
- Core: a **marked psychomotor disturbance**, which may be decreased motor activity, decreased engagement during interview or exam, or excessive and peculiar activity.
  - Immobility: severe (**stupor**) or moderate (**catalepsy, waxy flexibility**).
  - Decreased engagement: severe (**mutism**) or moderate (**negativism**).
  - Excess: complex (**stereotypy**) or simple (**agitation**), plus echolalia and echopraxia.
  - The same person may **wax and wane** between decreased and excessive activity.
- **Medical risks in severe catatonia:** malnutrition, exhaustion, **thromboembolism**, pressure ulcers, muscle contractures, **hyperpyrexia**, self-injury. Requires close supervision to prevent harm to self or others.
- Medical causes include cerebral folate deficiency and rare autoimmune and paraneoplastic disorders.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** The clinical picture is **dominated by ≥3** of:
  1. **Stupor:** no psychomotor activity; not actively relating to the environment
  2. **Catalepsy:** examiner passively places a posture that is then held against gravity
  3. **Waxy flexibility:** slight, even resistance to positioning by the examiner
  4. **Mutism:** no or very little verbal response (exclude if known aphasia)
  5. **Negativism:** opposes or does not respond to instructions or stimuli
  6. **Posturing:** spontaneously and actively holds a posture against gravity
  7. **Mannerism:** odd caricature of normal actions
  8. **Stereotypy:** repetitive, abnormally frequent, non-goal-directed movements
  9. **Agitation** not influenced by external stimuli
  10. **Grimacing**
  11. **Echolalia:** mimicking another's speech
  12. **Echopraxia:** mimicking another's movements
- Occurs during the course of a neurodevelopmental, psychotic, bipolar, depressive or other mental disorder.

## Coding
| Code | When |
|---|---|
| [primary disorder code], then F06.1 | Name the associated disorder: "F06.1 catatonia associated with [disorder]". **Code the associated mental disorder first.** |

- Example: *F25.1 schizoaffective disorder, depressive type; F06.1 catatonia associated with schizoaffective disorder*.
- The "with catatonia" specifier is available for brief psychotic disorder, schizophreniform disorder, schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, bipolar and depressive disorders, and neurodevelopmental and other disorders.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Typically diagnosed **inpatient**.
- Occurs in **up to 35% of people with schizophrenia**, but **most cases involve depressive or bipolar disorders**.
- About **9%** of clinical-sample patients had catatonia (meta-analysis).
- **Before** using the specifier, rule out infectious, metabolic and neurological medical causes (see Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition).
- Catatonia can be a **medication side effect** (see the Medication-Induced Movement Disorders chapter).
- Given its seriousness, specifically consider **G21.0 neuroleptic malignant syndrome**.

## Culture-related issues
- The catatonia–mood disorder association holds across a wide range of cultures.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Catatonic disorder due to AMC:** evidence of a direct physiological medical cause.
- **NMS:** catatonic-like features **plus** vital-sign or lab abnormalities (fever, autonomic instability, CK) in someone on neuroleptics.
- **Medication-induced movement disorders:** e.g., abnormal posture from **neuroleptic-induced acute dystonia**.
- **Delirium:** catatonic symptoms often occur within delirium. A separate diagnosis is not made if they occur only during delirium.
- **Unspecified catatonia:** use when the underlying disorder is unclear or full criteria are not met.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- List **each** of the ≥3 observed features with how it was elicited:
  - "held arm elevated after examiner positioned it × 60 s" (catalepsy)
  - "even, plastic resistance to passive movement" (waxy flexibility)
  - "no verbal output despite repeated prompts; no known aphasia" (mutism)
  - "resists eye opening / turns away when approached" (negativism)
  - "repeats examiner's last words" (echolalia)
  - "repetitive rocking" (stereotypy)
- A standardized catatonia rating scale can support counting. The text does not name one.
- Name the associated mental disorder and document its criteria separately.
- **Rule-outs:** vitals and temperature, CK, labs, medication review (antipsychotic exposure, recent dose changes, NMS), neurological and infectious work-up, delirium screen.
- **Medical risk:** oral intake and hydration, mobility (DVT/pressure-ulcer risk), temperature, safety and supervision needs.
