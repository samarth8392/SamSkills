# Brief Psychotic Disorder

> **Spark note:** Sudden positive psychotic symptoms lasting **≥1 day but <1 month**, with
> **full return to premorbid functioning**. The diagnosis is often made in retrospect: if
> symptoms reach 1 month, it must be revised. More than 50% relapse, so watch for an underlying recurrent
> disorder (bipolar, schizophrenia). Do not count culturally sanctioned experiences. (pp. 217–220)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** ≥1 of the following, with **≥1 being items 1–3**:
  1. Delusions
  2. Hallucinations
  3. Disorganized speech (e.g., frequent derailment, incoherence)
  4. Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior
  - Note: exclude a symptom that is a **culturally sanctioned response**.
- **B.** Episode lasts **≥1 day and <1 month**, with **eventual full return** to premorbid functioning.
- **C.** Not better explained by MDD or bipolar disorder with psychotic features, or another psychotic disorder (schizophrenia, catatonia). Not attributable to a substance or medical condition.
- Negative symptoms are **not** among the qualifying symptoms.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With marked stressor(s) (brief reactive psychosis):** response to events that would be markedly stressful to almost anyone in similar circumstances **in the person's culture**.
- **Without marked stressor(s):** no such events.
- **With peripartum onset:** onset during pregnancy or **within 4 weeks postpartum**.
- **With catatonia:** per the criteria for catatonia associated with another mental disorder. Add **F06.1**.
- **Current severity (optional):** Clinician-Rated Dimensions. Five psychosis domains, each 0–4, most severe in the past 7 days.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Requires ≥1 **positive** symptom. Total remission and return to baseline are **within 1 month** of onset. Psychosis may last only a few days.
- Assess cognition, depression and mania domains.
- If symptoms remit in under 1 month **because of medication**, separating it from schizophreniform is difficult.
- Watch for a recurrent disorder (bipolar, recurrent exacerbations of schizophrenia) behind recurring episodes.

## Associated features
- **Emotional turmoil, overwhelming confusion,** and rapid shifts between intense affects.
- Impairment can be **severe** despite the short duration. The person may need supervision for nutrition and hygiene, and protection from poor judgment, cognitive impairment or acting on delusions.
- **Increased suicidal behavior risk**, especially during the acute episode.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **2%–7%** of first-onset psychosis cases in several countries.
- Onset from adolescence across the lifespan. **Mean age at onset is the mid-30s.**
- **>50% relapse** after remission. Most still have **favorable** social and symptomatic outcomes.
- In **less than half** of cases (DSM-IV BPD / ICD-10 acute and transient psychotic disorder), the diagnosis later changes, more often to **schizophrenia spectrum**, less often to affective or other psychotic disorders.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Distinguish from **culturally sanctioned** experiences. Voices during religious ceremonies usually do not persist and the community does not see them as abnormal.
- In many cultures, bereaved relatives normally hear, see or interact with the spirit of the recently deceased, without pathological sequelae.
- Weigh cultural and religious background when judging whether a belief is delusional.

## Suicide risk
- Elevated risk of suicidal behavior, particularly during the acute episode (p. 218).

## Functional consequences
- Impairment may be severe while the episode lasts. Outcome is generally favorable.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Other medical conditions / delirium:** evidence from history, exam or labs that the psychosis directly results from a medical condition (e.g., Cushing's syndrome, brain tumor, subdural hematoma).
- **Substance-related** (induced psychotic disorder, substance-induced delirium, intoxication): a substance is judged to be etiologic. Use **UDS and BAL**, and the timing of intake vs onset.
- **Depressive and bipolar disorders:** excluded if psychosis occurs **only** during a full major depressive, manic or mixed episode.
- **Other psychotic disorders:** if psychosis lasts **≥1 month** → schizophreniform, delusional, MDD or bipolar with psychotic features, or other specified/unspecified, depending on the presentation.
- **Factitious disorder (psychological signs):** symptoms are intentionally produced. **Malingering:** feigned for an understandable goal.
- **Personality disorders:** stress can trigger transient psychotic symptoms that usually do not warrant a separate diagnosis. If they last **≥1 day**, an additional BPD diagnosis may be appropriate.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** name each positive symptom with examples (e.g., "responding to unseen stimuli," "states neighbors are broadcasting thoughts"). Document which of items 1–3 is present.
- **Crit B:** **exact onset date/time** and resolution date. Document return to baseline (work, self-care, relationships) and use collateral to establish premorbid level. Note if recovery is still pending; reassess by day 30.
- **Specifier evidence:** describe the stressor and why it would be markedly stressful in the person's culture. Record pregnancy or postpartum timing (≤4 weeks). Record catatonic signs (≥3 of 12) if present.
- **Rule-outs:** UDS, BAL, medication review, medical/neurological exam and labs, delirium screen (orientation, attention). Assess for mood episodes. Record cultural/religious context, and consider bereavement-related experiences.
- **Safety:** acute suicide risk, ability to meet nutrition and hygiene needs, risk of acting on delusions, need for supervision.
- MSE phrasing: "affect labile, shifting rapidly from tearful to elated"; "appears perplexed and confused"; "thought process disorganized with derailment"; "endorses AH of commenting voices onset 3 days ago following [event]."
