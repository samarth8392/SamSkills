# Unspecified Catatonia

> **Spark note:** Use this category for clinically significant catatonic symptoms when the underlying cause is unclear, full catatonia criteria are not met, or the available assessment is insufficient. Its recording convention requires two ordered codes (p. 252).

## Criteria at a glance
- Symptoms characteristic of catatonia cause clinically significant distress or impairment.
- At least one applies: the responsible mental/medical disorder is unclear; full catatonia criteria are unmet; or information is inadequate for a more specific diagnosis (including emergency assessment).
- The full ≥3-of-12 threshold for specified catatonia is **not** imposed on this residual category (p. 252).

## Specifiers & severity
No additional formal specifiers supplied. Record the actual signs and assessment limitations rather than assuming a complete syndrome.

## Coding
| Sequence | Code | Record |
|---|---|---|
| First | R29.818 | Other symptoms involving nervous and musculoskeletal systems |
| Second | F06.1 | Unspecified catatonia |

Both are required in this order by the source coding note (p. 252).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Catatonia is a psychomotor syndrome, not evidence by itself of schizophrenia. Full syndrome signs include immobility, maintained posture, reduced speech/response, unusual motor acts or stimulus-independent agitation, and imitation of speech/movements. The chapter emphasizes medical causes and medication complications, including neuroleptic malignant syndrome, in evaluating catatonia (pp. 248–251).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Catatonia associated with another mental disorder:** known qualifying mental disorder and ≥3 qualifying signs support the specific specifier and additional F06.1 coding.
- **Catatonic disorder due to another medical condition:** requires evidence of direct physiological causation and its full criteria.
- **Delirium:** do not mistake fluctuating attention/awareness for a primary psychiatric attribution; catatonic symptoms can occur during delirium.
- **Medication effects/NMS:** neuroleptic exposure, vital-sign/laboratory abnormalities and movement examination are important alternatives (pp. 250–251).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Name and describe each observed/reported motor, speech and response abnormality, timing and functional consequences.
- State whether the limitation is symptom threshold, unknown cause or insufficient information; document sources and pending evaluation.
- Record medical/neurological findings, medication exposure and assessment for delirium/NMS as actually performed.
- Preserve the R29.818 → F06.1 recording order and revise attribution when evidence becomes available.
