# Schizophreniform Disorder

> **Spark note:** Same Criterion A as schizophrenia, but **total duration is ≥1 month and <6
> months** and **functional decline is not required**. If the person has not yet recovered,
> the diagnosis is **"(provisional)"**. Change it to schizophrenia once the illness passes 6 months.
> About ⅓ recover. Most of the rest become schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder. (pp. 220–223)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** ≥2 of 5, each present for a significant portion of a **1-month** period (less if successfully treated). **≥1 must be items 1–3**:
  1. Delusions
  2. Hallucinations
  3. Disorganized speech
  4. Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior
  5. Negative symptoms (diminished emotional expression or avolition)
- **B.** An episode lasts **≥1 month but <6 months**. If diagnosing before recovery, label it **"provisional."**
- **C.** Schizoaffective disorder and MDD or bipolar disorder with psychotic features are ruled out: either no major depressive or manic episodes occurred **concurrently** with active-phase symptoms, or any that did were present for a **minority** of total active + residual duration.
- **D.** Not attributable to a substance or another medical condition.
- **No Criterion B (functional decline)** and no ASD/communication exclusion criterion, unlike schizophrenia.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With good prognostic features:** **≥2** of:
  - Onset of prominent psychotic symptoms **within 4 weeks** of the first noticeable change in behavior or functioning
  - Confusion or perplexity
  - Good premorbid social and occupational functioning
  - Absence of blunted or flat affect
- **Without good prognostic features:** fewer than 2 of the above.
- **With catatonia:** add **F06.1 catatonia associated with schizophreniform disorder**.
- **Current severity (optional):** Clinician-Rated Dimensions, 5 domains, 0–4, most severe in the past 7 days.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Duration counts **prodromal + active + residual** phases. It sits between brief psychotic (<1 mo) and schizophrenia (≥6 mo).
- Two scenarios:
  1. Episode lasted 1–6 months and the person has **recovered** → final diagnosis.
  2. Symptomatic for <6 months and **not yet recovered** → "(provisional)". Revise to schizophrenia if illness persists past 6 months.
- Impaired functioning **may** be present but is not required.
- Assess cognition, depression and mania domains.
- For associated features, age-related course, culture, sex/gender, differential and comorbidity, the text refers to **Schizophrenia**.

## Associated features
- No laboratory or psychometric test. Neuroimaging, neuropathological and neurophysiological abnormalities exist in several brain regions, but none are diagnostic.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Incidence across settings is likely similar to schizophrenia. In the US and other high-income countries it is low, possibly **fivefold less** than schizophrenia.
- In lower-income countries it may be higher, especially **"with good prognostic features,"** and sometimes as common as schizophrenia.
- About **⅓** with a provisional diagnosis recover within 6 months, and schizophreniform is their final diagnosis. Most of the remaining ⅔ are eventually diagnosed with **schizophrenia or schizoaffective** disorder.
- **Genetic:** relatives have increased risk for schizophrenia.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- See schizophrenia.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed (see schizophrenia).

## Functional consequences
- For those who progress to schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder, consequences are similar to those disorders: dysfunction at school or work, in relationships and in self-care.
- Those who recover have better functional outcomes.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Brief psychotic disorder:** duration <1 month.
- **Schizophrenia:** ≥6 months **and** functional decline (Crit B). The schizophrenia differential applies in full, because the two differ mainly in duration.
- Broad list to consider: psychotic disorder due to AMC or its treatment; delirium or major NCD; substance/medication-induced psychotic disorder or delirium; MDD or bipolar with psychotic or catatonic features; schizoaffective; other specified/unspecified bipolar; delusional disorder; other specified/unspecified schizophrenia spectrum; schizotypal, schizoid or paranoid PD; ASD; childhood disorders with disorganized speech; ADHD; OCD; PTSD; TBI.

## Comorbidity
- See schizophrenia.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** list ≥2 symptoms with dated examples, at least one being delusions, hallucinations or disorganized speech. Note if treatment shortened the active phase.
- **Crit B:** build a **timeline**: first prodromal change → active-phase onset → current status. Record total weeks and state "(provisional)" when recovery is pending. Set a reassessment date for the 6-month mark.
- **Crit C:** document mood screening (depressed mood, mania) and the timing of any mood episode relative to psychosis.
- **Crit D rule-outs:** UDS, medication list, medical and neurological work-up.
- **Prognostic specifier:** record rapid onset (≤4 wk), perplexity, premorbid functioning (school or work history, friendships) and affect range.
- MSE phrasing: "appears perplexed, asks 'what is happening to me'"; "affect full range" (supports good prognosis) or "affect blunted"; "endorses persecutory delusions × 6 weeks."
