# Delusional Disorder

> **Spark note:** One or more delusions for ≥1 month in someone who has **never** met
> schizophrenia Criterion A. Outside the delusion, the person often looks and functions normally.
> Common errors: missing that **any** catatonia, prominent hallucinations or disorganization
> with the delusions pushes toward schizophrenia-spectrum; diagnosing it when OCD/BDD with
> absent insight fits better; or diagnosing it when mood episodes are **not** brief relative to the delusions. (pp. 212–217)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** ≥1 delusion lasting **≥1 month**.
- **B.** Schizophrenia Criterion A has **never** been met.
  - Note: hallucinations, if present, are **not prominent** and fit the delusional theme (e.g., felt infestation with delusions of infestation).
- **C.** Apart from the delusion's impact or ramifications, functioning is **not markedly impaired** and behavior is **not obviously bizarre or odd**.
- **D.** Any manic or major depressive episodes have been **brief relative to** the total duration of the delusional periods.
- **E.** Not attributable to a substance or another medical condition. Not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., BDD, OCD).

## Specifiers & severity
**Subtype (specify whether), based on the central theme:**
- **Erotomanic:** another person is in love with the individual.
- **Grandiose:** great but unrecognized talent or insight, or an important discovery.
- **Jealous:** spouse or lover is unfaithful.
- **Persecutory:** being conspired against, cheated, spied on, followed, poisoned/drugged, maligned, harassed, or obstructed in long-term goals.
- **Somatic:** bodily functions or sensations.
- **Mixed:** no single theme predominates.
- **Unspecified:** dominant belief cannot be determined or is not a listed type (e.g., referential delusions without a prominent persecutory or grandiose component).

**With bizarre content:** delusions clearly implausible, not understandable and not from ordinary life experience (e.g., organs replaced by a stranger without scars).

**Course specifiers (only after 1 year of illness):** first episode, currently in acute episode / partial remission / full remission; multiple episodes, currently in acute episode / partial remission / full remission; continuous; unspecified. See _overview for definitions.

**Current severity (optional):** Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity. Delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, abnormal psychomotor behavior and negative symptoms are each rated 0–4 for the most severe level in the past 7 days.

- No "with catatonia" specifier. Catatonia plus delusions meets schizophrenia Crit A, which rules this diagnosis out.

## Subtype details (p. 214)
- **Erotomanic:** the object is usually of higher status (celebrity, superior at work) but may be a stranger. Attempts to contact the object are common. Legal trouble is possible.
- **Grandiose:** may involve a special relationship with a prominent person, or believing one *is* that person and the real one is an impostor. Content may be religious.
- **Jealous:** reached without due cause from small bits of "evidence" (e.g., disarrayed clothing). The person confronts the partner and tries to intervene in the imagined infidelity.
- **Persecutory:** small slights are magnified into a delusional system. Repeated legal or legislative action. Often resentful and angry, and **may become violent** toward perceived persecutors.
- **Somatic:** most commonly emitting a foul odor, insect infestation on or in the skin, an internal parasite, or body parts not working.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Delusions are the sine qua non. **Hallucinations and negative symptoms are uncommon**, and **disorganization is rare**.
- **Catatonia with delusions rules out delusional disorder**, because schizophrenia Crit A would be met.
- A subset has prominent **depressive** symptoms. **Cognitive impairment and mania are rare.**
- Assess cognition, depression and mania domains to separate it from other psychotic disorders.
- **"Factual insight" without true insight:** the person can report that others think the belief is irrational but cannot accept this.

## Associated features
- Social, marital and work problems arising from the delusion.
- Irritable or dysphoric mood, often a reaction to the delusion.
- Anger and violence with persecutory, jealous and erotomanic types. Litigiousness (e.g., hundreds of protest letters). Legal problems, especially with jealous and erotomanic types.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Lifetime prevalence about **0.2%** (Finnish sample). **Persecutory** is the most frequent subtype.
- **Jealous** type is probably more common in men. Overall, no major sex/gender difference in frequency or content.
- Global functioning is generally better than in schizophrenia. The diagnosis is usually stable.
- About **⅓** of cases lasting 1–3 months are later rediagnosed as schizophrenia. Change is much less likely once duration exceeds **6–12 months**.
- May be more prevalent in older individuals.
- **Genetic:** significant familial relationship with schizophrenia and schizotypal PD.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Traditional beliefs unfamiliar to Western clinicians may be wrongly called delusional. Assess cultural and religious context. Delusional content varies by culture.
- Men generally have more severe symptoms and worse functional outcomes than women.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Impairment is usually more **circumscribed** than in other psychotic disorders, but can be substantial (poor occupational functioning, isolation). The delusion itself usually drives it.
- Hallmark: behavior and appearance seem **normal** when the delusion is not being discussed or acted on.

## Differential diagnosis
- **OCD / BDD:** if fully convinced of OCD or BDD beliefs → OCD or BDD **with absent insight/delusional beliefs**, not delusional disorder.
- **Delirium, major NCD, psychotic disorder due to AMC:** e.g., simple persecutory delusions in major NCD → *major NCD, with behavioral disturbance*.
- **Substance/medication-induced psychotic disorder:** may look identical cross-sectionally. Distinguish by the timing of substance use relative to onset and remission.
- **Schizophrenia / schizophreniform:** absence of the other active-phase symptoms.
  - Delusion quality: schizophrenia → greater **disorganization** (less internally consistent, logical or systematized). Delusional disorder → greater **conviction**, **extension** (spreads into more areas of life) and **pressure** (preoccupation).
- **Depressive/bipolar disorders with psychotic features, schizoaffective:** delusions **only during** mood episodes → mood disorder with psychotic features. Mood episodes may be superimposed on delusional disorder only if their total duration is **brief** relative to the delusional disturbance. Otherwise → **other specified/unspecified schizophrenia spectrum** plus **other specified/unspecified depressive or bipolar disorder**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** state the delusion's content and theme, onset date, and duration ≥1 month. Document fixity (e.g., "unmoved by contrary evidence presented in session").
- **Crit B:** document **absence** of prominent AH/VH, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior and negative symptoms, now and by history. Any tactile or olfactory experience should be **theme-congruent** (e.g., "reports crawling sensation consistent with infestation belief").
- **Crit C:** record preserved functioning outside the delusion (employment, self-care, social), and behavior that is organized and not bizarre.
- **Crit D:** timeline of any mood episodes vs delusional periods. Mood episodes must be brief by comparison.
- **Crit E rule-outs:** UDS/substance history, medical work-up (neuro, endocrine, cognition screen), OCD/BDD insight assessment.
- **Risk:** for persecutory, jealous and erotomanic types, document **violence risk**, named targets, stalking or contact attempts, weapons access and legal involvement. Also document dysphoria and SI.
- MSE phrasing: "thought content notable for fixed, systematized persecutory belief about neighbors"; "thought process linear and goal-directed"; "no perceptual disturbances endorsed or observed"; "insight: acknowledges others disagree but maintains belief (factual insight only)"; "behavior appropriate when topic not engaged."
- Course specifiers only after 1 year. Note bizarre content if present.
