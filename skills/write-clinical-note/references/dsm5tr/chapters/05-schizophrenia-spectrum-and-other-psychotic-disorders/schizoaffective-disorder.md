# Schizoaffective Disorder

> **Spark note:** A major mood episode (MDE or manic) **concurrent** with schizophrenia
> Criterion A, **plus ≥2 weeks of delusions or hallucinations without** a mood episode at some point in the
> lifetime illness (separates it from mood disorder with psychotic features), **plus** mood
> episodes present for the **majority** of total active + residual illness (separates it from
> schizophrenia). Judge it **longitudinally** over the whole lifetime illness, not cross-sectionally.
> The diagnosis can shift over time. (pp. 232–238)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** An **uninterrupted** period of illness with a major mood episode (MDE or manic) **concurrent** with schizophrenia Criterion A.
  - Note: the MDE **must include depressed mood (A1)**. Anhedonia alone does not count, because loss of interest is common in schizophrenia.
- **B.** Delusions or hallucinations for **≥2 weeks without** a major mood episode during the lifetime illness.
- **C.** Symptoms meeting major mood episode criteria are present for the **majority** of total active + residual illness.
- **D.** Not attributable to a substance or another medical condition.
- Schizophrenia Criteria **B (dysfunction), C (6 months) and F (ASD/communication exclusion) are not required**.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Type (specify whether):**
  - **Bipolar type (F25.0):** a manic episode is part of the presentation. MDEs may also occur.
  - **Depressive type (F25.1):** only MDEs.
- **With catatonia:** add **F06.1 catatonia associated with schizoaffective disorder**.
- **Course specifiers** (after 1 year, if consistent with course criteria): first episode or multiple episodes (≥2 = episode, remission, ≥1 relapse), each currently in acute episode / partial remission / full remission; continuous; unspecified.
- **Current severity (optional):** Clinician-Rated Dimensions, 5 psychosis domains, 0–4, most severe in the past 7 days. Depression and mania dimensions are particularly relevant here.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The diagnosis is usually, but not necessarily, made during the psychotic period.
- **Criterion C requires lifetime review:** total psychotic illness (active + residual) vs the time when significant mood symptoms (untreated, or needing antidepressant or mood stabilizer) accompanied the psychosis. Requires sufficient history and judgment.
  - **Example that fails C:** 4 years of active + residual psychosis with mood episodes totalling ≤1 year → schizophrenia.
  - **Example that meets it (typical pattern):** 2 months psychosis alone → 4 months psychosis + full MDE → 1 month psychosis alone. Mood is present for 4 of 7 months (majority), with ≥2 wk psychosis without mood → schizoaffective.
- Mood symptoms in the **prodrome** of schizophrenia do not by themselves indicate schizoaffective disorder. Co-occurrence is what counts.
- If later follow-up shows only residual psychosis (subthreshold, negative symptoms) and no further mood episodes, the diagnosis may be changed to **schizophrenia**.
- Assess cognition, depression and mania domains.

## Associated features
- Occupational and social impairment is frequent but **not a criterion**. Restricted social contact and self-care problems are common.
- Negative symptoms are **less severe and less persistent** than in schizophrenia.
- **Anosognosia** is common but less severe and pervasive than in schizophrenia.
- Risk of later MDD or bipolar episodes if mood symptoms continue after the psychosis remits.
- Alcohol and other substance-related disorders may co-occur.
- No definitive test. Neuropsych deficits (executive function, verbal memory, processing speed) are possibly milder than in schizophrenia. **Gray matter volume loss** on imaging is similar to schizophrenia.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- About **⅓ as common as schizophrenia**. Lifetime prevalence **0.3%** (Finnish, DSM-IV criteria), **higher in women**. Expected to be lower with DSM-5's stricter Criterion C.
- Onset is typically **early adulthood**, but can be anywhere from adolescence to late life.
- Diagnoses shift: some people with another psychotic disorder are later rediagnosed as schizoaffective once the mood pattern becomes clear; others are first diagnosed with a mood disorder before independent psychosis appears. Change **from** schizoaffective **to schizophrenia** is more common than to a mood disorder, and more so under DSM-5's "majority" rule (DSM-IV said "substantial" portion).
- **Prognosis** is better than schizophrenia but worse than mood disorders.
- **Bipolar type** may be more common in young adults; **depressive type** in older adults.
- **Genetic:** increased risk in first-degree relatives of people with schizophrenia; also with a first-degree relative who has bipolar or schizoaffective disorder. Polygenic risk scores for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and MDD may all be elevated.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Consider cultural and socioeconomic background. Evil eye, curses and spirit beliefs may be normative.
- **African American and Hispanic** individuals meeting schizoaffective criteria are more likely to be diagnosed with **schizophrenia**. To reduce bias, evaluate **both psychotic and mood symptoms** comprehensively.
- Prevalence is higher in women (DSM-IV data).

## Suicide risk
- Lifetime suicide risk for schizophrenia and schizoaffective disorder is **5%**. **Depressive symptoms** correlate with higher risk.
- Suicide rates are reportedly higher in North American populations than in European, Eastern European, South American and Indian populations.

## Functional consequences
- Global dysfunction (social, occupational), but **not a diagnostic criterion**, and it varies widely between individuals.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Broad list:** delirium; major NCD; substance/medication-induced psychotic disorder or NCD; bipolar or MDD with psychotic features; depressive or bipolar with catatonic features; schizotypal, schizoid or paranoid PD; brief psychotic; schizophreniform; schizophrenia; delusional disorder; other specified/unspecified schizophrenia spectrum.
- **Psychotic disorder due to AMC / substances:** both can produce mixed psychotic and mood presentations. Exclude them.
- **Schizophrenia:** Criterion C separates them. Mood episodes are only a **minority** of the illness → schizophrenia.
  - Example: a 4-month severe MDE in the first 6 months of a chronic psychosis, followed by years of active or residual psychosis without another mood episode → change to schizophrenia.
- **MDD/bipolar with psychotic features:** Criterion B separates them. In mood disorders, psychosis occurs **only during** mood episodes. Schizoaffective requires ≥2 wk of prominent delusions or hallucinations without a mood episode.
- Collateral from **medical records and informants** is often needed to establish proportions and concurrence.

## Comorbidity
- Common: **substance use disorders** and **anxiety disorders**.
- Medical: increased **metabolic syndrome** and other conditions, with reduced life expectancy.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** document the concurrent mood episode (MDE with **depressed mood**, or mania) together with schizophrenia Crit A symptoms. List the MDE or manic symptoms counted.
- **Crit B:** document a specific **≥2-week window** of delusions or hallucinations with **no** mood episode (dates, source: patient, collateral, records).
- **Crit C:** a **lifetime illness timeline** with total psychotic illness duration vs cumulative mood-episode duration, and state "mood episodes present for majority (~X of Y months)."
- **Type:** document any lifetime manic episode (bipolar type) vs MDE only (depressive type).
- **Crit D rule-outs:** substances (UDS), medications such as steroids, medical work-up.
- **Risk:** depression-linked suicide risk, mania-related risk behaviors.
- **Bias check:** note that both mood and psychotic domains were assessed fully.
- MSE phrasing: "mood 'hopeless,' affect constricted and dysphoric"; "endorses persecutory AH persisting during euthymic period [dates] per collateral"; "pressured speech, decreased need for sleep concurrent with grandiose delusions."
