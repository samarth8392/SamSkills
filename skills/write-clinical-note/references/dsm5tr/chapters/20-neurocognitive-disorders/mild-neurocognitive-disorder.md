# Mild Neurocognitive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Mild NCD is a **modest**, measurable, acquired decline in ≥1 domain while the person **stays independent**. They still manage bills and medications, but only with more effort, compensatory strategies or accommodation. It is roughly "MCI". For medical etiologies, code **G31.84** with **no** etiology code and **no** coded behavioural specifier, which is the opposite of major NCD. Shared text (features, prevalence, culture, differential) is in `major-neurocognitive-disorder.md`. (pp. 917–919)

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 917–918)
- **A. Modest decline** from a previous level in **≥1 domain**, based on both:
  1. **Concern** (individual, knowledgeable informant or clinician) of a **mild** decline, **and**
  2. **Modest objective impairment**, preferably on standardized neuropsychological testing, or else another quantified clinical assessment.
- **B. Independence preserved:** complex IADLs (paying bills, managing medications) are still done, but may need **greater effort, compensatory strategies or accommodation**.
- **C.** Not exclusively during delirium.
- **D.** Not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., MDD, schizophrenia).

## Specifiers & severity (pp. 918–919)
- **Specify whether due to:** the same 13 etiologies as major NCD (AD, frontotemporal degeneration, Lewy body disease, vascular disease, TBI, substance/medication use, HIV infection, prion disease, Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease, another medical condition, multiple etiologies, unspecified etiology).
- **Without / with behavioral disturbance (specify):** psychotic, mood, agitation, apathy or other symptoms. This **cannot be coded but should be recorded**.
- **No severity specifier** for mild NCD.
- Add code(s) for psychiatric syndromes due to the same medical condition, e.g., F06.2 psychotic disorder due to TBI, with delusions; F06.32 depressive disorder due to HIV disease, with major depressive–like episode.

## Coding (pp. 918–920)
| Situation | Code |
|---|---|
| Mild NCD due to **any medical etiology** (AD, FTD, Lewy body, vascular, TBI, HIV, prion, PD, HD, AMC, multiple) | **G31.84**. Do **not** add codes for the etiological medical condition(s) |
| Substance/medication-induced mild NCD | Code by substance (see `substance-medication-induced-neurocognitive-disorder.md`) |
| Multiple etiologies including a substance | G31.84 **plus** the relevant substance/medication-induced mild NCD code(s) |
| Unspecified mild NCD | **R41.9** |

**Recording examples (p. 919):**
- *G31.84 Mild neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease, without behavioral disturbance.*
- *G31.84 Mild neurocognitive disorder due to traumatic brain injury, with behavioral disturbance; F06.34 bipolar and related disorder due to traumatic brain injury, with mixed features.*

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 922–923)
- Typical neuropsych performance is **1–2 SD below appropriate norms (3rd–16th percentile)**. Testing is **particularly critical** for mild NCD.
- The person describes tasks as harder, slower or needing compensation (lists, calendars, double-checking). Families may see this as normal aging, so **careful history** is essential.
- Functional changes are minimal, so neuropsychological assessment is the key marker.
- Characteristic subtype features are less clear at the mild level than at the major level, but are sometimes present.
- Mood symptoms (depression, anxiety, apathy) may be among the earliest features of mild NCD, so early recognition matters.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors (pp. 924–925)
- MCI is ~**2–10% at 65** and **5–25% by 85**. Estimates are very sensitive to definition, especially in community samples, where evaluations are less detailed and the decline from baseline may be less clear.
- Harder to recognize with age because of medical illness and sensory deficits. Subtypes are harder to tell apart with age because of multiple contributors.
- Risk factors are the same as for major NCD (age, vascular risk, education/literacy).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- See the major NCD file. Mild decline is more likely to be noticed in people with complex occupational, domestic or recreational demands.

## Suicide risk
- Elevated suicidal behaviour in NCDs generally (see the major NCD file).

## Functional consequences
- Independence is preserved, but subtle interference shows as more time, more effort and more errors at work or in IADLs.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 927)
- **Normal cognition / normal aging:** the boundary is arbitrary. Use quantified, **longitudinal** assessment and evidence of change from the person's own baseline.
- **Major NCD:** loss of independence in IADLs means major NCD.
- **Delirium (persistent):** assess attention and arousal.
- **Major depressive disorder:** consistent memory/executive deficits suggest NCD (e.g., AD). Variable or nonspecific performance suggests depression. Re-evaluate after treating the depression.
- **Specific learning disorder / neurodevelopmental disorders:** clarify baseline. Mild NCD is a change.

## Comorbidity
- The same as major NCD: delirium risk, depression, and age-related medical illness.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A1:** a specific concern with an example and a domain (e.g., "relies on calendar alerts now; misplaced two bill payments this quarter").
- **A2:** instrument and score, with percentile or SD where available (3rd–16th percentile supports mild). Note education, language and sensory limits.
- **B:** state explicitly that IADLs are **still performed independently** and describe the compensations used (lists, reminders, spouse double-checks, extra time).
- **C/D:** no fluctuation or attentional/arousal disturbance, and depression screened and addressed.
- **Etiology:** name the presumed etiology and its evidence. Record the behavioural disturbance in words, because it is not coded.
- **Code:** G31.84 alone for medical etiologies (no G30.9 etc.). Add any F06.x syndrome due to the same condition.
- **Plan cues:** baseline for longitudinal re-testing, safety review (driving, finances), vascular risk management, treatment of comorbid depression or sleep problems.
