# Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Huntington's Disease

> **Spark note:** Cognitive and psychiatric changes may precede overt Huntington motor disease by many years. The NCD criteria allow established disease **or** risk based on family history/genetics; distinguish this from the source's definition of manifest Huntington disease, which requires motor signs. (pp. 972–974)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Major/mild NCD. **B:** Insidious onset and gradual worsening. **C:** Clinically established Huntington disease or risk supported by family history/genetic evidence. **D:** Not better accounted for by another medical or mental disorder. (p. 972)
## Specifiers & severity
Establish the [major](major-neurocognitive-disorder.md) or [mild](mild-neurocognitive-disorder.md) syndrome first: acquired decline, concern plus objective assessment, functional threshold, and exclusion of delirium-only or another mental disorder. Major NCD severity describes cognitive effects on function: mild = instrumental tasks affected; moderate = basic self-care affected; severe = complete dependence. Record clinically significant behavioral symptoms individually with the with/without behavioral disturbance specifier. These are separate from the severity of the underlying disease. (pp. 916–920)
## Coding

Record major severity in words; the mild syndrome's behavioral specifier is also recorded without changing its code. Add an appropriate psychiatric diagnosis/code when clinically significant psychiatric symptoms are attributed to the same medical condition; see the relevant phenomenological chapter.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Executive organization, planning and speed often decline earlier than learning/memory. Chorea and bradykinesia characterize manifest disease, but cognitive and mood/behavioral changes may precede them by a decade or more. Basal ganglia atrophy may support assessment; genotype does not substitute for demonstrating an NCD syndrome. (pp. 972–974)

## Associated features
Irritability, apathy, anxiety, compulsive symptoms, depression and occasional psychosis; impaired insight and disinhibition can be prominent. Advanced disease affects gait, speech and swallowing. (pp. 972–973)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
The source estimates worldwide Huntington disease prevalence at 2.7/100,000; it varies considerably geographically. Onset is often 35–45, with survival about 10–20 years after diagnosis. Juvenile presentations may feature rigidity/dystonia rather than chorea. HTT CAG expansion is dominant; ≥40 repeats is fully penetrant, 36–39 partially penetrant, and longer expansions generally predict earlier onset. (pp. 973–974)

## Suicide risk
Elevated thoughts and suicide mortality occur both before and after manifest motor disease. Depression, anxiety, irritability, psychosis and apathy are associated risk factors. Record individual assessment; a cited European cohort reported suicide in 6.6% of deaths, which is not an individual prediction. (p. 974)

## Functional consequences
Work, driving and family roles may deteriorate early, particularly from slowing, initiation and attention deficits. Later motor and communication disability add substantial care needs beyond the cognitive impairment. (p. 974)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Primary mood/other psychiatric disorder:** consider the longitudinal motor/genetic context; use an appropriate due-to-medical-condition diagnosis if attributable psychiatric symptoms are the focus.
- **Other NCD:** executive/slowing profiles overlap with vascular and other disorders.
- **Other chorea:** Wilson disease, tardive dyskinesia, Sydenham chorea, lupus and other movement disorders; a Huntington-like syndrome without positive HTT testing may be a phenocopy. (p. 975)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Identify objective cognitive change and its functional impact, separate from motor/speech limitation.
- Distinguish documented manifest disease, positive family history and genetic risk; record what was actually established.
- Document gradual course, exclusions, behavioral symptoms and individual suicide assessment.
