# Major or Mild Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Cerebrovascular disease must be the dominant cause of the NCD. Look for onset **tied to stroke(s)**, **or** a decline led by **complex attention/processing speed and frontal-executive** function, with vascular disease on history, exam or imaging **sufficient** to explain it. It is the **only** major NCD that uses **F01.5x with no extra etiology code**. Incidental white-matter lesions are common in older adults, so do not blame them reflexively. (pp. 943–948)

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 943–944)
- **A.** Criteria are met for major or mild NCD.
- **B.** Features consistent with a vascular etiology, shown by **either**:
  1. Onset of deficits **temporally related to ≥1 cerebrovascular event**, or
  2. Decline prominent in **complex attention (incl. processing speed) and frontal-executive function**.
- **C.** Evidence of cerebrovascular disease from history, exam and/or neuroimaging **sufficient to account for** the deficits.
- **D.** Not better explained by another brain disease or systemic disorder.

**Probable** if ≥1 of:
1. Clinical criteria supported by **neuroimaging evidence of significant parenchymal injury** from cerebrovascular disease (neuroimaging-supported).
2. The syndrome is **temporally related to ≥1 documented cerebrovascular event**.
3. **Both clinical and genetic** evidence of cerebrovascular disease (e.g., **CADASIL**).

**Possible:** clinical criteria are met, but **no neuroimaging** is available **and** the temporal link to a cerebrovascular event is not established.

## Specifiers & severity
- Probable / possible.
- With / without behavioral disturbance: coded for major only.
- Severity (major): mild / moderate / severe. Recorded, not coded.
- **Clinical subtypes** (descriptive, not coded):
  1. Poststroke NCD (immediately after a stroke)
  2. Subcortical ischemic vascular NCD
  3. Multi-infarct (cortical) NCD
  4. Cortical-subcortical vascular NCD

## Coding (p. 944)
| Code | When |
|---|---|
| **F01.51** | Major NCD probably/possibly due to vascular disease, **with** behavioral disturbance |
| **F01.50** | Major, **without** behavioral disturbance |
| — | **No additional medical code** for the vascular disease |
| **G31.84** | Mild vascular NCD. No vascular code. Behavioural specifier recorded only |
| Additional | e.g., **F06.31** depressive disorder due to cerebrovascular disease, with depressive features |

**In multiple etiologies:** vascular disease is **not** coded first with the other medical conditions. Instead, **code also F01.5x** alongside F02.8x (see `ncd-due-to-multiple-etiologies.md`).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 944–945)
- Cerebrovascular disease must be the **dominant if not exclusive** pathology.
- Lesions range from large-vessel stroke to microvascular disease and can be focal, multifocal or diffuse, so presentations are **heterogeneous**.
- Mechanisms: hypoperfusion/hypoxia, oxidative stress and inflammation causing endothelial dysfunction, impaired autoregulation, and disrupted neurovascular coupling.

**Course patterns:**
- **Multi-infarct:** acute **stepwise or fluctuating** decline, with stable periods and even some improvement in between.
- **Small vessel disease** (white matter, basal ganglia, thalamus): **gradual onset and slow progression**, punctuated by small acute events that leave subtle deficits. Cortical-subcortical circuits are disrupted, affecting **processing speed** and **executive** function.
- Also: rapid onset followed by stability, or other complex patterns.

**Criterion C evidence:**
- **Clinical:** documented stroke temporally linked to decline, or stroke signs (**hemiparesis, pseudobulbar syndrome, visual field defect**).
- **Imaging (MRI preferred, or CT)**, any of:
  - ≥1 large-vessel infarct or haemorrhage
  - a **strategic single infarct/haemorrhage** (angular gyrus, thalamus, basal forebrain)
  - **≥2 lacunes outside the brainstem**
  - **extensive, confluent white matter lesions** (small vessel disease / subcortical ischaemic change)
- Without imaging, "silent" infarcts and WMLs can be missed. However, **well-documented stroke(s) temporally linked to the decline** allow a **probable** diagnosis without imaging.
- Research MRI targets: microbleeds, cortical microinfarcts, dilated perivascular spaces, diffusion/tract analyses.

**Lesion burden rule of thumb:**
- **Mild** vascular NCD: a single stroke or extensive WMD is generally enough.
- **Major** vascular NCD: generally needs **≥2 strokes, a strategic stroke, or WMD + ≥1 lacune**.
- The lesion–symptom link is imperfect, so clinical judgment is needed.

**Pointers away from vascular:**
- Early prominent memory deficit suggests AD.
- Early prominent parkinsonism suggests PD.
- Cognitive and depressive onset close together suggests depression-related cognitive impairment.

## Associated features (p. 945)
- History of stroke or **TIAs** and signs of infarction.
- Personality and mood changes, **abulia**, depression, **emotional lability**.
- **"Vascular depression":** late-onset depression with psychomotor slowing and executive dysfunction in progressive small-vessel disease.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors (pp. 945–947)
**Prevalence:**
- The **2nd most common cause of NCD** after AD.
- US vascular dementia: **0.98%** at 71–79, **4.09%** at 80–89, **6.19%** at ≥90.
- **20–30% are diagnosed with dementia within 3 months of a stroke.**
- European autopsy series (ages 60–103):
  - Pure vascular dementia **12.3%** (15.0% at 60–69 vs 8.7% at >90).
  - Mixed AD + vascular 5.5% (10.6% at >90 vs 5.2% at 60–69).
- Higher in **African Americans, Mexican Americans and South Asian Americans** than non-Latinx Whites, probably through diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
- In Japan and other Asian countries, AD has risen relative to vascular dementia. Among Japanese Americans, AD is 2.6× vascular dementia.
- Stroke is more common in men until 65, then in women. Vascular NCD rates are higher in men in some studies.

**Course:**
- Any age, rising **exponentially after 65**. In older patients, additional pathologies are almost always present.
- Ranges from acute onset with partial improvement, to stepwise decline, to progressive decline with fluctuations and plateaus.
- **Pure subcortical** vascular NCD can progress slowly and **mimic AD**.
- In the US, the risk of ischaemic stroke progressing to vascular NCD within 5 years is **~2× higher in African Americans** than in non-Latinx Whites, at younger ages. Likely contributors are hypertension, diabetes and adverse social determinants (limited education, low SES).

**Risk factors:**
- **Environmental (modifiers):** education, physical exercise and mental activity influence outcomes through neuroplasticity.
- **Genetic/physiological:**
  - The same as for stroke: **hypertension, diabetes, smoking, obesity, high cholesterol, high homocysteine**, athero- and arteriolosclerosis, **atrial fibrillation** and other embolic sources.
  - **Cerebral amyloid angiopathy** (amyloid in vessel walls causing haemorrhage).
  - **CADASIL** is a genetic cause. Overall, genetics contribute little.

## Diagnostic markers (p. 947)
- **Structural MRI/CT** is central. There are **no other established biomarkers**.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences (p. 947)
- Commonly accompanied by **physical deficits** that add disability.

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 947–948)
- **Other NCDs:** incidental infarcts and WMLs are common in older adults, so consider other causes.
  - **AD:** early memory deficit and progressive decline in memory, language, executive and perceptual-motor function **without matching focal lesions**. AD biomarkers (CSF Aβ/p-tau, amyloid/tau PET) may help.
  - **Lewy body NCD:** fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, spontaneous parkinsonism.
  - **FTD:** insidious, gradually progressive behavioural or language decline, which is not typical of vascular NCD.
- **Other medical conditions:** do not diagnose vascular NCD if a brain tumour, MS, encephalitis, or a toxic or metabolic disorder is severe enough to explain the deficits.
- **Delirium:** do not diagnose vascular NCD if symptoms are entirely due to delirium. A delirium superimposed on vascular NCD gets **both** diagnoses.
- **MDD:**
  - If MDD criteria are met and the cognitive impairment is **temporally tied to the onset of depression**, do **not** diagnose vascular NCD.
  - If the **NCD preceded** the depression, or the cognitive impairment is **out of proportion** to the depression, diagnose **depressive disorder due to cerebrovascular disease** instead of MDD.

## Comorbidity (p. 948)
- **Commonly co-occurs with AD**, and the text says **both diagnoses should be made**. In coding, this is captured through NCD due to multiple etiologies with F01.5x coded also.
- **Depression** frequently co-occurs.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion B:** either (1) "cognitive decline began abruptly after L MCA stroke 3/2025", or (2) a profile of slowed processing (prolonged trails, slow responses) and executive dysfunction (poor set-shifting, fluency), with **relatively better recognition memory** (retrieval rather than encoding deficit).
- **Criterion C:** list the vascular evidence:
  - Stroke/TIA history and dates.
  - Focal signs (hemiparesis, visual field cut, pseudobulbar affect, gait).
  - Imaging findings (number and location of infarcts, lacunes, confluent WMLs, strategic infarct).
  - Vascular risk factors (HTN, DM, AF, smoking, lipids).
- **Course:** stepwise vs gradual, and plateaus.
- **Certainty:** imaging-supported, documented event, or CADASIL → probable. No imaging and no clear event → possible.
- **Rule-outs:** AD pattern (early amnestic decline without focal lesions), Lewy body features, FTD, tumour/MS/encephalitis/metabolic causes, delirium, MDD timing (did depression come first?).
- **Associated:** abulia, emotional lability, late-onset "vascular depression".
- **Coding:** F01.50/F01.51 only (no I-code for major). Mild: G31.84. Add F06.31 if depressive disorder due to cerebrovascular disease is diagnosed.
- **Plan cues:** vascular risk control, rehab/PT for physical deficits, fall risk.
