# Neurocognitive Disorders

> **Spark note:** These disorders describe acquired cognitive change from an earlier level. Establish the syndrome, function and timeline before attributing a cause. Major versus mild NCD hinges on independent everyday functioning; delirium hinges on acute/fluctuating attention and awareness with a physiological cause. (pp. 904–905, 907–919)

## Core distinctions and differential logic
Both major and mild NCD require concern about a decline **and** objective cognitive evidence; neither a complaint nor an isolated low test score suffices. Interpret performance against baseline, education, language/cultural norms and sensory/motor limitations. Major NCD interferes with independence; mild NCD allows independent tasks with added effort/compensation. Typical test ranges are guides, not autonomous cutoffs. (pp. 916–919, 922–927)

| Etiology/presentation | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|
| Delirium | Hours/days onset and fluctuating attention/awareness; can overlay an NCD |
| Alzheimer | Gradual progression, usually amnestic; major requires ≥2 domains; certainty rules differ for major/mild |
| Frontotemporal | Early behavioral/social-executive or language change with relative early memory/perceptual-motor sparing |
| Lewy bodies | Fluctuating cognition, formed visual hallucinations, spontaneous parkinsonism; REM sleep behavior/neuroleptic sensitivity support attribution |
| Vascular | Sufficient vascular injury with stroke timing or executive/processing-speed prominence |
| TBI | Injury evidence plus cognitive onset immediately at injury/recovery of consciousness and persistence beyond the acute period |
| Substance/medication | Persistence beyond acute exposure effects and a compatible use/abstinence course |
| HIV | Established infection with attributable cognitive syndrome, excluding secondary CNS disease |
| Prion | Often rapidly progressive with motor findings or biomarker evidence |
| Parkinson | Established motor disease, gradual cognition; sequence and mixed causes determine certainty |
| Huntington | Progressive cognition with established disease or family/genetic risk; cognitive change may precede motor manifestation |
| Other/multiple medical | Evidence that the named process(es) physiologically contribute |

Source discussions: pp. 907–915, 928–978. Depression, delirium, developmental baseline differences, medications and mixed pathology can confound every attribution. Serial assessment is often needed. Parkinson/Lewy differential explicitly discusses differing expert conventions for cognition before/within the first motor-disease year; do not reduce it to an absolute one-year prohibition. (pp. 927–928, 942–943, 970–971)

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
Major NCD severity: mild = instrumental tasks affected; moderate = basic tasks affected; severe = complete dependence. These are distinct from the mild-NCD syndrome or underlying injury/disease grade. Record behavioral symptoms specifically, including psychosis, agitation, mood disturbance or apathy. (pp. 917, 921–922)

In this edition, most medically attributed major NCD uses its medical cause first, then F02.80/F02.81 for without/with behavioral disturbance. Vascular major NCD uses F01.50/F01.51 without an additional vascular medical code. Mild medical NCD uses G31.84 without an added presumed etiology code; unspecified major/mild uses R41.9. Substance-induced NCD has its own table and combination-code rules. Multiple etiologies have special sequencing and separate vascular/substance components. These reproduce source-edition conventions; applicable billing codes require separate verification. (pp. 919–920, 955–957, 977–978)

## Notable content in this edition
The chapter describes biomarker developments, contextualized cognitive testing, behavioral symptoms that may precede cognitive complaints, suicide associations and disparities in disease risk/access. These notes retain the edition's evidence context; no formal comparison against an earlier edition was performed. (pp. 924–927, 929–934, 942, 964, 967)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
Record prior function, who notices change, affected domains and quantified assessment. Describe cognitive independence separately from motor/sensory or psychiatric disability. State onset/course and positive evidence for each etiological contribution, delirium assessment and alternative explanations, behavioral disturbance and actual suicide assessment. Distinguish confirmed evidence from presumed attribution and plan reassessment when uncertain.
