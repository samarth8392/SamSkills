# Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Multiple Etiologies

> **Spark note:** Multiple diagnoses in the medical history do not automatically establish mixed NCD. Each contributing process needs evidence of causation. Substances are excluded from the medical-etiology criterion and receive their own substance-induced NCD code when contributing. (pp. 977–978)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Major/mild NCD. **B:** History, examination or laboratory findings support more than one physiological etiological process, excluding substances from this particular criterion; use each specific NCD's criteria to assess attribution. **C:** Not better explained by another mental disorder and not limited to delirium. (p. 977)
## Specifiers & severity
Establish the [major](major-neurocognitive-disorder.md) or [mild](mild-neurocognitive-disorder.md) syndrome first: acquired decline, concern plus objective assessment, functional threshold, and exclusion of delirium-only or another mental disorder. Major NCD severity describes cognitive effects on function: mild = instrumental tasks affected; moderate = basic self-care affected; severe = complete dependence. Record clinically significant behavioral symptoms individually with the with/without behavioral disturbance specifier. These are separate from the severity of the underlying disease. (pp. 916–920)

## Coding
Edition-specific sequencing from pp. 919–920, 977–978:

| Component | Major NCD | Mild NCD |
|---|---|---|
| Medical etiologies other than vascular | List each causative medical code first | Do not add presumed etiology codes |
| Cognitive syndrome | One F02.80 (without behavioral disturbance) or F02.81 (with it) for the combined medical etiologies | G31.84 |
| Vascular contribution | Also F01.50 or F01.51; no separate vascular medical code | Included in G31.84 |
| Substance/medication contribution | Also the relevant substance-induced major NCD code | Also the relevant substance-induced mild NCD code |

Record major severity and behavioral details in words. Additional psychiatric disorders attributed to the same causes may be coded as appropriate.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
The category captures probable contributions from several medical diseases, such as Alzheimer disease plus vascular injury. Assess the extent, timing and characteristic cognitive profile of each cause. Incidental abnormalities alone do not prove a mixed etiology. (pp. 934, 947, 977–978)

## Differential diagnosis
Distinguish several causal processes from a single dominant etiology with incidental comorbid illnesses. Delirium and depression can coexist and must not be used as an unexamined explanation for all deficits. (pp. 927–928, 977)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Map each suspected cause to specific evidence and relevant subtype criteria; state uncertainty explicitly.
- Record the general NCD evidence, functional severity, and actual behavioral disturbances.
- Separate medical, vascular and substance contributions to support the source's coding sequence.
