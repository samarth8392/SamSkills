# Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder With Lewy Bodies

> **Spark note:** The key features are **fluctuating** cognition and alertness (it can look like a delirium), **well-formed visual hallucinations**, and **parkinsonism that comes after** the cognitive decline, with **REM sleep behavior disorder** often present years earlier. Early deficits are attention, executive and **visuoperceptual**, not memory. The safety point: **up to 50% have severe neuroleptic sensitivity**, so antipsychotics need extreme caution. (pp. 939–943)

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 939–940)
- **A.** Criteria are met for major or mild NCD.
- **B.** Insidious onset and gradual progression.
- **C.** A combination of core and suggestive features:
  - **Core features:**
    - (a) **fluctuating cognition**, with pronounced variation in attention and alertness
    - (b) **recurrent, well-formed, detailed visual hallucinations**
    - (c) **spontaneous parkinsonism**, starting **after** cognitive decline has developed
  - **Suggestive features:**
    - (a) meets criteria for **REM sleep behavior disorder**
    - (b) **severe neuroleptic sensitivity**
  - **Probable:** **2 core**, **or 1 suggestive + ≥1 core**.
  - **Possible:** **only 1 core**, **or ≥1 suggestive** (with no core).
- **D.** Not better explained by cerebrovascular disease, another neurodegenerative disease, a substance, or another mental, neurological or systemic disorder.

## Specifiers & severity
- Probable / possible.
- With / without behavioral disturbance: coded for major only.
- Severity (major): mild / moderate / severe. Recorded, not coded.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 940–941)
- **Major NCDLB = dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB).**
- Early changes are in **attention, executive function and visuoperceptual ability**, not learning and memory.
- Other features:
  - Complex visual hallucinations.
  - **RBD**, which can be a very early sign.
  - Hallucinations in other modalities.
  - Apathy, anxiety, depression, delusions.
- **Fluctuations can resemble delirium**, with or without an adequate precipitant.
  - The variable presentation means a brief visit can miss features, so **caregiver observation** and **fluctuation scales** help.
- **Parkinsonism:**
  - Often mild, with a variable levodopa response.
  - **Up to 25% of probable NCDLB never develop extrapyramidal signs**, and these signs are not required.
  - Must be distinguished from **neuroleptic-induced** EPS.
- **Neuroleptic sensitivity:** up to **50%** have severe sensitivity, so use antipsychotics with **extreme caution** if NCDLB is even suspected.
- **Mild NCDLB:** appropriate when core features are present but impairment is below the major threshold, especially with prominent **nonamnestic** deficits. Evidence for a single etiology is often insufficient, so **unspecified NCD** may fit better.

## Associated features (p. 941)
- **Repeated falls, syncope, transient episodes of unresponsiveness.**
- **Autonomic dysfunction:** orthostatic hypotension, constipation, urinary incontinence.
- **Hypersomnia and hyposmia.**

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors (pp. 941–942)
**Prevalence:**
- 0–1.2% of the elderly population; 0–9.7% of dementia cases.
- Mean **4.2% of dementias in the community** and **7.5% in clinics**. Not significantly affected by age or sex.
- **Lewy body pathology is found in 20–35% of dementia cases** (US/UK).
- In a Minnesota records study, incidence was **~3× higher in men** aged ≥65.

**Course:**
- Insidious and gradual, but often with a **prodrome of acute confusional episodes (delirium)** triggered by illness or surgery.
- **NCDLB vs PD-NCD:** Lewy bodies in NCDLB are mainly **limbic** (± neocortical); PD starts in the **brainstem**. The order in which cognitive and motor symptoms emerge separates them, and in NCDLB cognitive decline comes **early**.
- Onset at **50–89**, most in the **mid-70s**. Occasional plateaus, then progression through severe dementia to death.
- **Survival 5.5–7.7 years** from cognitive onset.

**Risk:**
- Familial aggregation can occur and several risk genes are known, but **most cases have no family history**.
- Genetic risk appears as important as in AD or PD.

## Diagnostic markers (p. 942)
**Indicative biomarkers** (weight equivalent to core features):
- **Low striatal dopamine transporter uptake** on SPECT/PET (DaT scan).
- **Low uptake on MIBG myocardial scintigraphy** (cardiac sympathetic denervation).
- **Polysomnography-confirmed REM sleep without atonia.** RBD can also be identified by questioning the patient or informant.

**Supportive biomarkers** (more limited evidence):
- Relative **preservation of medial temporal lobe volume** versus AD on MRI.
- Generalized low perfusion/metabolism with **reduced occipital activity** ± the **cingulate island sign** on FDG-PET.
- Prominent EEG slow-wave activity with periodic fluctuations in the pre-alpha/theta range.

**Other:**
- Pathology: **α-synuclein** misfolding and aggregation, confirmed postmortem.
- Neuropsychological testing beyond brief screens is often needed.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences (p. 942)
- **More functionally impaired than the cognitive deficits would predict**, compared with AD, because motor and autonomic problems affect toileting, transfers and eating.
- Sleep disorders and psychiatric symptoms add to the impairment.
- **Quality of life is often worse than in AD.**

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 942–943)
- **NCD due to Parkinson's disease:** decided by the timing and sequence of motor and cognitive symptoms.
  - DLB consensus criteria: to attribute dementia to PD, **PD must be diagnosed ≥1 year before** cognition reaches the major NCD level. In NCDLB, cognition may begin before, with, or without parkinsonism.
  - PD consensus criteria allow PD to be diagnosed even if cognitive decline came first.
  - So when major NCD starts **before or within 12 months of PD**, the clinician chooses between NCDLB and PD-NCD.
  - If PD was diagnosed **≥1 year before cognitive symptoms**, both sets of criteria agree on **NCD due to PD**.
  - At the **mild** level, when the sequence is unclear, use **unspecified NCD** until the course clarifies.
- **Delirium:** fluctuation mimics delirium. Look for a medical precipitant, acute onset, and a progressive course with the other core features.
- **AD:** early memory deficits, hallucinations and parkinsonism uncommon early, medial temporal atrophy.
- **Neuroleptic-induced parkinsonism:** must be distinguished from spontaneous parkinsonism.

## Comorbidity (p. 943)
- Lewy pathology often coexists with **AD, TDP-43 pathology and cerebrovascular disease**, especially in the oldest patients. TDP-43 proteinopathy is also seen in ALS and FTD.
- Mixed pathology predicts **faster decline and shorter survival**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Fluctuation (core a):** describe the variation, e.g., "per daughter, alternates between near-baseline days and days of staring spells, daytime drowsiness >2 h, disorganized speech." Name any fluctuation scale used.
- **Visual hallucinations (core b):** recurrent, well-formed and detailed (e.g., "sees children/animals in the living room"). Note insight and distress.
- **Parkinsonism (core c):** rigidity, bradykinesia, rest tremor, gait. **Document onset relative to cognitive decline** and whether the patient had prior antipsychotic exposure.
- **Suggestive:** RBD history (dream enactment, punching or kicking in sleep, bed-partner report, PSG); any severe reaction to antipsychotics.
- **Certainty count:** e.g., "2 core features → probable."
- **Biomarkers:** DaT scan, MIBG, PSG, if done.
- **Associated:** falls, syncope, orthostatic BP readings, constipation/incontinence, anosmia.
- **Rule-outs:** delirium (labs, medications, infection), PD timing (the **1-year rule**), AD, vascular, drug-induced parkinsonism.
- **Treatment-relevant flag:** "suspected NCDLB, avoid or use extreme caution with antipsychotics (neuroleptic sensitivity)."
- **Coding:** G31.83 → F02.80/81 (major); G31.84 (mild). F06.0 if psychotic disorder due to Lewy body disease is diagnosed.
