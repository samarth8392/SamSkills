# Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Parkinson's Disease

> **Spark note:** Establish Parkinson's disease and then characterize the cognitive syndrome and its chronology. Probable attribution requires both clear motor-disease precedence and absence of mixed causes; possible attribution requires only one of these. The source discusses differing expert conventions rather than an exceptionless one-year rule. (pp. 968–971)

## Criteria at a glance
**A:** Major/mild NCD. **B:** Established Parkinson's disease. **C:** Insidious onset and gradual progression. **D:** No better medical or mental explanation.

**Probable:** both (1) no competing contributing etiology and (2) Parkinson's disease clearly began before NCD. **Possible:** one, but not both, is established. (pp. 968–969)
## Specifiers & severity
Establish the [major](major-neurocognitive-disorder.md) or [mild](mild-neurocognitive-disorder.md) syndrome first: acquired decline, concern plus objective assessment, functional threshold, and exclusion of delirium-only or another mental disorder. Major NCD severity describes cognitive effects on function: mild = instrumental tasks affected; moderate = basic self-care affected; severe = complete dependence. Record clinically significant behavioral symptoms individually with the with/without behavioral disturbance specifier. These are separate from the severity of the underlying disease. (pp. 916–920)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Early deficits often involve attention, executive function, processing speed, memory and visuospatial ability, with relative preservation of many language skills. Use tests that minimize demands on hand movement or speed, so motor slowing is not mislabeled cognitive decline. Dopamine-transporter imaging may distinguish Lewy-related disease from some alternatives; it does not independently separate Parkinson NCD from Lewy-body NCD. (pp. 969–970)

## Associated features
Apathy, depression/anxiety, hallucinations/delusions, REM sleep behavior disorder, daytime sleepiness, falls, gait/postural instability, autonomic changes and reduced smell may accompany cognition. (p. 969)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Up to 80% with Parkinson's may eventually develop major NCD; about 25%–27% without major NCD have mild NCD in cited studies. Disease typically begins in later adulthood; mild cognitive change may develop earlier in the disease course than major impairment. Older onset, more severe disease, gait/autonomic disturbance, and REM sleep behavior disorder increase risk. GBA mutations and APOE*E4 are associated with poorer cognition. (pp. 969–970)

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
Parkinson disease and associated NCD prevalence are greater among men, but incidence differences for NCD are unclear. The text also discusses Guam's parkinsonism-dementia/ALS complex as a distinct cultural/geographic consideration. (pp. 969–970)

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed in this etiological section.

## Functional consequences
Motor limitations, mood/psychotic symptoms, sleep disturbance and apathy add to cognitive disability; assess their separate contributions. (p. 971)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Lewy-body NCD:** DLB consensus favors Parkinson attribution when motor diagnosis precedes major NCD by ≥1 year. Parkinson expert criteria allow attribution even when cognition starts before motor diagnosis. The DSM text allows clinical judgment before/within the first year; uncertain mild cases may need unspecified NCD pending longitudinal clarity.
- **Alzheimer's:** early prominent amnestic pattern; can coexist with Parkinson disease.
- **Vascular NCD:** vascular chronology/imaging and insufficient features for idiopathic Parkinson disease help distinguish it.
- **Other brain disease:** assess progressive supranuclear palsy, corticobasal degeneration, multiple system atrophy, hydrocephalus and tumors.
- **Drug-induced parkinsonism:** consider dopamine-blocking medication exposure in a person with another NCD. (pp. 970–971)

## Comorbidity
Alzheimer and cerebrovascular pathology are common in older patients; evidence of mixed causes does not erase a Parkinson contribution. (p. 971)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record evidence for established Parkinson disease and dates of motor diagnosis, cognitive change and loss of independence.
- Specify probable/possible with the two supporting conditions, domain testing adapted for motor limitations, and independent-function evidence.
- Record other etiologies, relevant medication effects, behavioral symptoms, and separate motor versus cognitive disability.
