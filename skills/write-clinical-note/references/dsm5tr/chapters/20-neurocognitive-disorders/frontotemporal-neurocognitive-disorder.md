# Major or Mild Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder

> **Spark note:** A progressive NCD of **behaviour/personality** (behavioral variant) or **language** (language variant), usually starting in the 50s, with **memory and perceptual-motor function relatively spared** early on. Patients often see a **psychiatrist first** and get a misdiagnosis of depression, bipolar disorder or schizophrenia, because insight is poor and early cognitive tests can look nearly normal. (pp. 934–939)

## Criteria at a glance (p. 935)
- **A.** Criteria are met for major or mild NCD.
- **B.** Insidious onset and gradual progression.
- **C.** Either (1) or (2):
  1. **Behavioral variant:**
     - a. **≥3 of 5:**
       - (i) behavioural disinhibition
       - (ii) apathy or inertia
       - (iii) loss of sympathy or empathy
       - (iv) perseverative, stereotyped or compulsive/ritualistic behaviour
       - (v) hyperorality and dietary changes
     - **plus** b. **prominent decline in social cognition and/or executive abilities.**
  2. **Language variant:** a. prominent decline in language: speech production, word finding, object naming, grammar or word comprehension.
- **D.** **Relative sparing of learning and memory and perceptual-motor function.**
- **E.** Not better explained by cerebrovascular disease, another neurodegenerative disease, a substance, or another mental, neurological or systemic disorder.

**Probable vs possible:**
- **Probable** if either: (1) a causative FTD mutation (family history or genetic testing), **or** (2) disproportionate **frontal and/or temporal** involvement on neuroimaging.
- **Possible** if there is no evidence of a mutation **and** neuroimaging has not been done.

## Specifiers & severity
- Variant: behavioral or language (many patients have features of both).
- Probable / possible.
- With / without behavioral disturbance: coded only for major NCD.
- Severity (major NCD): mild / moderate / severe. Recorded, not coded.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 936–937)
- **Syndromic variants:** behavioural, plus two language variants (**semantic** and **agrammatic/nonfluent**), each with distinct atrophy and pathology.
- **Behavioral variant:**
  - Apathy (lost interest in socializing, self-care and responsibilities) and/or disinhibition (socially inappropriate behaviour).
  - **Insight impaired**, which delays consultation. **First referral is often psychiatric.**
  - Changes in social style and in **religious/political beliefs**; repetitive movements, **hoarding**, eating changes, **hyperorality**.
  - Late: loss of sphincter control.
  - Cognitive decline is less prominent, and early formal testing may show **few deficits**. Typical deficits are poor planning/organization, distractibility, poor judgment, and impaired mental flexibility, abstract reasoning and response inhibition.
  - Memory relatively spared. Perceptual-motor **almost always preserved early**.
- **Language variant:** gradual-onset **primary progressive aphasia**, semantic or agrammatic/nonfluent.
  - **Logopenic** progressive aphasia (left temporoparietal) is often caused by **AD pathology**. Do not assume FTD.
- **Probable status:** comes from genetics (e.g., MAPT) or distinctive frontotemporal atrophy or hypoactivity on structural or functional imaging.

## Associated features (p. 937)
- Extrapyramidal features in some patients (overlap with **PSP, corticobasal degeneration**).
- **Motor neuron disease** features (atrophy, weakness).
- A subset develop **visual hallucinations**.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors (p. 937)
**Prevalence:**
- A common cause of **early-onset (<65)** NCD. Population prevalence is **2–31 per 100,000**, generally equal in men and women.
- **20–25%** of cases occur after 65.
- About **5%** of dementia in unselected autopsy series.
- **Behavioral variant ≈60%** of cases.

**Course:**
- Onset usually in the **50s** (range 20s–80s). Gradual progression.
- **Median survival 6–11 yrs from symptom onset, 3–4 yrs from diagnosis.** Shorter survival and faster decline than typical AD.

**Risk:**
- **~40%** have a family history of early-onset NCD. **~10%** are autosomal dominant.
- Genes: **MAPT, GRN, C9orf72**. Many familial cases have no known mutation.
- **Motor neuron disease predicts faster deterioration.**

## Diagnostic markers (pp. 937–938)
**CT/MRI atrophy by variant:**
- **Behavioral:** bilateral frontal (especially **medial frontal**) + **anterior temporal**.
- **Semantic:** middle, inferior and anterior temporal lobes, bilateral but asymmetric, **left > right**.
- **Nonfluent:** **left posterior frontal-insular**.

**Other markers:**
- Functional imaging shows hypoperfusion or hypometabolism in the same regions, **possibly before structural change**.
- AD biomarkers (CSF Aβ/tau, amyloid PET) help, but separating FTD from AD can remain hard.
- Familial genes: MAPT, GRN, C9ORF72, TDP-43 (TARDBP), VCP, CHMP2B, FUS.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences (p. 938)
- Early onset disrupts **work and family**.
- Function is impaired relatively early because language and behaviour are involved.
- Behavioral variant: family disruption, **legal involvement** and workplace problems before the diagnosis is clear.
- Behavioural/language impairment (hyperorality, impulsive wandering, disinhibition) can **far exceed** the cognitive impairment and lead to nursing home placement. Disruptive behaviour persists even in structured care, especially in physically healthy, nonfrail patients.

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 938–939)
- **AD:** early learning/memory decline. However, **10–30%** of clinically suspected FTD shows AD pathology at autopsy, more often with a dysexecutive syndrome without behaviour or movement changes, or with the **logopenic** variant.
- **Lewy body NCD:** core and suggestive Lewy features are required.
- **Parkinson's NCD:** spontaneous parkinsonism comes **well before** cognitive decline.
- **Vascular NCD:** can cause executive loss and apathy, but a **temporally related cerebrovascular event** and imaging lesions sufficient to explain the picture point to vascular.
- **PSP:** supranuclear gaze palsy, axial parkinsonism, pseudobulbar signs, **retropulsion** (backward falls). Cognition shows psychomotor slowing, poor working memory and executive dysfunction.
- **Corticobasal degeneration:** asymmetric rigidity, limb apraxia, postural instability, myoclonus, **alien limb**, cortical sensory loss.
- **Motor neuron disease:** many bvFTD patients have mixed upper and predominantly **lower** MND features.
- **Primary mental disorders (MDD, bipolar, schizophrenia):** progressive neurocognitive decline over time separates them.
  - Do a medical workup for treatable causes (metabolic, nutritional, infectious).
  - If psychiatric symptoms (e.g., delusions) are due to FTD, diagnose e.g. **psychotic disorder due to frontotemporal degeneration, with delusions**, not a primary psychotic disorder.

## Comorbidity
Not specifically addressed beyond overlap with PSP, CBD and MND.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Behavioral variant:** count and document **≥3 of 5** with concrete examples:
  - Disinhibition: "made sexual comments to strangers; shoplifted."
  - Apathy/inertia: "stopped bathing, sits all day."
  - Loss of empathy: "indifferent to wife's illness."
  - Perseverative/compulsive: "repeatedly hums/claps; rigid routines; hoarding."
  - Hyperorality/diet change: "binges sweets, puts non-food items in mouth."
  - Add **social cognition/executive** evidence (failed theory-of-mind or emotion-recognition tasks, poor planning, impulsivity).
- **Language variant:** naming errors, loss of word meaning (semantic), effortful agrammatic speech (nonfluent), comprehension of single words.
- **Criterion D:** document relatively preserved memory (orientation, recall) and visuospatial function (copy and navigation intact).
- **Insight:** document poor awareness and lean on collateral history.
- **Certainty:** genetics/family history (autosomal dominant early-onset dementia, ALS in relatives) and imaging (frontal/anterior temporal atrophy, hypometabolism). If neither is available, "possible".
- **Rule-outs:** primary mood/psychotic disorder (age of onset, progression, absence of prior episodes), AD (memory profile, biomarkers), vascular events, PSP/CBD motor signs, metabolic/nutritional/infectious causes.
- **Coding:** G31.09 → F02.80/81 (major); G31.84 (mild). Consider F07.0 personality change (disinhibited) or F06.33 when diagnosed.
- **Safety/legal:** financial or sexual disinhibition, driving, workplace issues, caregiver burden.
