# Substance/Medication-Induced Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder

> **Spark note:** Persistent cognition after intoxication/acute withdrawal is a different diagnosis from an acute drug effect. Demonstrate the general NCD syndrome, an agent/exposure capable of causing it and a compatible abstinence course; a history of substance use alone does not prove causation. (pp. 955–960)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** Major or mild NCD criteria are met.
- **B:** Deficits are not restricted to delirium and outlast the usual intoxication/acute withdrawal interval.
- **C:** Agent plus exposure extent/duration can produce the impairment.
- **D:** Cognitive course fits substance use and abstinence, such as stabilization/improvement after abstinence.
- **E:** No better medical or mental explanation. (p. 955)

## Specifiers & severity
**Persistent:** important impairment continues after an extended abstinent period; no universal month threshold is specified. Record behavioral disturbance and, for major NCD, functional severity in words; these do not change the substance code. Alcohol-induced major NCD distinguishes **amnestic-confabulatory** from **nonamnestic-confabulatory** type. The prominent new-learning deficit of the former can occur with confabulation and Wernicke-associated neurological features. (pp. 955–957)

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
Alcohol commonly affects executive function and learning/memory; sedatives may disproportionately affect memory. Inhalants can impair speed/executive function. Stimulant effects may resemble vascular profiles and may accompany vascular injury. Deficits preceding substance use must not be misattributed to it. The source explicitly describes limited prospective evidence for persistent drug-caused NCD beyond alcohol, other depressants and inhalants. (pp. 957–958)

## Associated features
Mood/sleep symptoms, apathy, motor slowing, incoordination/ataxia or systemic disease can compound cognition. Workplace solvent exposure can cause impairment without a use disorder. (p. 958)

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Prevalence is incompletely known. About 30%–40% with alcohol use disorder have mild NCD during the first two abstinent months in cited data; this does not predict permanent impairment. Recovery over months can be substantial. Older age, longer exposure, persistence after age 50, nutrition deficits, liver disease and vascular factors can worsen outcome. Imaging changes and cognition can improve with abstinence; either can be abnormal without the other. (pp. 958–960)

## Suicide risk
No separate quantitative NCD-specific estimate is supplied here; evaluate the associated substance/psychiatric syndromes individually.

## Functional consequences
Cognitive inefficiency and poor concentration can impair independent tasks; motor effects add disability. Separate cognitive from systemic/motor contributions. (p. 960)

## Differential diagnosis
TBI, HIV, hepatitis C, syphilis and other medical etiologies can accompany substance use. Psychiatric/neurodevelopmental comorbidities may contribute independently. Stimulant-related vascular injury may require vascular NCD assessment in addition to substance-related attribution. Acute intoxication, withdrawal and delirium are excluded as the sole context of the NCD. (pp. 955, 960)

## Comorbidity
Other substance syndromes, PTSD, psychotic/mood and neurodevelopmental disorders, TBI, vascular disease and systemic organ damage can coexist. (p. 960)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Document cognitive baseline, concern plus quantified impairment, domains affected and independence.
- Name agent, route, amount/duration and timing of acute recovery and serial abstinent assessments.
- Record evidence exposure can cause the observed pattern, alternative causes and the limits of causal confidence.
- Keep NCD level/severity separate from SUD criterion count; state persistent status only with supported abstinence/course evidence.
