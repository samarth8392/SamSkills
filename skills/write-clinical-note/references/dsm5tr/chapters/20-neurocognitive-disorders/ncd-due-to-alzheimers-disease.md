# Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Alzheimer's Disease

> **Spark note:** The prototypical NCD is an insidious, steadily progressive decline that **starts with learning and memory**. Two things trip people up. First, **major** NCD due to AD needs **≥2 domains** impaired, and "probable" in **major** NCD can rest on a typical clinical picture alone. Second, **mild** NCD can only be "probable" with a **causative genetic mutation**, so without one it is "possible" at best. Neuropsychiatric symptoms appear in nearly all patients (98% at 5 years) and often come **before** the memory loss. (pp. 928–934)

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 928–929)
- **A.** Criteria are met for major or mild NCD.
- **B.** **Insidious onset and gradual progression** in ≥1 domain. **Major NCD needs ≥2 domains.**
- **C. Probable vs possible:**

| | **Probable AD** if either: | **Possible AD** |
|---|---|---|
| **Major NCD** | (1) Causative AD mutation, from family history or genetic testing; **or** (2) **all 3**: (a) clear decline in **memory/learning + ≥1 other domain** (detailed history or serial testing); (b) steady, gradual progression **without extended plateaus**; (c) **no evidence of mixed etiology** (no other neurodegenerative, cerebrovascular, neurological, mental or systemic contributor) | When neither (1) nor (2) is met |
| **Mild NCD** | Only with a **causative AD mutation** (genetic testing or family history) | No mutation **and** all 3: (1) clear decline in memory/learning; (2) steady, gradual progression without extended plateaus; (3) no evidence of mixed etiology |

- **D.** Not better explained by cerebrovascular disease, another neurodegenerative disease, a substance, or another mental, neurological or systemic disorder.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Probable / possible** (certainty level; required).
- **With / without behavioral disturbance:** coded for major NCD, recorded only for mild NCD.
- **Severity (major only):** mild (IADLs), moderate (basic ADLs), severe (fully dependent). Recorded, **not** coded.

## Coding (p. 929)
| Code | When |
|---|---|
| **G30.9** then **F02.81** | Major NCD due to probable/possible AD **with** behavioral disturbance (G30.9 Alzheimer's disease coded first) |
| **G30.9** then **F02.80** | Major NCD due to probable/possible AD **without** behavioral disturbance |
| **G31.84** | Mild NCD due to AD. **No** G30.9. Behavioural specifier recorded, not coded |
| F06.2 / F06.32 etc. | Add for clinically significant psychiatric syndromes due to AD (e.g., F06.2 psychotic disorder due to Alzheimer's disease, with delusions; F06.32 depressive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease, with major depressive–like episode) |

Example: *G30.9 Alzheimer's disease; F02.80 major neurocognitive disorder due to probable Alzheimer's disease, without behavioral disturbance, mild.*

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 929–930)
- The typical presentation is **amnestic**. **Nonamnestic variants** exist: **visuospatial** (posterior) and **logopenic aphasic**.
- **Probably more than half** first show behavioural symptoms **before** cognitive ones. Record them with the specifier.
- **Mild stage:** memory/learning ± executive deficits.
- **Major stage:** visuoconstructional/perceptual-motor and language (word retrieval) decline join in, especially at moderate–severe levels.
- **Social cognition is preserved until late**, except in uncommon dysexecutive/behavioural variants.
- **Certainty:**
  - "Probable" is the highest certainty in the DSM framework.
  - A causative gene means a positive genetic test, or an autosomal dominant family history plus autopsy confirmation or a genetic test in an affected relative.
  - **Biomarkers increasingly raise certainty**: amyloid/tau PET or CSF.
  - Some people show long periods of very slow progression.
  - In mild NCD, the typical picture supports only "possible", because progression is less certain. Biomarkers may change this.
  - **If the etiology looks mixed**, diagnose **NCD due to multiple etiologies**.

## Associated features (pp. 930–931)
- Neuropsychiatric symptoms (behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia, BPSD): agitation, apathy, depression, delusions, sleep disorders.
  - Nearly universal: **98%** develop them within 5 years in one US population sample.
  - They drive disability, poorer quality of life, ADL loss, **faster decline**, caregiver burden, earlier institutionalization and higher mortality.
  - They are often more distressing than the cognitive symptoms and are frequently why care is sought.
- **Staging of symptoms:**
  - Mild NCD / mildest major NCD: depression, irritability, apathy.
  - Moderately severe: delusions, agitation, combativeness, wandering.
  - Late: gait disturbance, dysphagia, incontinence, myoclonus, seizures.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors (pp. 930–932)
**Prevalence:**
- High-income countries: 5–10% at ages 60–69, then **≥25%** after that.
- US 2016: **5.4 million** Americans, ~200,000 with onset before 65.
- 11% of people aged ≥65 and **32% of people aged ≥85**. 81% of people with the disease are aged ≥75.
- AD accounts for **~60% to >90%** of dementias, depending on setting and criteria. Mild NCD due to AD is likely a large share of MCI.
- US prevalence at ≥65 ranges 3.5–14.4% by ethnoracial group. It is higher in African Americans and Caribbean-origin Latinx after adjustment.

**Course:**
- Gradual decline, sometimes with plateaus, through severe dementia to death.
- **Mean survival ~10 years** after diagnosis, reflecting the patients' age; some live up to 20 years.
- Late stage: mute and bedbound. **Aspiration** is the most common cause of death.
- In mild NCD due to AD, impairment and function worsen until the major threshold is reached.

**Onset:**
- Usually **70–89**. Early onset at 40–59 is often (not always) mutation-related.
- Symptoms and pathology differ little by age of onset. Younger patients more often survive the full course; older patients have more comorbidity and mixed pathology.
- US Latinx may have onset **up to 4 years earlier** than non-Latinx Whites. African Americans show **slower decline**. Both groups may survive longer.

**Risk:**
- **Modifiable:** low education; midlife hypertension, obesity and **hearing loss**; late-life smoking, depression, physical inactivity, social isolation and diabetes. Multiple vascular risk factors add risk.
- **TBI** may raise risk, especially in men (controversial).
- **Age** is the strongest risk factor. **Heritability is 60–80% of attributable risk.**
  - Rare autosomal dominant mutations on **chromosomes 1, 14 and 21**.
  - **Down syndrome** (trisomy 21): AD can develop by midlife.
  - Polygenic: >45 loci.
  - **APOE*E4** raises risk and lowers onset age, most in homozygotes, though some homozygotes never develop symptoms. The association is not consistent across ethnoracial groups.
  - PSEN1 Gly206Ala in Puerto Rican descent (early onset). **ABCA7** association is stronger in African Americans.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 932)
- **Culture:** harder to detect where memory loss is seen as normal in old age, where daily cognitive demands are low, or where very low education complicates testing.
- **Sex/gender:**
  - Several European studies found higher incidence in women. Most North American studies found similar incidence.
  - Some studies suggest faster progression in women.
  - Women's verbal memory advantage may distort cut-offs, so **sex-specific cut-offs** may help when assessing MCI.

## Diagnostic markers (pp. 932–933)
- **Pathology:** amyloid neuritic plaques, tau neurofibrillary tangles, neuronal loss and regional atrophy (hippocampal, parietal, frontal). Confirmed postmortem.
- **Genes:** **APP, PSEN1, PSEN2** in early-onset autosomal dominant cases. Testing is available but usually has little clinical utility. **APOE*E4 is a risk factor, not a diagnostic marker** (neither necessary nor sufficient).
- **Amyloid:** amyloid PET; **low CSF Aβ42**.
- **Tau:** tau PET; **raised CSF total tau / phospho-tau**.
- **Neuronal injury** (less specific): hippocampal/temporoparietal atrophy on MRI, temporoparietal hypometabolism on FDG-PET.
- Blood biomarkers are emerging.

## Suicide risk (p. 933)
- **Moderate suicide risk, persisting years after diagnosis.** Assess mood and suicidality on an ongoing basis.
- In Denmark, hospital-diagnosed dementia carried **3- to 8-fold** suicide risk. Other studies are mixed.
- Impaired decision-making and reduced cognitive inhibition are linked to late-life suicidal behaviour.

## Functional consequences (p. 933)
- Early: memory loss, disorientation and mood symptoms threaten independence and safety (**driving**). Early-onset cases may force early retirement.
- Then progressive IADL → ADL loss → full dependence.
- **Caregivers** lose social networks and develop health and mental health problems, which harms outcomes for both.

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 933–934)
- **Other neurodegenerative NCDs:** also insidious and gradual, but with distinct core features.
  - **Lewy body NCD:** early marked cognitive fluctuation, parkinsonism, gait imbalance, visual hallucinations.
  - **FTD:** behavioural variant (disinhibition, apathy, perseveration, often first diagnosed as psychiatric) or language variant.
- **Vascular NCD:** stroke temporally linked to onset, with infarcts/hemosiderin on imaging sufficient to explain the picture. AD pathology often coexists.
  - **White matter change alone is not enough** to call mixed etiology when the rest supports AD.
  - Interpret subcortical ischaemic change cautiously.
- **Other neurological or systemic illness:** consider when the timing and severity fit. At the mild level, AD can be hard to separate from **thyroid disease or B12 deficiency**.
- **Major depression** (especially at the mild level): low function and poor concentration can mimic NCD, and improvement with treatment helps tell them apart. If MDE-level symptoms are due to AD, diagnose **depressive disorder due to AD, with major depressive–like episode**, not MDD.

## Comorbidity (p. 934)
- Mostly elderly patients with multiple medical conditions.
- Commonly co-occurs with **cerebrovascular disease**. If a comorbid condition contributes, diagnose **NCD due to multiple etiologies**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Onset/course (B):** "gradual onset ~3 yrs ago per spouse, steadily worsening without plateaus or stepwise drops."
- **Domains:** memory/learning first, e.g., poor delayed recall **not helped by cues** (encoding deficit), repetitive questioning. For major NCD, document **≥2 domains** (e.g., + word-finding, + clock-drawing/visuospatial).
- **Certainty (C):** state probable or possible and why:
  - Family history or genetics (autosomal dominant pattern, known PSEN1/APP/PSEN2).
  - Typical progressive course with no mixed etiology (major).
  - Supporting biomarkers (amyloid/tau PET, CSF).
- **Rule-outs (D):** imaging for stroke/vascular burden; TSH, B12 and other reversible causes; medication/substance review; depression screen; features of Lewy body (fluctuation, VH, RBD, parkinsonism) and FTD (early disinhibition, aphasia).
- **Behavioral disturbance:** list each disturbance (apathy, depression, irritability, persecutory delusions of theft, agitation with care, wandering, sleep disturbance). Add F06.x when a due-to-AD syndrome is diagnosed.
- **Severity:** describe the IADL/ADL dependence that supports mild/moderate/severe.
- **Coding line:** G30.9 → F02.80/F02.81 (major); G31.84 only (mild).
- **Safety/ongoing:** driving, finances, medication supervision, wandering, **suicide risk reassessment**, caregiver strain.
