# Separation Anxiety Disorder

> **Spark note:** Fear or anxiety about being apart from home or from key attachment figures that is excessive for the person's developmental level. The person worries that something will happen to those figures, or to themselves, that keeps them apart. It is **not only a childhood disorder**: adults can develop it de novo without any childhood history. Adults tend to show it as constant checking-in (texting or calling all day). Common errors are calling it GAD, or missing it because the patient is an adult.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Fear or anxiety about separation from attachment figures that is developmentally inappropriate and excessive, shown by **≥3 of 8**:
  1. Recurrent excessive distress when separation from home or major attachment figures happens or is anticipated
  2. Persistent, excessive worry about losing attachment figures or about harm to them (illness, injury, disaster, death)
  3. Persistent, excessive worry about an untoward event to *self* (getting lost, kidnapped, an accident, illness) that would cause separation
  4. Persistent reluctance or refusal to go out (school, work, elsewhere) because of separation fear
  5. Persistent, excessive fear of or reluctance about being alone or without the attachment figure, at home or elsewhere
  6. Persistent reluctance or refusal to sleep away from home or to fall asleep without the attachment figure nearby
  7. Repeated nightmares with separation themes
  8. Repeated physical complaints (headache, stomachache, nausea, vomiting) when separation happens or is anticipated
- **B.** Persistent: **≥4 weeks in children/adolescents (<18 y)**; **typically ≥6 months in adults** (a flexible guide).
- **C.** Clinically significant distress or impairment (social, academic, occupational, other).
- **D.** Not better explained by another mental disorder:
  - ASD: refusing to leave home because of resistance to change
  - Psychosis: delusions or hallucinations about separation
  - Agoraphobia: refusing to go out without a trusted companion
  - GAD: worries about ill health or harm to significant others
  - Illness anxiety: concern about having an illness

## Specifiers & severity
- No formal subtypes. Add **"with panic attacks"** if panic attacks occur (e.g., attacks triggered by anticipated separation).
- Severity: use the chapter's dimensional severity scale (same format across anxiety disorders).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- A1 to A8 in practice: needs to know where attachment figures are and stays in contact (A2); "shadowing" or clinging to a parent around the house, cannot be in a room alone (A5); bedtime struggles, ends up in the parents' or a sibling's bed (A6); refuses camp, sleepovers and errands. Adults are uncomfortable travelling alone or staying in hotels.
- Nightmare themes include destruction of the family (fire, murder, catastrophe).
- Somatic complaints are common in children. Cardiovascular symptoms (palpitations, dizziness, faintness) are **rare in young children** but can occur in adolescents and adults.
- The adult duration threshold is flexible.

## Associated features
- When separated: social withdrawal, apathy, sadness, poor concentration on work or play.
- Age-typical fears: animals, monsters, the dark, burglars, kidnappers, car accidents, plane travel. Homesickness.
- **School refusal**, leading to academic problems and social isolation.
- Anger or occasional aggression toward the person enforcing separation.
- Young children alone at night may report **unusual perceptual experiences** (people peering in, creatures, eyes staring). These are misperceptions, not hallucinations.
- Children may come across as demanding or intrusive. As parents, adults may be dependent and overprotective. Adults text or call repeatedly and check whereabouts. The family often becomes frustrated and resentful.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:**
  - Children (6–12-month): about **4%**. US adolescents (12-month): **1.6%**. Prevalence falls from childhood to adulthood.
  - US adults (12-month): **0.9–1.9%**. Cross-national adults (18 countries): mean **1.0%**, range <0.1% (Romania 0.3%) to 2.7% (Colombia); women 1.3% vs men 0.8%.
  - Toddlers: no sex difference. School-age community samples: more girls. Clinical child samples: equal.
  - Children report more symptoms than their parents report.
- **Course:**
  - Normative separation anxiety (e.g., around 1 y, stranger anxiety) reflects secure attachment.
  - Onset can be in preschool or any time in childhood or adolescence. Adults report median onset in late adolescence (high/upper-middle-income countries) or the mid-20s (low/lower-middle-income).
  - Adult course fluctuates, with exacerbations and remissions. **Most affected children are free of impairing anxiety disorders over their lifetime.**
  - Expression by age: young children show anxiety only at the moment of separation and don't express specific worries. Older children develop specific-danger worries. Adults are overconcerned about offspring, spouse, parents and pets, and struggle with life transitions (moving, marriage).
- **Risk factors:**
  - **Environmental:** life stress, especially loss (death of a relative or pet, illness, school change, divorce, a move, immigration, a disaster with separation). **Being bullied**. In young adults: leaving home, a romantic relationship, becoming a parent. Parental overprotection or intrusiveness.
  - **Genetic/physiological:** heritability about **73%** (6-year-old twins; higher in girls). Increased sensitivity to **CO2-enriched air**. Runs in families.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Cultures vary in how much separation is expected or tolerated and in the age at which children are expected to leave home. Do not pathologize a culture's value on family interdependence.
- Taiwanese youth endorse more symptoms than US youth.
- More common in women and in school-age girls.
- Impairment is reported as greater in high/upper-middle-income countries.

## Suicide risk
- In youth, may be associated with increased suicide risk. This is **not specific** to SAD and is tied to comorbidity.
- Childhood bullying predicts suicidal thoughts in young adulthood (twin study).

## Functional consequences
- Limits independence: avoids school or camp, has trouble sleeping alone, doesn't go away to college, doesn't leave the parental home, won't travel without attachment figures, won't work outside the home.
- Adults rearrange work schedules to stay near (or virtually in touch with) attachment figures. They are often frustrated by these limits. Symptoms can be debilitating across many domains.

## Differential diagnosis
- **GAD:** worry spans many domains. In SAD, worry is mostly about separation, and any other worries are not excessive.
- **Panic disorder:** panic attacks are unexpected, with fear of dying or going crazy. SAD attacks are cued by anticipated or actual separation.
- **Agoraphobia:** fear of being trapped or helpless with incapacitating symptoms. SAD fears being away from safe places tied to attachment figures.
- **Conduct disorder:** truancy without separation anxiety. The youth stays *away from* home rather than returning to it.
- **Social anxiety disorder:** school avoidance driven by fear of judgment.
- **PTSD:** separation fears after disaster are common. In PTSD the core is intrusions about, and avoidance of, trauma memories.
- **Illness anxiety disorder:** worries about one's own illness, not about separation from loved ones.
- **Prolonged grief disorder:** yearning and preoccupation with someone who has died. SAD is fear of a *possible* separation.
- **Depressive/bipolar disorders:** reluctance to leave home comes from low motivation, not fear for attachment figures. (SAD patients can become depressed around separations.)
- **ODD:** diagnose only if oppositionality persists apart from separation situations.
- **Psychotic disorders:** SAD perceptual experiences are misperceptions of real stimuli, limited to situations like nighttime, and end when the attachment figure is present.
- **Personality disorders:** dependent PD relies on others indiscriminately, whereas SAD centres on the proximity and safety of *specific* attachment figures. Borderline PD fears abandonment but also has core problems with identity, self-direction and impulsivity.

## Comorbidity
- **Children:** GAD and specific phobia.
- **Adults:** specific phobia, PTSD, panic disorder, GAD, social anxiety, agoraphobia, OCD, prolonged grief disorder, personality disorders (Cluster C: dependent, avoidant, OCPD), depressive and bipolar disorders.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Count A symptoms (≥3)** with concrete examples: "becomes tearful and pleads before drop-off" (A1); "calls/texts spouse 15+ times daily to confirm safety" (A2); "fears being kidnapped while parent is away" (A3); "missed 8 school days this month due to refusal to separate" (A4); "follows mother room to room" (A5); "sleeps in parents' bed nightly" (A6); "nightmares of family dying in a fire" (A7); "stomachaches on school mornings, resolving on weekends" (A8).
- **Duration:** record the onset date. Document ≥4 weeks for under-18s; for adults note the duration and why any flexibility was applied.
- **Impairment:** school attendance and grades, work limits, family conflict.
- **Rule-outs to document:** worry is limited to separation (not GAD); attacks are cued (not panic disorder); the feared outcome is separation, not being trapped (not agoraphobia); no psychotic features (perceptual experiences are night-limited and reversible); not grief; not ASD resistance to change; cultural norms about family interdependence considered.
- **MSE/phrases:** anxious affect that rises on discussing separation; clings to or seeks the caregiver in session; calm when the attachment figure is present; thought content centred on catastrophe befalling loved ones; reality testing intact.
- Note precipitating losses or stressors and any bullying history. Screen for suicidal ideation, especially with comorbidity.
