# Substance/Medication-Induced Anxiety Disorder

> **Spark note:** Prominent anxiety or panic that develops during or soon after intoxication, withdrawal, or exposure to (or withdrawal from) a medication or toxin, where that agent can plausibly cause the symptoms. Use it **instead of** plain intoxication or withdrawal **only when** the anxiety dominates the picture and needs its own clinical attention. Two tests rule it out in favour of an independent anxiety disorder: did the anxiety **predate** the substance, or **persist about a month or more** after it stopped? Coding includes any comorbid use disorder in a single code.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Panic attacks or anxiety **predominate** in the clinical picture.
- **B.** History, exam or labs show **both**:
  1. Onset during or soon after intoxication or withdrawal, or after exposure to or withdrawal from a medication
  2. The substance or medication is **capable** of producing these symptoms
- **C.** Not better explained by an independent (non-induced) anxiety disorder. Evidence of an independent disorder:
  - symptoms **preceded** the substance use
  - symptoms **persist** a substantial time (**about 1 month**) after acute withdrawal or severe intoxication ends
  - other evidence, such as a history of recurrent non-substance episodes
- **D.** Not exclusively during a **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note:** diagnose instead of intoxication or withdrawal only when the A symptoms predominate and are severe enough to warrant clinical attention.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With onset during intoxication:** intoxication criteria are met and symptoms develop during intoxication.
- **With onset during withdrawal:** withdrawal criteria are met and symptoms develop during or shortly after withdrawal.
- **With onset after medication use:** onset at the start of a medication, after a change in its use, or during its withdrawal.
- Which onset specifiers apply to which class: see Table 1 in the Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders chapter.

**Recording procedure:**
- Name the specific agent (e.g., cocaine, salbutamol).
- Order: [severity] [substance] use disorder (if any) → "with [substance]-induced anxiety disorder" → onset specifier.
  - Example with a use disorder: **F13.280 severe lorazepam use disorder with lorazepam-induced anxiety disorder, with onset during withdrawal**.
  - Example without one: **F16.980 psilocybin-induced anxiety disorder, with onset during intoxication**.
- Agents that fit no class (e.g., salbutamol), or an unknown class → "other (or unknown)" codes, e.g., **F19.980 salbutamol-induced anxiety disorder, with onset after medication use**.
- With **multiple substances**, list each separately (e.g., F15.280 severe methylphenidate use disorder with methylphenidate-induced anxiety disorder, with onset during intoxication; plus F19.980 salbutamol-induced…).
- For a mild use disorder, record "mild [substance] use disorder" first (e.g., "mild cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced anxiety disorder").

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The agent can be a drug of abuse, a medication or a toxin.
- For a prescribed medication, onset must occur **while taking it** (or during its withdrawal, if it has a withdrawal syndrome).
- After discontinuation, symptoms usually improve or remit **within days to several weeks to about a month**, depending on half-life and withdrawal.
- Don't diagnose if the anxiety **preceded** the intoxication or withdrawal, or **persists more than about 1 month** afterwards. In that case, look for other causes.

## Associated features
- **Intoxication** can cause anxiety: alcohol, caffeine, cannabis, PCP, other hallucinogens, inhalants, stimulants (including cocaine), other/unknown.
- **Withdrawal** can cause anxiety: alcohol; opioids; sedatives, hypnotics and anxiolytics; stimulants (including cocaine); other/unknown.
- **Medications:** anesthetics and analgesics, sympathomimetics and bronchodilators, anticholinergics, insulin, thyroid preparations, oral contraceptives, antihistamines, antiparkinsonian drugs, corticosteroids, antihypertensives and cardiovascular drugs, anticonvulsants, lithium, antipsychotics, antidepressants.
- **Toxins:** heavy metals, organophosphate insecticides, nerve gases, carbon monoxide, CO2, volatile substances (gasoline, paint).

## Prevalence
- Unclear. US general-population 12-month about **0.002%** (rare); likely higher in clinical populations.

## Diagnostic markers
- Lab tests (e.g., **urine toxicology**) help document intoxication.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Substance intoxication / withdrawal:** anxiety is common in both and the intoxication or withdrawal diagnosis usually suffices. Use the induced-anxiety diagnosis only when anxiety predominates and needs attention (panic and anxiety are characteristic of alcohol withdrawal, for example).
- **Independent anxiety disorder:** anxiety seen at other times, either **before** use began or **persisting** a substantial time after intoxication, withdrawal or medication use ended.
- **Delirium:** anxiety occurring only during delirium is part of the delirium and is not diagnosed separately.
- **Anxiety disorder due to AMC:** anxiety from the illness's physiology rather than from its treatment. A medication substitution or discontinuation trial may clarify. If **both** contribute, **give both diagnoses**.
- **Insufficient evidence** to decide between substance, medical and primary → **other specified or unspecified anxiety disorder**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** describe the anxiety or panic and why it dominates the picture beyond the usual intoxication or withdrawal symptoms.
- **B1:** timeline. Date of last use or medication start/change, and date of anxiety onset ("anxiety began 2 days after abrupt discontinuation of alprazolam"; "panic began within a week of starting prednisone 40 mg").
- **B2:** name the agent and note that it is known to cause anxiety.
- **C:** document **no anxiety history before use**; plan to reassess after about 1 month of abstinence or discontinuation; record any prior non-substance episodes (which would point to an independent disorder).
- **D:** oriented; attention intact; no fluctuating consciousness (rules out delirium).
- **Coding:** record the use-disorder severity (mild / moderate / severe / none), pick the 4th character accordingly, and **do not code the SUD separately**. Add the onset specifier.
- **Supporting data:** urine or serum toxicology, medication list, caffeine intake (mg/day), toxin exposure history.
- **MSE/phrases:** autonomic signs consistent with intoxication or withdrawal (tremor, diaphoresis, tachycardia); anxious affect; no psychotic features; sensorium clear.
