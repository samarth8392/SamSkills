# Anxiety Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** Anxiety or panic is a direct physiological consequence of an established medical condition. Worry about the meaning of an illness is a different mechanism and may support adjustment disorder or another anxiety diagnosis (pp. 402–405).

## Criteria at a glance
- **A:** Anxiety or panic attacks dominate the presentation.
- **B:** History, examination or laboratory findings support direct physiological causation by another medical condition.
- **C:** Another mental disorder does not better explain the presentation.
- **D:** Not confined to delirium.
- **E:** Clinically significant distress or functional impairment (pp. 402–403).

## Specifiers & severity
No formal subtypes/severity specifiers supplied. Describe predominant anxiety/panic and impairment; do not require the symptom count/duration of GAD or panic disorder.

## Coding
| Sequence | Code | Use |
|---|---|---|
| First | Condition-specific medical code | Established causal medical illness |
| Immediately after | F06.4 | Anxiety disorder due to [name of medical condition] |

Name the medical condition in the psychiatric diagnosis, and list its separate code immediately before it (pp. 402–403).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Establish the illness and a plausible known mechanism, then assess the individual causal link. Temporal correspondence of onset, worsening or remission, atypical age/course and literature on mechanism help; association alone is insufficient.
- Examples include thyroid/adrenal/glucose disorders, arrhythmias/heart failure/pulmonary embolism, asthma/COPD/pneumonia, B12 deficiency/porphyria and neurological or vestibular disease. These examples do not establish causation in a particular person (p. 403).
- Physical symptoms may be prominent. New panic after age 45 or atypical vertigo, loss of consciousness, incontinence, slurred speech or amnesia warrant assessment of medical/substance causes (p. 405).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Prevalence is unclear. Anxiety is frequent in many illnesses for multiple reasons; this does not estimate prevalence of direct physiological causation. Course often follows the medical illness; older adults may instead develop an independent anxiety disorder while living with chronic illness (p. 404).

## Diagnostic markers
Appropriate medical examination/laboratory assessment establishes the associated illness; no single psychiatric biomarker is supplied (p. 404).

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Delirium:** anxiety only during delirium is not separately diagnosed here.
- **Major/mild NCD:** both may be coded if prominent anxiety independently follows the causal neurological process.
- **Mixed medical psychiatric symptoms:** choose the medical-related mental diagnosis based on the predominant syndrome.
- **Substance/medication-induced anxiety:** assess exposure, withdrawal, medication timing and toxin effects; corticosteroids, estrogens and metoclopramide are cited examples. Both diagnoses may apply when both etiologies are supported.
- **Independent panic/GAD or other anxiety disorder:** no demonstrated direct physiological cause; coexistence with a medical condition is insufficient.
- **Illness anxiety disorder:** disease/pain preoccupation rather than physiological production of anxiety.
- **Adjustment disorder:** maladaptive stress response to illness meaning, prognosis or management, rather than direct physiology (pp. 404–405).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe predominant anxiety/panic, onset/course and actual distress/impairment.
- Name the confirmed illness, evidence source and specific causal reasoning; separate established findings from pending medical assessment.
- Record medication/substance timeline, delirium assessment and independent anxiety/adjustment alternatives.
- Include medical code first and F06.4 with the causal condition named; do not infer causation merely from co-occurrence.
