# Panic Disorder

> **Spark note:** The core is **recurrent unexpected panic attacks** (more than one full-symptom attack "out of the blue") followed by **≥1 month** of worry about further attacks or their consequences, and/or maladaptive changes in behaviour. Panic attacks are common across diagnoses; panic disorder is not. People also wrongly assume that having expected attacks rules it out (about half of patients have both), or they forget to rule out medical and substance causes, especially when onset is after 45 or symptoms are atypical. Agoraphobia, if present, is a **separate** diagnosis.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** **Recurrent unexpected** panic attacks. A panic attack is an abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort that **peaks within minutes**, with **≥4 of 13** symptoms. The surge can start from calm or from anxiety.
  - **Physical:**
    1. Palpitations, pounding or racing heart
    2. Sweating
    3. Trembling or shaking
    4. Shortness of breath or smothering
    5. Choking feeling
    6. Chest pain or discomfort
    7. Nausea or abdominal distress
    8. Dizzy, unsteady, light-headed or faint
    9. Chills or heat sensations
    10. Paresthesias
  - **Mixed:** 11. Derealization or depersonalization
  - **Cognitive:**
    12. Fear of losing control or "going crazy"
    13. Fear of dying
  - Culture-specific symptoms (tinnitus, neck soreness, headache, uncontrollable screaming or crying) **do not count** toward the 4.
- **B.** At least one attack is followed by **≥1 month** of one or both:
  1. Persistent concern or worry about more attacks or their consequences (losing control, heart attack, going crazy)
  2. Significant **maladaptive behaviour change** related to the attacks (e.g., avoiding exercise or unfamiliar situations)
- **C.** Not due to a substance or medication, or to a medical condition (e.g., hyperthyroidism, cardiopulmonary disease).
- **D.** Not better explained by another mental disorder. The attacks don't occur *only* in response to social situations (SocAD), phobic objects (SP), obsessions (OCD), trauma reminders (PTSD), or separation (SAD).

## Specifiers & severity
- No subtypes. "With panic attacks" is **not** used, because attacks are built into the criteria.
- Severity: dimensional scale. Markers of greater severity: fear of dying during attacks, nocturnal plus daytime attacks, and full-symptom (vs limited) attacks.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Recurrent** = more than one unexpected attack. **Unexpected** = no obvious cue at the time (relaxing, waking from sleep). **Expected** = obvious cue or trigger.
- The **clinician decides** whether an attack was expected, by questioning the sequence of events and asking whether the patient felt it came from nowhere. Cultural attributions can change this judgment.
- About **half** of US and European patients also have expected attacks, so expected attacks do not exclude PD.
- **Frequency varies:** weekly for months, or daily bursts separated by weeks or months, or about 2/month for years. Infrequent attackers resemble frequent ones in every respect.
- Full (≥4 symptoms) and limited (<4) attacks often co-occur, and symptom profiles vary between attacks. **More than one unexpected full-symptom attack is required.**
- **Nocturnal panic** means waking from sleep *in* panic, not panicking after fully waking. It occurs at least once in about ¼–⅓ of US patients, most of whom also have daytime attacks. Having both points to more severe PD.
- **Criterion B worry themes:**
  - physical (cardiac disease, seizure)
  - social (embarrassment at visible symptoms)
  - mental (going crazy, losing control)
- **Behaviour changes:** avoiding exertion, arranging for help to be available, restricting activities, avoiding agoraphobic-type situations. If agoraphobia criteria are met, diagnose it separately.

## Associated features
- Ongoing or intermittent anxiety about health and mental health. Catastrophizes mild symptoms or side effects ("headache = brain tumor"). **Poor tolerance of medication side effects.**
- Worries about coping with daily tasks and stress.
- Uses alcohol, prescribed medications or illicit drugs to control attacks.
- Extreme control behaviours (severe food restriction, avoiding foods or medications that cause physical sensations).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:**
  - 12-month **2–3%** in adults and adolescents (US, Europe). Global lifetime **1.7%**; projected lifetime risk 2.7%.
  - Lower in US Latinx, African American, Caribbean Black and Asian American people than non-Latinx Whites. American Indians 2.6–4.1%. Asian, African and Latin American countries 0.1–0.8%.
  - Female:male about **2:1**; the gap appears in adolescence, before 14.
  - **<0.4% before 14 y**. Rises through adolescence (possibly after puberty) and peaks in adulthood. Declines in older adults (1.2% >55 y; 0.7% >64 y).
- **Course:**
  - **Median onset 20–24 y (US)**; 32 y cross-nationally; **mean 34.7 y**. Childhood onset is rare; **onset after 55 is unusual**.
  - Untreated course is **chronic and waxing-waning**: some have episodic outbreaks with years of remission, others continuous severe symptoms. About ¼ recur within 2 years (Netherlands). Only a minority have full remission without relapse.
  - Complicated by other anxiety, depressive and substance use disorders.
  - African American adults have a more chronic course (racism, stigma, access to care).
  - "Fearful spells" are often dated back to childhood. Adolescent PD is chronic and comorbid, and presents like adult PD, but adolescents may worry less about further attacks and may be **reluctant to disclose** them.
  - **Older adults:** dampened autonomic response; "panicky feelings" as a **hybrid of limited attacks and generalized anxiety**. They attribute attacks after the fact to a situation (a medical procedure, a social setting), so unexpected attacks get under-endorsed. Ask whether the attack was expected *before* the person entered the situation.
- **Risk factors:**
  - **Temperamental:** negative affectivity, **anxiety sensitivity**, behavioral inhibition, harm avoidance. A history of "fearful spells" (limited attacks), especially if appraised negatively. Childhood separation anxiety is an inconsistent risk factor.
  - **Environmental:** identifiable stressors in the months before the first attack (interpersonal, health, bad drug experiences, illness or death in the family). Chronic stress → more severe PD. **10–60% report trauma.** Childhood adversity. Parental overprotection and low warmth. Low economic resources. **Smoking.**
  - **Genetic/physiological:** polygenic. Higher risk in offspring of parents with anxiety, depressive or bipolar disorders. **CO2 hypersensitivity**. Associated with asthma and other respiratory disturbance.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Cultural fear of mental or somatic symptoms affects rates.
- Cultural attribution can make an attack "expected": **trúng gió** (Vietnamese, "hit by the wind"), **ataque de nervios** (Latin American; may include screaming, crying, aggression, suicidality and dissociation, and last longer than minutes; some presentations meet criteria for FND instead), **khyâl attacks** and "soul loss" (Cambodian).
- Feared situations differ by culture: arguments (ataque), exertion (khyâl), wind (trúng gió). Clarify the cultural attribution before judging an attack expected.
- In US Asian, Hispanic and African Americans, PD is associated with discrimination and racism. African Americans have more impairment; Caribbean Blacks have higher objective severity. Among people of African descent the criteria may be endorsed only at high severity.
- About **2× more common in women**. Women relapse more (a more unstable course, also seen in adolescents) and have a larger hit to quality of life (anxiety sensitivity, agoraphobia and depression comorbidity). MAOA-uVNTR alleles may be a female-specific risk factor.

## Diagnostic markers
- Attentional bias to threat.
- Attacks are provoked more readily than in controls by sodium lactate, caffeine, isoproterenol, yohimbine, CO2 and CCK. Respiratory sensitivity is somewhat specific (shared with SAD).
- Chronic baseline hyperventilation and frequent sighing.
- **None of these is diagnostic.**

## Suicide risk
- Panic attacks and 12-month PD are linked to higher 12-month suicidal thoughts and behaviour, even after controlling for comorbidity and abuse.
- About **25% of primary-care PD patients** report suicidal thoughts.
- May raise the risk of future suicidal **behaviour** but not of suicide deaths.
- Cognitive symptoms (derealization) are associated with ideation; physical symptoms (dizziness, nausea) are associated with suicidal behaviour.
- PD is one of the three anxiety disorders most tied to the move from ideation to attempt.

## Functional consequences
- High social, occupational and physical disability and high costs. **The most medical visits of any anxiety disorder.** Effects are strongest with agoraphobia.
- Absences from work or school for doctor and ER visits → unemployment or dropping out.
- Older adults: caregiving and volunteering affected, lower health-related quality of life, more ED use.
- Full-symptom attacks bring more morbidity than limited ones.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Only limited-symptom attacks:** if a full unexpected attack has never occurred, PD can't be diagnosed → other specified or unspecified anxiety disorder.
- **Anxiety disorder due to AMC:**
  - Causes include hyperthyroidism, **hyperparathyroidism**, pheochromocytoma, vestibular dysfunction, seizures, and cardiopulmonary disease (arrhythmia, SVT, asthma, COPD).
  - Tests: serum calcium; Holter monitor; physical exam.
  - **Red flags:** onset after 45; atypical symptoms (vertigo, loss of consciousness, bladder or bowel incontinence, slurred speech, amnesia).
- **Substance/medication-induced:**
  - Stimulant (cocaine, amphetamines, caffeine) or **cannabis** intoxication; withdrawal from CNS depressants (alcohol, barbiturates).
  - If attacks continue **outside** substance use, or **predate** it, consider PD (plus SUD).
- **Other anxiety disorders with panic attacks:** attacks cued by social situations, phobic objects, agoraphobic situations, worry (GAD) or separation (SAD) → that disorder only. An unexpected attack can mark the onset of another anxiety disorder and then become cued. Add PD only if there are also unexpected attacks **plus** Criterion B.
- **Psychotic and other disorders** with panic as an associated feature.

## Comorbidity
- Rarely alone in clinics. **80%** of community cases have a lifetime comorbid diagnosis.
- Raised rates with other anxiety disorders (especially **agoraphobia**), MDD, bipolar I/II, and possibly mild alcohol use disorder. PD often starts *after* the comorbid disorder and may mark its severity.
- **MDD:** lifetime comorbidity 10–65%. Depression comes first in about ⅓ and coincides with or follows PD in about ⅔.
- Substance-related disorders (self-medication). Illness anxiety disorder.
- Medical: dizziness, arrhythmias, hyperthyroidism, asthma, COPD, IBS (causality unclear). Mitral valve prolapse and thyroid disease are more common, but inconsistently.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** describe at least 2 unexpected full attacks: date and context ("woke from sleep at 3 a.m."; "while watching TV, no trigger identified"), time to peak (minutes), and the symptom count with names (≥4 of 13). Exclude culture-specific symptoms from the count. Note any expected attacks as well.
- **B:** a month or more of anticipatory worry ("checks pulse repeatedly; fears heart attack") and/or behaviour change ("stopped going to gym", "won't drive on freeway alone", "keeps phone and benzodiazepine on hand").
- **C rule-outs:** medical workup (ECG/Holter, TSH, calcium, glucose; consider pheochromocytoma or seizure if indicated); caffeine, stimulant, cannabis and alcohol/sedative withdrawal history; urine tox if indicated; note the age at onset and any atypical symptoms.
- **D rule-outs:** the attacks aren't limited to social, phobic, obsessional, trauma-reminder or separation cues.
- **Agoraphobia screen:** ask about all 5 situations. If ≥2 are feared, evaluate and code F40.00 separately.
- **MSE/phrases:** hypervigilance to bodily sensations; catastrophic interpretation of symptoms; anxious affect; reality testing intact; reports derealization only during attacks.
- **Suicide screen** at every assessment (PD raises the risk of suicidal behaviour).
- Older adults and adolescents: ask carefully about unexpectedness and probe for under-reporting.
