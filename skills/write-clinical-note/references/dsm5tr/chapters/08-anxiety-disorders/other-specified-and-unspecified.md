# Other Specified and Unspecified Anxiety Disorder

> **Spark note:** These categories cover impairing/distressing anxiety that lacks full specific anxiety-disorder criteria **and** does not meet adjustment disorder with anxiety or mixed anxiety/depressed mood. F41.8 records why; F41.9 omits the reason or reflects limited information (pp. 405–406).

## Criteria at a glance
- Symptoms characteristic of an anxiety disorder predominate and cause clinically significant distress or impairment.
- No specific anxiety disorder is fully met.
- Neither adjustment disorder with anxiety nor adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood is met.
- **Other specified:** the clinician names the specific reason for not meeting a full anxiety diagnosis.
- **Unspecified:** the reason is not specified, including insufficient information in emergency settings (pp. 405–406).

## Example presentations (other specified)
1. **Limited-symptom attacks:** panic-like attacks below full panic-attack symptom threshold.
2. **Generalized anxiety less frequent than required:** worry/anxiety does not occur on a majority of days.
3. **Khyâl cap (wind attacks).**
4. **Ataque de nervios (attack of nerves).**

The last two are culturally described presentations cross-referenced to Section III. Their presence alone does not automatically establish a mental disorder; assess context, impairment and better-fitting diagnoses (p. 406).

## Specifiers & severity
No formal duration or symptom-count threshold is added for these residual categories. The named reason is a recording description.

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| F41.8 | Other specified anxiety disorder; append the specific reason |
| F41.9 | Unspecified anxiety disorder; reason omitted or insufficient information |

## Differential diagnosis
- **Specific anxiety disorder:** assess full symptom, duration and focus criteria first.
- **Adjustment disorders:** explicitly assess the anxiety or mixed anxiety/depression variants before either residual category.
- **Medical/substance causes:** refine attribution when evidence supports the corresponding etiological diagnosis; uncertainty may support residual coding while assessment continues (p. 402).
- **Normative/cultural experiences:** distress and impairment must be evaluated in context rather than presumed from a cultural label.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record anxiety form/focus, attack symptom count when relevant, frequency/course and actual distress/interference.
- State which threshold is unmet for F41.8; for F41.9 record what is unknown without asserting an unassessed absence.
- Address adjustment-disorder criteria, medical/substance possibilities and cultural context, using only information actually available.
