# Anxiety Disorders: Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** All of these disorders share fear (a response to imminent threat, real or perceived) and/or anxiety (anticipation of future threat), plus avoidance. They differ mainly by **what** is feared or avoided and **the thought behind it**. Ask "what are you afraid will happen?" before you pick a diagnosis. The chapter is ordered by typical age at onset, earliest first.

## What unites the chapter (p. 350–352)
- **Fear vs anxiety.** Fear goes with autonomic surges, thoughts of immediate danger and escape (fight/flight). Anxiety goes with muscle tension, vigilance, and cautious or avoidant behaviour. Pervasive avoidance can mask how much fear the person has.
- **Pathological vs normative.** A disorder is diagnosed when the fear is excessive or persists past the developmentally appropriate age. It must also be persistent, not transient: "typically ≥6 months" is a **guide, not a hard rule**. Children can meet it with shorter durations (SAD ≥4 wk; selective mutism ≥1 mo).
- **Out of proportion.** Patients overestimate danger, so **the clinician decides** whether the fear is excessive, taking cultural context into account.
- Most of these disorders begin in childhood and persist without treatment. Females outnumber males about **2:1** for most of them.
- **Primary anxiety diagnoses:** exclude substance/medication effects, medical physiological causes and better explanations by another mental disorder; induced and medical-condition variants classify those specific etiologies.
- **Panic attacks** are a type of fear response, not a disorder. They can occur across many diagnoses and serve as a **severity/prognosis marker**. Record them with the "with panic attacks" specifier on any anxiety disorder or other mental disorder (e.g., depressive disorders, PTSD).
- **Severity scales:** each disorder has its own scale. All share one format (behavioral, cognitive and physical items), so they can be compared when a patient has several anxiety disorders.
- **Suicide:** having an anxiety disorder raises the risk of suicidal thoughts, attempts and death. **Panic disorder, GAD and specific phobia** are the anxiety disorders most strongly linked to the move from ideation to attempt (p. 352).

## How the disorders differ: the core cognition / trigger
| Disorder | Feared focus / cognition | Minimum duration | Typical onset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation anxiety | Harm to, or loss of, attachment figures; being apart from them or from home | ≥4 wk in <18 y; typically ≥6 mo in adults | Preschool through adolescence; can begin in adulthood |
| Selective mutism | Speaking in specific social settings where speech is expected | ≥1 mo, not counting only the first month of school | Before 5 y |
| Specific phobia | One circumscribed object or situation; **no specific cognition required** | Typically ≥6 mo | Median 7–11 y |
| Social anxiety | Scrutiny and negative evaluation (humiliation, rejection, offending others) | Typically ≥6 mo | Median 13 y |
| Panic disorder | Unexpected attacks, then worry about more attacks or their consequences, or behaviour change | ≥1 mo of worry/behaviour change after an attack | Median 20–24 y (US) |
| Agoraphobia | Escape would be hard or help unavailable if panic-like or embarrassing symptoms hit; **≥2 of 5 situations** | Typically ≥6 mo | Mean 21 y |
| GAD | Many everyday domains; worry is hard to control; ≥3 of 6 somatic/cognitive symptoms | ≥6 mo, more days than not | Mean 35 y (N. America) |
| Substance/med-induced | Anxiety or panic caused by intoxication, withdrawal or a medication | No fixed remission requirement; persistence around a month beyond acute effects supports assessment of an independent disorder | Any |
| Due to AMC | Direct pathophysiological effect of a medical illness | Follows the illness | Any; suspect it with late onset |

## Chapter-level pitfalls & differential logic
- **Panic attacks are not panic disorder.** Expected (cued) attacks inside another anxiety disorder → diagnose that disorder **+ "with panic attacks"** if needed. Panic disorder needs **recurrent unexpected full-symptom** attacks **plus** ≥1 month of worry or maladaptive behaviour change. Expected attacks alongside unexpected ones do **not** rule it out (about half of PD patients have both).
- **Agoraphobia is coded separately from panic disorder** (no longer "panic disorder with agoraphobia"). When both are met, give both.
- **Agoraphobia vs situational specific phobia.** Count the agoraphobic situations: one → specific phobia; ≥2 → agoraphobia. Then check the cognition. Fear of the situation itself (the plane crashing) → specific phobia. Fear of being trapped or helpless with symptoms → agoraphobia.
- **School refusal** can come from SAD (separation), social anxiety (judgment), specific phobia, or conduct disorder (truancy: the child stays *away from* home, not anxious).
- **Avoiding leaving home** can come from agoraphobia (panic-like symptoms), SAD (separation), MDD (apathy/anhedonia), social anxiety (scrutiny), or PTSD (trauma reminders).
- **Worry content decides the diagnosis:** GAD (many domains) vs illness anxiety disorder (only one's own health) vs SAD (only separation) vs OCD (intrusive, ego-dystonic obsessions, not future-oriented worry).
- **Red flags for medical or substance causes of panic:** onset **after age 45**, or atypical features (vertigo, loss of consciousness, bladder/bowel incontinence, slurred speech, amnesia).
- **Culture:** taijin kyofusho (social anxiety / BDD / delusional), ataque de nervios, khyâl attacks, trúng gió. Culture-specific symptoms (tinnitus, neck soreness, headache, uncontrollable screaming or crying) **do not count** toward the 4 panic symptoms.
- **Older adults:** anxiety is often attributed to medical problems; panic is under-endorsed because elders give it a retrospective cause; fear of falling is common. They often show mixed anxiety-depression → unspecified anxiety disorder may fit.

## Shared specifiers & coding conventions
- **"With panic attacks"**: a specifier for any anxiety disorder (except panic disorder, where attacks are already in the criteria) and for other mental disorders. It is not coded.
- **Specific phobia**: code by stimulus type. When several types are present, give a separate diagnosis and code for each.
- **Social anxiety disorder**: one specifier, "performance only".
- **Substance/medication-induced**: the code depends on the substance class **and** whether a mild vs moderate/severe use disorder is present (4th character 1 / 2 / 9). Do not list a separate use-disorder diagnosis. Specify onset during intoxication, during withdrawal, or after medication use.
- **Due to AMC**: code the medical condition first, then F06.4 with the condition named in the title.
- **Other specified (F41.8)** vs **unspecified (F41.9)**: record the reason with F41.8; use F41.9 when the information is insufficient (e.g., ER). Both require that adjustment disorder with anxiety (or with mixed anxiety and depressed mood) is not met.

## TR changes / notable updates mentioned in the text
- Prolonged grief disorder now appears in the SAD differential and comorbidity list (yearning for the deceased vs fear of a *possible* separation).
- Updated ICD-10-CM codes: blood-injection-injury phobia now splits into **F40.230–F40.233** (blood / injections & transfusions / other medical care / injury). Opioid-induced anxiety uses **F11.188/.288/.988**. Caffeine-induced anxiety is **F15.980** (no use-disorder codes).
- Expanded material on culture, racism and discrimination (linked to social anxiety, panic disorder and GAD), and a section on suicidal thoughts/behavior for each disorder.
- Selective mutism's differential lists "intellectual developmental disorder (intellectual disability)", which is TR terminology.
