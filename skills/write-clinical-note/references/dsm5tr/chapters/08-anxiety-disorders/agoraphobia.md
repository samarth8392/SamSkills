# Agoraphobia

> **Spark note:** Fear of **≥2 of 5** situation types, because the person thinks **escape would be hard or help unavailable** if panic-like, incapacitating or embarrassing symptoms struck (falling, incontinence, vomiting). It is diagnosed **independently of panic disorder**; if both are met, give both. The two questions that settle most differentials are: how many situation types are feared, and what is the feared outcome?

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Marked fear or anxiety about **≥2 of 5**:
  1. Public transportation (cars, buses, trains, ships, planes)
  2. Open spaces (parking lots, marketplaces, bridges)
  3. Enclosed places (shops, theaters, cinemas)
  4. Standing in line or being in a crowd
  5. Being outside the home alone
- **B.** Feared or avoided because of thoughts that **escape might be difficult or help unavailable** if panic-like or other incapacitating or embarrassing symptoms occur (e.g., fear of falling in the elderly, fear of incontinence).
- **C.** The situations **almost always** provoke fear or anxiety.
- **D.** **Actively avoided**, **require a companion**, or are endured with intense fear.
- **E.** Out of proportion to the actual danger and to the sociocultural context.
- **F.** Persistent, **typically ≥6 months**.
- **G.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **H.** If a medical condition is present (IBD, Parkinson's), the fear is **clearly excessive**.
- **I.** Not better explained by another mental disorder. Symptoms are not:
  - confined to situational SP
  - limited to social situations (SocAD)
  - tied only to obsessions (OCD), appearance flaws (BDD), trauma reminders (PTSD), or separation (SAD)
- **Note:** diagnose regardless of panic disorder. If both are met, **assign both**.

## Specifiers & severity
- "With panic attacks" can be added if attacks occur and PD criteria are not met.
- Severity: dimensional scale. **Agoraphobia severity drives disability, independent of panic.**

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The listed examples are not exhaustive; other situations can count.
- **"Panic-like symptoms"** = any of the 13 panic attack symptoms. **"Other incapacitating or embarrassing symptoms"** = vomiting, inflammatory bowel symptoms, falling (older adults), disorientation or getting lost (children).
- Fear can be anticipatory or in the moment, and can include full or limited expected panic attacks.
- **Near-invariable:** anxious in line only 1 time in 5 → doesn't qualify.
- **Active avoidance** is current and intentional.
  - Behavioural: changing routines, choosing a job nearby to avoid transit, getting food delivered.
  - Cognitive: distraction.
  - It can progress to being **completely homebound**.
- A **companion** (partner, friend, health professional) helps the person face situations. **Safety behaviours**: sitting near exits.
- **Proportionality** is hard to judge:
  - Reasonable fears (a bad storm, a high-crime area) don't count.
  - Cultural norms count (e.g., orthodox Muslim women in some regions are expected not to leave home alone, which is not agoraphobia).
  - Older adults over-attribute fear to age-related limits and under-rate how excessive it is.
  - People with agoraphobia overestimate the danger of bodily symptoms.
- The duration requirement excludes short-lived problems.

## Associated features
- Severe cases are fully homebound and depend on others for basic needs.
- **Demoralization and depressive symptoms.** Alcohol and sedative misuse as self-medication.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:**
  - About **1–1.7%** of adolescents and adults per year worldwide. Women **2×** men.
  - Older adults: 0.4% (>65, US); 0.5% (>55, Europe and N. America).
  - Agoraphobia **without** panic disorder: **0.2–0.8%** of adults (12-month).
- **Course:**
  - Panic attacks or PD come before agoraphobia in **30%** (community) to **>50%** (clinical).
  - **Two-thirds** start before 35; **mean onset 21 y**. Without preceding panic, onset is **25–29 y**. Childhood onset is rare.
  - High-incidence periods: adolescence and early adulthood, then a **second peak after 40**. About **10%** of older adults with agoraphobia had first onset **after 65**.
  - **Persistent and chronic. Complete remission is rare (about 10%) without treatment.** More severity → less remission and more relapse. About **36%** of remitters relapse.
  - PD plus agoraphobia with onset before 20 → more recurrence.
  - Long-term risk of secondary MDD, PDD and SUD.
  - Feared situations by age: **children** fear being outside alone and getting lost; **adults** fear panic symptoms; **older adults** fear shops, lines and open spaces, and falling.
  - Children need multi-informant assessment. Adolescents, especially boys, under-disclose.
  - In older adults, check whether somatic symptom disorder, medical complications or motor disturbance (a sense of falling) is the stated reason, and judge proportionality carefully.
- **Risk factors:**
  - **Temperamental:** behavioral inhibition, negative affectivity, trait anxiety, **anxiety sensitivity** (characteristic).
  - **Environmental:** negative childhood events (separation, death of a parent); being attacked or mugged; a family climate low in warmth and high in overprotection.
  - **Genetic:** **heritability 61%**, the strongest and most specific link among the phobias to the genetic "phobia-proneness" factor. Family history of anxiety → earlier onset (<27). Family history of PD is especially linked.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Avoidance must be judged against cultural norms (see Criterion E).
- Women have different comorbidity patterns; men have more comorbid SUD.

## Suicide risk
- About **15%** report suicidal thoughts or behaviour.
- In people with panic disorder, agoraphobic symptoms may be a risk factor for suicidal thoughts.

## Functional consequences
- Considerable impairment in role functioning, work productivity and disability days. Severity determines disability.
- Can be completely homebound or unable to work.
- PD with agoraphobia and onset before 20 → less likely to marry.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Specific phobia, situational:** fear limited to **one** agoraphobic situation → SP. ≥2 is a robust separator. If the fear is of direct harm (plane crash) rather than of symptoms, SP fits better.
- **Separation anxiety disorder:** the cognition is about being away from attachment figures and home, not about symptoms.
- **Social anxiety disorder:** fear of negative evaluation; social situational clusters.
- **Panic disorder:** if PD is met but avoidance doesn't reach **≥2** agoraphobic situations → no agoraphobia diagnosis.
- **ASD/PTSD:** fear or avoidance limited to trauma reminders and not reaching ≥2 agoraphobic situations → no agoraphobia.
- **MDD:** stays home from apathy, low energy, low self-esteem or anhedonia, not fear of symptoms.
- **Medical conditions:** realistic fear of incapacitation (fainting with TIAs) or embarrassment (diarrhea with Crohn's). Diagnose only if the fear clearly exceeds what's expected.

## Comorbidity
- About **90%** have other mental disorders: other anxiety disorders (SP, PD, SocAD), MDD, PTSD, alcohol use disorder.
- SAD, SP and PD usually **precede** it. Depression and SUD usually **follow** it (though sometimes SUD comes first).
- Comorbid MDD → a more treatment-resistant course.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** list each of the 5 situations as feared or not feared, with examples. Confirm **≥2**.
- **B:** record the feared outcome in the patient's words, e.g., "afraid she'll have a panic attack in the grocery line and not be able to get out", "fears losing bowel control on the bus with no restroom", "fears falling with no one to help".
- **C/D:** near-invariable fear; avoidance specifics ("has not driven on highway in 2 years"; "only shops with husband"; "groceries delivered"); safety behaviours (aisle seats, sitting near exits); homebound status.
- **E:** clinician judgment of excess, accounting for neighbourhood safety, cultural norms and age-related physical limits.
- **F/G:** duration ≥6 months; impairment (work, errands, medical appointments missed).
- **H:** if IBD, Parkinson's or fall risk is present, document why the fear is **excessive** relative to the real risk.
- **I rule-outs:** not limited to one situation (SP); not evaluation-focused (SocAD); not separation-focused (SAD); not trauma-reminder-only (PTSD); not obsession-driven (OCD); not appearance-driven (BDD); not anergic withdrawal (MDD).
- **Panic disorder:** assess separately; code F41.0 + F40.00 if both are met.
- **MSE/phrases:** anxious affect on discussing leaving home; attended with a companion; reports reliance on a "safe person"; catastrophic thoughts about symptoms in public; insight variable (often limited in elders).
- Screen alcohol and sedative use, depression, and suicidal ideation.
