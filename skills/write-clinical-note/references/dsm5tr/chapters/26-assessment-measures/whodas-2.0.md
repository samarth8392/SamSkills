# WHODAS 2.0 (World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0)

> **Spark note:** The WHODAS is the DSM's **disability** measure. It is a 36-item self-report covering the **past 30 days** across **6 domains** of functioning, and it was built for *any* health condition, not just mental disorders. It quantifies the "impairment" half of the clinical-significance criterion and tracks functional recovery over time. DSM-5-TR recommends **average scores** (raw sum ÷ number of items), which read on the 1–5 none-to-extreme metric. With **10 or more missing items**, general scores **may not be useful**; sufficiently complete domains can still be informative. Check work/school applicability before scoring.

## What it is and when to use it (p. 1109, 1120)
- Developed by the WHO. It corresponds to the **International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF)**.
- **Adult self-administered version:** 36 items, **age 18+**. It has been validated across many cultures and is **sensitive to change**.
- **Proxy-administered version** for adults with impaired capacity (e.g., major NCD). It is completed by a knowledgeable informant and is available online.
- Each item asks how much difficulty the person has had in an area of functioning **during the past 30 days**.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Most criteria sets require clinically significant distress or **impairment**.
  - People with mental disorders tend to have more severe impairment than people with chronic medical conditions.
  - Many seek help *because* of functional impact.
  - **Residual impairment after symptom remission** can drive recurrence or relapse (e.g., MDD, anxiety disorders).
- **Use it** at baseline to document impairment, then serially to track functioning alongside symptom measures.

## Six domains (p. 1109, 1120)
| Domain (assessment label) | Scoring label | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding and communicating | Cognition | Understanding and communicating with others |
| Getting around | Mobility | Moving about |
| Self-care | Self-care | Self-care tasks |
| Getting along with people | Getting along | Interpersonal relations |
| Life activities | Life activities | Household and work/school activities |
| Participation in society | Participation | Participation in community/society |

The printed form is image-only in this PDF. Its functional areas and work/school skip instruction were visually checked and paraphrased in [the printed-form supplement](whodas-printed-form-and-item-domains.md) (pp. 1122–1123). Use the actual instrument for administration.

## WHO scoring options (p. 1120)
**Item response anchors:** none (1), mild (2), moderate (3), severe (4), extreme (5).

| Method | How | Range | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | Sum the raw item scores (1–5) across all 36 items. No recoding or weighting | Max **180** | Hand scoring in busy clinics or paper-and-pencil interviews. The text calls it sufficient to describe the degree of functional limitation |
| **Complex (IRT-based)** | Computer program from WHO. Step 1: recode items 1–5 → 0–4 and sum within each domain (total raw max **144**). Step 2: sum the 6 domain scores. Step 3: convert to a **0–100** metric (0 = no disability, 100 = full disability) | 0–100 | Weighted by item difficulty and severity level. Population norms are available from WHO |

- IRT scoring also produces **domain-specific scores** for cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation.

## DSM-5-TR scoring guidance (p. 1121)
- **Clinician review:**
  - Go through each item with the person during the interview.
  - Enter the self-reported score in the "Clinician Use Only" section.
  - If clinical information suggests a different value, you **may enter a corrected raw item score**.
- **Average scores (recommended).** In DSM-5 Field Trials (6 US sites and 1 Canadian site) they were reliable, easy and clinically useful.
  - **Average domain score** = raw domain score ÷ number of items in that domain.
    - Example: all 6 "understanding and communicating" items rated moderate → 18/6 = **3 = moderate**.
  - **Average general disability score** = raw total ÷ **36** in the narrative's complete 36-item example. The printed form directs people not working/studying to skip D5.5–D5.8; do not count inapplicable items as zero or invent an adjusted denominator. Check the instrument's scoring instructions before interpreting an incomplete/applicability-limited total.
  - Interpret on the 1–5 metric: **1 none, 2 mild, 3 moderate, 4 severe, 5 extreme**.
- **Missing data rules:**
  - Encourage the person to answer every item.
  - If **≥10 items** are missing (more than 25% of 36), the simple and average **general** disability scores may not be useful.
  - If ≥10 items are missing overall but some **domains are 75%–100% complete**, you can still use the simple or average **domain** scores for those domains.

## Frequency of use (p. 1121)
- Repeat at regular intervals as clinically indicated, depending on symptom stability and treatment status.
- Consistently high domain scores mark problem areas that may need further assessment and intervention.

## Clinical pearls
- WHODAS measures **functioning, not symptoms**. A patient can be in symptomatic remission with persisting moderate disability, which is a relapse-risk flag according to the text.
- Average scores make domains comparable even though they have different numbers of items.
- The "life activities" domain separates household from work/school. This is useful when documenting occupational impairment for disability or return-to-work planning.
- For adults with cognitive impairment, psychosis with poor insight, or limited literacy, use the **proxy** version or clinician-assisted administration, and record who supplied the information.
- The adult WHODAS is for **18+**. The book does not describe a child version in this chapter.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record: "WHODAS 2.0, 36-item self-administered (or proxy, informant = ___), past 30 days, scoring method = simple / average / IRT."
- Report the average general disability score with its label, plus each domain's average, e.g., "Average general 2.8 (≈moderate). Getting along 3.4, Life activities 3.2, Participation 3.0. Mobility 1.2."
- Note any clinician-corrected items and why, e.g., "self-rated self-care 1, corrected to 3 per caregiver report of needing prompting to bathe".
- Note missing items. If ≥10 are missing, say the general score was not interpreted and which domains were usable.
- Link the results to the diagnosis's **impairment criterion** and to treatment goals, e.g., "functional target: reduce Life activities average from 3.2 to ≤2 within 12 wk".
- Trend over time next to symptom measures, e.g., "PHQ/Level 2 depression improved; WHODAS unchanged → residual impairment, continue functional rehab focus".
