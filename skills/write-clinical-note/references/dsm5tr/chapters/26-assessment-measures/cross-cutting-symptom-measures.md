# Cross-Cutting Symptom Measures (Level 1 and Level 2)

> **Spark note:** The Level 1 measure is a quick **review of mental systems**. The adult form has 1–3 items per domain; the parent form includes some four-item domains. Most items cover the past 2 weeks, but the parent suicide-attempt item asks about lifetime history. A threshold guides **Level 2** assessment or clinical inquiry; it does not make a diagnosis. Use the highest item score in each domain. Adult suicidality, psychosis and substance use, and parent-rated inattention and psychosis, have lower numeric inquiry thresholds; parent suicide/substance items use Yes/No/Don't Know responses.

## Purpose and when to use (p. 1108–1110)
- The measure is modeled on the general-medical **review of systems**. It helps a comprehensive mental status assessment at the first evaluation by drawing attention to domains the person may not bring up.
- **Use it at intake and at follow-up visits.** It is a core part of **measurement-based care**: standardized tools given repeatedly to track progress and adjust the care plan.
- Use it to:
  - find **emerging** symptoms and concerns outside the presenting problem
  - guide further assessment
  - track change and treatment response
- Clinical practice guidelines name cross-cutting measures as important parts of psychiatric diagnostic assessment (p. 1109).

- The 2-week window applies to all items except the **lifetime** suicide-attempt item.
- 19 of the 25 parent items use the 0–4 scale. The suicidal ideation, suicide attempt and substance items are Yes / No / Don't Know (p. 1113).
- The text and table label the substance threshold "Yes" and list the child-rated ASSIST against "Don't Know". The paired suicide rows give "None" for Level 2 under both "Yes" and "Don't Know".

## Scoring and interpretation (p. 1112–1113)
- **Rating scale (0–4):**
  - 0 = none / not at all
  - 1 = slight / rare, less than a day or two
  - 2 = mild / several days
  - 3 = moderate / more than half the days
  - 4 = severe / nearly every day
- **Domain score = highest item score in the domain.** Record it in the clinician "Highest Domain Score" column.
- **Review the individual items too**, especially when no Level 2 is indicated, to see *which* symptom drives the domain (e.g., hallucinations vs thought broadcasting in psychosis).
- **Adult thresholds:**
  - **≥2 (mild)** on any item: further inquiry, and possibly Level 2. This applies to all domains except the three below.
  - **≥1 (slight)** for **substance use, suicidal ideation, psychosis**.
- **Parent thresholds:**
  - **≥2** on 0–4 items: further inquiry. This applies to all domains except the two below.
  - **≥1** for **inattention and psychosis**.
  - A **"Don't Know"** on suicide or substance items (especially ages 11–17) warrants further probing with the child. A child-rated Level 2 substance measure can contribute where appropriate; **no Level 2 suicide measure is listed**, so suicide concerns require direct clinical assessment.
- **Child-rated version:** same rules as the parent version, but there is no Don't Know category.

## Level 2 measures (p. 1113)
- When a Level 1 domain reaches its threshold, there is a *possible* need for detailed inquiry. Level 2 measures are **one way** to get it, and they inform diagnosis, treatment planning and follow-up.
- Adult and pediatric (parent and child) versions exist online for most Level 1 domains.
- Domains **without** a Level 2 measure need a clinical interview or other tools:
  - Adult: suicidal ideation, psychosis, memory, dissociation and personality functioning.
  - Child: psychosis, repetitive thoughts/behaviors and suicide.
  - Suicide and psychosis domains call for direct clinician assessment.

## Frequency of use (p. 1113–1114)
- Repeat Level 1 and the relevant Level 2 measures **at regular intervals as clinically indicated**. How often depends on how stable the symptoms are and on treatment status.
- For impaired-capacity adults and children 6–17, the **same informant / same parent or guardian** should complete follow-ups when possible.
- **Consistently high** domain scores point to significant problems that may need further assessment, treatment and follow-up. Clinical judgment decides.

## Evidence base noted in the text (p. 1111)
- DSM-5 Field Trials (adult samples in the US and Canada; pediatric samples in the US) found the measures **clinically useful with good reliability**.
- When patients' ratings were shared with clinicians before the visit, patients said it **helped communication**.
- Clinicians in both academic and routine settings found the measures feasible.
- In a large study of non-treatment-seeking US college students, the adult self-rated version showed acceptable internal consistency and internal validity.

## Clinical pearls
- Treat Level 1 as a **broad net** run at intake. It often finds comorbid domains (e.g., substance use or sleep in a depression referral) that change the differential.
- Any positive on **suicidal ideation (adult ≥1, or a parent Yes/Don't Know)** needs a **same-visit risk assessment**. There is no Level 2 measure to defer to.
- Any positive on **psychosis (≥1)** needs a clinician interview. For an established psychotic illness, the [Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity](severity-measures-and-psychosis-dimensions.md) can quantify it.
- Positive adult **personality functioning** or **dissociation** items have no Level 2. Consider a structured interview, or the Alternative Model's Level of Personality Functioning (Section III).
- The adult **somatic** domain includes a feeling that one's illness is not taken seriously. That item is relevant to somatic symptom disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record: "DSM-5 Level 1 CCSM (adult self-rated / parent-rated / child-rated / informant), date, past-2-week frame."
- List each domain at threshold with its **highest item score**. Example: "Depression 3, Anxiety 2, Sleep 3, Suicidal ideation 1. All others below threshold."
- Document the follow-up for each positive domain, e.g., "Level 2 PROMIS Depression SF administered", "clinical suicide risk assessment completed", "substance history expanded" or "no Level 2 available; clinical interview performed".
- For "Don't Know" parent answers, note that the child was interviewed or given the child-rated version.
- For serial use, note the score trend and the informant's identity, e.g., "Level 1 Depression domain 3 → 1 over 8 wk, same parent rater".
- Do not record a diagnosis on the basis of Level 1 alone. Link positive domains to criterion-level evidence from the interview.
