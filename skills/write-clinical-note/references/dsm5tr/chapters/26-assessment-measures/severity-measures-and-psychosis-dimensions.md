# Severity Measures and the Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity

> **Spark note:** Severity measures are **disorder-specific**. Each closely follows the criteria of the disorder it rates and quantifies how bad that disorder is now. Use them after a diagnosis, or for a significant subthreshold syndrome, and repeat them to track response. The one printed in the book is the **Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity**. It has 8 items, each rated 0–4 at its **worst point in the past 7 days**. In schizophrenia-spectrum chapters it is the tool behind the "current severity" specifier. A common mistake is rating the average week instead of the peak.

## Disorder-specific severity measures: general (p. 1109)
- **What they are:** dimensional ratings that track criterion symptoms of a specific diagnosis. They can measure:
  - symptom frequency, intensity or duration
  - overall severity
  - symptom type (e.g., depression, anxiety, sleep disturbance)
- **Who gets them:**
  - people with a diagnosis
  - people with a **clinically significant syndrome below full criteria**
- **Rater:** some are self-rated and some are clinician-rated from observation.
- **When:** at the first interview and over time, to track severity and treatment response.
- **Coverage:** many but not all DSM-5 diagnoses. The examples the text gives are generalized anxiety disorder, social anxiety disorder, psychotic disorders, PTSD, autism spectrum disorder and social (pragmatic) communication disorder.
- **Availability:** only the psychosis measure is printed. The others are online at www.psychiatry.org/dsm5.
- **Use:** to help with diagnosis, symptom monitoring and treatment planning.

## Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity

### Rationale (p. 1117–1118)
- Psychotic disorders are **heterogeneous**. Symptom severity can predict aspects of the illness such as the degree of cognitive and/or neurobiological deficit.
- Dimensional ratings help with treatment planning, prognosis and research on mechanisms.
- **Cognition** is included because cognitive impairment is common in psychosis and predicts functional ability and prognosis.
- **Depression and mania** are included to catch co-occurring mood pathology. Mood severity in psychosis has prognostic value and guides treatment.

### Administration (p. 1118)
- 8 items, completed by the **clinician** at the time of clinical assessment.
- **Time frame:** each symptom is rated **at its most severe during the past 7 days**.
- The clinician uses **all available information** (interview, observation, collateral, records) and clinical judgment. Tick the level that fits best and enter it in the "Score" column.

### Scale (p. 1118–1120)
General anchors: **0 = not present; 1 = equivocal; 2 = present, mild; 3 = present, moderate; 4 = present, severe.** Each domain has its own definitions, paraphrased here.

| Domain | 1 Equivocal | 2 Mild | 3 Moderate | 4 Severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I. Hallucinations | Severity/duration not enough to count as psychosis | Little pressure to act on them; not very bothered | Some pressure to respond, or somewhat bothered | Severe pressure to respond, or very bothered |
| II. Delusions | Not enough to count as psychosis | Little pressure to act on beliefs; not very bothered | Some pressure to act, or somewhat bothered | Severe pressure to act, or very bothered |
| III. Disorganized speech | Not enough to count as disorganization | Some difficulty following speech | Speech often hard to follow | Speech almost impossible to follow |
| IV. Abnormal psychomotor behavior | Not enough to count as abnormal | Occasional abnormal/bizarre motor behavior or catatonia | Frequent | Almost constant |
| V. Negative symptoms (restricted emotional expression or avolition) | Equivocal drop in facial expressivity, prosody, gestures or self-initiated behavior | Mild drop | Moderate drop | Severe drop |
| VI. Impaired cognition | Not clearly outside the range expected for age/SES (**within 0.5 SD** of mean) | Some reduction; **0.5–1 SD** below the mean expected for age/SES | Clear reduction; **1–2 SD** below | Severe reduction; **>2 SD** below |
| VII. Depression | Occasional sadness/hopelessness; concern about having failed someone or something, without preoccupation | Frequent periods of marked sadness or moderate depression/hopelessness; failure concern with some preoccupation | Frequent periods of deep depression/hopelessness; preoccupied with guilt or wrongdoing | Deep depression/hopelessness daily; **delusional guilt** or self-reproach grossly out of proportion |
| VIII. Mania | Occasional elevated, expansive or irritable mood, or some restlessness | Frequent periods of somewhat elevated/expansive/irritable mood or restlessness | Frequent periods of extensively elevated/expansive/irritable mood or restlessness | Daily, extensive elevated/expansive/irritable mood or restlessness |

SD = standard deviation; SES = socioeconomic status.

### Frequency of use (p. 1118)
- Repeat at regular intervals as clinically indicated, depending on symptom stability and treatment status.
- Consistently high domain scores flag areas that need more assessment, treatment and follow-up. Clinical judgment always governs.

## Clinical pearls
- **Rate the peak.** The rating is the worst level in the past 7 days, not a typical day. This matters for fluctuating hallucinations or agitation.
- **"Equivocal" (1)** uses a domain-specific anchor. For hallucinations and delusions, it denotes insufficient severity/duration to establish psychosis; the speech, motor, negative, cognitive and mood domains have their own meanings. A score of 1 does not establish **attenuated psychosis syndrome**; that research condition has separate criteria (see [conditions for further study](../29-conditions-for-further-study/attenuated-psychosis-syndrome.md)).
- **Cognition anchors are normed**, using SD below age/SES expectations. Formal or bedside cognitive testing makes the rating defensible.
- **Depression item 4** includes delusional guilt. Look at mood-congruent psychotic features and at schizoaffective vs mood disorder with psychotic features.
- The profile across the 8 domains is clinically useful. A predominantly negative/cognitive profile has different treatment and prognostic implications from a predominantly positive one.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record: "Clinician-Rated Dimensions of Psychosis Symptom Severity, past 7 days (worst point), sources: interview, observation, collateral, chart."
- List all 8 domain scores, e.g., "Halluc 3, Delus 2, Disorg speech 1, Psychomotor 0, Negative 2, Cognition 2, Depression 1, Mania 0."
- Give the behavioral evidence for each nonzero rating, e.g., "responds to AH by covering ears, pressure to comply" (supports Halluc 3) or "flat prosody, minimal gestures, does not initiate ADLs" (supports Negative 2).
- For the cognition rating, cite the test or performance basis and the comparison to age/SES expectations.
- Trend the scores across visits and tie the changes to medication or psychosocial interventions.
- For other disorder-specific severity measures (online), record the instrument, version, score and how the result changed the diagnosis or treatment plan.
