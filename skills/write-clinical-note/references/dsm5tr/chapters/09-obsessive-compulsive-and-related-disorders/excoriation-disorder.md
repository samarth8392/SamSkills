# Excoriation (Skin-Picking) Disorder

> **Spark note:** Recurrent picking of one's own skin causing lesions, with repeated attempts to
> stop, and distress or impairment. It is a BFRB, not driven by obsessions. The diagnosis depends
> on **what motivates the picking**:
> - fixing a perceived appearance flaw → BDD
> - a delusion of parasites or formication → psychosis
> - intent to self-harm → NSSI
> - cocaine or other substance → substance-induced OCRD
> - deception about self-inflicted lesions → factitious disorder or malingering
>
> Picking that has become independent of an underlying skin condition such as acne still counts.
> (pp. 434–437)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Recurrent skin picking **resulting in skin lesions**.
- **B.** **Repeated attempts to decrease or stop.**
- **C.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **D.** Not attributable to a substance (e.g., cocaine) or another medical condition (e.g.,
  scabies).
- **E.** Not better explained by another mental disorder:
  - delusions or tactile hallucinations in psychosis
  - appearance-fixing in BDD
  - stereotypies in stereotypic movement disorder
  - **intent to self-harm in NSSI**

## Specifiers & severity
None. As with trichotillomania, **focused** (tension, then relief) and **automatic** (without
awareness) styles are described, and most people mix them.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Sites:** most often **face, arms, hands**; many pick from multiple sites.
- **Targets:** healthy skin, minor irregularities, pimples, calluses, scabs from earlier picking.
- **Methods:** mostly fingernails; also tweezers, pins and other tools. May also rub, squeeze,
  lance or bite the skin.
- Often takes **several hours/day** and can last months or years.
- **Concealment** with makeup or clothing is common.
- **"Distress"** includes loss of control, embarrassment and shame.
- Impairment comes partly from avoiding social situations.

## Associated features
- **Rituals:** seeking a particular kind of scab; examining or playing with the skin; mouthing or
  swallowing it.
- **Triggers:** anxiety or boredom; rising tension before picking or while resisting.
- **Afterward:** gratification, pleasure or relief.
- Some pick in response to a minor skin irregularity or to relieve an uncomfortable bodily
  sensation.
- **Pain is not routinely reported.**
- Usually done alone or only in front of immediate family. Some pick other people's skin.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Prevalence:** US online survey (n>10,000, age- and sex-matched): **2.1% current**, **3.1%
lifetime**. **≥3/4 are women** in community samples.

**Development and course**
- Onset can occur at any age, but most often in **adolescence, at or after puberty**.
- **Frequently begins with a dermatological condition such as acne.**
- Sites vary over time.
- Untreated course is chronic and waxing-waning; it may come and go over weeks to years.

**Genetic risk:** there is evidence of genetic vulnerability. More common in people with OCD and
their first-degree relatives.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Cross-cultural data are limited. Features appear similar in the US and other countries.
- Women predominate. Comorbid depression seems more common in women.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Social and occupational impairment.
- **Most spend ≥1 h/day** picking, thinking about it, or resisting urges.
- Many avoid social events, entertainment or going out in public.
- Most report work interference at least daily or weekly.
- Students report missed school and trouble studying or managing responsibilities.
- **Medical complications** can be life-threatening:
  - tissue damage, scarring, infection
  - frequent antibiotic courses; occasionally surgery
  - rarely, **wrist synovitis**

## Diagnostic markers
- Biopsy is rarely needed because most patients admit to picking.
- Histopathology may show characteristic features.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Psychotic disorder:** picking in response to a delusion (**parasitosis**) or tactile
  hallucination (**formication**) → not excoriation disorder.
- **OCD:** lesions from contamination-driven *washing* → OCD.
- **BDD:** picking to improve appearance → BDD.
- **Other BFRB disorder** (other specified OCRD) excludes presentations that meet excoriation
  criteria.
- **Stereotypic movement disorder:** self-injurious stereotypy with **early developmental onset**.
  Example: early skin picking in **Prader-Willi syndrome** may meet stereotypic movement criteria.
- **Tourette's:** tics can cause self-injury, but excoriation picking is not tic-like.
- **Dermatitis artefacta** ("factitious dermatitis"): unexplained, presumably self-induced lesions
  whose cause the patient denies.
  - Deception with external incentive → **malingering**.
  - Deception without obvious reward → **factitious disorder**.
  - No deception and repeated attempts to stop → excoriation disorder can be diagnosed.
- **NSSI:** picking mainly intended to harm oneself → NSSI.
- **Other medical conditions:**
  - **Scabies:** scratching is explained by the itch.
  - **Acne:** a dermatological condition can trigger or worsen excoriation disorder ("acne
    excoriée"). Decide whether the picking **has become independent** of the skin condition.
- **Substance/medication-induced:** e.g., cocaine → not excoriation disorder. If clinically
  significant, consider **substance/medication-induced OCRD**.

## Comorbidity
- OCD, trichotillomania, MDD. Depression is more common in women.
- Other BFRBs such as nail biting are common. They may warrant **other specified OCRD (other
  body-focused repetitive behavior disorder)**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Criterion A:**
  - Sites, tools, frequency, time per day.
  - **Observed lesions** on exam: excoriations, scabs, scars, signs of infection.
  - Concealment strategies.
- **Criterion B:** specific attempts to stop and their outcome.
- **Criterion C:** shame, avoidance (public outings, events), work or school interference, medical
  sequelae (antibiotics, surgery).
- **Motivation and phenomenology:**
  - Focused vs. automatic; triggers (boredom, anxiety, skin irregularity); tension and relief.
  - Explicitly document what the picking is *not* for: improving appearance (BDD), removing
    "bugs" (psychosis), self-harm (NSSI).
- **Rule-outs:**
  - stimulant or cocaine use
  - scabies or other pruritic dermatoses
  - whether picking is independent of acne
  - developmental history (stereotypy, Prader-Willi)
  - signs of deception (factitious disorder, malingering)
- **Medical referral:** flag infected or deep lesions.
- **Phrasing example:** "Observed multiple healing excoriations on forearms and face; reports
  ~2 h/day picking scabs, mostly automatic during screen use; denies intent to self-harm or belief
  in infestation; wants to stop and has tried bandaging and fidget tools without success."
