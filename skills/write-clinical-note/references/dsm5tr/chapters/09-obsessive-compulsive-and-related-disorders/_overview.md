# Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders: Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** The chapter groups disorders with either (a) **preoccupations plus repetitive
> behaviors or mental acts** (OCD, body dysmorphic disorder [BDD], hoarding) or (b) **body-focused
> repetitive behaviors (BFRBs)** with repeated attempts to stop (trichotillomania, excoriation).
> They differ from normal rituals and worries by being excessive or persisting past the
> developmentally appropriate period, and by causing distress or impairment. The chapter sits
> right after Anxiety Disorders because the two groups are closely related. Screen for the other
> disorders here whenever one is present. (pp. 408–410)

## What unites the chapter and how the disorders differ
- **Shared validators.** The disorders are related across a range of diagnostic validators, and
  grouping them is clinically useful. Validators and treatment still differ between them (p. 408).
- **Cognitive-component disorders** (OCD, BDD, hoarding) all carry an **insight specifier** (p. 410).
- **BFRB disorders** (trichotillomania, excoriation) are *not* driven by obsessions or
  preoccupations. Anxiety or boredom may precede them, tension may build beforehand, and relief or
  gratification may follow. Awareness ranges from **focused** to **automatic**, and many people
  show a mix (p. 409).
- **Etiological categories:** substance/medication-induced and due to another medical condition
  (AMC). Neither has a minimum duration. Both depend on temporal and physiological linkage.
- **No minimum durations** apply anywhere in the chapter. Thresholds rest on time consumed (OCD:
  e.g., >1 h/day), distress or impairment, and "persistent"/"recurrent" wording.

| Disorder | Core | Distinguishing hook |
|---|---|---|
| OCD | Obsessions and/or compulsions | Content is not limited to one domain. Often both obsessions and compulsions |
| BDD | Appearance preoccupation with flaws others see as absent or slight, plus repetitive acts | Focus is appearance only. Insight is often poor or absent. High suicidality |
| Hoarding | Difficulty discarding, perceived need to save, active living areas cluttered | Saving is intentional. Distress comes when items are discarded |
| Trichotillomania | Pulling → hair loss, repeated attempts to stop | No obsession. Not cosmetic |
| Excoriation | Picking → lesions, repeated attempts to stop | Not cosmetic (BDD), not intended self-harm (NSSI), not delusional |

## TR changes and notable points in the text
- **ICD-10-CM codes are disorder-specific:** OCD **F42.2**, hoarding **F42.3**, excoriation **F42.4**,
  other specified **F42.8**, unspecified **F42.9**, BDD **F45.22**, trichotillomania **F63.3**,
  due to AMC **F06.8**.
- **Substance-induced codes** follow the TR scheme. The 4th character is 1 for mild use disorder,
  2 for moderate/severe, and 9 for no use disorder. Only **stimulant (F15.x88)**, **cocaine (F14.x88)**
  and **other/unknown (F19.x88)** are listed (p. 438).
- **Other specified examples** now include **olfactory reference disorder**, **shubo-kyofu** and
  **koro** alongside obsessional jealousy, BDD-like presentations and other BFRB disorder
  (pp. 445–446).
- **Suicide sections** for OCD and BDD give updated data. OCD: Swedish registry, OR 9.8 for suicide
  death. BDD: meta-analysis, pooled OR 3.87 for suicidal ideation (pp. 415, 422).
- **BDD by proxy** and **excessive "selfies"** appear in the BDD text (p. 420).
- **PANS/PANDAS and Sydenham's chorea** are covered under the due-to-AMC text (p. 443).

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
**Insight specifier** (OCD, BDD, hoarding):
- **With good or fair insight:** recognizes the beliefs are definitely or probably not true, or
  may or may not be true. For hoarding: recognizes the beliefs and behaviors are problematic.
- **With poor insight:** thinks the beliefs are probably true. For hoarding: *mostly* convinced
  they are not problematic despite contrary evidence.
- **With absent insight/delusional beliefs:** *completely* convinced the beliefs are true (or, for
  hoarding, not problematic).
- **Key rule:** absent insight/delusional beliefs → do **not** add a psychotic disorder
  diagnosis. The exception is delusional content that goes beyond the disorder's own themes. The
  text's example is a BDD patient who believes their food is poisoned (p. 410).

Other specifiers:
- OCD: **tic-related**, for a current or past tic disorder.
- BDD: **with muscle dysmorphia**.
- Hoarding: **with excessive acquisition**.
- Substance-induced: onset specifiers.
- Due to AMC: symptom-predominance specifiers.

Trichotillomania and excoriation have **no specifiers**. No disorder in the chapter has a
DSM-defined severity rating.

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **Ask "what drives the behavior?"**
  - Obsession → OCD.
  - Appearance concern → BDD. This covers cosmetic picking, plucking or hair removal.
  - Need to save → hoarding.
  - Urge or tension without an obsession → trichotillomania or excoriation.
  - Delusion or tactile hallucination → psychotic disorder.
  - Intent to self-harm → NSSI.
  - Early-onset developmental stereotypy → stereotypic movement disorder.
- **OCD vs. GAD:** GAD worries are about real-life concerns. Obsessions are often odd or magical
  and come with linked compulsions.
- **OCD vs. depressive rumination:** rumination is mood-congruent, not felt as intrusive, and has
  no compulsions.
- **OCD vs. OCPD:** OCPD has no intrusive obsessions. It is a pervasive pattern of perfectionism
  and control. Both diagnoses can be given.
- **"Compulsive" gambling, sex or substance use** is pleasurable. The person resists it only
  because of its consequences, so it is not an OCD compulsion.
- **Hoarding inside OCD:** saving driven by OCD fears (harm, incompleteness, contamination) or by
  avoidance of rituals → OCD only. Bizarre items (trash, feces, nails) point to OCD. If severe
  hoarding is independent of the OCD, give both.
- **BDD vs. eating disorder:** concern about fat or weight in someone who meets eating disorder
  criteria belongs to the eating disorder. If both are present, give both diagnoses.
- **Delusional conviction** within the disorder's theme stays an OCRD with the absent-insight
  specifier. It is *not* delusional disorder.
- **Other BFRBs** (nail biting, lip biting, cheek chewing) occur alongside trichotillomania and
  excoriation. Code them as other specified OCRD (other body-focused repetitive behavior disorder).
- **Acute onset, late onset or atypical features** → rule out substance or medical causes, e.g.,
  striatal infarct, Huntington's, Sydenham's chorea, PANS.
- **Cross-screening:** OCD is ~12% prevalent in schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorder and elevated in
  bipolar disorder, eating disorders, BDD and Tourette's. Assess for it in those disorders (p. 418).
