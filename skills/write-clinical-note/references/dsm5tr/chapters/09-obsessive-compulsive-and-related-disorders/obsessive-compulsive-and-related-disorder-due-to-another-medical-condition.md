# Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** OCRD-type symptoms (obsessions, compulsions, appearance preoccupation, hoarding,
> pulling, picking, other BFRBs) that are best explained as the **direct pathophysiological
> effect** of a medical condition. Evidence must come from history, exam or labs. Classic causes are
> **striatal damage** (infarct, Huntington's) and **Sydenham's chorea**. Abrupt pediatric onset
> raises the question of PANDAS/PANS. **Acute onset, late onset or atypical symptoms** should
> prompt a medical work-up. Code the medical condition first. (pp. 441–444)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Obsessions, compulsions, appearance preoccupation, hoarding, skin picking, hair pulling,
  other BFRBs or other OCRD-type symptoms **predominate**.
- **B.** History, exam or labs show the disturbance is the **direct pathophysiological
  consequence** of another medical condition.
- **C.** Not better explained by another mental disorder.
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.

## Specifiers & severity
Specify the predominant presentation:
- **With obsessive-compulsive disorder–like symptoms**
- **With appearance preoccupations**
- **With hoarding symptoms**
- **With hair-pulling symptoms**
- **With skin-picking symptoms**

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| [medical condition code] | List it first, immediately before F06.8 |
| F06.8 | OCRD due to [named medical condition] |

Example: **"I69.398 cerebral infarction; F06.8 obsessive-compulsive and related disorder due to
cerebral infarction."** Put the medical condition's name in the disorder name.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The medical condition must be **present at the onset** of the OCRD symptoms.
- A plausible **pathophysiological mechanism** must link them, and it must be the best explanation.
- **Supporting clues** (no infallible rules exist):
  - a clear **temporal association** between onset, exacerbation or remission of the condition and
    of the symptoms
  - **atypical features** for a primary OCRD (atypical age at onset or course)
  - **literature support** for a known mechanism, e.g., striatal damage from cerebral infarction
- It must not be better explained by a primary OCRD, a substance-induced OCRD or another mental
  disorder.
- **Sydenham's chorea** (the neurological form of rheumatic fever after **Group A strep**):
  - Motor plus nonmotor symptoms. Nonmotor: obsessions, compulsions, attention deficits, emotional
    lability.
  - May come with carditis or arthritis.
  - OCD-like presentation → **diagnose OCRD due to AMC**.
- **PANDAS:** sudden-onset obsessions, compulsions and/or tics with other acute neuropsychiatric
  symptoms after GAS infection, *without* chorea, carditis or arthritis.
- **PANS** is the broader term, because other infections or insults can cause the same picture:
  - **abrupt, dramatic onset** of OC symptoms **or severely restricted food intake**
  - plus other neuropsychiatric symptoms
  - assessment guidelines exist

## Associated features
Striatal-damage conditions such as **cerebral infarction** and **Huntington's disease** can present
with OCRD symptoms.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- The course generally **follows the underlying illness**.
- **Diagnostic markers:** labs and/or medical exam are needed to confirm the medical condition.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Delirium:** not diagnosed separately if symptoms occur only during delirium.
- **Major NCD:** can be diagnosed **in addition to** dementia if the OC symptoms come from the
  dementing process and are **prominent**.
- **Mixed presentation:** if mood and OC symptoms are both due to a medical condition, the specific
  "due to AMC" diagnosis depends on which symptoms **predominate**.
- **Substance/medication-induced OCRD:** consider it with recent or prolonged substance or
  psychoactive medication use, withdrawal, or toxin exposure.
  - Urine or blood drug screens are helpful.
  - Symptoms **within 4 weeks** of intoxication, withdrawal or medication use especially suggest a
    substance cause.
- **Primary OCRD:** no specific, direct physiological mechanism can be shown. Acute onset, late
  onset or atypical symptoms → thorough work-up.
- **Illness anxiety disorder:** preoccupation with having or getting an illness; a medical
  condition may or may not be present.
- **Associated feature of another mental disorder:** OC symptoms within schizophrenia or anorexia
  nervosa.
- **Other specified or unspecified OCRD:** use when it's unclear whether the symptoms are primary,
  substance-induced or due to AMC.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Criterion B evidence:**
  - Name the medical condition and the date of diagnosis or onset.
  - Record the temporal link (e.g., symptoms began 2 weeks after stroke; abrupt onset after strep
    pharyngitis).
  - Record actual supporting medical findings (e.g., a demonstrated striatal lesion or chorea) and the basis for a causal judgment. Infection history or an isolated test result alone does not establish the full psychiatric diagnosis.
- **Atypical features:** onset age outside the usual range, abrupt onset, absence of family or
  personal OCRD history.
- **Predominant symptom type** → choose the specifier.
- **Rule-outs:** delirium (attention/awareness, fluctuation, cognition, and clinical course), substance or medication timeline,
  toxicology, prior primary OCRD history.
- **Coding sequence:** medical code, then F06.8 with the named condition.
- **Pediatric abrupt onset:** document infection history, accompanying symptoms (tics, food
  restriction, emotional lability, ADHD-like features), and referral for medical assessment.
- **Phrasing example:** "New-onset checking and contamination rituals in a 62-year-old beginning
  weeks after right caudate infarct on MRI; no prior psychiatric history; alert and oriented."
