# Other Specified and Unspecified Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder

> **Spark note:** These are residual categories for OCRD-type symptoms that cause distress or
> impairment but don't meet full criteria for any disorder in the class. Use **other specified
> (F42.8)** when you state the reason, e.g., "other specified OCRD, obsessional jealousy." Use
> **unspecified (F42.9)** when you don't, or can't for lack of information, e.g., in the ER. The
> category includes **other body-focused repetitive behavior disorder** (nail biting,
> lip biting, cheek chewing) coded alongside trichotillomania or excoriation. The category also
> covers uncertain etiology, i.e., unclear whether primary, substance-induced or due to AMC.
> (pp. 409–410, 444–446)

## Criteria at a glance
- OCRD-type symptoms **predominate** and cause clinically significant distress or impairment.
- They do **not** meet full criteria for any OCRD.
- **Other specified:** the clinician records the specific reason.
- **Unspecified:** the clinician chooses not to give a reason, or there is insufficient
  information.

## Coding
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| F42.8 | Other specified obsessive-compulsive and related disorder. Record "other specified obsessive-compulsive and related disorder" + reason |
| F42.9 | Unspecified obsessive-compulsive and related disorder. No reason given, or insufficient information |

## Other specified examples (pp. 445–446)
1. **Body dysmorphic–like disorder with actual flaws**
   - Like BDD, but the flaws are **clearly observable**, more than slight.
   - The preoccupation is clearly excessive and causes distress or impairment.
2. **Body dysmorphic–like disorder without repetitive behaviors**
   - Meets BDD criteria except the person has **never** done repetitive behaviors or mental acts
     (fails BDD Criterion B).
3. **Other body-focused repetitive behavior disorder**
   - Recurrent BFRBs **other than hair pulling or skin picking**, e.g., **nail biting, lip biting,
     cheek chewing**.
   - Requires **repeated attempts to decrease or stop** and distress or impairment.
   - Excludes presentations that meet excoriation criteria.
   - Common comorbid add-on in trichotillomania and excoriation.
4. **Obsessional jealousy**
   - **Nondelusional** preoccupation with a partner's perceived infidelity.
   - May lead to repetitive behaviors or mental acts, such as checking or reviewing.
   - Causes distress or impairment.
   - Not better explained by **delusional disorder, jealous type** or **paranoid personality
     disorder**.
5. **Olfactory reference disorder (olfactory reference syndrome)**
   - Persistent preoccupation with the belief of emitting a foul or offensive **body odor** that
     others don't notice or barely notice.
   - Repetitive or excessive responses: checking for odor, excessive showering, reassurance
     seeking, camouflaging.
   - Causes distress or impairment.
   - Known in Japan as **jikoshu-kyofu**, a variant of taijin kyofusho.
   - Excluded from BDD by the BDD differential.
6. **Shubo-kyofu**
   - A taijin kyofusho variant similar to BDD: excessive fear of having a **bodily deformity**.
7. **Koro**
   - Related to dhat syndrome.
   - **Sudden, intense anxiety** that the penis (or vulva and nipples) will **retract into the
     body**, possibly causing death.
   - Often epidemic in Southeast Asia.
   - Focus is on **death**, not ugliness (BDD differential, p. 424).

## Other uses (from chapter intro and differentials)
- **Atypical presentation or uncertain etiology:** insufficient evidence to tell substance-induced
  from due to AMC from primary → other specified or unspecified (p. 441, p. 444).
- **Specific syndromes not listed in Section II** (p. 410).

## Differential diagnosis
- Make sure full criteria aren't met for BDD, trichotillomania, excoriation, OCD or hoarding.
- Obsessional jealousy vs. **delusional disorder, jealous type**: decide by **degree of
  conviction**. Unlike other OCRDs, the jealousy example requires nondelusional preoccupation. Also
  rule out **paranoid PD**.
- BDD with actual flaws vs. a normal reaction to a visible defect: the preoccupation must be
  *clearly excessive* and impairing.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Other specified:** always write the reason in the diagnosis line, e.g., "F42.8 other specified
  obsessive-compulsive and related disorder, other body-focused repetitive behavior disorder (nail
  biting)."
- **Other BFRB disorder:**
  - Behavior, frequency and physical sequelae (damaged nail beds, lip or cheek lesions).
  - Repeated attempts to stop.
  - Distress or impairment.
- **Obsessional jealousy:**
  - Document that the belief is held with doubt (nondelusional).
  - Record checking behaviors (phone, location, interrogation) and relationship impact.
  - Screen for intimate partner violence risk as clinically indicated.
- **Olfactory reference:** document whether an odor was actually assessed and what was observed; record showering and checking
  frequency, avoidance, and conviction level.
- **Unspecified:** note why the information is limited (e.g., "ED evaluation; collateral
  unavailable") and plan to clarify.
