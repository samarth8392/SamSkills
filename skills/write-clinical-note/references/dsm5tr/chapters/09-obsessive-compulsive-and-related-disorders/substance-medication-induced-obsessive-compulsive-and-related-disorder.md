# Substance/Medication-Induced Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder

> **Spark note:** OCRD-type symptoms (obsessions, compulsions, skin picking, hair pulling, other
> BFRBs) that dominate the picture and are caused by a substance or medication. They start during
> or soon after intoxication or withdrawal, or after medication exposure or withdrawal. The
> substance must be capable of causing them. Typical culprits are **stimulants and cocaine**.
> **Don't** diagnose it if symptoms preceded the substance use or persist about 1 month or more after
> acute intoxication or withdrawal. The comorbid substance use disorder is folded into the single
> code, not coded separately. Very rare. (pp. 437–441)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Obsessions, compulsions, skin picking, hair pulling, other BFRBs or other OCRD-type
  symptoms **predominate**.
- **B.** History, exam or labs show **both**:
  1. symptoms arose **during or soon after** substance intoxication or withdrawal, or after
     exposure to or withdrawal from a medication
  2. the substance or medication **can produce** these symptoms
- **C.** Not better explained by an independent (non-induced) OCRD. Evidence for an independent
  disorder:
  - symptoms **preceded** substance or medication use
  - symptoms **persist a substantial time (~1 month)** after acute withdrawal or severe
    intoxication ends
  - other evidence, e.g., a history of recurrent episodes unrelated to substances
- **D.** Not exclusively during **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note on intoxication/withdrawal:** make this diagnosis in addition to intoxication or
  withdrawal only when Criterion A symptoms **predominate and are severe enough to need clinical
  attention** on their own.

## Specifiers & severity
Specify the onset. Use Table 1 of the Substance-Related chapter for which onsets apply to each class.
- **With onset during intoxication:** intoxication criteria are met and symptoms develop during
  intoxication.
- **With onset during withdrawal:** withdrawal criteria are met and symptoms develop during or
  shortly after withdrawal.
- **With onset after medication use:** symptoms start when the medication is started, when its use
  changes, or during withdrawal from it.

## Coding
The 4th character encodes a comorbid use disorder of the **same class**:
- **1** = mild use disorder
- **2** = moderate or severe use disorder
- **9** = no use disorder, e.g., after one heavy use

**Do not add a separate same-class SUD code already represented by this induced-disorder code.** Other independently assessed substance classes are not prohibited.

| Substance | With mild use disorder | With moderate/severe use disorder | Without use disorder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amphetamine-type substance (or other stimulant) | F15.188 | F15.288 | F15.988 |
| Cocaine | F14.188 | F14.288 | F14.988 |
| Other (or unknown) substance | F19.188 | F19.288 | F19.988 |

**Recording procedure**
1. Write the comorbid SUD first, with its severity.
2. Then "with [substance]-induced obsessive-compulsive and related disorder."
3. Then the onset specifier.

- Example with a use disorder: **"F14.288 severe cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced
  obsessive-compulsive and related disorder, with onset during intoxication"** (skin picking during
  intoxication).
- Example without one: **"F15.988 amphetamine-induced obsessive-compulsive and related disorder,
  with onset during intoxication."**
- Use the **F19** codes for substances outside the listed classes (e.g., **ropinirole**) or when the
  class is unknown.
- If several substances contribute, list each one separately.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- If caused by a prescribed treatment, onset must occur **while the patient is taking the
  medication**.
- After the medication stops, symptoms usually improve or remit within **days to weeks**, depending
  on half-life and any withdrawal.
- Don't diagnose if onset preceded use, or if symptoms persist **usually >1 month** after severe
  intoxication or withdrawal.

## Associated features
- Obsessions, compulsions, hair pulling, skin picking and BFRBs occur with intoxication from
  **stimulants (including cocaine)** and other or unknown substances.
- **Heavy metals and toxins** can also cause them.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
Prevalence is **very rare** in the US general population, and data are very limited.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Substance intoxication or withdrawal:**
  - Usually the intoxication or withdrawal diagnosis is enough.
  - Use the induced-OCRD diagnosis when symptoms **exceed what is usual** for intoxication or
    withdrawal and need clinical attention on their own.
- **Primary OCRD:**
  - Weigh onset, course and other factors. For drugs of abuse, there must be evidence of use or
    intoxication from history, exam or labs.
  - A primary disorder may *precede* the substance use.
  - An **atypical age at onset** suggests a substance cause.
  - Persistence of **≥~1 month** after intoxication, or a prior OCRD history → primary OCRD.
- **OCRD due to another medical condition:**
  - If the medical condition causes the symptoms (rather than its treatment) → due to AMC.
  - Changing or stopping a medication can clarify the cause.
  - If both the condition and a substance contribute → give **both** diagnoses.
  - If it's unclear whether the cause is a substance, a medical condition or neither → **other
    specified or unspecified OCRD**.
- **Delirium:** symptoms only during delirium are an associated feature and are not diagnosed
  separately.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Timeline (Criterion B/C):**
  - Date of first use or medication start, dose changes, last use.
  - Date symptoms started.
  - Any pre-existing OCRD symptoms before use.
  - Whether symptoms persist ≥1 month after abstinence.
- **Evidence of the substance:** self-report, collateral, **urine or blood toxicology**, physical
  signs of stimulant use.
- **Symptom description:** e.g., stimulant-associated skin picking ("punding"-like repetitive acts
  are not named in the text; describe the behavior itself). Note whether it is out of proportion to
  ordinary intoxication.
- **Delirium exclusion:** assess acute/fluctuating attention and awareness, cognition, medical context, and symptom course. Orientation alone does not exclude delirium; determine whether OCRD symptoms occur exclusively within it.
- **SUD severity:** count SUD criteria to choose the 4th character (1/2/9). Do not list the SUD
  separately.
- **Medication cases:** name the agent (e.g., ropinirole → F19.x88). Record any medication-management plan actually made by the treating clinician and the reassessment plan; do not imply a dose change occurred if it did not.
- **Phrasing example:** "Repetitive skin picking began during a 3-day cocaine binge, with no prior
  history; urine tox positive for cocaine; oriented ×4; reassess for persistence after 4 weeks of
  abstinence."
