# Body Dysmorphic Disorder

> **Spark note:** BDD is preoccupation with perceived appearance flaws that others cannot see or
> think are slight. It comes with repetitive behaviors or mental acts (checking, comparing,
> camouflaging, grooming, reassurance seeking) and causes distress or impairment. Insight is poor
> on average, and **one-third or more** are currently delusional. That is still BDD with absent
> insight, **not** delusional disorder. Suicidality is high: ~4× the odds of suicidal ideation.
> Cosmetic treatment rarely helps and can make things worse. (pp. 418–422)

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Preoccupation with ≥1 perceived defect or flaw in appearance that is not observable or
  looks slight to others.
- **B.** At *some point* in the course, the person has done repetitive behaviors (mirror checking,
  grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking) or mental acts (comparing with others) in response
  to the concern.
- **C.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **D.** Not better explained by body fat or weight concerns in someone who meets eating disorder
  criteria.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With muscle dysmorphia:** preoccupied with the idea that their build is too small or not
  muscular enough.
  - Use it **even if other body areas are also involved**, which is common.
  - It occurs almost exclusively in males.
  - The body is actually normal-looking or very muscular.
  - Most diet, exercise or lift weights excessively, sometimes to the point of injury.
  - Some use anabolic-androgenic steroids.
- **Insight** about BDD beliefs (e.g., "I look ugly," "I look deformed"):
  - **With good or fair insight:** the beliefs are definitely or probably untrue, or may or may
    not be true.
  - **With poor insight:** the beliefs are probably true.
  - **With absent insight/delusional beliefs:** completely convinced their view is accurate and
    undistorted.
- **On average insight is poor**, and **≥1/3** are currently delusional.
- Delusional BDD carries greater morbidity, e.g., suicidality. This seems to reflect greater BDD
  severity rather than delusionality itself.
- **Severity:** no formal DSM severity specifier.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Formerly called dysmorphophobia.** Perceived flaws range from "unattractive" or "not right" to
  "hideous" or "like a monster."
- **Common sites:**
  - skin: acne, scars, lines, wrinkles, paleness
  - hair: thinning, or excessive body or facial hair
  - nose
- **Any area can be involved:** eyes, teeth, weight, stomach, breasts, legs, face, lips, chin,
  eyebrows, genitals, or asymmetry.
- **Preoccupation takes 3–8 h/day on average.** It is intrusive, unwanted and hard to resist.
- **Repetitive behaviors** are driven and not pleasurable, and they may increase anxiety. Examples:
  - comparing; mirror and reflection checking; excessive **selfies**
  - grooming: combing, shaving, plucking
  - reassurance seeking; touching the area
  - excessive exercise or weight lifting; seeking cosmetic procedures
  - tanning; changing clothes; compulsive shopping for beauty products
- **Skin picking** to "fix" skin is common and can cause damage, infection or ruptured vessels.
- **Camouflaging** is very common and may itself be repetitive: makeup, hats, rearranging hair.
- **Usually both distress and impairment** are present.
- **BDD by proxy:** preoccupation with perceived defects in *another* person's appearance, usually
  a partner, sometimes a parent, child, sibling or stranger.

## Associated features
- **Ideas or delusions of reference** are common: others notice, mock or take special note of their
  appearance.
- High anxiety, social anxiety and avoidance, depressed mood, neuroticism, rejection sensitivity,
  perfectionism. Low extraversion and low self-esteem.
- **Elevated hostility and aggression.**
- **Shame and secrecy:** reluctant to disclose.
- **Most receive cosmetic treatment**, mainly dermatologic or surgical; dental and electrolysis also
  occur. Some operate on themselves.
  - Response is poor and symptoms may worsen.
  - Some sue or become violent toward the surgeon or clinician.
- **Cognitive and perceptual differences:**
  - deficits in emotion recognition, attention and executive function
  - a bias toward reading faces and ambiguous situations as negative or threatening
  - **detail-focused rather than holistic visual processing**

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Point prevalence**
- US adults **2.4%** (women 2.5%, men 2.2%); international studies 1.7–2.9%.
- **Clinical settings:**
  - dermatology 11–13%
  - cosmetic surgery 13–15%
  - **rhinoplasty 20%**
  - jaw surgery 11%
  - orthodontia/cosmetic dentistry 5–10%
- Higher in girls and young women among adolescents and students.

**Development and course**
- Onset: mean 16–17 y, median 15, mode 12–13. **Two-thirds start before 18.**
- Subclinical symptoms begin around 12–13 y and usually progress gradually. Abrupt onset also
  occurs.
- Usually chronic; improvement is likely with evidence-based treatment.
- **Onset before 18:** more comorbidity and more gradual onset.
- Occurs in older adults, but little is known about BDD in that age group.

**Risk factors**
- **Environmental:** childhood neglect, abuse and trauma; teasing.
- **Genetic:** elevated in first-degree relatives of people with OCD. Heritability **37–49%**
  (twins), possibly higher in women. Shares genetic vulnerability with OCD and also has
  BDD-specific genetic influences.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- **Culture:**
  - Sex ratio, body areas, behaviors and impairment are consistent across cultures.
  - In collectivistic settings such as Japan, the focus may be on fear of *offending others*.
  - Culture-specific concerns: eyelids in Japan; muscle dysmorphia in the West.
  - **Shubo-kyofu**, a BDD-like subtype of taijin kyofusho, means "the phobia of a deformed body."
- **Sex/gender:**
  - Muscle dysmorphia occurs almost only in males. Males have more comorbid SUD; females more
    comorbid eating disorders.
  - Most features are similar across sexes, including severity, suicidality and cosmetic treatment.
  - **Males** more often focus on genitals, body build and thinning hair.
  - **Females** more often focus on weight, breasts or chest, buttocks, legs, hips, and excess body
    or facial hair.

## Suicide risk
- Meta-analysis of 17 studies, compared with healthy controls *and* with patients with eating
  disorders, OCD or anxiety disorders: **suicidal ideation ~4× (pooled OR 3.87)**; **attempts ~2.6×
  (pooled OR 2.57)**.
- German population studies: SI 19% vs. 3% and 31.0% vs. 3.5%; suicidal behavior 7% vs. 1% and
  22.2% vs. 2.1%.
- **Risk rises with severity** and is independent of comorbidity, though some comorbidities add
  risk.
- Many attribute their SI or attempts mainly to appearance concerns.
- **General suicide-death risk factors are common in BDD:** high SI and attempt rates,
  unemployment, perceived abuse, low self-esteem, comorbid MDD, eating disorders and SUD.

## Functional consequences
- Nearly all have impaired psychosocial functioning, from moderate (avoiding some situations) to
  incapacitating (**housebound**).
- Quality of life is markedly poor, and worse with greater severity.
- Most have job, school or role impairment, often severe.
- **~20% of youths drop out of school** mainly because of BDD.
- High rates of psychiatric hospitalization in adolescents and adults.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Normal appearance concerns:**
  - BDD is excessive, time-consuming and hard to resist, and causes distress or impairment.
  - Clearly noticeable defects are *not* BDD; use other specified OCRD, "with actual flaws."
  - *Exception:* lesions caused by BDD skin picking still count as BDD.
- **Eating disorders:**
  - Fat or weight concern in someone with an eating disorder belongs to the eating disorder.
  - Weight concerns can occur in BDD.
  - If both disorders are present, diagnose both.
- **OCD:** BDD focuses only on appearance and has poorer insight, more depression and more SI.
- **Excoriation disorder:** picking *to improve the look* of skin → BDD.
- **Trichotillomania:** hair removal *to fix perceived hair defects* → BDD.
- **Illness anxiety disorder:** no fear of disease; somatization is not elevated.
- **MDD:** BDD has prominent appearance preoccupation and repetitive behaviors. Depression is often
  secondary. Diagnose BDD in depressed patients who meet criteria.
- **Social anxiety, agoraphobia, avoidant PD:** in BDD, avoidance is driven by perceived defects
  and fear of being seen as ugly, ridiculed or rejected.
- **GAD:** BDD worry is limited to appearance.
- **Psychotic disorders:**
  - Delusional appearance beliefs → BDD with absent insight, not delusional disorder.
  - Appearance-related ideas or delusions of reference are common in BDD.
  - BDD has no disorganization or other psychotic symptoms.
- **Gender dysphoria:** discomfort with primary or secondary sex characteristics is not BDD.
- **Olfactory reference disorder:** belief in emitting body odor → other specified OCRD, not BDD.
- **Body integrity dysphoria** (ICD-11, not DSM-5): wants an amputation, but the limb is not seen as
  ugly, only as not belonging.
- **Koro:** fear that genitals or nipples are retracting, with focus on *death*, not ugliness.
- **Dysmorphic concern:** a broader non-DSM construct of overconcern with slight or imagined flaws.

## Comorbidity
- **MDD is the most common.** It usually starts *after* BDD.
- Social anxiety disorder, OCD and substance-related disorders are common. In muscle dysmorphia
  this includes anabolic-androgenic steroids.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Criterion A:**
  - Name the body areas and the patient's words ("my nose is deformed").
  - Note that the examiner observes no defect or only a slight one.
  - Record hours/day preoccupied.
- **Criterion B:** list behaviors and frequency, e.g., mirror checking (count/time), selfies,
  camouflage (makeup, hats, posture), reassurance seeking, comparing, grooming or picking, cosmetic
  consultations or procedures.
- **Criterion C:** document avoidance (social, school, work), missed days, housebound status and
  relationship impact.
- **Criterion D:** screen for an eating disorder. If weight or shape concerns are present, document
  whether eating disorder criteria are met.
- **Specifiers:**
  - Muscle dysmorphia: physique concerns, training or diet extremes, **steroid or supplement use**.
  - Insight: rate the degree of conviction.
- **Suicide risk assessment is essential.** Document SI, attempts, and whether they are linked to
  appearance; comorbid MDD or SUD; unemployment.
- **Ideas of reference:** document them as appearance-related (e.g., "believes strangers stare at
  his skin") and note the absence of other psychotic features.
- **Rule-outs:** eating disorder, OCD (broader content), gender dysphoria, olfactory reference
  concerns, psychosis, an actual noticeable defect.
- **MSE phrasing examples:**
  - "Appearance: heavy makeup, hat kept on throughout; repeatedly checks reflection."
  - "Thought content: fixed belief that facial skin is 'scarred and hideous'; no observable lesions;
    insight absent."
