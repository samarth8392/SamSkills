# Encopresis

> **Spark note:** Repeated passage of feces into inappropriate places (clothing, floor), **≥1×/month for ≥3 months**, at age **≥4** (developmental). Most cases are **involuntary overflow from constipation and retention**, which resolves when the constipation is treated. Soiling **without** constipation (normal stool, sometimes deposited prominently) points toward ODD or conduct disorder. Pitfall: unlike enuresis, a medical cause is **allowed only through a constipation mechanism**.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Repeated passage of feces into inappropriate places (e.g., clothing, floor), **involuntary or intentional**.
- **B.** **≥1 event per month for ≥3 months**.
- **C.** Chronological age **≥4 years** (or equivalent developmental level).
- **D.** Not attributable to a substance (e.g., **laxatives**) or another medical condition, **except through a mechanism involving constipation**.

## Specifiers & severity
**Specify whether:**
- **With constipation and overflow incontinence:** constipation is evident on **physical exam or by history**. Stool is typically (not always) **poorly formed**. Leakage ranges from infrequent to continuous, day and sometimes during sleep. Only part of the stool is passed at toileting. **Incontinence resolves after the constipation is treated.**
- **Without constipation and overflow incontinence:** no constipation on exam or by history. Stool is of **normal form and consistency**, soiling is **intermittent**, and feces may be left in a **prominent location**. Usually associated with **ODD or conduct disorder**, or may result from **anal masturbation**. Less common than the constipated subtype (p. 581–582).

There are no severity or remission specifiers. Course descriptors: **primary** (never achieved fecal continence) vs **secondary** (after a period of continence).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Involuntary** soiling is often from **constipation → impaction → retention → overflow**.
- Constipation can have **psychological** roots (anxiety about defecating in a particular place; a broader anxious or oppositional pattern → avoidance and **volitional withholding**).
- It can also have **physiological** roots: ineffective straining or **paradoxical defecation dynamics** (sphincter or pelvic floor contracts rather than relaxes).
- Other causes of constipation: **low fluid intake** and other dietary habits, **celiac disease**, **hypothyroidism**, medication side effects.
- Once established, constipation can lead to **anal fissure → painful defecation → more retention** (a vicious cycle).
- Stool consistency varies, from normal to **liquid** (overflow around retained stool) (p. 582).

## Associated features
- **Shame**. The child avoids situations such as camp or school where accidents could cause embarrassment.
- Impairment depends on effects on self-esteem, peer ostracism, and caregiver anger, punishment and rejection.
- **Smearing** may be deliberate **or accidental** (trying to clean or hide involuntary stool).
- Clearly **deliberate** incontinence → look for **ODD or conduct disorder** features.
- Many children with constipated encopresis also have **enuresis** and possibly **urinary reflux** (bladder or ureters) → chronic UTIs. These may remit when the constipation is treated (p. 582).

## Diagnostic markers
- This is a **clinical diagnosis** (history and physical). Testing is generally not required.
- **Digital rectal exam** finding impaction supports the constipated subtype. An **abdominal X-ray** showing impaction also supports it, but X-ray is not indicated for diagnosis.
- **Colonic transit study** (radiopaque markers): **retained** markers suggest the constipated subtype; **prompt evacuation** suggests the non-constipated subtype.
- **Anorectal manometry** assesses anorectal function and sensation in some children.
- With **refractory** symptoms or red flags, evaluate further to exclude medical causes (p. 583).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Most children >4 years with encopresis have the **constipated/overflow** subtype.
- **1–4%** of children in high-income countries. **2–8%** reported in some Asian countries (Iran, South Korea, Sri Lanka).
- **>4% at ages 4–6** vs **<2% at ages 10–12**. Higher with **early abuse or neglect** and in **low-income** youth (p. 582).
- Predisposing: **inadequate or inconsistent toilet training** and psychosocial stress (e.g., entering school, birth of a sibling).
- Primary vs secondary course types. It can persist with **intermittent exacerbations for years**.
- **Painful defecation** → constipation → withholding cycle.
- **Male gender** and **pre-adolescent age** are risk factors.
- Contributors: anxiety, depression, behavioral disorders, stressors (bullying, poor school performance), lower SES.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Dietary and fluid differences, **hot tropical climates** and psychosocial adversity may affect constipation, unexplained abdominal pain and retention.
- Some families may not seek care for sociocultural reasons. For example, **Turkish and Moroccan parents in the Netherlands** may not report it because of religious concerns about the impurity of urine and feces (p. 583).
- **Sex ratio is equal under age 5**. After that, boys outnumber girls from **2:1 (US) to 6:1**.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Significantly lower **health-related quality of life** and **family functioning**, especially in **older children** (p. 584).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Other medical conditions:** encopresis is appropriate with a medical condition **only if the mechanism is constipation that the medical condition cannot explain**. Fecal incontinence from **chronic diarrhea, spina bifida or anal stenosis** does **not** warrant encopresis (p. 584).
- **Substances:** soiling due to laxatives is excluded (Criterion D).
- **ODD / conduct disorder:** these are associated features (deliberate soiling), not exclusions. Diagnose both when criteria are met.

## Comorbidity
- **Enuresis** is often present, particularly in encopresis **without** constipation and overflow incontinence (p. 584). (The associated-features text also notes enuresis symptoms in many children with **constipated** encopresis.)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion A:** where soiling happens (underwear, floor, other), whether involuntary or intentional (with the evidence for each), and any smearing (deliberate vs attempts to clean or hide).
- **Criterion B:** a monthly count showing **≥1/month for ≥3 months**, from a parent diary or report.
- **Criterion C:** chronological age (≥4) or developmental age with its source.
- **Criterion D:** medical review (laxative use, chronic diarrhea, spinal or anorectal anomalies, celiac disease, thyroid) and pediatric exam findings.
- **Subtype evidence:** **constipation** history (infrequent, hard or large stools; painful defecation; withholding posturing; clogged toilets; abdominal pain), exam or imaging findings (rectal impaction, X-ray, transit study). For the **non-constipated** subtype, note normal stool, intermittent soiling, prominent placement, and ODD/CD features.
- **Course:** primary vs secondary. Precipitating stressors (school entry, new sibling, bullying, abuse or neglect).
- **Co-occurring:** enuresis, UTIs, anxiety, depression, behavior problems.
- **Psychosocial impact:** shame, avoidance of school or camp, peer teasing, punitive caregiver responses. Screen for maltreatment where indicated.
- **Phrases:** "Daily soft-stool leakage into underwear; parent reports large painful BMs every 5–7 days and withholding behaviors; PCP palpated fecal mass (with constipation and overflow incontinence)." "Formed stools found behind sofa ~2×/month for 4 months; co-occurring oppositional behavior; no constipation (without constipation subtype)."
