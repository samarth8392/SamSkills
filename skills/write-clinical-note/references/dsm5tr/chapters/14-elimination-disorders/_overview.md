# Elimination Disorders — Overview

> **Spark note:** Two disorders, **enuresis** (urine; age ≥5) and **encopresis** (feces; age ≥4). Both are usually first diagnosed in childhood. Each can be voluntary or involuntary, and the age floor is **developmental** age, not just chronological age. They often co-occur. Enuresis is sorted by **when** wetting happens (night vs day). Encopresis is sorted by **constipation with overflow** vs none. Most encopresis is constipation-driven, so treat the constipation (p. 577, 582).

## What unites the chapter / how disorders differ
- **Common thread:** inappropriate voiding of urine or feces, typically diagnosed in childhood or adolescence. Minimum ages use **developmental (mental) age** for children with delays (p. 577).
- **Enuresis (F98.0):** repeated voiding into bed or clothes. Either **≥2×/week for ≥3 consecutive months** OR clinically significant distress or impairment. Age **≥5**. Subtypes: nocturnal only, diurnal only, nocturnal and diurnal.
- **Encopresis (F98.1):** repeated passage of feces into inappropriate places, **≥1×/month for ≥3 months**. Age **≥4**. Subtypes: with vs without constipation and overflow incontinence.
- **Medical exclusions differ:** enuresis excludes any substance or medical cause. Encopresis allows a medical cause **only through a constipation mechanism**.
- **Course descriptors (not formal specifiers):** "primary" (continence never achieved) vs "secondary" (onset after a period of continence) for both.

## TR changes / notable updates in the text
- **ICD-10-CM codes for the residual categories** are split by symptom type:
  - Other specified, urinary: **N39.498**. Other specified, fecal: **R15.9**.
  - Unspecified, urinary: **R32**. Unspecified, fecal: **R15.9**.
  - These are non-F (medical) codes.
- The text is updated on **sleep-disordered breathing**: about 50% of enuretic children with proven SDB become dry after adenotonsillectomy (p. 581).
- The text is updated on **bladder subtypes**: urge incontinence (detrusor instability) vs voiding postponement (p. 578).
- Encopresis **diagnostic markers**: digital rectal exam, abdominal X-ray, colonic transit (radiopaque marker) studies, anorectal manometry (p. 583).
- Culture section examples: traditional Chinese medicine attributes enuresis to kidney yang deficiency. Turkish and Moroccan families in the Netherlands may under-report encopresis because of religious concerns about impurity (p. 579, 583).

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **Medical first:** for enuresis, rule out neurogenic bladder, structural anomalies (posterior urethral valves, ectopic ureter), polyuric conditions (diabetes mellitus or insipidus), acute UTI, tethered cord, spina bifida and seizures. For encopresis, rule out chronic diarrhea, spina bifida and anal stenosis.
- **Exception:** a medical condition or medication does **not** block the diagnosis if incontinence was regularly present **before** it started, or **persists after** appropriate treatment (enuresis, p. 580).
- **Medication-induced enuresis** (antipsychotics, diuretics, drugs causing constipation, polyuria or executive-function change): note it as a **medication side effect**, not enuresis alone.
- **Intentional soiling** with normal stool, often deposited prominently, suggests **encopresis without constipation**. It is associated with **ODD or conduct disorder** (or anal masturbation).
- **Smearing** can be deliberate or an accidental attempt to clean or hide involuntary soiling. Do not assume intent.
- **Enuresis and encopresis co-occur**. Constipation drives both. Many constipated encopretic children also have enuresis, urinary reflux and recurrent UTIs.

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- Enuresis: **Specify whether** nocturnal only, diurnal only, or nocturnal and diurnal.
- Encopresis: **Specify whether** with or without constipation and overflow incontinence.
- There are no severity or remission specifiers in this chapter.
- Residual categories: record "other specified elimination disorder" + reason (e.g., "low-frequency enuresis"). The code depends on urinary vs fecal symptoms (see table above).
