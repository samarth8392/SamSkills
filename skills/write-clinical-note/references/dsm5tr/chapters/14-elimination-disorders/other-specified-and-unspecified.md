# Other Specified and Unspecified Elimination Disorder

> **Spark note:** Use these for elimination symptoms that cause clinically significant distress or impairment and **predominate**, but do not meet full criteria for enuresis or encopresis (e.g., wetting below the frequency threshold). The ICD-10-CM code depends on **urinary vs fecal** symptoms. These are **non-F (medical) codes**, and fecal symptoms use **R15.9** for both categories.

## Criteria at a glance
- Symptoms characteristic of an elimination disorder.
- They cause clinically significant distress or impairment (social, occupational, other) and **predominate**.
- **Full criteria are not met** for enuresis or encopresis.
- **Other specified:** the clinician states the specific reason, recording "other specified elimination disorder" + reason (e.g., **"low-frequency enuresis"**).
- **Unspecified:** the clinician chooses not to specify, or information is **insufficient** (e.g., emergency room settings).

## Coding
| Category | Urinary symptoms | Fecal symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Other specified elimination disorder | **N39.498** | **R15.9** |
| Unspecified elimination disorder | **R32** | **R15.9** |

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- The only example the text gives is **"low-frequency enuresis"**. Other reasonable reasons follow from the criteria (e.g., soiling present for <3 months, or less than monthly), but they are not listed in the text.
- Enuresis Criterion B can be met by distress or impairment alone. So "low-frequency" wetting that *is* clinically significant may already qualify as **enuresis**. Check this before using the residual category.
- The medical and substance exclusions of the parent disorders still guide judgment. Medication-induced incontinence should be noted as a medication side effect.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- State which full criterion is unmet and why. For enuresis, frequency below 2×/week is **not sufficient by itself** to establish subthreshold status when clinically significant distress or impairment meets Criterion B's alternative pathway. The source nevertheless lists "low-frequency enuresis" as an example; preserve that source example without using it to override the full criteria. A fecal presentation below the monthly frequency or 3-month duration requirement is a clearer illustration of an unmet threshold, if clinically significant.
- Record the urinary vs fecal symptom type, because it sets the code.
- Document the distress or impairment that justifies a diagnosis (shame, activity avoidance, family conflict).
- Document medical evaluation and medication review.
- **Unspecified:** note what information is missing and the plan to clarify (e.g., voiding/stool diary, pediatric exam).
