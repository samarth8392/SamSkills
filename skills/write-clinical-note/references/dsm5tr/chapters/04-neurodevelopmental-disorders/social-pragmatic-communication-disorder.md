# Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder

> **Spark note:** A primary deficit in the **social use** of language and nonverbal communication — greeting, adapting to listener and context, conversational turn-taking, understanding inference and nonliteral meaning — without the restricted/repetitive behaviours of autism and not due to poor structural language or low cognition. ASD **supersedes** SPCD: always take a careful **lifetime** history of RRBs before choosing SPCD (pp. 151–154).

## Criteria at a glance (p. 152)
- **A.** Persistent difficulty in social use of verbal and nonverbal communication, with **all 4**:
  1. Deficits using communication for social purposes (greeting, sharing information) appropriately for context.
  2. Impaired ability to adjust communication to context or listener (classroom vs playground; child vs adult; avoiding overly formal language).
  3. Difficulty following conversational and storytelling rules (turn-taking, rephrasing when misunderstood, using verbal/nonverbal signals to regulate interaction).
  4. Difficulty understanding what is not explicit (inference) and nonliteral/ambiguous language (idioms, humour, metaphor, context-dependent meanings).
- **B.** Functional limits in communication, social participation, social relationships, academic achievement or occupational performance.
- **C.** Onset in early developmental period (may not fully manifest until social demands exceed capacity).
- **D.** Not attributable to another medical/neurological condition or to low structural language ability (word structure, grammar); not better explained by **ASD**, IDD, global developmental delay or another mental disorder.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 152)
- Core problem = pragmatics in naturalistic contexts: social rules of verbal/nonverbal communication, adjusting language to listener/situation, conversation and narrative rules.
- Not explained by structural language, cognitive ability or ASD.
- The ASD note (p. 156): people with marked social-communication deficits who don't meet ASD should be evaluated for SPCD.

## Associated features (pp. 152–153)
- Most common: **language impairment** — history of delayed milestones and past (if not current) structural language problems.
- May avoid social interactions.
- ADHD, emotional/behavioural problems and SLD more common.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** not given.
- **Course (p. 153):** depends on adequate speech/language → rare <4 y; identifiable by 4–5 y; milder forms may appear only in early adolescence as language/social demands grow.
- Outcome variable: some improve substantially, others persist into adulthood. Even with improvement, early pragmatic deficits can leave lasting social-relationship and behaviour problems and weaker written expression, reading comprehension and oral reading.
- **Genetic:** family history of ASD, communication disorders or SLD raises risk, including in siblings of affected children.

Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
Limits in communication, participation, relationships, academic/occupational performance (Criterion B); lasting social effects even after improvement (p. 153).

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 153–154)
- **ASD:** the primary consideration. ASD has restricted/repetitive behaviours, interests or activities; SPCD does not. RRBs may have been present only early in development → obtain comprehensive history; **past** RRBs still support ASD. Consider SPCD only if current symptoms **and** history show no RRBs meeting ASD Criterion B. Social-communication symptoms in SPCD may be milder but are qualitatively similar.
- **ADHD:** primary ADHD deficits can impair social communication and participation.
- **Social anxiety disorder:** key = timing. SPCD: never had effective social communication. Social anxiety: skills developed normally but are not used because of fear/distress.
- **IDD / GDD:** social communication may be deficient; give SPCD only if deficits **clearly exceed** intellectual limits.

## Comorbidity
Language disorder (most common associated feature), ADHD, emotional/behavioural problems, SLD (p. 153).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A1–A4:** give concrete examples for each (e.g., "does not greet or say goodbye unless prompted"; "uses same formal register with peers and teachers"; "monologues, does not repair when misunderstood"; "interprets 'break a leg' literally; misses sarcasm").
- **B:** impact on friendships, group work, workplace interactions.
- **C:** developmental history of language delay and early pragmatic problems.
- **D / rule-outs:** explicitly document **absence of current and past RRBs** (stereotypies, insistence on sameness, restricted interests, sensory reactivity) via caregiver history; structural language testing; IQ/developmental level; social anxiety history (were skills ever present?).
- MSE phrases: "speech grammatically intact but poorly adapted to listener; difficulty with turn-taking"; "literal interpretation of idioms"; "no stereotyped movements, restricted interests or sensory sensitivities reported now or historically".
