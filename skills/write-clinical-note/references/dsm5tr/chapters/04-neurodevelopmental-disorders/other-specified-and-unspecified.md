# Residual Neurodevelopmental, ADHD, Communication, Intellectual Developmental, and Tic Diagnoses

> **Spark note:** The residual categories retain the developmental/functional context but do not justify guessing which full disorder is present. Unspecified intellectual developmental disorder has a distinct restricted use: exceptional assessment barriers in people older than 5, with reassessment required (p. 142).

## Criteria at a glance
- **Unspecified IDD:** age **>5 years** and assessment of degree is difficult/impossible with locally available methods because of sensory/physical impairment, severe behavior or coexisting mental disorder. Reserve for exceptional circumstances and reassess (p. 142).
- **Unspecified communication disorder:** communication symptoms predominate with clinically significant distress/impairment; no full specific communication or other neurodevelopmental diagnosis fits; reason omitted or information insufficient (p. 154).
- **Other specified/unspecified ADHD:** impairing/distressing ADHD-like symptoms predominate without full ADHD or another specific NDD. Other specified names the reason, e.g., too few inattention symptoms; unspecified omits it or lacks information (pp. 177–178).
- **Other specified/unspecified tic disorder:** distressing/impairing tic-like symptoms without a full specific tic/NDD diagnosis. Other specified identifies the reason (e.g., onset after 18); unspecified omits it or lacks information. Known proximal medical/substance causes can lead to other specified tic disorder after evaluation. Respect previously met higher tic diagnoses (pp. 199–201, 204–205).
- **Other specified/unspecified NDD:** developmental symptoms predominate and impair important function, but no specific NDD is fully met. Use other specified with the reason and unspecified when omitted or information insufficient (pp. 205–206).

## Specifiers & severity
These are separate residual categories, not severity specifiers. Do not force ADHD, tic or intellectual-assessment exceptions into one generic category.

F88 also codes global developmental delay, which has a separate note and different indication; a shared code does not make the diagnoses interchangeable.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
The chapter's explicit other-specified NDD example is developmental disabilities associated with **prenatal alcohol exposure** (pp. 205–206). This is not permission to infer prenatal exposure from behavior, nor does this brief entry supply Section III research criteria for neurobehavioral disorder associated with prenatal alcohol exposure.

## Differential diagnosis
- Prefer a full specific diagnosis when its criteria are actually established.
- In IDD assessment, distinguish exceptional test barriers from ordinary incomplete assessment and from global developmental delay in children under 5.
- Before residual tics, obtain childhood history and examine medical, substance and functional movement alternatives.
- A skill difference from language/cultural exposure or inadequate learning opportunity is not itself a neurodevelopmental disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Name the symptom family, onset/course and concrete impairment; identify what is established versus unknown.
- For other specified, state the exact unmet criterion/reason. For unspecified, document the information limitation without asserting unassessed negative findings.
- For F79 identify the assessment barrier, methods attempted and reassessment need.
- For prenatal-alcohol association, identify the source of exposure history and the actual developmental deficits; do not invent exposure evidence.
- For tics record lifetime hierarchy and medical/substance assessment before coding.
