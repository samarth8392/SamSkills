# Intellectual Developmental Disorder (Intellectual Disability)

> **Spark note:** A developmental-period disorder needing three things: intellectual deficits confirmed by clinical assessment **and** individualized standardized IQ testing, adaptive deficits requiring ongoing support, and onset in childhood/adolescence. Severity is graded by **adaptive functioning, not IQ**. The classic error is treating an IQ score as the diagnosis (or as the severity), or assuming IDD because of a genetic syndrome (pp. 134–140).

## Criteria at a glance (p. 134)
All 3 required:
- **A. Intellectual deficits** — reasoning, problem solving, planning, abstract thinking, judgment, academic learning, learning from experience. Must be confirmed by **both** clinical assessment **and** individualized, standardized intelligence testing.
- **B. Adaptive deficits** — failure to meet developmental and sociocultural standards for personal independence and social responsibility. Without ongoing support, limits ≥1 activity of daily life (communication, social participation, independent living) across multiple environments (home, school, work, community).
- **C. Onset** of intellectual and adaptive deficits during the developmental period.
- Note on name: matches ICD-11 "Disorders of Intellectual Development"; "intellectual disability" kept in parentheses; US Rosa's Law (PL 111-256) replaced "mental retardation".

**Criterion B threshold (p. 138):** met when ≥1 domain (conceptual, social **or** practical) is impaired enough that ongoing support is needed to perform adequately across multiple environments.

## Specifiers & severity (pp. 134–136)
Severity defined by **adaptive functioning** (it determines the supports needed; IQ is less valid at the low end). Each level has its own code.

| Level | Conceptual | Social | Practical |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild (F70)** | Preschool: may be no obvious differences. School-age/adults: trouble learning reading, writing, math, time, money; support needed. Adults: impaired abstract thinking, executive function, short-term memory, functional academics; concrete approach | Immature interactions; misreads peers' cues; concrete/immature language; emotion/behaviour regulation problems noticed by peers; limited risk understanding; **gullibility**, risk of manipulation | Age-appropriate personal care; needs some support for complex daily tasks (shopping, transport, child-care/home organizing, nutrition, banking). Competitive employment possible in non-conceptual jobs; support for health/legal decisions and raising a family |
| **Moderate (F71)** | Skills lag markedly all through development; adult academics ~elementary level; support for all academic use; daily help with conceptual tasks, others may take over | Marked differences; spoken language much simpler; real friendships, sometimes romance; misreads cues; caretakers help with life decisions; work needs significant social support | Self-care (eating, dressing, toileting, hygiene) achievable in adulthood after extended teaching, reminders; household tasks with long teaching; employment in low-demand jobs with considerable support; maladaptive behaviour in a significant minority |
| **Severe (F72)** | Limited; little understanding of written language, number, quantity, time, money; extensive lifelong problem-solving support | Very limited vocabulary/grammar; single words/phrases, may use augmentative means; here-and-now focus; understands simple speech/gesture; family relationships a source of pleasure | Needs support for **all** ADLs; supervision at all times; cannot make responsible decisions about well-being; long-term teaching; maladaptive behaviour incl. self-injury in significant minority |
| **Profound (F73)** | Physical world rather than symbolic; goal-directed object use; matching/sorting; motor/sensory impairments may block use | Very limited symbolic understanding; may follow simple instructions/gestures; expresses wants largely nonverbally; enjoys familiar people | Dependent on others for all physical care, health and safety; may help with simple tasks (carrying dishes); recreation with support; sensory/physical impairments frequent barriers |

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (pp. 136–138)
- **IQ anchor:** ~2 SD or more below mean including ±5 measurement error → on a mean-100/SD-15 test, **65–75 (70 ± 5)**.
- Score confounds: practice effects; **Flynn effect** (outdated norms inflate scores); brief screens or group tests are invalid; widely discrepant subtests can invalidate the full-scale IQ; tests must be normed for sociocultural background and native language; communication/motor/sensory disorders depress scores.
- Neuropsychological profiles and cross-battery assessment are more useful than a single IQ.
- IQ alone is **insufficient**: someone scoring somewhat above 65–75 can function like a lower IQ because of severe adaptive (e.g., social judgment) deficits.
- **Adaptive domains:**
  - Conceptual (academic): memory, language, reading, writing, math reasoning, practical knowledge, problem solving, judgment in novel situations.
  - Social: awareness of others' thoughts/feelings, empathy, interpersonal communication, friendship, social judgment.
  - Practical: personal care, job responsibilities, money, recreation, behaviour self-management, task organization.
- Adaptive functioning assessed with clinical evaluation **plus** standardized, culturally appropriate measures using knowledgeable informants (parent, teacher, counselor, care provider) and the person when possible.
- When standardized testing is impossible (sensory impairment, severe problem behaviour) → unspecified IDD.
- In controlled settings (prisons, detention), seek corroboration of functioning outside the setting.
- **Comprehensive evaluation:** IQ + adaptive; genetic and nongenetic etiologies; associated medical conditions (cerebral palsy, seizures); co-occurring mental disorders. May include pre/perinatal history, 3-generation pedigree, physical exam, genetic testing (karyotype, chromosomal microarray, specific syndromes), metabolic screening, neuroimaging.

## Associated features (pp. 138)
- Heterogeneous, multiple causes.
- Problems with social judgment, risk assessment, self-management of behaviour/emotions/relationships, motivation.
- Increased accidental injury (poor awareness of danger).
- Communication deficits may predispose to disruptive/aggressive behaviour.
- **Gullibility** (naiveté, easily led) → exploitation, victimization, fraud, unintentional criminal involvement, **false confessions**, physical/sexual abuse risk. Relevant in criminal cases incl. **Atkins-type** death-penalty hearings.
- Individuals may be distressed about their own limitations — clinically important even if not impairing.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** ~10/1,000 overall; ~16/1,000 middle-income vs ~9/1,000 high-income countries; higher in youth than adults; US rates do not vary significantly by ethnoracial group (p. 138).
- **Onset:** severe forms → delayed motor, language, social milestones within first 2 years; mild forms may not be identified until school age (p. 139).
- All criteria (incl. C) can be met by history or current presentation.
- Some children <5 who later meet IDD criteria first meet criteria for **global developmental delay**.
- Genetic syndromes may have characteristic appearance (Down syndrome) or **behavioural phenotype** (Lesch-Nyhan).
- Acquired forms may be abrupt (meningitis, encephalitis, head trauma in the developmental period).
- Generally nonprogressive; some syndromes worsen then stabilize (Rett) or progress (Sanfilippo, Down syndrome). Down syndrome → high risk of NCD due to Alzheimer's in adulthood → give both diagnoses.
- Generally lifelong but severity may change; early/ongoing intervention can improve adaptive function — sometimes enough that IDD no longer applies. Hence common practice to **delay diagnosis in infants/young children** until after an intervention course.
- Key judgment: is improvement **stable, generalized new skill** (diagnosis may no longer apply) or **contingent on supports** (diagnosis still applies)?
- **Risk factors (p. 140):**
  - Prenatal: genetic syndromes (sequence variants, CNVs, chromosomal), inborn errors of metabolism, brain malformations, maternal/placental disease, alcohol, drugs, toxins, teratogens.
  - Perinatal: labour/delivery events → neonatal encephalopathy.
  - Postnatal: hypoxic-ischemic injury, TBI, infections, demyelinating disorders, seizure disorders (infantile spasms), severe chronic social deprivation, toxic-metabolic syndromes, lead/mercury.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 140)
- Occurs across ethnoracial groups; prevalence differences track environmental risk tied to SES and health-care access (e.g., Western Australia: Aboriginal children 39/1,000 vs 16/1,000 non-Aboriginal youth).
- Assess within the person's socioeconomic, ethnic, cultural, linguistic background and community.
- Cultural explanations may include supernatural causes or punishment for parental wrongdoing → shame, underreporting.
- Males > females: mild ~1.6:1, severe ~1.2:1 (wide variation); sex-linked genes, CNVs, male vulnerability to brain insult.

## Suicide risk (p. 140)
- Risk linked to **comorbid mental disorder**, **higher** intellectual/adaptive function, and recent stressors.
- Comorbid disorders may present atypically → screen for suicidal thoughts; watch for **change in behaviour**.

## Functional consequences
Not given a separate section; consequences are captured in the severity table (supports needed across conceptual/social/practical domains) and associated features (victimization, injury, legal vulnerability).

## Differential diagnosis (pp. 140–141)
- Diagnose whenever A, B and C are met; **do not assume** IDD because of a genetic/medical condition.
- **Major/mild neurocognitive disorder:** NCD = loss of previously attained function. Both can be given (Down syndrome + Alzheimer's; IDD with further loss after head injury; stabilized post-injury cognition with onset in childhood and no ongoing decline).
- **Communication disorders and SLD:** limited to communication/learning domains, no general intellectual/adaptive deficit; both diagnosed if full criteria for each are met.
- **ASD:** IDD common in ASD; testing complicated by social-communication deficits; reassess across development because early IQ in ASD is unstable.
- **Global developmental delay:** use when <5 y and severity cannot be reliably assessed.

## Comorbidity (pp. 141–142)
- Mental disorders, cerebral palsy and epilepsy at **3–4×** general-population rates.
- Most common: ADHD; depressive and bipolar disorders; anxiety disorders; ASD; stereotypic movement disorder (± self-injury); impulse-control disorders; major NCD.
- MDD can occur at all severity levels.
- **Self-injurious behaviour needs prompt attention** and may warrant a separate stereotypic movement disorder diagnosis.
- Aggression, property destruction more common with greater severity.
- More health problems incl. obesity; often cannot verbalize physical symptoms → undiagnosed illness.
- Knowledgeable informants essential to identify irritability, mood dysregulation, aggression, eating and sleep problems.

## Relationship to other classifications (p. 142)
- ICD-11: disorders of intellectual development (mild, moderate, severe, profound) — described as a developmental metasyndrome analogous to dementia in later life.
- AAIDD: "intellectual disability"; multidimensional, supports-based profile rather than severity specifiers.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Criterion A:** name the individually administered IQ test, date, full-scale and index scores, confidence interval; note validity concerns (Flynn effect/norm date, practice effects, language/sensory barriers, subtest scatter). State that a clinical assessment corroborates the scores.
- **Criterion B:** name the standardized adaptive measure and informants; give concrete examples per domain (e.g., "needs prompts to bathe and choose weather-appropriate clothing"; "cannot make change or budget"; "easily persuaded by peers to give away money"). State which domain(s) need **ongoing support** and in which settings.
- **Criterion C:** cite evidence of childhood onset (milestone delays, special education history, early evaluations).
- **Severity:** justify level from adaptive descriptions, not the IQ number.
- **Rule-outs:** hearing/vision screening; neurocognitive decline (document whether functioning was ever higher); cultural/linguistic test fit; ASD and communication disorders; deprivation.
- **Etiology/medical:** genetic testing results, seizure history, co-occurring syndromes (code separately).
- **Risk:** suicide screening adapted to communication level; behaviour change from baseline; self-injury; vulnerability to exploitation/abuse.
- MSE phrases: "concrete in reasoning; literal interpretation of proverbs"; "simple sentence structure, limited vocabulary for age"; "relies on caregiver to supply history"; "social judgment immature (e.g., would give personal information to stranger)".
