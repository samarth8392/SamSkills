# Neurodevelopmental Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** These disorders start in the developmental period; impairment is central to most diagnoses, although the three specific tic-disorder criteria do not require distress or impairment; symptoms sit on a continuum with typical development, so there is no clean cutoff. These disorders co-occur with one another constantly (ASD + IDD, ADHD + SLD, tics + OCD), so the default is "diagnose all that meet criteria", not "pick one". The commonest error is diagnosing a narrower disorder (language, SPCD, ADHD, DCD, SLD) when the deficit is simply in line with global intellectual level — each specific disorder requires deficits **in excess of** mental age (pp. 131–133).

## What unites the chapter
- **Timing:** onset in the developmental period, often before school entry (p. 131). Some disorders "may not become fully manifest" until demands exceed capacity (ASD, SPCD, SLD).
- **Mechanism:** developmental deficits or differences in brain processes → impairment of personal, social, academic or occupational functioning (p. 131).
- **Range:** from very specific limits (one academic subskill, executive control) to global impairment (IDD) (p. 131).
- **Dimensional:** severity is continuous; diagnosis = symptoms + impairment (p. 131).
- **Excess/deficit framing:** some are "too much" of normal behaviour (hyperactivity, repetition) and some are delays in milestones (p. 132).

### How the families differ
| Family | Core problem | Key timing/duration anchor | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intellectual developmental disorders | General mental ability + adaptive function | Onset in developmental period; GDD only <5 y | Severity by **adaptive** function, not IQ |
| Communication disorders | Language, speech, fluency, pragmatics | Early developmental onset | Must exceed sensory/IDD explanation |
| Autism spectrum disorder | Social communication + RRBs | Early period; may be masked later | Severity rated separately for 2 domains |
| ADHD | Inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity | ≥6 mo; several sx before age 12; ≥2 settings | 5 sx (not 6) at age ≥17 |
| Specific learning disorder | Keystone academic skills | ≥6 mo despite targeted intervention; onset school-age | Code each domain separately |
| Motor disorders | Coordination; stereotypies; tics | Tics: onset <18 y; >1 y = persistent/Tourette's (formal criteria) | Tic diagnoses are hierarchical |

## Communication-disorders section intro (p. 143)
- **Speech** = expressive sound production: articulation, fluency, voice, resonance.
- **Language** = form, function and use of a rule-governed symbol system (spoken, signed, written, pictures).
- **Communication** = any verbal/nonverbal behaviour (intentional or not) that can influence another person.
- Assessment must account for cultural and language context, especially **bilingual** upbringing; norms must fit the cultural/linguistic group.
- Boys > girls (partly due to sex differences in early communication development). High comorbidity with ASD, ADHD, SLD, IDD, anxiety, seizure disorders, chromosomal abnormalities.

## TR changes and notable updates visible in the text
- **Name:** "intellectual developmental disorder (intellectual disability)" — aligns with ICD-11 "Disorders of Intellectual Development"; "intellectual disability" kept in parentheses; Rosa's Law noted (p. 134).
- **ASD Criterion A** is explicitly "**all** of the following" (all 3 social-communication deficits required), "currently or by history" (pp. 154–155).
- **ASD specifiers:** "associated with a **neurodevelopmental, mental, or behavioral problem**" (not "another disorder"); the associated-condition specifiers do **not** imply causation; catatonia coded with **F06.1** catatonia associated with ASD (pp. 156–158).
- **New suicide sections** ("Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior") for IDD, ASD, ADHD, SLD and tic disorders.
- **SLD written-expression code F81.81**; separate coding of each impaired domain (pp. 179–180).
- **Tic disorders:** specifier "motor tics only/vocal tics only" required only for persistent tic disorder; "functional tic disorder"/tic attacks added to the differential (pp. 199, 204).
- **Other specified NDD** example: neurodevelopmental disorder associated with prenatal alcohol exposure (p. 206).
- **Adult-onset fluency disorder** coded F98.5 (p. 149).
- Expanded culture sections (ethnoracial underdiagnosis/misdiagnosis in ASD and ADHD).

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- **Severity specifiers:** IDD (mild–profound, by adaptive domain, each its own code F70–F73), ASD (Levels 1–3, rated separately for social communication and RRBs), ADHD (mild/moderate/severe), SLD (mild/moderate/severe), stereotypic movement disorder (mild/moderate/severe) (p. 133).
- **Current-presentation specifiers:** ADHD (presentation, partial remission), SLD (domains/subskills), persistent tic disorder (motor only/vocal only) (p. 133).
- **"Associated with a known genetic or other medical condition or environmental factor":** ASD and stereotypic movement disorder; **code the associated condition additionally**; recording format "X associated with [condition]" (pp. 133, 156, 194).
- **Hierarchy within tics:** Tourette's > persistent > provisional > other specified/unspecified; once a higher one is met, lower ones are never used (p. 199).
- **F88 is used twice**: Global developmental delay and Other specified neurodevelopmental disorder share F88.
- Comorbid diagnoses are encouraged when full criteria are met (IDD + communication disorder/SLD/ASD/ADHD/DCD; ASD + ADHD; ADHD + SLD; ADHD + DCD).

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **"In excess of mental age" rule:** Language disorder, SPCD, ASD (Criterion E), ADHD, SLD and DCD all require deficits beyond what IDD/GDD explain.
- **IDD vs NCD:** IDD = intellectual and adaptive deficits arising during development; these may follow acquired childhood injury. NCD = **decline** from earlier functioning. Both may be given when full criteria are met (Down syndrome + Alzheimer's; childhood TBI with stabilization) (pp. 139–141).
- **IQ is not the diagnosis:** IDD severity is set by adaptive functioning; IQ ~65–75 (70 ± 5) only a guide.
- **ASD vs SPCD:** SPCD requires that current/history findings do not establish ASD's restricted/repetitive-behavior criteria; ASD supersedes SPCD when full ASD criteria are met (p. 153).
- **SPCD vs social anxiety:** SPCD = skills never developed; social anxiety = skills present but not used due to fear.
- **ASD vs ADHD:** RRBs and developmental course; both may be diagnosed.
- **ADHD onset after 13:** more likely another mental disorder or substance effects.
- **Tics vs stereotypies:** stereotypies onset <3 y, rhythmic, fixed, prolonged, arms/hands/whole body, enjoyed (ego-syntonic), no premonitory urge; tics onset 4–6 y, brief, variable, eyes/face/head/shoulders, ego-dystonic, premonitory urge, wax and wane.
- **Stereotypic movement disorder in ASD:** only when self-injury or severity makes it a treatment focus.
- **Regression:** language loss at any age → rule out seizures (Landau-Kleffner; routine and sleep EEG). Regression after ≥2 y normal development (old childhood disintegrative disorder) → more extensive medical workup.
- **Stuttering onset in/after adolescence** = adult-onset dysfluency (F98.5), not the neurodevelopmental disorder.
- **Bilingualism** does not cause language disorder; a true disorder shows in **both** languages.
- Specify cultural/linguistic fit of all tests (IQ, language, achievement, motor).

## Documentation cues (chapter-wide)
- Record **age of onset/first concern** and the developmental history source (caregiver, school records, prior evaluations).
- Record **standardized test names, dates and scores** (IQ, adaptive, language, achievement, motor) plus clinical judgment.
- Record **sensory screening** (hearing, vision) and neurological exam where the criteria require it.
- Document **multiple informants/settings**, cultural-linguistic context, and educational opportunity.
- Document **why** a broader disorder (IDD/GDD, ASD) does or does not explain the presentation.
- Screen and document **suicide risk** (elevated in ASD, ADHD, SLD, tics, and IDD with comorbidity).
