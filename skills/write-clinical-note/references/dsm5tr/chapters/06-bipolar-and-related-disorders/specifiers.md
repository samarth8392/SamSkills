# Specifiers for Bipolar and Related Disorders (pp. 291–300)

> **Spark note:** Specifiers attach to the **current episode**, or to the **most recent episode** if the disorder is in partial or full remission. Exceptions: **rapid cycling** and **seasonal pattern** describe the **pattern** of episodes over time. Severity and psychotic features are coded (for BP-I) only when full episode criteria are currently met. Remission is used only when they are not.

## With anxious distress (pp. 291–292)
- **Threshold:** **≥2 of 5** symptoms on the **majority of days** of the current (or most recent) manic, hypomanic or major depressive episode, or on the majority of symptomatic days in cyclothymia:
  1. Feeling keyed up or tense
  2. Unusual restlessness
  3. Trouble concentrating because of worry
  4. Fear that something awful will happen
  5. Feeling one might lose control of oneself
- **Severity:**
  - **Mild** = 2 symptoms
  - **Moderate** = 3
  - **Moderate-severe** = 4–5
  - **Severe** = 4–5 **plus motor agitation**
- **Why it matters:** prominent in bipolar and MDD in primary care and specialty settings. High anxiety → **↑ suicide risk, longer illness, more treatment non-response**. Rate it for treatment planning and monitoring.

## With mixed features (pp. 292–293)
Applies to the current (or most recent) manic, hypomanic or MDE in BP-I, and to the hypomanic or MDE in BP-II.

**Manic or hypomanic episode, with mixed features**
- **A.** Full manic or hypomanic criteria are met **+ ≥3 of 6 depressive symptoms** on the majority of days of the episode:
  1. Prominent dysphoria or depressed mood (reported or observed)
  2. Diminished interest or pleasure in (almost) all activities
  3. Psychomotor **retardation** nearly every day (observable)
  4. Fatigue or loss of energy
  5. Worthlessness or excessive/inappropriate guilt
  6. Recurrent thoughts of death, SI, plan or attempt
- **B.** Mixed symptoms are **observable by others** and a change from usual behaviour.
- **C.** If full criteria for **both** mania and MDE are met simultaneously → diagnose **manic episode, with mixed features** (because full mania brings marked impairment and severity).
- **D.** Not due to a substance (drug, medication, other treatment).

**Depressive episode, with mixed features**
- **A.** Full MDE criteria are met **+ ≥3 of 7 manic/hypomanic symptoms** on the majority of days:
  1. Elevated, expansive mood
  2. Grandiosity / inflated self-esteem
  3. More talkative / pressured speech
  4. Flight of ideas / racing thoughts
  5. ↑ energy or goal-directed activity
  6. ↑ involvement in risky activities with painful consequences
  7. **Decreased need for sleep** (rested on less sleep; contrast with insomnia)
- **B–D:** as above (observable change; simultaneous full mania + MDE → manic episode with mixed features; not substance-induced).
- **Note:** in a major depressive episode, mixed features are a **significant risk factor for developing BP-I or BP-II**.
- **Excluded from the mixed-feature lists** because they overlap both poles: distractibility, irritability, psychomotor agitation (mania side), and insomnia, ↓ concentration (depression side).

## With rapid cycling (pp. 293–294)
- **≥4 mood episodes in the previous 12 months** that meet full criteria:
  - BP-I: manic, hypomanic or MDE.
  - BP-II: hypomanic or MDE.
- **Demarcation:** each episode must be separated by **partial or full remission lasting ≥2 months** or a **switch to the opposite polarity** (e.g., MDE → mania). The interpretive note specifically names full remission or a polarity switch.
- **Manic and hypomanic episodes count as the same pole** (mania → hypomania is not a switch).
- Episodes may occur in any order or combination and must meet **both duration and symptom-count** criteria.
- Episodes are otherwise identical to non-rapid-cycling episodes.
- **Exclude** episodes caused directly by a substance (cocaine, corticosteroids) or a medical condition.
- Associated with poorer outcomes. More common in women. Seen in 5–15% of BP-II.

## With melancholic features (pp. 294–295)
Applies to the MDE (current, or most recent if in remission) in BP-I or BP-II, judged at the **most severe period** of the episode.
- **A. ≥1 of:**
  1. Loss of pleasure in all or almost all activities
  2. **Lack of mood reactivity** to usually pleasurable stimuli (doesn't feel much better even briefly when something good happens)
- **B. ≥3 of:**
  1. **Distinct quality** of depressed mood: profound despondency, despair, moroseness, or "empty" mood
  2. Depression regularly **worse in the morning**
  3. **Early-morning awakening** (≥2 h before usual)
  4. Marked psychomotor agitation or retardation
  5. Significant anorexia or weight loss
  6. Excessive or inappropriate guilt
- **Interpretive notes:**
  - Capacity for pleasure is **nearly absent**, not merely reduced.
  - Non-reactivity guide: even highly desired events don't markedly brighten mood (no brightening, or only partial, ~20–40% of normal, for minutes).
  - "Distinct quality" means qualitatively different. A mood that is only more severe, longer-lasting or without reason does **not** qualify.
  - Psychomotor changes are nearly always present and observable.
  - Modest tendency to recur across episodes. More common in inpatients, in severe episodes and with psychotic features.

## With atypical features (p. 295)
Applies when these **predominate during the majority of days** of the MDE (current, or most recent if in remission).
- **A. Mood reactivity:** mood brightens with actual or potential positive events.
- **B. ≥2 of:**
  1. Significant weight gain or ↑ appetite
  2. **Hypersomnia**: ≥10 h/day (nighttime + naps), or ≥2 h more than when not depressed
  3. **Leaden paralysis**: heavy, leaden limbs, usually ≥1 h/day, often hours
  4. **Long-standing interpersonal rejection sensitivity** (trait-like, not limited to episodes) causing significant social or occupational impairment
- **C.** Criteria for **melancholic features or catatonia are not met** in the same episode.
- **Notes:**
  - "Atypical" is historical; it is **not** uncommon.
  - Mood may become euthymic for extended periods while circumstances stay favourable.
  - Rejection sensitivity has early onset, persists through adulthood, and is present whether or not the person is depressed (worse when depressed).

## With psychotic features (p. 296)
Delusions or hallucinations at **any time** in:
- BP-I: the current (or most recent) **manic or major depressive** episode.
- BP-II: the current (or most recent) **major depressive** episode only.
- Hypomania with psychosis is by definition mania.
- **BP-I coding:** F31.2 (manic) / F31.5 (depressed), **regardless of severity**.

Specify congruence:

| Episode | Mood-congruent | Mood-incongruent |
|---|---|---|
| **Manic** (BP-I) | **All** content fits manic themes: grandiosity, invulnerability. Can include suspiciousness or paranoia about others doubting the person's abilities or accomplishments. | Content does **not** involve manic themes, or is a **mixture** of congruent and incongruent themes |
| **Major depressive** (BP-I/II) | **All** content fits depressive themes: personal inadequacy, guilt, disease, death, nihilism, deserved punishment | Content lacks those themes, or is a **mixture** |

## With catatonia (p. 296)
- Applies to the current (or most recent) **manic or major depressive** episode in BP-I, or the **MDE** in BP-II, when catatonic features are present for **most of the episode**.
- Criteria: see **catatonia associated with another mental disorder** (Schizophrenia Spectrum chapter).
- **Coding: add F06.1.**

## With peripartum onset (pp. 297–298)
- Applies to the current (or most recent) manic, hypomanic or MDE in BP-I, or the hypomanic or MDE in BP-II.
- **Mood symptom onset during pregnancy or within 4 weeks after delivery.**
- **Notes and key numbers:**
  - ~**50%** of "postpartum" MDEs actually begin **before delivery**, hence "peripartum".
  - MDE prevalence: ~**9%** between conception and birth; just under **7%** from birth to 12 months.
  - Episodes can be with or without psychosis. **Infanticide** (rare) is most often linked to postpartum psychotic episodes with **command hallucinations** to kill the infant or delusions that the infant is possessed.
  - **Postpartum psychotic mood episodes: 1/500–1/1,000 deliveries**, possibly more common in primiparas.
    - Risk ↑ with a prior postpartum psychotic episode, prior depressive or bipolar disorder (especially **BP-I**), and family history of bipolar disorder.
    - **Recurrence 30–50%** with each subsequent delivery.
  - Differentiate from **postpartum delirium**, which has fluctuating awareness or attention.
  - Differentiate from **"maternity/baby blues"**: sudden mood changes and tearfulness without depression or functional impairment, possibly sleep disturbance or brief confusion, self-limited (**improves within ~1 week**). Not a mental disorder.
  - Perinatal thyroid or other medical causes → **depressive disorder due to another medical condition** instead of MDE with peripartum onset.
  - Pregnancy itself doesn't raise episode risk in bipolar disorder **unless medication is stopped**, while the **postpartum period strongly raises** the risk of mania and depression (BP-I text).

## With seasonal pattern (pp. 298–299)
Describes the **lifetime pattern**. ≥1 episode type (mania, hypomania or depression) shows a regular seasonal pattern; the other types need not.
- **A.** Regular temporal relationship between **onset** of manic, hypomanic or major depressive episodes and a particular time of year (e.g., fall/winter) in BP-I or BP-II.
  - Exclude obvious seasonal psychosocial stressors (e.g., regular winter unemployment, school schedule).
- **B.** **Full remissions** (or a switch of polarity) also occur at a characteristic time of year (e.g., depression lifts in spring).
- **C.** In the **last 2 years**, episodes of that polarity have shown the seasonal relationship, with **no non-seasonal episodes of that polarity** in those 2 years.
- **D.** Seasonal episodes **substantially outnumber** non-seasonal episodes of that type over the lifetime.
- **Applies to:** the MDE pattern in BP-I and BP-II; the manic/hypomanic pattern in BP-I; the hypomanic pattern in BP-II.
- **Notes:**
  - Seasonal depression is usually fall/winter onset with spring remission; summer depression is less common.
  - Within bipolar disorders, a seasonal depressive pattern is **more likely in BP-II** than BP-I.
  - Seasonal mania or hypomania peaks **spring–summer**.
  - Winter-type prevalence ↑ with **higher latitude** and **younger age**, and varies by sex.

## Remission specifiers (p. 299)
Used when full criteria for a manic, hypomanic or MDE are **not currently** met.
- **In partial remission:** symptoms of the immediately preceding episode remain without full criteria, **or** there has been a symptom-free period of **<2 months** since the episode ended.
- **In full remission:** **no significant signs or symptoms for the past 2 months.**

## Severity specifiers (p. 299)
Severity is based on symptom count, symptom intensity and functional disability. Used only when full criteria are currently met.

**Manic episode**
- **Mild:** minimum symptom criteria met.
- **Moderate:** very significant ↑ in activity or impaired judgment.
- **Severe:** **almost continual supervision** needed to prevent physical harm to self or others.

**Major depressive episode**
- **Mild:** few or no symptoms beyond the threshold; distressing but manageable; minor social/occupational impairment.
- **Moderate:** between mild and severe on symptom number, intensity or impairment.
- **Severe:** symptoms substantially exceed the threshold; seriously distressing and unmanageable; marked interference with functioning.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Anxious distress:** list which of the 5 symptoms are present on most days, the count, the resulting severity, and whether there is motor agitation (needed for "severe").
- **Mixed features:**
  - Name ≥3 opposite-pole symptoms, state that they are present on the **majority of days**, and that they are observable by others.
  - If full mania and a full MDE coexist, record **manic episode with mixed features**.
  - Rule out substance causes.
- **Rapid cycling:** list each episode in the past 12 months with type, dates and how it was demarcated (≥2-month remission or polarity switch). Exclude substance- or medically-induced episodes.
- **Melancholic:** document non-reactivity ("no brightening when daughter visited"), morning worsening, early waking (time), observable psychomotor change.
- **Atypical:** document mood reactivity, sleep hours/day, appetite or weight gain, leaden limbs, and a **lifelong** rejection-sensitivity pattern with its impairment.
- **Psychotic features:** quote the content in your own summary and classify it as congruent or incongruent. For BP-I, this selects the F31.2/F31.5 code.
- **Catatonia:** list the catatonic signs observed and their duration across the episode; add F06.1.
- **Peripartum:** record delivery date or gestational week at mood-symptom onset (≤4 weeks postpartum); screen for psychosis, thoughts of harming the infant, delirium and thyroid status.
- **Seasonal:** chart episode onset and offset months for ≥2 years; note the absence of a seasonal stressor explanation and the lifetime seasonal vs non-seasonal count.
- **Remission:** date the end of the last episode and the length of the symptom-free period (≥2 months = full).
