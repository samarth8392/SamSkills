# Cyclothymic Disorder

> **Spark note:** Cyclothymia is a **chronic, low-grade, back-and-forth mood instability** lasting ≥2 years (≥1 year in youth). There are many subthreshold "ups" and "downs", but **criteria for a hypomanic, manic or major depressive episode have never been met**. If a full episode ever occurs, the diagnosis is replaced. People often mistake it for borderline PD; in borderline PD, elation and increased energy are not characteristic.

Specifier detail is in [specifiers.md](specifiers.md).

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 279–280)
- **A.** Over **≥2 years (≥1 year in children/adolescents)**, there are **numerous periods of hypomanic symptoms** that fall short of a hypomanic episode **and numerous periods of depressive symptoms** that fall short of an MDE.
- **B.** During that period, Criterion A symptoms are present **≥half the time**, with **no symptom-free interval >2 months**.
- **C.** **Never** met criteria for an MDE, manic episode or hypomanic episode.
- **D.** Not better explained by schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia, schizophreniform, delusional or other specified/unspecified psychotic disorder.
- **E.** Not attributable to a substance (drug, medication) or medical condition (e.g., **hyperthyroidism**).
- **F.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.

## Specifiers & severity
- **With anxious distress** is the only listed specifier. It requires ≥2 of 5 anxiety symptoms on the majority of symptomatic days and is rated mild/moderate/moderate-severe/severe. See [specifiers.md](specifiers.md#with-anxious-distress).
- There are no severity or episode specifiers.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Subthreshold means short on **number, severity, pervasiveness and/or duration** of symptoms.
- During the first 2 years (1 year in youth), symptoms are present **more days than not**.
- **Diagnostic replacement after the initial 2-year (1-year youth) window:**
  - A later MDE → MDD.
  - A later manic episode → BP-I.
  - A later hypomanic episode → other specified/unspecified BRD (hypomanic episode without prior MDE).
  - In each case cyclothymia is **dropped**.
- If mood swings are better explained by a psychotic disorder, they are treated as associated features of that disorder.
- Some function well during hypomanic stretches, but over time the **unpredictability** itself harms relationships and role performance (Criterion F).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Prevalence**
- Lifetime **0.4–2.5%** (US/Europe); **3–5%** in mood-disorder clinics.
- Equal M:F in the community; females present for treatment more often.

**Development & course**
- Onset in adolescence or early adulthood, sometimes viewed as a **temperamental predisposition** to other bipolar disorders.
- Most affected youth have onset **before age 10**.
- Insidious onset, persistent course.
- **15–50%** later develop BP-I or BP-II; conversion is higher in youth.
- Late-adult onset → rule out BRD or depressive disorder due to another medical condition (e.g., MS).

**Risk & prognostic factors**
- *Genetic:* MDD, BP-I and BP-II are more common in first-degree relatives; possibly ↑ familial substance-related disorders. Cyclothymia may be more common in relatives of BP-I probands.

## Differential diagnosis
- **BRD due to another medical condition:** mood disturbance is the physiological effect of a (usually chronic) medical condition such as hyperthyroidism, shown by history, exam or labs. If the mood symptoms are a *psychological* reaction to the illness, or unrelated to it, code cyclothymia **plus** the medical condition.
- **Substance/medication-induced bipolar or depressive disorder:** a substance (especially **stimulants**) is etiologic. The swings usually resolve once use stops.
- **BP-I or BP-II with rapid cycling:** both involve frequent marked shifts, but rapid cycling requires **full mood episodes**. Cyclothymia never has them.
- **Borderline PD:** brief marked mood shifts and self-damaging acts can occur in both.
  - In cyclothymia, risky behaviour must come with other hypomanic symptoms.
  - BPD instability involves anxiety, irritability and sadness; **elation, euphoria and ↑ energy are not BPD features**.
  - Both may be diagnosed if criteria are met for both.

## Comorbidity
- Substance-related disorders; sleep disorders (difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep).
- Children with cyclothymia in outpatient care have comorbidity rates **higher than children with disruptive behavior/ADHD** and similar to children with BP-I/II.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A/B timeline:**
  - Document duration (≥24 months, or ≥12 in youth).
  - Give the approximate proportion of days symptomatic (≥50%).
  - Record the longest symptom-free stretch (must be ≤2 months).
  - A mood chart or diary is ideal evidence.
- **Describe both poles with subthreshold detail**, e.g., "2–3-day periods of ↑ energy, talkativeness, ↓ sleep without fatigue; never ≥4 days with ≥3 sx" and "periods of low mood, low energy, poor concentration lasting days, never 2 weeks with ≥5 sx".
- **Crit C:** state explicitly that the person has **never met** MDE, manic or hypomanic criteria (lifetime screen done).
- **Rule-outs:** thyroid function or other medical work-up as indicated; stimulant and other substance history; psychotic symptoms.
- **Crit F:** impairment from unpredictability (relationships, job inconsistency).
- **MSE:** often euthymic-to-mildly-shifted at a single visit, so rely on longitudinal history. Note the affect range and any current elevation or dysphoria.
- **Anxious distress:** if present, record which of the 5 symptoms and the severity level.
