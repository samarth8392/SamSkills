# Bipolar II Disorder

> **Spark note:** BP-II needs **≥1 hypomanic episode + ≥1 major depressive episode, with no mania ever**. It is **not a "milder" BP-I**. It runs more chronically and spends more time depressed, with a similar suicide-attempt risk and possibly more lethal attempts. People present depressed and rarely mention hypomania, which can feel good to them, so collateral history is key. Once hypomania has occurred, the diagnosis never reverts to MDD. If mania ever occurs, it becomes BP-I.

Full specifier definitions are in [specifiers.md](specifiers.md). Episode criteria are written out in full in [bipolar-i-disorder.md](bipolar-i-disorder.md); a condensed version follows.

## Criteria at a glance

### Hypomanic Episode (A–F) (pp. 268–269)
- **A.** Distinct period of abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive or irritable mood **+** abnormally and persistently ↑ activity/energy. Lasts **≥4 consecutive days**, most of the day, nearly every day.
- **B. ≥3 of 7 (≥4 if mood is only irritable).** Symptoms persisted, are a noticeable change and are significant in degree:
  1. Grandiosity / inflated self-esteem
  2. ↓ need for sleep
  3. Talkative / pressured speech
  4. Flight of ideas / racing thoughts
  5. Distractibility
  6. ↑ goal-directed activity or psychomotor agitation
  7. Risky activities with painful consequences
- **C.** Unequivocal change in functioning, uncharacteristic of the person when well.
- **D.** Mood and functional change **observable by others**.
- **E.** **No** marked impairment and no hospitalization. **Psychosis → the episode is manic by definition.**
- **F.** Not due to a substance or medical condition.
  - Hypomania that emerges on antidepressants or ECT and persists fully beyond the treatment's physiological effect counts.
  - 1–2 symptoms (irritability, edginess, agitation) after an antidepressant are **not** sufficient.

### Major Depressive Episode (A–C) (pp. 269–270)
- **A. ≥5 of 9, same 2 weeks, change from baseline; ≥1 = depressed mood or anhedonia.** Exclude symptoms clearly due to a medical condition.
  1. Depressed mood (irritable mood possible in youth)
  2. Anhedonia
  3. Weight/appetite change (>5%/month; in kids, failure to gain)
  4. Insomnia/hypersomnia
  5. Observable psychomotor agitation/retardation
  6. Fatigue
  7. Worthlessness/excessive guilt
  8. ↓ concentration/indecisiveness
  9. Recurrent death thoughts, SI, plan or attempt
- **B.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **C.** Not due to a substance or medical condition.
- The bereavement/significant-loss note applies (judge an MDE in addition to a normal grief response; see the grief footnote, p. 300).

### Bipolar II Disorder (pp. 270–271)
- **A.** ≥1 hypomanic episode **and** ≥1 MDE.
- **B.** **Never a manic episode.**
- **C.** At least one hypomanic episode and at least one MDE are not better explained by schizoaffective disorder and are not superimposed on schizophrenia, schizophreniform, delusional or other specified/unspecified psychotic disorder.
- **D.** Depressive symptoms, **or** the unpredictability from frequent alternation between depression and hypomania, cause clinically significant distress or impairment. (Hypomania itself is by definition not impairing.)

## Specifiers & severity
Full definitions are in [specifiers.md](specifiers.md).
- **Specify current or most recent episode:** hypomanic **or** depressed.
- **If the current or most recent episode is hypomanic:**
  - Record "in partial/full remission" if full hypomanic criteria are not currently met.
  - Allowed specifiers: with anxious distress, with mixed features, with rapid cycling, with peripartum onset, with seasonal pattern.
- **If the current or most recent episode is depressed:**
  - Record mild/moderate/severe if full MDE criteria are currently met; otherwise in partial/full remission.
  - Allowed specifiers: with anxious distress, with mixed features, with rapid cycling, with melancholic features, with atypical features, with mood-congruent / mood-incongruent psychotic features, with catatonia (add F06.1), with peripartum onset, with seasonal pattern.
- **Psychotic features can apply only to the MDE in BP-II** (never to hypomania).
- Remission: partial = some symptoms, or <2 months symptom-free; full = no significant signs or symptoms for 2 months.
- Rapid cycling and seasonal pattern describe the pattern of episodes.

- **Recording order (hypomanic):** bipolar II disorder → current or most recent episode hypomanic → in partial/full remission (if applicable) → hypomanic-episode specifiers.
- **Recording order (depressed):** bipolar II disorder → current or most recent episode depressed → mild/moderate/severe **or** partial/full remission → MDE specifiers.
- Examples: "F31.81 bipolar II disorder, current episode depressed, moderate severity, with mixed features"; "F31.81 bipolar II disorder, most recent episode depressed, in partial remission".

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- People typically present **during an MDE**. They don't report hypomania because they don't recognize it or find it desirable. **Get informant data** from friends or relatives.
- Impairment comes from the MDEs or the pattern of unpredictable mood shifts and unreliable functioning, **not** from the hypomania.
- **Don't mistake** the several days of euthymia and restored energy that can follow remission of an MDE for hypomania.
- Psychotic symptoms never occur in hypomania and are less frequent in BP-II MDEs than in BP-I MDEs.
- MDEs in BP-II are often **more frequent and longer** than in BP-I. BP-II has greater chronicity and more time depressed.
- At least one hypomanic episode and at least one MDE must not be substance- or medically caused.
- Antidepressant-emergent hypomania persisting **≥4 days at full syndrome** beyond the drug effect **counts** toward BP-II.
- **MDE clues pointing to BP-II rather than MDD:**
  - Atypical symptoms (hypersomnia, hyperphagia) are more common in BP-II.
  - Co-occurring insomnia + hypersomnia is overrepresented in women with BP-II.
  - Co-occurring hypomanic symptoms during depression.
- **Mixed states are common**, especially in females. Hypomania with mixed features may be experienced as "depression with more energy or irritability".

## Associated features
- **Impulsivity** contributes to suicide attempts and SUD.
- Possible ↑ creativity during hypomania. The relationship is nonlinear; unaffected relatives may be more creative. Attachment to creativity can produce **ambivalence about treatment and non-adherence**.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
**Prevalence**
- US 12-month **0.8%**; international 12-month 0.3%.
- Pediatric BP-II prevalence is hard to establish. Combined BP-I/II/NOS is 1.8% (2.7% in youth ≥12 y).

**Development & course**
- Mean onset **mid-20s**. Age at onset does not distinguish BP-I from BP-II.
- Usually begins with depression. **~12%** of people first diagnosed with MDD later convert to BP-II when hypomania appears.
- Anxiety, SUD or eating disorders may come first. Several MDEs often precede the first recognized hypomania, giving a **>10-year lag** to diagnosis.
- Highly recurrent: **>50% have a new episode within 1 year**. More seasonal variation than BP-I.
- More lifetime episodes than MDD or BP-I. Inter-episode intervals shorten with age.
- Depression is more enduring and disabling over time.
- **5–15%** have rapid cycling (≥4 episodes in 12 months). It is more common in women and may reflect a worsening course.
- Switches into hypomania or mania can occur spontaneously or during depression treatment.
- **5–15% eventually have a manic episode, which converts the diagnosis permanently to BP-I.**
- **Youth:**
  - Hard to diagnose when irritability or hyperarousal is **non-episodic**. Non-episodic irritability predicts later anxiety and MDD, **not** bipolar disorder.
  - Symptoms must exceed developmental and cultural expectations. Youth onset → more severe lifetime course.
- **Older adults (>60):** 3-year first-onset incidence 0.34%. Late vs early onset has no clinical utility. Co-occurring hypomanic symptoms during depression help separate BP-II from MDD. Always consider medical or neurological causes.

**Risk & prognostic factors**
- *Genetic:* risk is highest among relatives of BP-II probands. ~1/3 report a family history of bipolar disorder. Genetic architecture is partly distinct from BP-I and schizophrenia.
- *Course modifiers:* rapid cycling → poorer prognosis. Younger age and less severe depression → better return of social function. More education, fewer years ill and being married → functional recovery.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Gender ratio findings are mixed. There is no clear difference in the community; some clinical samples are female-predominant, possibly reflecting treatment-seeking.
- Females report more **hypomania with mixed features** and **rapid cycling**.
- **Childbirth can trigger hypomania** (10–20% of females in nonclinical samples, usually early postpartum). It is hard to separate from normal postpartum elation and reduced sleep. About half of those with postpartum "highs" later develop depression.
- Perimenopause is a period of instability.
- No major sex differences in number of depressive episodes, onset age/polarity, symptoms or severity.

## Suicide risk
- **~1/3 report a lifetime suicide attempt.**
- Attempt and death rates are roughly similar to BP-I, and both are far above the general population.
- Time spent depressed is the key correlate of attempt risk.
- **Lethality may be higher in BP-II** (lower ratio of attempts to deaths).
- First-degree relatives of BP-II probands have a **6.5-fold** higher suicide risk than relatives of BP-I probands.

## Functional consequences
- **≥15%** have inter-episode dysfunction; **20%** pass directly into another episode without recovery.
- Functional (especially occupational) recovery lags symptomatic recovery, giving lower SES.
- Cognitive test deficits contribute to vocational difficulty.
- Prolonged unemployment is associated with more depressive episodes, older age, current panic disorder and lifetime alcohol use disorder.

## Differential diagnosis
- **MDD:** no hypomania or mania. Subthreshold hypomanic symptoms (too few, too brief) are still compatible with MDD. **Only eliciting ≥1 past full hypomanic episode distinguishes BP-II**, because the MDE criteria are identical.
- **Cyclothymic disorder:** numerous subthreshold hypomanic and depressive periods; never a full hypomanic episode or MDE.
- **Schizophrenia:** active-phase psychosis, with MDEs absent or present only a minority of the time. Psychosis **only** during MDEs → BP-II with psychotic features.
- **Schizoaffective disorder:** delusions/hallucinations for ≥2 weeks without an MDE. Psychosis confined to MDEs → BP-II with psychotic features.
- **BRD due to another medical condition:** hypomania is the direct physiological effect of a medical condition (Cushing's, MS).
- **Substance/medication-induced BRD:** substance (stimulants, PCP) or medication (steroids) is etiologic for the hypomanic or depressive episodes. Clarify whether substance use is a consequence of hypomania. Confirm symptoms persist off the substance. Antidepressant-emergent hypomania persisting beyond the drug effect = BP-II.
- **ADHD:** overlap in talkativeness, distractibility and ↓ sleep, especially in youth. Avoid double-counting by requiring a **distinct episode** and a **clear increase over baseline**.
- **Borderline and other personality disorders:** same rule (distinct episode, increase over baseline). **Don't diagnose a PD during an untreated mood episode** unless lifetime history supports it.
- **BP-I:** check carefully for any past mania.
- **Other specified/unspecified BRD:** confirm the hypomania and depression are **fully syndromal**.

## Comorbidity
- **~60% have ≥3 co-occurring disorders.** **75% have an anxiety disorder**: social anxiety 38%, specific phobia 36%, GAD 30%. Anxiety worsens the course; in youth it is more common than in BP-I and usually comes first.
- Anxiety tracks depressive symptoms; SUD tracks hypomanic symptoms moderately. They are not truly independent courses.
- SUD rates are similar to BP-I: **alcohol 42%, cannabis 20%**.
- Less PTSD than BP-I.
- **~14% have a lifetime eating disorder**, with binge-eating more common than BN or AN.
- **PMS/PMDD are common**, especially in BP-II, and bring more severe mood symptoms and lability.
- Medical: cardiovascular disease, migraine, autoimmune disorders.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Hypomania (Crit A–F):**
  - Dates and duration (**≥4 consecutive days**); mood quality; ↑ energy.
  - The B-symptom count with examples (≥3, or ≥4 if irritable only).
  - **Collateral confirmation** that others noticed the change (Crit D) and that functioning was clearly different from usual (Crit C).
  - Explicit note of **no marked impairment, no hospitalization, no psychosis** during the high (Crit E). If any were present, reconsider BP-I.
- **MDE:** ≥5/9 symptoms over ≥2 weeks including mood or anhedonia, with specific examples; impairment.
- **Rule-out mania:** document a lifetime screen ("denies any period of elevated mood lasting ≥1 week or requiring hospitalization").
- **Rule-outs:** substance and medication timeline (antidepressant-emergent? persisted beyond the drug effect?), UDS, medical causes (thyroid, neuro), psychosis outside mood episodes.
- **Crit D:** link impairment to depression or to the unpredictable mood swings (e.g., "missed 3 weeks of work during depression; erratic job performance with swings").
- **MSE phrases:**
  - Hypomanic: "euthymic-to-elevated, talkative but redirectable, no psychotic content, insight partial".
  - Depressed: "constricted affect, psychomotor slowing, hopeless content, passive SI without plan".
- **Specifiers to capture:** episode type and current status; severity or remission; mixed features (≥3 opposite-pole symptoms); anxious distress level; rapid cycling (count episodes in the last 12 months); peripartum or seasonal timing.
- **Risk:** suicide assessment every visit when depressed (history of attempts, lethality, impulsivity, alcohol use).
