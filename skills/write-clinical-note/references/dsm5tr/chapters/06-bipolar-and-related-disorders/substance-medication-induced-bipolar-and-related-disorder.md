# Substance/Medication-Induced Bipolar and Related Disorder

> **Spark note:** A manic-like syndrome (elevated, expansive or irritable mood **plus** ↑ activity/energy) that is **caused by** a drug, medication or toxin. Classic causes are stimulants, PCP and steroids. **Key exception:** mania or hypomania that starts on an **antidepressant or ECT** and **persists at full syndrome after the treatment's physiological effect has worn off is true bipolar disorder (BP-I/BP-II), not this diagnosis.** One or two symptoms of agitation or edginess on an antidepressant are a side effect, not bipolar disorder.

Related specifier definitions are in [specifiers.md](specifiers.md).

## Criteria at a glance (pp. 282–283)
- **A.** A prominent, persistent mood disturbance that **predominates** the clinical picture: abnormally elevated, expansive or irritable mood **and** abnormally ↑ activity or energy.
- **B.** Evidence from history, exam or labs of **both**:
  1. Onset **during or soon after** substance intoxication or withdrawal, or after exposure to or withdrawal from a medication; **and**
  2. The substance or medication is **capable** of producing the Criterion A symptoms.
- **C.** Not better explained by an **independent** (non-induced) bipolar or related disorder. Evidence for an independent disorder:
  - symptoms **preceded** substance or medication use; **or**
  - symptoms **persist for a substantial time (~1 month)** after acute withdrawal or severe intoxication ends; **or**
  - other evidence, e.g., a **history of recurrent non-substance-related episodes**.
- **D.** Not exclusively during a **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.
- **Note:** diagnose this **instead of** intoxication or withdrawal only when Criterion A symptoms **predominate** and are severe enough to warrant independent clinical attention.

## Specifiers & severity
Onset specifier (check the substance-class table in the Substance-Related chapter for which applies):
- **With onset during intoxication:** intoxication criteria are met and symptoms arise during intoxication.
- **With onset during withdrawal:** withdrawal criteria are met and symptoms arise during or shortly after withdrawal.
- **With onset after medication use:** symptoms arise at medication initiation, with a change in use, or during medication withdrawal.

**Recording procedures (p. 284)**
- The name starts with the **specific substance** (e.g., cocaine, dexamethasone).
- Substances that fit no class (e.g., **dexamethasone**), or an etiologic substance of unknown class → **"other (or unknown)" F19.x4**.
- Order: [severity] [substance] use disorder → "with" → [substance]-induced bipolar and related disorder → onset specifier.
  - e.g., "F14.24 severe cocaine use disorder with cocaine-induced bipolar and related disorder, with onset during intoxication".
  - No use disorder (e.g., one-time heavy use): "F15.94 amphetamine-induced bipolar and related disorder, with onset during intoxication".
- If **several substances** contribute, list each separately (e.g., F15.24 severe methylphenidate use disorder with methylphenidate-induced BRD …; F19.94 dexamethasone-induced BRD …).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Typical agents: **stimulants, phencyclidine, steroids**, and emerging synthetics (e.g., "bath salts"). Corticosteroids and some **immunosuppressants** are also implicated.
- **Antidepressant- or ECT-emergent rule:** syndromes that persist beyond the physiological effect point to true bipolar disorder.
  - Full mania that persists → BP-I.
  - Full hypomania that persists → BP-II **only if preceded by an MDE**.
- An induced syndrome may still **suggest an underlying bipolar diathesis** in someone never diagnosed.
- Antidepressant side effects (edginess, agitation) resemble mania but are distinct. Mania symptoms are specific (simple agitation ≠ excess purposeful activity), and a sufficient number of symptoms is needed.

## Prevalence · Development & course
- Epidemiological data are limited. Prevalence depends on substance availability and cultural norms around use.
- **Timing by agent:**
  - **PCP:** may first present as a **delirium with affective features** that becomes an atypical manic or mixed state. Onset within hours to a few days of ingestion or inhalation.
  - **Stimulants:** onset **minutes to 1 hour** after one or several doses. The episode is **very brief, resolving over 1–2 days**.
  - **Corticosteroids and immunosuppressants:** mania (or a mixed or depressed state) follows **several days** of use. **Higher doses → greater likelihood.**

## Diagnostic markers
- Blood or urine markers can identify the substance and corroborate the diagnosis.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Other bipolar disorders (BP-I/II):**
  - Symptoms precede use, persist ~1 month after use stops, or there is a history of non-induced episodes.
  - Antidepressant- or ECT-emergent syndromes that persist → BP-I (mania) or BP-II (hypomania + prior MDE).
- **Substance intoxication or withdrawal:** euphoria, irritability and ↑ energy are common (e.g., stimulant intoxication, **cannabis withdrawal**). Intoxication or withdrawal alone usually suffices. Use this diagnosis only when manic symptoms **predominate and warrant independent attention**.
- **Substance-induced delirium:** if the symptoms occur only in delirium, diagnose delirium.
- **Medication side effects:** isolated irritability, edginess or agitation ≠ manic syndrome.

## Comorbidity
- Comorbidity reflects illicit use (illegal stimulants, PCP) or diversion of prescribed stimulants. For steroids and immunosuppressants, it reflects the medical indication.
- **Delirium** may precede or accompany manic symptoms with PCP, steroids or immunosuppressants.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** describe the mood (elevated, expansive, irritable) and the energy/activity increase, and that they **dominate** the presentation. Note the manic-type symptoms observed (pressured speech, ↓ sleep, grandiosity).
- **Crit B:** build a **timeline**: substance, dose and route, last use, onset relative to intoxication, withdrawal, or medication start/dose change/stop. Cite UDS or serum levels. Note that the agent is known to cause manic symptoms.
- **Crit C:** document **no** pre-use history of mania or hypomania and no recurrent independent episodes. Plan reassessment after ~1 month of abstinence or after the medication is stopped.
- **Crit D:** orientation, attention and level of consciousness intact (no delirium). Repeat if fluctuating.
- **Crit E:** impairment or reason for clinical attention beyond routine intoxication.
- **Coding elements:** substance class, presence and severity of use disorder (sets the 4th character), onset specifier.
- **Antidepressant cases:** note whether the symptoms persisted after expected drug clearance (if so, reconsider BP-I/II).
- **MSE:** psychomotor activity, rate and pressure of speech, flight of ideas, grandiosity, irritability, sensorium/orientation (delirium screen), autonomic signs of intoxication.
