# Bipolar and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** A manic or hypomanic picture that is the **direct physiological result** of a medical illness. Best-known causes are Cushing's disease, multiple sclerosis, stroke, TBI and anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis. The diagnosis rests on temporal sequence and biological plausibility. Don't use it if mania clearly **preceded** the medical condition (that is bipolar disorder). Remember that the illness's own treatment (steroids, interferon) may be the real cause.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 287)
- **A.** A prominent, persistent period of abnormally elevated, expansive or irritable mood **and** abnormally ↑ activity or energy that **predominates** the clinical picture.
- **B.** History, exam or labs show it is the **direct pathophysiological consequence** of another medical condition.
- **C.** Not better explained by another mental disorder.
- **D.** Not exclusively during a **delirium**.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment, **or** hospitalization needed to prevent harm, **or** psychotic features.

## Specifiers & severity
| Code | Specifier | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **F06.33** | With manic features | Full criteria for a manic or hypomanic episode are **not** met |
| **F06.33** | With manic- or hypomanic-like episode | All criteria are met **except** Criterion D (manic) or F (hypomanic), i.e., everything but the etiology exclusion |
| **F06.34** | With mixed features | Depressive symptoms are also present but **do not predominate** |

## Coding
- The code is set by the specifier (above).
- **Name the medical condition in the diagnosis** (e.g., "F06.33 bipolar disorder due to hyperthyroidism, with manic features").
- **Code and list the medical condition separately, immediately before** the mental disorder (e.g., "E05.90 hyperthyroidism; F06.33 bipolar disorder due to hyperthyroidism, with manic features").

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Onset is usually during the **initial presentation** of the medical condition (**within ~1 month**). Exceptions: chronic conditions whose worsening or relapse heralds the mania.
- **Not diagnosed** if manic or hypomanic episodes clearly **preceded** the medical condition; that is bipolar disorder. The exception is when all prior episodes (or the single prior episode) were substance- or medication-associated.
- The list of causal conditions is never complete; clinical judgment is the essence.
- **Known causes:** Cushing's disease, multiple sclerosis, stroke, traumatic brain injury, **anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis** (manic or mixed mood + psychosis).
- Course: acute or subacute onset in the first weeks to month. It may remit before or just after the medical condition remits, especially with effective treatment of the manic symptoms.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Differences follow the medical condition: e.g., MS and stroke rates vary geographically; SLE is more common in females; stroke is more common in middle-aged males.

## Diagnostic markers
- Markers follow the medical condition: e.g., blood or urine steroid levels for Cushing's (which can cause manic **or** depressive syndromes); confirmatory tests for MS.

## Functional consequences
- Bipolar symptoms can worsen medical impairment and **interfere with medical treatment**, leading to worse outcomes.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Delirium:** no separate diagnosis if the mood disturbance occurs only during delirium.
- **Major/mild NCD:** this diagnosis **may be added** to NCD if the mood change is a physiological consequence of the same pathological process and irritability or elevated mood is prominent.
- **Catatonia and acute anxiety:** distinguish mania from **excited catatonia** and from anxiety-related agitation.
- **Medication-induced symptoms:** the medical condition may be treated with mood-altering drugs (steroids, **alpha-interferon**). Use all the evidence to judge the most likely or most important cause.
- **Primary bipolar disorder:** mania predates the medical condition.

## Comorbidity
- Comorbidities are those of the causal medical condition. **Delirium** can precede or accompany mania in Cushing's disease.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Crit A:** describe the manic or hypomanic symptoms and their predominance. Note whether full episode criteria are met (this determines the specifier: manic features vs manic/hypomanic-like episode) and whether depressive symptoms coexist (mixed features → F06.34).
- **Crit B:**
  - Name the medical diagnosis and its supporting data (labs, imaging, neuro exam, cortisol, thyroid tests, CSF/antibodies).
  - Give the **temporal relationship** (mood onset relative to medical onset, flare or relapse).
  - Explain why the link is **physiologically plausible**.
- **Rule-outs:**
  - Prior history of mania or hypomania independent of the medical condition.
  - Medication or substance contributions (steroids, interferon, stimulants), with your judgment of the primary cause.
  - **Delirium screen** (attention, orientation, fluctuation).
  - Catatonic excitement; anxiety-driven agitation.
- **Crit E:** impairment, need for hospitalization or psychotic features.
- **Coding:** list the medical condition's ICD-10 code first, then F06.33/F06.34 with the condition named.
- **MSE:** level of consciousness, attention, orientation, cognition (to separate from delirium/NCD); elevated or irritable affect; pressured speech; grandiosity; psychotic content.
