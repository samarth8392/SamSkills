# Bipolar and Related Disorders — Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Bipolar I requires lifetime mania; bipolar II requires hypomania plus major depression and no mania. Bipolar II can carry substantial depressive burden and is not inherently a mild form. Cyclothymia describes prolonged subthreshold fluctuation, while induced/medical diagnoses require causal evidence (p. 255).

## What unites the chapter and how presentations differ
| Presentation | Essential distinction |
|---|---|
| Bipolar I | ≥1 manic episode; depression/hypomania not required |
| Bipolar II | ≥1 hypomanic episode and ≥1 major depressive episode; no lifetime mania; distress/impairment from depression or unpredictable alternation |
| Cyclothymic | Numerous subthreshold hypomanic/depressive periods ≥2 years (≥1 year in youth), symptoms ≥half the time, no gap >2 months, never full mania/hypomania/MDE |
| Substance/medication-induced | Exposure-linked elevated/irritable mood syndrome with causal evidence |
| Due to another medical condition | Direct physiological cause established |
| Other specified/unspecified | Clinically significant bipolar-type symptoms without a full specific diagnosis |
| Unspecified mood disorder | Cross-class uncertainty between bipolar and depressive residual diagnoses |

An episode is not itself a separate diagnosis: establish its syndrome, then the lifetime disorder (pp. 256–259, 268–271, 279–280, 289–291).

## Chapter-level pitfalls and differential logic
- **Mania:** sustained mood change **and** increased energy/activity, ≥1 week (any duration if hospitalization necessary), ≥3 associated symptoms (≥4 if mood only irritable), with marked impairment, necessary hospitalization or psychosis. Ordinary enthusiasm, irritability or insomnia alone is insufficient (p. 256).
- **Hypomania:** ≥4 consecutive days, same symptom counts, unequivocal observable functional change, without marked impairment, hospitalization or psychosis. Psychosis makes the episode manic (pp. 256–257, 268–269).
- **Major depression:** ≥5 of 9 symptoms in the same ≥2-week period; depressed mood or anhedonia required, with significant distress/impairment. The same syndrome can occur in bipolar and depressive disorders; lifetime history decides classification (pp. 257–259).
- **Antidepressant emergence:** a fully syndromal episode persisting beyond treatment physiology may count; isolated activation, agitation or edginess does not establish hypomania (pp. 256–257, 269).
- **Psychotic disorders:** a manic episode superimposed on schizophrenia-spectrum illness does not automatically establish bipolar I; apply the revised disorder-level exclusions and schizoaffective timeline (pp. 259, 270, 290–291).
- **Cyclothymia:** full subsequent mood episodes change the diagnosis; do not retain cyclothymia as an additional diagnosis after that transition (p. 280).
- **Mixed features:** opposite-pole symptoms within a full episode are different from distinct alternating episodes. Simultaneous full mania and depression is mania with mixed features (pp. 292–293).
- **Rapid cycling:** ≥4 full episodes/12 months; use ≥2-month partial/full remission or a polarity switch to separate episodes. Mania and hypomania are the same pole. Hourly shifts alone do not establish rapid cycling (pp. 293–294).

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- Bipolar I coding depends on current/most recent episode, severity/psychosis and remission. Bipolar II remains **F31.81**, with episode and other features written descriptively. See disorder notes for complete tables (pp. 259–260, 271).
- Name episode type, then applicable severity/psychotic/remission features, then remaining specifiers. Use current episode features if full criteria are met; remission if not.
- Shared options include anxious distress, mixed features, rapid cycling, melancholic/atypical/psychotic features, catatonia, peripartum onset and seasonal pattern, with diagnosis/episode-specific applicability. They are not interchangeable across all disorders (pp. 291–300).
- Catatonia adds **F06.1** after the mood disorder; hypomania cannot carry psychotic features. Medical-condition variants name and code the causal illness first; induced codes depend on substance and use-disorder status.
- **F31.89** = other specified bipolar; **F31.9** = unspecified bipolar; **F39** = unresolved bipolar-versus-depressive classification (pp. 289–291).

## TR changes and notable updates visible in the source
Unspecified mood disorder is included with **F39**. The disorder-level bipolar I/II psychotic-disorder exclusions explicitly address episodes superimposed on schizophrenia-spectrum disorders; the other-specified examples include a manic episode superimposed on those disorders, excluding episodes already encompassed by schizoaffective disorder (pp. 259, 270, 290–291). Do not describe every longstanding DSM-5 specifier as newly introduced by TR.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
Build a lifetime timeline with episode boundaries, baseline comparison, mood plus energy/activity, sleep need, collateral observations, impairment/psychosis and exposure/medical timing. Distinguish reported history from observed current state, and support each specifier with its own timing/count requirements. A depressive presentation warrants assessment of past elevation before assigning a unipolar formulation.
