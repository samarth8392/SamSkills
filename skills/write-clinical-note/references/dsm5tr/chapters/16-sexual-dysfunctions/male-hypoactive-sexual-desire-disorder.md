# Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder

> **Spark note:** Persistently deficient or absent sexual thoughts/fantasies **and** desire for sexual activity (both needed) for ~6 months, with distress; the clinician judges "deficient" in light of age and sociocultural context. A desire discrepancy with a partner is not enough, and a temporary reaction to a stressor shouldn't be diagnosed. Rarely the only sexual diagnosis in men, so look for erectile and ejaculatory problems, depression, and endocrine causes.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Persistently/recurrently deficient or absent **sexual/erotic thoughts or fantasies and desire for sexual activity**; deficiency judged by clinician considering age and general/sociocultural context.
- **B.** ≈**6 months**.
- **C.** Clinically significant distress in the individual.
- **D.** Not better explained by a nonsexual mental disorder or severe relationship distress/stressors; not attributable to substance/medication or medical condition.

## Specifiers & severity
- Lifelong / Acquired; Generalized / Situational.
- Severity = distress (mild / moderate / severe-extreme).

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Consider interpersonal context; **desire discrepancy alone is insufficient**.
- **Both** low desire and deficient fantasies are required.
- Duration criterion guards against diagnosing a reactive, temporary drop (e.g., after being fired or a business failure). If the stressor and low desire persist >6 months, clinician judgment decides.

## Associated features
- Sometimes linked with erectile/ejaculatory concerns (persistent ED may lead to lost interest).
- Often stops initiating and is minimally receptive to partner initiation; sexual activity (masturbation or partnered) can still occur despite low desire.
- Consider couple's initiation preferences: if the man prefers his partner to initiate, assess his receptivity rather than his lack of initiation.
- Five assessment factors: partner; relationship; individual vulnerability/psychiatric comorbidity/stressors; cultural/religious; medical.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- 3–17% in representative samples; ages 16–24: 3–14%; ages 60–74: 16–28%.
- Persistent lack of interest ≥6 months: ~6%; clinically significant distress: **<2%**. Only 10.5% of men with sexual problems sought help.
- Normative age decline: ~5.2% at age 27 → 18.5% at age 50.
- Men, like women, have varied desire triggers and reasons for sex; visual cues may be stronger in younger men.
- **Temperamental:** mood and anxiety symptoms strongly predict low desire (up to half of men with psychiatric history vs 15% without); self-view, perceived partner desire, emotional connection; restrictive/conservative sexual beliefs; lack of erotic thoughts; erection worries and low erectile confidence.
- **Environmental:** alcohol; problematic relationships, reduced attraction to partner, long-term relationship, sexual boredom, work stress; cohort trend toward lower desire in some high-income countries.
- **Genetic/physiological:** hyperprolactinemia, hypogonadism; age. Low desire common in hypogonadal men; possible testosterone threshold effect.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Low desire ranges from 12.5% (Northern European) to 28% (Southeast Asian) men ages 40–80; distress linked to occupational stress in a Portugal/Croatia/Norway survey.
- Desire and arousal kept separate in men (unlike FSIAD); men report higher desire intensity/frequency on average, but desire-erectile overlap is common clinically.
- Low desire more often reported by gay (19%) than heterosexual (9%) men.
- Despite the name, criteria are not sex-dependent and may be applied to gender-diverse people by clinical judgment.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Nonsexual mental disorders** (MDD with pervasive anhedonia) better explain → no MHSDD.
- **Substance/medication** onset/offset linked → substance/medication-induced.
- **Medical** (hypogonadism, diabetes, thyroid dysfunction, CNS disease) better explains → no MHSDD.
- **Interpersonal factors** (severe relationship distress, stressors) → no MHSDD.
- **Other sexual dysfunctions** do not rule it out: up to half of men with low desire have erectile difficulties; slightly fewer have early ejaculation.
- **Asexual** self-identification explains low desire → no diagnosis.

## Comorbidity
- Rarely the sole sexual diagnosis: ED, delayed ejaculation, premature ejaculation.
- Depression and other mental disorders; endocrine factors.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** document both elements: "rarely has sexual thoughts or fantasies" and "little or no desire for sexual activity"; note initiation and receptivity patterns; account for age and cultural context.
- **B:** ≥~6 months; lifelong vs acquired; generalized vs partner-specific.
- **C:** client's own distress (not just partner complaint/desire discrepancy).
- **Rule-outs:** depression/anxiety screen; medications (SSRIs, antipsychotics, 5-α-reductase inhibitors, opioids); alcohol; labs via PCP (testosterone, prolactin, thyroid, glucose); recent stressors; relationship quality; asexual identity.
- Assess co-occurring erectile/ejaculatory problems.
