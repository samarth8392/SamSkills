# Other Specified and Unspecified Sexual Dysfunction

> **Spark note:** Residual categories for distressing sexual dysfunction symptoms that predominate but don't meet full criteria for any specific disorder. Use **other specified** (F52.8) when you state the reason, e.g., "sexual aversion". Use **unspecified** (F52.9) when you choose not to say why, or there isn't enough information. Note that both require distress **in the individual**; the text does not mention impairment.

## Criteria at a glance
- Symptoms characteristic of a sexual dysfunction.
- Cause clinically significant **distress in the individual**.
- Predominate in the clinical picture.
- Do not meet full criteria for any disorder in the sexual dysfunctions class.

## Coding
| Code | Use | Recording |
|---|---|---|
| F52.8 | Other specified sexual dysfunction — clinician states why criteria aren't met | "Other specified sexual dysfunction, [reason]" e.g., "sexual aversion" |
| F52.9 | Unspecified sexual dysfunction — reason not stated, or insufficient information (e.g., ED/first contact) | "Unspecified sexual dysfunction" |

## Example presentations
- **Named in the criteria box:** sexual aversion (p. 710).
- **Named elsewhere in the chapter as belonging here:**
  - **Anhedonic ejaculation:** normal ejaculation with reduced pleasure/orgasm sensation (p. 677).
  - **Male urological genito-pelvic pain** (e.g., chronic pelvic pain syndrome) is a residual example (p. 696). For gender-diverse clients, anatomy-linked diagnosis follows current anatomy rather than assigned sex (p. 673).
- Other logical uses (clinician judgment, not listed in text): subthreshold duration (<~6 months) with marked distress; frequency below ~75% of occasions; mixed presentation not fitting one category.

## Differential diagnosis
- Check first whether a specific disorder fits (thresholds often say "approximately," allowing judgment).
- Substance/medication causation → substance/medication-induced sexual dysfunction instead.
- Fully medical cause → no mental disorder diagnosis.
- Better explained by nonsexual mental disorder, relationship distress/IPV, stressors, inadequate stimulation → no sexual dysfunction diagnosis (Z code if relationship-driven, e.g., Z63.0).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Describe the specific sexual symptom(s) and the client's distress.
- State which criterion is not met (for F52.8), e.g., "symptoms for 3 months only," "pain pattern in male client," "normal ejaculation with absent pleasure."
- Document rule-outs: medication/substance review, medical referral, depression screen, relationship/IPV screen.
- For F52.9: note what information is missing and the plan to clarify.
