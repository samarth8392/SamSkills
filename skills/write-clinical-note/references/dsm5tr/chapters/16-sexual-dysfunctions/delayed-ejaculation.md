# Delayed Ejaculation

> **Spark note:** Marked delay, infrequency or absence of ejaculation during **partnered** sex on almost all occasions, without wanting the delay, for ~6 months, with distress. Many men can ejaculate with self-stimulation but not with a partner. No precise time cutoff exists, so it rests on clinical judgment. Do not diagnose if dissatisfaction is entirely due to unrealistic expectations, normal aging, a medication (e.g., an SSRI) or a urologic/medical cause.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Either of the following on almost all/all occasions (~75–100%) of **partnered** sexual activity (in identified situations or, if generalized, all contexts), **without the individual desiring delay**:
  1. Marked delay in ejaculation.
  2. Marked infrequency or absence of ejaculation.
- **B.** Duration ≈ **6 months** minimum.
- **C.** Clinically significant distress **in the individual**.
- **D.** Not better explained by a nonsexual mental disorder or severe relationship distress/other significant stressors; not attributable to a substance/medication or another medical condition.

## Specifiers & severity
- **Lifelong** (since sexually active) vs **Acquired** (after relatively normal function).
- **Generalized** vs **Situational** (certain stimulation, situations, partners).
- **Severity by distress:** mild / moderate / severe-extreme distress over Criterion A.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Despite adequate stimulation and desire to ejaculate (p. 674).
- Partnered activity includes manual, oral, coital or anal stimulation.
- Diagnosis usually by self-report; in heterosexual couples, the **female partner's distress** often drives help-seeking.
- Typical intravaginal ejaculatory latency time (IELT) for most men is ~4–10 minutes, but there is no agreed definition of "delay" (p. 675).
- No clear line between DE and age-related delay → judge using psychosexual and medical history, age, relationship context and stimulation patterns.
- **Exclude** if dissatisfaction is entirely due to unrealistic expectations.

## Associated features
- Prolonged thrusting to exhaustion/genital discomfort, sometimes injury to self or partner; avoidance of sex.
- Linked with highly frequent masturbation, idiosyncratic masturbation techniques a partner can't easily duplicate, and a gap between masturbation fantasies and partnered sex.
- Less coital activity, more relationship distress, sexual dissatisfaction, lower subjective arousal, performance anxiety, general health issues.
- Six assessment factors: partner; relationship; individual vulnerability (e.g., hypoactive desire)/psychiatric comorbidity/stressors; cultural/religious; **medical** (hypogonadism, MS, diabetic neuropathy); **substances/medications** (serotonergic drugs).

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Prevalence: US ~1–5%; up to 11% internationally (definition variability).
- Lifelong begins with early sexual experiences and persists; acquired follows normal function. Either may be generalized or situational.
- Prevalence rises with age: reduced ejaculate volume, force, sensation and longer refractory time.
- Psychosocial: **depression** and **relationship dissatisfaction** are predominant contributors.
- Medical: radical prostatectomy and other procedures disrupting sympathetic/somatic genital innervation; spinal cord injury, stroke, MS, pelvic surgery, severe diabetes, epilepsy, hormonal abnormalities, sleep apnea; alcohol misuse, bowel dysfunction, cannabis, environmental factors.
- Medications: α-adrenergic inhibitors (e.g., tamsulosin), antihypertensives, antidepressants (SSRIs), antipsychotics.
- Aging: loss of fast-conducting peripheral sensory nerves, decreased sex steroids/androgens.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- By definition given only to males (anatomy-based; for gender-diverse people use current anatomy, see overview). Distressing orgasm difficulty in women → female orgasmic disorder.
- Definitions apply across sexual orientations, but research is largely on male-female intercourse.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
- Considerable psychological distress in one or both partners.
- May contribute to difficulty conceiving and lead to fertility assessment; men often don't disclose unless asked directly.

## Differential diagnosis
- **Another medical condition/injury or its treatment:** main challenge is separating DE fully explained by medical causes from multifactorial DE.
- **Urological conditions:** retrograde ejaculation, anejaculation, ejaculatory duct obstruction (hormonal, neurological, anatomical causes).
- **Substance/medication use:** antidepressants, antipsychotics, α-sympathetic drugs, alcohol, opioids → substance/medication-induced sexual dysfunction instead.
- **Dysfunction with orgasm:** clarify whether the complaint is ejaculation, orgasm sensation, or both. Normal ejaculation with reduced pleasure (**anhedonic ejaculation**) → other specified/unspecified sexual dysfunction, not DE.
- Unrealistic expectations / normal aging → no diagnosis.

## Comorbidity
- May be more common in **severe major depressive disorder**.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **A:** client's estimate of latency or ejaculation frequency in partnered sex; % of occasions affected; whether ejaculation occurs with masturbation; client does not want the delay.
- **B:** onset date and duration ≥~6 months.
- **C:** client's own distress (frustration, avoidance, worry) — note if distress is mainly the partner's.
- **D / rule-outs:** medication list (SSRIs, antipsychotics, tamsulosin, antihypertensives, opioids), alcohol/cannabis use, medical history (diabetes, prostate surgery, neurological disease, low testosterone), urology referral; screen for depression; relationship quality.
- Subtypes: "since first partnered experience" (lifelong) vs "after years of normal function" (acquired); "only with partner, not with self-stimulation" (situational).
- Hypothetical phrasing example (not client evidence): "Reports reaching ejaculation in fewer than 1 in 4 partnered encounters for ~9 months despite adequate arousal and desire to ejaculate; ejaculates normally with masturbation. Describes marked distress and avoiding intimacy. No serotonergic medications; PCP labs pending."
