# Borderline Personality Disorder (Cluster B)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive instability of relationships, self-image, and affect plus marked impulsivity,
> organized around abandonment sensitivity. Reactive mood shifts usually last **hours, rarely more than a few days**. Assess full mood-episode criteria separately; duration alone does not rule bipolar or depressive disorders in or out. Don't diagnose BPD from a cross-sectional presentation during a mood episode
> without documenting early onset and long-standing course. Prognosis is better than once believed: stable remissions are common.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1003–1004)
Pervasive instability of interpersonal relationships, self-image, affects, and marked impulsivity; by early adulthood;
multiple contexts; **≥5 of 9**:
1. Frantic efforts to avoid real or imagined abandonment (*exclude* suicidal/self-mutilating behavior — that's item 5)
2. Unstable, intense relationships alternating between idealization and devaluation
3. Identity disturbance: markedly, persistently unstable self-image or sense of self
4. Impulsivity in **≥2** potentially self-damaging areas (spending, sex, substances, reckless driving, binge eating)
   (*exclude* suicidal/self-mutilating behavior)
5. Recurrent suicidal behavior, gestures, threats, or self-mutilation
6. Affective instability from marked mood reactivity (episodic dysphoria, irritability, anxiety usually **hours, rarely > a few days**)
7. Chronic feelings of emptiness
8. Inappropriate intense anger or difficulty controlling anger (temper displays, constant anger, recurrent fights)
9. Transient, stress-related paranoid ideation or severe dissociative symptoms

No separate exclusion criterion beyond the General PD criteria.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1004–1005)
- **Abandonment (1):** perceived separation/rejection or loss of structure → profound shifts in self-image, affect, cognition,
  behavior; panic/fury at brief lateness or end of session; equate abandonment with being "bad"; intolerance of being alone.
- **Relationships (2):** idealize early, demand time, overshare quickly; switch to devaluation (others not giving/caring/"there"
  enough); nurture others expecting reciprocal availability on demand; others seen as beneficent or cruelly punitive.
- **Identity (3):** abrupt role shifts (needy supplicant → righteous avenger); self as bad/evil; at times feel they don't exist;
  worse in unstructured school/work.
- **Impulsivity (4):** gambling, overspending, binge eating, substance misuse, unsafe sex, reckless driving.
- **Self-harm (5):** suicidality often the reason for presenting; precipitated by separation/rejection or increased responsibility;
  cutting/burning common, may occur during dissociation; relieves by restoring feeling or expiating badness.
- **Affect (6):** baseline dysphoria punctuated by anger, panic, despair; rarely relieved by well-being; reactive to interpersonal stress.
- **Emptiness (7):** with aloneness; boredom → excitement seeking.
- **Anger (8):** sarcasm, bitterness, outbursts when caregiver seen as neglectful/abandoning; followed by shame/guilt.
- **Stress sx (9):** transient (minutes–hours) paranoid ideation or dissociation (e.g., depersonalization), usually not
  warranting extra diagnosis; often remit when caregiver nurturance returns.

## Associated features (p. 1005–1006)
- Self-sabotage when a goal is near (quitting before graduation; regressing after praise of progress; ending good relationships).
- Psychotic-like sx under stress (hallucinations, body-image distortion, ideas of reference, hypnagogic phenomena).
- More secure with transitional objects (pet, possession).
- Premature death by suicide, especially with comorbid depressive or substance use disorders; but deaths from accidents/illness
  are **>2×** as common as suicide.
- Physical handicaps from self-harm/failed attempts; recurrent job loss, interrupted education, separation/divorce.
- Childhood histories: physical/sexual abuse, neglect, hostile conflict, early parental loss.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- NCS-R 1.4%; NESARC 5.9%; median of 7 studies 2.7%. ~6% primary care, ~10% outpatient MH, ~20% psychiatric inpatients (p. 1006).
- Typically considered adult onset, but full criteria seen at age 12–13 in treatment settings.
- Course: severity lessens in 30s–40s; prospective studies show **stable remissions of 1–8 years very common**; impulsive sx
  remit fastest, affective sx slowest. **Recovery** (remission + good psychosocial functioning) harder and less stable; lack
  of recovery linked to disability benefits and poor physical health.
- **Environmental:** childhood abuse and emotional neglect; sexual abuse higher in inpatients than outpatients → risk factor
  for severity; sexual abuse is neither necessary nor sufficient.
- **Genetic:** ~**5×** more common in first-degree relatives; familial risk for SUD, anxiety, ASPD, depressive/bipolar disorders.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1007)
- Found worldwide. Contexts with demands for self-affirmation/acceptance, ambiguous/conflictual authority relationships,
  or adaptation uncertainty can foster impulsivity, instability, aggression, dissociation — either BPD or transient
  contextual reactions. Evaluate sx (number of partners, relationship shifting, substance use) against cultural norms.
- More common in women **clinically**; **no sex difference in community samples** — may reflect help-seeking.
  Similar presentation; possibly more externalizing in males, internalizing in females.

## Suicide risk (p. 1007)
- Impulsive and antisocial behaviors associated with increased suicide risk (longitudinal study).
- Hospitalized BPD followed 24 yrs: **~6% died by suicide** vs. 1.4% in other-PD comparison group.
- 10-yr follow-up: recurrent suicidal behavior defining; attempt rates declined **79% → 13%** over time.
- The associated-features text particularly notes premature suicide death with comorbid depressive or substance use disorders (p. 1005).

## Functional consequences
- Job loss, interrupted education, divorce, physical sequelae of self-harm; disability with non-recovery.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1007–1009)
- **Depressive and bipolar disorders:** often co-occur → diagnose both if met; avoid BPD from cross-sectional picture
  alone without documenting early onset and long-standing course.
- **Separation anxiety disorder (adults):** both fear abandonment; BPD additionally centers on identity, self-direction,
  interpersonal dysfunction, impulsivity.
- **Histrionic PD:** shares attention seeking, manipulation, shifting emotions; BPD has self-destructiveness, angry ruptures,
  chronic deep emptiness and loneliness.
- **Schizotypal PD:** paranoid ideas/illusions in BPD are more transient, interpersonally reactive, responsive to structure.
- **Paranoid & narcissistic PD:** anger at minor stimuli, but relatively stable self-image and lack of self-destructiveness,
  repetitive impulsivity, abandonment concerns.
- **Antisocial PD:** manipulation for profit/power vs. BPD's aim of gaining caretakers' concern.
- **Dependent PD:** abandonment → BPD emptiness, rage, demands vs. DPD appeasement, submissiveness, seeking replacement;
  BPD has unstable intense relationships.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**
- **Identity problem** (developmental, e.g., adolescence; not a mental disorder): transient instability, existential dilemmas,
  uncertainty, sexual-orientation conflicts, career pressures — especially with substance use — can mimic BPD.

## Comorbidity (p. 1009)
- Depressive and bipolar disorders, SUDs, anxiety disorders (esp. panic and social anxiety disorder), eating disorders
  (esp. bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder), PTSD, ADHD; frequently other PDs.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **Pattern evidence:** map each endorsed criterion to dated examples across varied contexts and to onset by early adulthood.
- **1:** reactions to separations (texts/calls, pleading, showing up) — note excluded if the behavior is self-harm.
- **2:** relationship history with idealization→devaluation cycles (including toward clinicians).
- **3:** shifting goals, values, identity labels; "feels like no one."
- **4:** list ≥2 specific self-damaging impulsive domains with frequency.
- **5:** dates/methods of self-harm and suicide attempts, precipitants, lethality, function of self-harm.
- **6:** mood shifts lasting hours (record duration explicitly to separate from mood episodes).
- **7–8:** emptiness descriptions; anger episodes, property damage, fights.
- **9:** transient dissociation/paranoia tied to stress, duration minutes–hours.
- **MSE:** labile/reactive affect, intense or angry tone, possible self-harm scars, splitting language, transient dissociation;
  reality testing generally intact between stress episodes.
- **Rule-outs:** current MDE/hypomania/mania (duration, neurovegetative sx), substance intoxication, PTSD, medical/neurologic
  causes, developmental identity problem in adolescents.
- **Risk:** document the actual assessment of suicidal thoughts/behavior, nonsuicidal self-injury, protective factors, and management needs; the chapter does not mandate a particular risk instrument or visit schedule.
