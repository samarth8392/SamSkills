# Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder (Cluster C)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, and mental/interpersonal control at the
> cost of flexibility, openness, and efficiency. OCPD is distinguished from OCD by its pervasive personality pattern; true obsessions/compulsions require separate assessment. Traits are often ego-syntonic. Both can be diagnosed when both are met. Moderate traits can be adaptive in high-performance
> settings — require inflexibility and impairment.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1026)
Pervasive preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, mental/interpersonal control, at expense of flexibility, openness,
efficiency; by early adulthood; multiple contexts; **≥4 of 8**:
1. Preoccupied with details, rules, lists, order, organization, schedules — major point of activity lost
2. Perfectionism interferes with task completion
3. Excessive devotion to work/productivity, excluding leisure and friendships (not from economic necessity)
4. Overconscientious, scrupulous, inflexible about morality/ethics/values (not explained by cultural or religious identification)
5. Can't discard worn-out/worthless objects without sentimental value
6. Reluctant to delegate or work with others unless they do it exactly their way
7. Miserly toward self and others; money hoarded for future catastrophes
8. Rigidity and stubbornness

## Specifiers & severity
- None.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1026–1028)
- Control via rules, trivial details, lists, schedules; repeated checking; loses track of time; searches for a lost list rather than recreating it.
- Others annoyed by delays; time poorly allocated; key tasks left to last moment.
- Endless rewrites; missed deadlines or all-nighters; other life areas fall into disarray.
- No time for evenings/weekends off; postpones vacations; brings work on leisure; turns play into structured tasks
  (corrects a child's play order); hobbies approached with methodical intensity.
- Rigid moral standards imposed on self/others; merciless self-criticism; rigid deference to authority and literal rule compliance
  (won't lend a dollar on principle).
- "Pack rat" — keeps items "in case"; clutter of unread material/unfinished projects; upset if others discard them.
- Micromanages; one right way to load a dishwasher; refuses help even when behind.
- Living standard far below means.
- Plans in meticulous detail; won't change; refuses compromise on "principle."

## Associated features (p. 1028)
- Painful, time-consuming decisions when rules don't dictate; may never start.
- Anger when control is lost, usually expressed indirectly (ruminating over a tip) or as righteous indignation over minor matters.
- Attentive to dominance–submission; excessive deference to respected authority, resistance to others.
- Difficulty sharing emotions; stilted affection; uncomfortable with emotional people; formal, serious; rarely compliments;
  preoccupied with logic.
- Occupational difficulty when flexibility/compromise needed.

## Prevalence
- NCS-R 2.4%; NESARC 7.9%; median of 5 studies 4.7% (p. 1029) — highest median among the PDs listed.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1029)
- Exclude culturally sanctioned habits/styles. Some communities, religious groups, professions, migrants embrace rigid codes,
  work demands, scrupulosity, perfectionism reinforced by group norms — not by themselves OCPD.
- Equal prevalence in men and women in large population studies.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1029–1030)
- **OCD:** true obsessions and compulsions; record both if both met.
- **Hoarding disorder:** consider especially if hoarding extreme (fire hazard, blocked passage); record both if both met.
- **Narcissistic PD:** both profess perfectionism; NPD believes they've achieved it, OCPD self-critical.
- **Narcissistic/antisocial PD:** lack generosity but indulge themselves; OCPD miserly toward self and others.
- **Schizoid PD:** formality/detachment in OCPD from emotional discomfort and work devotion; schizoid lacks capacity for intimacy.
- **Adaptive traits:** only inflexible, maladaptive, impairing traits qualify.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**

## Comorbidity (p. 1030)
- Anxiety disorders (GAD, separation anxiety, social anxiety, specific phobias) and OCD raise likelihood of OCPD — but most
  people with OCD do **not** have OCPD.
- Overlap with "type A" features (work preoccupation, competitiveness, time urgency) — possible MI risk group.
- Possible association with depressive, bipolar, and eating disorders.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **1/2:** missed deadlines due to rewrites/checking; time lost to lists and procedures — cite specific projects.
- **3:** weekly work hours, absent leisure, vacations deferred; note whether economic necessity explains it.
- **4:** moral rigidity examples; document cultural/religious context considered.
- **5/7:** saved worthless items (distinguish from hoarding disorder severity: living space usable?); frugality inconsistent with means.
- **6/8:** conflicts from refusing to delegate, inflexibility in plans.
- **MSE:** formal, controlled, stiff affect; detailed/circumstantial over-precision; content centered on correctness and
  productivity; assess intrusive obsessions/ritualized compulsions independently rather than treating their absence as a requirement; OCD can coexist.
- **Rule-outs:** OCD (ask explicitly about intrusive thoughts and neutralizing rituals), hoarding disorder, depressive episode
  (indecision), medical/substance causes.
- **Impairment:** relationships, productivity paradox (perfectionism lowers output), family conflict.
