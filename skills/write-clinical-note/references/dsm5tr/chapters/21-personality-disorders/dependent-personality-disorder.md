# Dependent Personality Disorder (Cluster C)

> **Spark note:** A pervasive, excessive need to be cared for, producing submissive, clinging behavior and fear of
> separation, rooted in a self-view of being unable to function alone. Critical caveats: realistic fear of
> retribution (e.g., abusive partner) does not count, tolerating abuse counts only if other options clearly exist,
> and dependency appropriate to a medical condition, age, or culture is not pathological.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1021–1022)
Pervasive excessive need to be taken care of → submissive, clinging behavior and separation fears; by early adulthood;
multiple contexts; **≥5 of 8**:
1. Difficulty with everyday decisions without excessive advice/reassurance
2. Needs others to assume responsibility for most major life areas
3. Difficulty expressing disagreement from fear of losing support/approval (*exclude realistic fear of retribution*)
4. Difficulty initiating or doing things alone (from lack of self-confidence, **not** low motivation/energy)
5. Goes to excessive lengths for nurturance, even volunteering for unpleasant tasks
6. Uncomfortable/helpless alone due to exaggerated fear of inability to self-care
7. Urgently seeks a new relationship for care when a close one ends
8. Unrealistically preoccupied with fear of being left to care for self

## Specifiers & severity
- None.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1022–1023)
- Behaviors aim to elicit caregiving; self-perception of inability to function without help.
- Even trivial choices (shirt color, umbrella) need reassurance; often a single person (parent/spouse) decides residence, job, friends.
- Adolescents may let parents choose clothes, friends, school.
- Must exceed age- and situation-appropriate needs (children, elderly, disabled); with serious medical illness/disability, the
  dependency must go beyond what the condition explains.
- Agrees with things they think are wrong; suppresses anger toward supporters.
- Present as inept; function adequately if supervised/approved; may fear appearing competent (loss of support) → fail to learn
  independent living skills.
- May submit to unreasonable demands, extraordinary self-sacrifice, tolerate verbal/physical/sexual abuse (counts only when
  other options are clearly available).
- After a breakup or caregiver's death, quickly and indiscriminately attaches to a new person.
- Fears must be excessive and unrealistic (example: an elderly man with cancer moving in with his son is appropriate dependence).

## Associated features (p. 1023)
- Pessimism, self-doubt, belittling own abilities; criticism taken as proof of worthlessness.
- Seek overprotection/dominance; occupational impairment if initiative required; avoid responsibility; anxious with decisions.

## Prevalence · Development & course
- NCS-R 0.6%; NESARC 0.5%; median of 6 studies 0.4% (p. 1024).
- Great caution, if at all, in children/adolescents.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1024)
- Appropriateness of dependence varies by age and sociocultural group; threshold for each criterion must consider them.
- Count only when clearly beyond cultural norms or reflecting unrealistic concerns; deference, politeness, passivity emphasized
  in some societies can be misread; societies may foster dependence differently by sex.
- DPD shows pervasive indecision, subjugation, lack of initiative, silence, social distancing far beyond cultural politeness norms.
- Diagnosed more often in women (clinical and community).

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1024–1025)
- **Separation anxiety disorder (adults):** overconcern for offspring, spouse, parents, pets and distress on separation from them;
  DPD fears being unable to care for self.
- **Other mental disorders and medical conditions:** dependency as a consequence of depressive disorders, panic disorder,
  agoraphobia, or medical illness.
- **Other PDs:** many have dependent features; DPD marked by predominantly submissive, clinging behavior and self-perceived inability to function.
  - **Borderline:** abandonment → emptiness, rage, demands (BPD) vs. appeasement, submissiveness, replacement relationship (DPD);
    BPD has unstable intense relationships.
  - **Histrionic:** both need reassurance and may seem childlike; HPD gregarious, flamboyant, actively demands attention, less insight
    into dependency; DPD self-effacing and docile.
  - **Avoidant:** both inadequacy and need for reassurance; AvPD withdraws until certain of acceptance; DPD seeks/maintains connections.
- **Traits:** only inflexible, maladaptive, impairing traits qualify.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**

## Comorbidity (p. 1025)
- Increased risk of depressive, anxiety, adjustment disorders.
- Often co-occurs with borderline, avoidant, histrionic PD.
- Chronic physical illness or persistent separation anxiety disorder in childhood/adolescence may predispose.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **1/2/4:** concrete examples of decisions deferred to others (who decides finances, housing, daily choices); waits for others to start tasks.
- **3:** inability to disagree — explicitly document assessment of safety and whether fear of retribution is realistic (IPV screen).
- **5:** submission to unreasonable demands; if abuse tolerated, document that alternative options exist.
- **6/8:** distress when alone; fear of being left to self-care disproportionate to circumstances.
- **7:** rapid relationship replacement after losses (timeline).
- **MSE:** deferential, seeks reassurance from clinician, difficulty answering without guidance, self-deprecating, anxious;
  no psychotic features.
- **Rule-outs:** depressive/panic/agoraphobic disorders causing state-dependent dependency; medical/disability-appropriate needs;
  age and cultural norms; separation anxiety disorder (worry for others vs. self).
- Assess **IPV/safety** since DPD may tolerate abuse.
