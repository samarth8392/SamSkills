# Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition

> **Spark note:** A persistent personality disturbance that is a *change* from the person's baseline and is the direct
> physiological result of a medical condition (e.g., frontal lobe injury, temporal lobe epilepsy). Unlike PDs, onset is
> variable and etiology specific. Pitfall: psychological adjustment to illness (e.g., dependency after a stroke) does
> **not** qualify — a direct pathophysiological mechanism must be established.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1030–1031)
- **A.** Persistent personality disturbance = change from prior characteristic pattern.
  *Children:* marked deviation from normal development or significant change in usual behavior, lasting **≥1 year**.
- **B.** Evidence (history, exam, labs) of direct pathophysiological consequence of another medical condition.
- **C.** Not better explained by another mental disorder (incl. another mental disorder due to AMC).
- **D.** Not exclusively during delirium.
- **E.** Clinically significant distress or impairment.

## Specifiers & severity (subtypes) (p. 1031)
| Subtype | Predominant feature |
|---|---|
| Labile type | Affective lability |
| Disinhibited type | Poor impulse control (e.g., sexual indiscretions) |
| Aggressive type | Aggressive behavior |
| Apathetic type | Marked apathy and indifference |
| Paranoid type | Suspiciousness or paranoid ideation |
| Other type | None of the above |
| Combined type | More than one feature predominates |
| Unspecified type | (listed without definition) |

## Coding (p. 1031)
| Code | Use |
|---|---|
| F07.0 | Personality change due to [name medical condition] |
| Recording order | Code the medical condition first, immediately before F07.0 — e.g., G40.209 temporal lobe epilepsy; F07.0 personality change due to temporal lobe epilepsy |
| Other specified [substance]-related disorder with personality change | When an etiological link to substance use is intended instead (p. 1033) |

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1031–1032)
- Common manifestations: affective instability, poor impulse control, aggression/rage grossly out of proportion to stressors,
  marked apathy, suspiciousness, paranoid ideation. Others describe the person as "not himself/herself."
- Distinct from PDs in specific etiology, phenomenology, and more variable onset/course.
- Presentation depends on lesion: **frontal lobe** injury → poor judgment/foresight, facetiousness, disinhibition, euphoria;
  **right hemisphere stroke** → personality change with unilateral spatial neglect, anosognosia, motor impersistence, other deficits.

## Associated features (p. 1032)
- Causes: CNS neoplasms, head trauma, cerebrovascular disease, Huntington's disease, epilepsy, CNS infections (e.g., HIV),
  endocrine conditions (hypothyroidism, hypo-/hyperadrenocorticism), autoimmune with CNS involvement (e.g., SLE).
- Exam, labs, prevalence, onset reflect the underlying condition.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1032–1033)
- **Chronic medical conditions with pain/disability:** diagnose only if direct mechanism; not for behavioral/psychological
  adjustment (e.g., dependency needing assistance after head trauma, cardiovascular disease, dementia).
- **Delirium or major NCD:** not given if exclusively during delirium; **may** be added to major NCD if the change is a
  physiological consequence of the same pathology and is a prominent part of the presentation.
- **Another mental disorder due to AMC:** not given if better explained (e.g., depressive disorder due to brain tumor).
- **Substance use disorders:** personality change common with long-standing SUD; use other specified [substance]-related disorder
  with personality change to indicate etiology.
- **Other mental disorders** (schizophrenia, delusional, depressive/bipolar, other specified/unspecified disruptive/impulse-control/
  conduct disorders, panic disorder): personality change may be associated, but no specific physiological factor.
- **Other personality disorders:** require change from baseline + specific medical etiology.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **A:** baseline personality description (collateral often helpful) vs. current change, with onset date relative to medical event;
  in children, ≥1-year deviation.
- **B:** cite the medical evidence (neuroimaging, EEG, labs, neuro exam, diagnosis date) and temporal link.
- **C/D:** assess competing mental disorders, including those due to the medical condition. Establish that the personality disturbance is not confined to delirium; a single normal orientation/attention examination is insufficient to determine the full course.
- **E:** functional/safety consequences (job loss, aggression incidents, disinhibited acts).
- **Subtype:** record the predominant feature and actual reported/observed examples; do not infer aggression or a TBI history from this prompt.
- **MSE:** disinhibition, facetiousness, lability, apathy, anosognosia, perseveration; cognitive screen results.
- **Coding line:** "[ICD code] [medical condition]; F07.0 personality change due to [condition], [subtype]."
