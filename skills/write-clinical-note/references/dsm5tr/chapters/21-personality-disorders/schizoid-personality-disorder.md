# Schizoid Personality Disorder (Cluster A)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive detachment from relationships plus restricted emotional expression — the person
> genuinely does not want closeness. The classic error is confusing it with avoidant PD (who *wants* relationships
> but fears rejection) or with milder autism spectrum disorder (which has stereotyped behaviors/interests).

## Criteria at a glance (p. 990)
- **A.** Pervasive detachment from social relationships + restricted range of emotional expression in interpersonal settings;
  by early adulthood; multiple contexts; **≥4 of 7**:
  1. Neither desires nor enjoys close relationships, including family
  2. Almost always chooses solitary activities
  3. Little or no interest in sexual experiences with another person
  4. Pleasure in few, if any, activities
  5. No close friends/confidants other than first-degree relatives
  6. Appears indifferent to praise or criticism
  7. Emotional coldness, detachment, or flattened affect
- **B.** Not exclusively during schizophrenia, bipolar/depressive disorder with psychotic features, another psychotic
  disorder, **or autism spectrum disorder**; not attributable to another medical condition.
- **Note:** If met before schizophrenia onset, add "premorbid."

## Specifiers & severity
- None beyond "(premorbid)."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 991)
- Lacks desire for intimacy; little satisfaction from family/groups; "loners."
- Prefers mechanical/abstract tasks (e.g., computer or math games).
- Reduced pleasure from sensory, bodily, interpersonal experiences.
- Oblivious to social subtleties; seems inept or superficial/self-absorbed; "bland" exterior; rarely reciprocates smiles/nods.
- Claims rarely to feel strong emotions; constricted, cold, aloof.
- Rarely, when comfortable, may disclose painful feelings related to social interaction.

## Associated features (p. 991)
- Difficulty expressing anger even when provoked; lives seem directionless ("drift").
- Passive reactions to adversity; difficulty responding to important life events.
- Few friendships, infrequent dating, often unmarried.
- Work impaired if interpersonal; may do well in socially isolated jobs.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- Uncommon clinically. NCS-R 4.9%; NESARC 3.1%; median of 6 studies 1.3% (p. 992).
- Early signs: solitariness, poor peer relations, underachievement → teasing.
- **Genetic:** increased prevalence in relatives of people with schizophrenia or schizotypal PD.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 992)
- Defensive styles may be mislabeled schizoid: rural-to-metropolitan movers may show months of "emotional freezing"
  (solitary activity, constricted affect, communication deficits); immigrants misperceived as cold/hostile, possibly reacting
  to ostracism.
- Sex: possibly more common in men; other research shows no difference.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 992–993)
- **Psychotic disorders/mood with psychotic features:** persistent psychotic sx; schizoid PD added only if it preceded and
  persists in remission → "(premorbid)."
- **Autism spectrum disorder:** very hard to separate in milder forms; ASD has stereotyped behaviors and interests.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**
- **Schizotypal PD:** has cognitive/perceptual distortions.
- **Paranoid PD:** has suspiciousness/paranoid ideation.
- **Avoidant PD:** isolation from fear of embarrassment/rejection; schizoid has pervasive detachment and limited desire.
- **OCPD:** apparent detachment from work devotion and emotional discomfort, but retains capacity for intimacy.
- **Introverted "loners":** only inflexible, maladaptive, impairing traits qualify.

## Comorbidity (p. 993)
- Very brief psychotic episodes (minutes–hours) under stress; may precede delusional disorder or schizophrenia.
- Sometimes MDD.
- Most often co-occurs with schizotypal, paranoid, avoidant PD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **A1/A2/A5:** document social network map (no confidants outside first-degree relatives), preference for solitary activities,
  and stated *lack of desire* (not fear) for relationships — "states he prefers being alone; denies wish for closer friendships."
- **A3/A4:** low sexual interest; few pleasurable activities (distinguish from episodic anhedonia of depression).
- **A6/A7:** observed indifference to feedback; flat/constricted affect, minimal reciprocal nonverbal cues.
- **MSE:** constricted or flat affect, limited eye contact/reciprocity, organized thought, no delusions/hallucinations,
  no odd beliefs or perceptual distortions (separates from schizotypal).
- **Rule-outs:** ASD screen (restricted/repetitive behaviors, early developmental history); psychotic sx history; depressive
  episode (anhedonia timeline); medical/substance causes.
- **Culture:** note recent migration/relocation that could explain emotional freezing.
