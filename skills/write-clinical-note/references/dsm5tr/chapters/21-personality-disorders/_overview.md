# Personality Disorders — Chapter Overview (Section II)

> **Spark note:** A personality disorder (PD) is a long-standing, inflexible, pervasive pattern of inner
> experience and behavior that departs from cultural expectations, starts by adolescence/early adulthood,
> and causes distress or impairment. The ten specific PDs are evaluated within the **General Personality Disorder** framework; acquired medical personality change uses a separate criteria set. The commonest error is diagnosing a PD from a cross-sectional snapshot (e.g., during a depressive
> episode, intoxication, or acute stress) rather than documenting the long-term pattern.

## What unites the chapter
- Shared definition: enduring pattern in ≥2 of cognition, affectivity, interpersonal functioning, impulse control;
  inflexible + pervasive; distress/impairment; stable, onset traceable to adolescence/early adulthood; not better
  explained by another mental disorder, substance, or medical condition (p. 982).
- Section II is the **categorical** model (DSM-IV-TR criteria carried into DSM-5/DSM-5-TR). Section III contains the
  hybrid dimensional–categorical **Alternative DSM-5 Model for Personality Disorders (AMPD)** — see
  `../28-alternative-dsm-5-model-for-personality-disorders/_overview.md` (p. 980, 981).
- Traits are frequently **ego-syntonic** → multiple interviews spaced over time and collateral informants are often needed (p. 983).

### How the members differ
| Axis | Specific PDs (F60.x / F21) | Personality change due to AMC (F07.0) | Other specified / unspecified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | By adolescence/early adulthood | Any age; a *change* from baseline | Same as general PD |
| Etiology | Not attributable to substance/medical | Direct pathophysiological effect of a medical condition | — |
| Threshold | Specific criteria count met | Persistent change + distress/impairment | General PD criteria met (OS) or PD-like features predominate |

## Clusters (descriptive only; "serious limitations," not consistently validated) (p. 981)
- **Cluster A (odd/eccentric):** paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal. Median prevalence ~3.6%.
- **Cluster B (dramatic/emotional/erratic):** antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic. Median ~4.5%.
- **Cluster C (anxious/fearful):** avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive. Median ~2.8%.
- Any PD: median ~10.5% across countries; prevalence varies by country and ethnicity (instrument/definition effects possible).
- Cross-cluster co-occurrence is common; diagnose **all** PDs whose criteria are met.

## Edition-specific points visible in the text
- Schizotypal PD is coded **F21** (grouped with schizophrenia spectrum in ICD-10-CM) even though it sits in this chapter.
- Narcissistic PD = **F60.81**; other specified PD = **F60.89** (ICD-10-CM codes; note that "other specified" ≠ F60.9).
- Culture text throughout addresses racism, discrimination, marginalization, acculturation, clinician cultural bias,
  instruments not normed for the population; adaptive mistrust/guardedness in oppressed groups should not be read as
  paranoid traits (p. 984, 988).
- Sex/gender text: BPD female preponderance in clinical samples may reflect help-seeking; no difference in community
  samples; insufficient evidence on cis vs. transgender differences (p. 984, 1007).
- Prevalence figures cite NCS-R Part II subsample, NESARC, and median across epidemiological reviews for each PD.
- Antisocial PD text notes somatization disorder was replaced by somatic symptom disorder in DSM-5 (p. 1001).
- Narcissistic PD discussion includes cognitive vs. emotional empathy, vulnerable/low-self-esteem presentations,
  suicide attempts less impulsive and more lethal (p. 1014–1016).
- OCPD lists hoarding disorder as a differential; both diagnosed when criteria met (p. 1029).

## Chapter-level pitfalls & differential logic
- **State vs. trait:** Be cautious diagnosing a PD during a depressive or anxiety episode — cross-sectional symptoms
  mimic traits and distort retrospective recall (p. 985).
- **Psychotic disorders:** Paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal each require the pattern not occur *exclusively* during
  schizophrenia, bipolar/depressive disorder with psychotic features, or another psychotic disorder (schizoid and
  schizotypal also exclude ASD). If the PD preceded schizophrenia, record it with "(premorbid)" (p. 985).
- **PTSD:** Personality changes that emerge after extreme stress → consider PTSD (p. 985).
- **Substance use:** Do not base a PD on behaviors that are consequences of intoxication/withdrawal or that serve to
  sustain use (e.g., theft for drugs) (p. 985).
- **Medical:** New personality change in mid/late life → evaluate for personality change due to AMC or unrecognized SUD (p. 983).
- **Spectrum relations:** schizotypal ↔ schizophrenia; avoidant ↔ social anxiety disorder (p. 984).
- **Early-onset persistent disorders** (e.g., persistent depressive disorder) may be hard — and not very useful — to separate from PD (p. 984).
- **Traits vs. disorder:** Every specific PD text repeats that traits count only when inflexible, maladaptive, persisting
  and causing significant impairment/distress.
- **Culture:** Do not confuse acculturation problems, customs, religious/political values, or adaptive responses to
  cultural constraints (e.g., staying in an abusive marriage where divorce is proscribed) with PD (p. 984).
- **Gender stereotypes:** Avoid over/underdiagnosis by sex based on role stereotypes (p. 984).

### Quick discriminators between neighbours
| Pair | Key discriminator |
|---|---|
| Paranoid vs. schizotypal | Schizotypal adds magical thinking, perceptual oddities, odd speech/behavior |
| Schizoid vs. avoidant | Schizoid lacks desire for closeness; avoidant wants it but fears rejection |
| Schizoid vs. schizotypal | Schizoid lacks cognitive-perceptual distortions |
| Schizotypal vs. borderline | BPD psychotic-like sx are transient, stress/affect-linked, dissociative |
| Antisocial vs. narcissistic | ASPD: impulsivity, aggression, deceit, conduct disorder history |
| Antisocial vs. borderline/histrionic | Manipulation for material gain (ASPD) vs. nurturance (BPD/HPD) |
| Borderline vs. dependent | Abandonment → rage/emptiness/demands (BPD) vs. appeasement/replacement relationship (DPD) |
| Histrionic vs. narcissistic | HPD accepts being seen as fragile to get attention; NPD needs admiration for superiority |
| Avoidant vs. dependent | Avoidant withdraws from proximity; dependent seeks proximity |
| OCPD vs. OCD | OCD has true obsessions/compulsions; diagnose both if both met |
| OCPD vs. narcissistic | NPD believes perfection achieved; OCPD self-critical |
| Avoidant vs. paranoid (reluctance to confide) | Fear of humiliation vs. fear of malicious use |

## Shared specifiers & coding conventions
- Specific PDs have **no severity or course specifiers** in Section II.
- "(premorbid)" annotation for paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal PD that preceded schizophrenia (and more generally any PD
  preceding a persistent mental disorder such as schizophrenia) (p. 985).
- Age: PD can be diagnosed <18 y only if features present ≥1 year; **antisocial PD never <18 y** (p. 983).
- Personality change due to AMC: code the medical condition first, then F07.0 with the condition named; choose a subtype (p. 1031).
- Other specified: record "other specified personality disorder" + reason (e.g., "mixed personality features") (p. 1033).
- Adult antisocial behavior (Z-code, "Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention") for criminal behavior
  without PD features (p. 1003).
- Personality change in context of SUD with etiological link → other specified [substance]-related disorder with
  personality change (p. 1033).
