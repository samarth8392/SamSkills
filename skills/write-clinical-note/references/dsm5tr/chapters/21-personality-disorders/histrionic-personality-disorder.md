# Histrionic Personality Disorder (Cluster B)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive excessive emotionality and attention seeking — the person needs to be the focus and will
> accept appearing fragile or dependent to get it. Impairment is generally lower than in many PDs and mainly
> interpersonal. Pitfall: cultural, gender, and age norms for expressiveness and appearance vary widely —
> judge against them and require real impairment/distress.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1009)
Pervasive excessive emotionality + attention seeking; by early adulthood; multiple contexts; **≥5 of 8**:
1. Uncomfortable when not the center of attention
2. Inappropriately sexually seductive or provocative interactions
3. Rapidly shifting, shallow emotional expression
4. Consistently uses physical appearance to draw attention
5. Speech excessively impressionistic, lacking detail
6. Self-dramatization, theatricality, exaggerated emotion
7. Suggestible (easily influenced by others/circumstances)
8. Considers relationships more intimate than they are

## Specifiers & severity
- None.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1009–1010)
- Lively, dramatic, charming initially; wears thin with constant demand for attention; "life of the party"; creates scenes or
  stories when not the focus.
- With clinicians: flattery, gifts, dramatic symptom descriptions that change each visit.
- Seductiveness extends to social, occupational, professional relationships beyond context.
- Excess time/money on grooming; fishes for compliments; upset by an unflattering photo or comment.
- Strong opinions with flair but vague rationale (e.g., calls someone wonderful, can't give examples).
- Public emotional displays (embracing acquaintances, sobbing at minor events, tantrums); emotions switch on/off quickly → seen as fake.
- Suggestible, follows fads; overly trusting of strong authorities seen as magical problem-solvers; acts on hunches.
- Calls acquaintances "dear friends"; uses first names with physicians met once or twice.

## Associated features (p. 1010–1011)
- Lower overall impairment than many PDs; impairment chiefly interpersonal.
- Social dominance style: "warmer" intrusive/exhibitionistic to "colder" arrogant, controlling, aggressive.
- Romantic relationships particularly impaired (more divorce or never marrying); difficulty with emotional intimacy.
- Same-sex friendships strained by provocative style; alienates friends with attention demands.
- Depressed/upset when not the center; craves novelty, bored by routine; poor tolerance of delayed gratification;
  enthusiastic starts that fade; neglects long relationships for new excitement.

## Prevalence
- NCS-R 0.0%; NESARC 1.8%; median of 5 studies 0.9% (p. 1011).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1011)
- Norms for interpersonal behavior, appearance, expressiveness vary across cultures, genders, ages. Before counting traits
  (emotionality, seductiveness, dramatic style, novelty seeking, sociability, charm, impressionability, somatization),
  confirm clinically significant impairment/distress.
- Distinguish from reactive/contextual expression under competitive peer socialization ("need to be liked").
- Diagnosed more in females clinically, but ratio mirrors the proportion of females in those settings; structured assessments
  show similar rates by sex.

## Suicide risk (p. 1011)
- True risk unknown; clinical experience suggests increased risk of **suicidal gestures and threats**.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1012)
- **Borderline PD:** also attention seeking, manipulative, shifting emotions — but BPD has self-destructiveness, angry
  relationship ruptures, chronic deep emptiness, identity disturbance.
- **Antisocial PD:** shared impulsivity, superficiality, excitement seeking, recklessness, seduction, manipulation; HPD more
  emotionally exaggerated, not characteristically antisocial; HPD manipulates for nurturance, ASPD for profit/power.
- **Narcissistic PD:** NPD wants praise for "superiority"; HPD will be seen as fragile/dependent if it earns attention. NPD
  exaggerates intimacy by emphasizing friends' VIP status/wealth.
- **Dependent PD:** dependence on others for praise/guidance but without flamboyant, exaggerated emotionality.
- **Traits:** only inflexible, maladaptive, impairing traits qualify.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**

## Comorbidity (p. 1012)
- Higher rates of borderline, narcissistic, paranoid, dependent, antisocial PD; alcohol/substance use; aggression and violence.
- Thought to relate to somatic symptom disorder, functional neurological symptom disorder (conversion), and MDD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **1/6:** behaviors that redirect attention (in session and per collateral); dramatic narratives; theatrical affect.
- **2/4:** dress/grooming used to draw attention, seductive behavior in non-romantic contexts (describe objectively, avoid
  judgmental language; note cultural norms considered).
- **3:** affect shifts rapidly and appears shallow — note observed shift times.
- **5:** speech global and impressionistic, few concrete specifics when prompted.
- **7:** opinions change with others' influence; overreliance on authority figures.
- **8:** describes recent acquaintances as intimate.
- **MSE:** dramatic, labile/shallow affect, impressionistic speech, possibly flirtatious or overly familiar with clinician;
  reality testing intact.
- **Impairment:** document interpersonal consequences (romantic instability, friendship losses) — required to exceed trait level.
- **Rule-outs:** BPD features (self-harm, emptiness), hypomania/mania (episodic), substance effects, medical causes; screen
  for suicidal gestures/threats.
