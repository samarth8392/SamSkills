# Paranoid Personality Disorder (Cluster A)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive, early-onset distrust in which others' motives are read as malevolent, without
> delusional conviction. The key pitfalls: (1) it is not a psychotic disorder — if persistent delusions exist,
> think delusional disorder/schizophrenia; (2) guardedness in people facing racism, discrimination, or migration
> stress can be adaptive and must not be labelled paranoid.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 985–986)
- **A.** Pervasive distrust/suspiciousness, others' motives read as malevolent; begins by early adulthood; multiple contexts;
  **≥4 of 7**:
  1. Suspects without sufficient basis that others exploit, harm, or deceive them
  2. Preoccupied with unjustified doubts about loyalty/trustworthiness of friends or associates
  3. Reluctant to confide due to unwarranted fear info will be used maliciously
  4. Reads hidden demeaning/threatening meanings into benign remarks or events
  5. Persistently bears grudges (unforgiving of insults, injuries, slights)
  6. Perceives attacks on character/reputation not apparent to others; quick angry reaction/counterattack
  7. Recurrent unjustified suspicions about fidelity of spouse/sexual partner
- **B.** Not exclusively during schizophrenia, bipolar or depressive disorder with psychotic features, or another psychotic
  disorder; not attributable to physiological effects of another medical condition (e.g., temporal lobe epilepsy).
- **Note:** If criteria met before onset of schizophrenia, add "premorbid" → "paranoid personality disorder (premorbid)."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 986–987)
- Assumes exploitation/harm without evidence; expects sudden attack; feels deeply, irreversibly injured.
- Scrutinizes friends' actions for hostility; loyalty is disbelieved; expects abandonment or attack when in trouble.
- Refuses personal questions ("nobody's business").
- Misreads compliments/help as criticism or coercion; honest mistakes as deliberate.
- Minor slights → major, long-lasting hostility; quick counterattack.
- Pathological jealousy: gathers trivial "evidence," controls/interrogates partner about whereabouts and fidelity.

## Associated features (p. 987)
- Hard to get along with; argumentative, complaining, or hostile aloofness; labile affect with hostile, stubborn, sarcastic tone.
- Self-fulfilling cycle: hostility provokes hostile responses that confirm expectations.
- Needs control; rigid, critical, cannot collaborate, yet cannot accept criticism; blames others; litigious.
- Projects own fears; thinly hidden grandiose fantasies; attuned to power/rank; negative stereotypes of outgroups;
  prefers simplistic formulations; may be seen as "fanatics" and form tight "cult"-like groups.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- NCS-R subsample 2.3%; NESARC 4.4%; median across 6 studies 3.2%; forensic settings up to 23% (p. 988).
- Childhood/adolescent signs: solitariness, poor peer relations, social anxiety, school underachievement, interpersonal
  hypersensitivity. Adolescent onset linked to childhood maltreatment, externalizing sx, bullying peers, later interpersonal aggression.
- **Environmental:** socioeconomic inequality, marginalization, racism → decreased trust (sometimes adaptive); social stress +
  childhood maltreatment explains higher paranoid sx in groups facing discrimination; childhood trauma a confirmed risk factor.
- **Genetic:** some excess in relatives of schizophrenia probands; more specific familial link with delusional disorder, persecutory type.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 988–989)
- Migrants, oppressed ethnic/racialized groups, and others facing adversity may show guarded/defensive behavior due to
  unfamiliarity (language, rules) or majority-society hostility/neglect. Low generalized trust toward outgroups may be cultural.
- Guardedness, limited emotionality, rigidity, distance, defensiveness in unfair situations can provoke clinician frustration →
  mutual mistrust cycle; do not mistake for PPD. Clinical evaluation itself can reinforce the label.
- Sex: meta-analysis → more common in men; NESARC → more common in women.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 989)
- **Delusional disorder (persecutory), schizophrenia, mood disorder with psychotic features:** persistent psychotic sx. PPD
  additionally only if present before psychosis and persists during remission → "(premorbid)."
- **Personality change due to AMC:** traits are a direct physiological consequence.
- **Substance use disorders:** sx associated with persistent use.
- **Paranoid traits with physical handicaps** (e.g., hearing impairment).
- **Schizotypal PD:** shares suspiciousness/aloofness/paranoid ideation but adds magical thinking, unusual perceptions, odd thinking/speech.
- **Schizoid PD:** cold/aloof/eccentric but usually lacks prominent paranoid ideation.
- **Borderline / histrionic PD:** anger at minor stimuli but not pervasive suspiciousness; BPD more impulsive and self-destructive.
- **Avoidant PD:** reluctance to confide from fear of embarrassment/inadequacy, not malice.
- **Antisocial PD:** PPD antisocial acts driven by revenge, not personal gain/exploitation.
- **Narcissistic PD:** suspiciousness/withdrawal from fear of flaws being revealed.
- **Adaptive paranoid traits** in threatening environments — diagnose only if inflexible, maladaptive, persistent, impairing.
- Multiple PDs can be diagnosed when each is met.

## Comorbidity (p. 990)
- Very brief psychotic episodes (minutes–hours) under stress.
- May be premorbid antecedent of delusional disorder or schizophrenia.
- MDD; increased risk of agoraphobia and OCD; alcohol/other SUDs frequent.
- Common PD co-occurrence: schizotypal, schizoid, narcissistic, avoidant, borderline.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **A1–A3:** quote-paraphrase client beliefs about being exploited/betrayed and the evidence offered (note "minimal/none");
  refusal to share history "because it could be used against me."
- **A4:** examples of benign events misread (e.g., interpreting a scheduling change as deliberate disrespect).
- **A5–A6:** long-held grudges (years), rapid angry rebuttals, litigation history.
- **A7:** partner-monitoring behaviors, accusations without corroboration.
- **MSE:** guarded, evasive, hostile or sarcastic tone, scrutinizes clinician's motives, thought content with suspiciousness
  but describe actual reality testing and any psychotic symptoms, including their timeline; brief stress-linked psychosis or a separately established disorder can coexist.
- **Rule-outs:** assess persistent delusions/hallucinations and whether the personality pattern predates and persists outside psychosis; mood episode status; substance use; neuro history
  (temporal lobe epilepsy, head injury); hearing/sensory impairment.
- **Cultural context:** record experiences of discrimination/migration and why mistrust exceeds what context warrants.
- **Onset:** evidence of pattern since adolescence/early adulthood across ≥2 settings.
