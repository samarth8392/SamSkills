# Antisocial Personality Disorder (Cluster B)

> **Spark note:** A pattern of violating others' rights that starts as conduct disorder before 15 and continues
> into adulthood (≥18). Three gates are often missed: age ≥18, documented conduct disorder onset <15, and
> not confined to schizophrenia/bipolar episodes. Criminal behavior alone is not ASPD — consider adult antisocial
> behavior (Z-code) when the personality features are absent.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 998)
- **A.** Pervasive disregard for/violation of others' rights **since age 15**, **≥3 of 7**:
  1. Failure to conform to lawful norms — repeated acts that are grounds for arrest
  2. Deceitfulness — repeated lying, aliases, conning for profit or pleasure
  3. Impulsivity or failure to plan ahead
  4. Irritability and aggressiveness — repeated physical fights or assaults
  5. Reckless disregard for safety of self or others
  6. Consistent irresponsibility — repeated failure to sustain work or honor financial obligations
  7. Lack of remorse — indifferent to or rationalizes hurting, mistreating, stealing
- **B.** Age **≥18**.
- **C.** Evidence of **conduct disorder with onset before age 15**.
- **D.** Not exclusively during schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

- None provided in this Section II criteria set.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 999–1000)
- Also called psychopathy, sociopathy, dyssocial PD. Deceit/manipulation are central → **integrate collateral sources**.
- Conduct disorder categories: aggression to people/animals, property destruction, deceitfulness/theft, serious rule violation.
- A1 counts arrestable acts whether or not arrested (property destruction, harassment, theft, illegal occupations).
- A2: lies, aliases, conning, malingering for money, sex, power.
- A3: spur-of-moment job/residence/relationship changes.
- A4: fights, assaults incl. spouse/child beating; **self-defense/defense of others does not count**.
- A5: speeding, DUI, accidents, high-risk sex/substance use, child endangerment.
- A6: unemployment despite jobs available, quitting without plan, unexplained absences, debt default, unpaid child support.
- A7: rationalizations ("life's unfair"), victim blaming, minimizing, no amends.

## Associated features (p. 1000)
- Lack empathy; callous, cynical, contemptuous; inflated arrogant self-appraisal; opinionated, cocky.
- Glib superficial charm, verbal facility/jargon — traditional psychopathy features more predictive of recidivism in forensic settings.
- Exploitative sexual relationships, many partners; irresponsible parenting (child malnutrition, poor hygiene, no caretaker,
  squandering household money).
- Dishonorable discharge, failure to self-support, poverty/homelessness, long incarceration.
- **Higher premature death from natural causes and suicide.**

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- NCS-R 0.6%; NESARC 3.6%; median of 7 studies 3.6%. **>70%** in men with most severe alcohol use disorder and in
  substance clinics, prisons, forensic settings. Similar in non-Latinx White and Black individuals; lower in Latinx and
  Asian Americans; higher with poverty or migration adversity (p. 1000).
- Chronic but may become less evident/remit with age, **often by 40** — especially criminal behavior; decline across the
  spectrum of antisocial behaviors and substance use (p. 1001).
- **Environmental:** child abuse/neglect, unstable/erratic parenting, inconsistent discipline increase evolution of CD → ASPD.
- **Genetic:** more common in first-degree relatives; relatives also at risk for somatization disorder (now somatic symptom
  disorder) and SUDs; in such families males more often ASPD/SUD, females more often somatization.
- Higher risk of adult ASPD when CD onset is **childhood (<10 y)** with comorbid ADHD (p. 1003).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1001–1002)
- Associated with low SES and urban settings; may be misapplied where antisocial-seeming behavior is a protective survival
  strategy (e.g., youth gangs amid violence and discrimination).
- Contexts with high maltreatment/violence: may be a risk factor or evoke reactive, contextual behavior that is not a PD.
- Some oppressed groups risk overdiagnosis because they are more likely to be misdiagnosed with CD in adolescence.
- **~3× more common in men.** Women with ASPD more likely to have childhood/adult adversity (e.g., sexual abuse).
  Men more irritability/aggression and reckless disregard for others; SUD comorbidity more in men; mood/anxiety more in women.
  Possible underdiagnosis in women because CD criteria emphasize aggression.

## Suicide risk
- Higher-than-population premature death from suicide (p. 1000). No further detail.

## Functional consequences
- Occupational instability, financial default, incarceration, homelessness, parenting failures (see associated features).

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1002–1003)
- **Conduct disorder:** in adults >18, CD given only if ASPD criteria not met.
- **Substance use disorders:** ASPD not diagnosed for adult antisocial acts tied to SUD unless signs were present in childhood
  and continued; if both began in childhood and persisted, diagnose both even if some acts serve drug use.
- **Schizophrenia/bipolar:** antisocial behavior only during these → not ASPD.
- **Narcissistic PD:** shares toughness, glibness, exploitation, low empathy; does not necessarily entail impulsivity, aggression, or deceit and usually lacks a conduct-disorder history;
  NPD typically depends more on admiration.
- **Histrionic PD:** shares impulsivity, superficiality, seduction, manipulation; HPD more emotionally exaggerated, not antisocial.
- **Histrionic/borderline:** manipulate for nurturance; ASPD for profit/power/material gain. ASPD less emotionally unstable, more aggressive than BPD.
- **Paranoid PD:** antisocial acts from revenge, not gain.
- **Criminal behavior not associated with a mental disorder:** crime for gain without PD features → adult antisocial behavior.

## Comorbidity (p. 1003)
- Dysphoria (tension, boredom intolerance, depressed mood); anxiety, mood, substance use, somatic symptom, gambling disorders.
- Often meets criteria for borderline, histrionic, narcissistic PD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **Criterion C (essential):** document specific CD behaviors before age 15 with sources (school suspensions, juvenile
  records, collateral). Absence = cannot diagnose ASPD.
- **Criterion B:** record DOB/age ≥18.
- **A items:** arrest/charge history (and unarrested illegal acts), aliases, fight history (exclude self-defense), DUI/speeding,
  employment and debt record, child support status; client's stated attitude toward victims (rationalization/indifference).
- **MSE:** may be superficially charming or glib, minimizing, externalizing blame, inconsistent history across sources,
  low expressed remorse, intact reality testing.
- **Rule-outs:** whether antisocial acts only during mania/psychosis; SUD timeline (did antisocial pattern predate/persist
  apart from use?); contextual/survival behaviors; assess intentional feigning separately if concrete evidence supports concern; an external incentive alone does not establish malingering.
- **Collateral:** note discrepancies between self-report and records.
- **Risk:** document violence risk factors and suicide risk screen.
