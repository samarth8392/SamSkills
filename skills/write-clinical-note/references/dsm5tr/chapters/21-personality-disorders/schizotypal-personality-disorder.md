# Schizotypal Personality Disorder (Cluster A; coded F21)

> **Spark note:** Social/interpersonal deficits (acute discomfort with and reduced capacity for closeness) plus
> cognitive-perceptual distortions and eccentric behavior, *below* the delusional threshold. It is on the
> schizophrenia spectrum and is coded F21. Key pitfall: culturally sanctioned supernatural/religious beliefs can look
> schizotypal to an uninformed clinician.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 993–994)
- **A.** Pervasive social/interpersonal deficits (acute discomfort with, reduced capacity for close relationships) + cognitive
  or perceptual distortions + eccentric behavior; by early adulthood; multiple contexts; **≥5 of 9**:
  1. Ideas of reference (**not** delusions of reference)
  2. Odd beliefs/magical thinking influencing behavior, inconsistent with subcultural norms (superstition, clairvoyance,
     telepathy, "sixth sense"; in youth: bizarre fantasies/preoccupations)
  3. Unusual perceptual experiences, incl. bodily illusions
  4. Odd thinking and speech (vague, circumstantial, metaphorical, overelaborate, stereotyped)
  5. Suspiciousness or paranoid ideation
  6. Inappropriate or constricted affect
  7. Odd, eccentric, or peculiar behavior or appearance
  8. No close friends/confidants other than first-degree relatives
  9. Excessive social anxiety that does not lessen with familiarity and is tied to paranoid fears, not negative self-judgment
- **B.** Not exclusively during schizophrenia, bipolar/depressive disorder with psychotic features, another psychotic disorder,
  or autism spectrum disorder.
- **Note:** If met before schizophrenia, add "premorbid."

- "(premorbid)" annotation only.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 994–995)
- Ideas of reference = misreading casual events as having special personal meaning, without delusional conviction.
- Magical control over others (direct, or via rituals, e.g., passing an object three times to prevent harm); sensing events
  before they happen; reading thoughts.
- Perceptual alterations: sensing a presence; hearing a voice murmur one's name.
- Speech: idiosyncratic phrasing; loose, digressive, vague **without derailment or incoherence**; overly concrete or abstract;
  odd word use.
- Paranoid ideation (e.g., colleagues undermining them).
- Stiff, constricted, inappropriate interactions; unkempt, mismatched dress; avoids eye contact; can't join banter.
- Says they're unhappy about lack of relationships but behavior shows reduced desire; feels different, doesn't "fit in."
- Social anxiety does not reliably diminish with familiarity; it may increase over an evening as suspiciousness grows.

## Associated features (p. 995)
- Often present for anxiety or depression rather than the PD itself.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- NCS-R 3.3%; NESARC 3.9%; median of 5 studies 0.6% (p. 995).
- Relatively stable course; only a small proportion develop schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder (p. 996).
- **Genetic:** familial aggregation; more prevalent in first-degree relatives of schizophrenia probands; modest increase in
  psychosis in relatives of STPD probands; twin studies show stable genetic and transient environmental factors; schizophrenia
  risk variants may be linked; neuroimaging shows group-level brain differences.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 996)
- Evaluate distortions in cultural milieu: beliefs about afterlife, speaking in tongues, voodoo, shamanism, mind reading,
  sixth sense, evil eye, magical health beliefs can appear schizotypal.
- Cross-national prevalence differences may be true or reflect cultural acceptance.
- Slightly more common in men.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Differential diagnosis (p. 996–998)
- **Psychotic disorders:** periods of persistent psychosis; STPD added only if it preceded and persists in remission → "(premorbid)."
- **Neurodevelopmental disorders (children):** milder ASD shows even less social awareness/reciprocity and stereotyped
  behaviors/interests; language/communication disorders show primacy and severity of language impairment on specialized assessment.
- **Personality change due to AMC; substance use disorders.**
- **Paranoid/schizoid PD:** lack cognitive-perceptual distortions and marked oddness.
- **Avoidant PD:** wants relationships, constrained by fear of rejection; STPD lacks desire, persistent detachment.
- **Narcissistic PD:** suspicion/withdrawal from fear of revealed flaws.
- **Borderline PD:** psychotic-like sx are transient, tied to affective shifts under stress, more dissociative
  (derealization/depersonalization); BPD isolation follows interpersonal failures; BPD shows impulsive/manipulative behavior.
  High co-occurrence — distinction not always feasible.
- **Adolescence:** schizotypal features may reflect transient emotional turmoil.

## Comorbidity (p. 998)
- Transient psychotic episodes (minutes–hours) under stress, usually too brief for brief psychotic or schizophreniform disorder;
  sometimes full brief psychotic, schizophreniform, delusional disorder, or schizophrenia develops.
- Considerable co-occurrence with schizoid, paranoid, avoidant, borderline PD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **A1–A3:** record specific referential ideas, magical beliefs and how they affect behavior, perceptual oddities — and note
  that conviction is **not delusional** (client can entertain alternatives) and beliefs exceed subcultural norms.
- **A4:** speech sample description: vague, circumstantial, metaphorical; goal-directed overall, no derailment.
- **A6/A7:** constricted/incongruent affect; idiosyncratic dress/mannerisms.
- **A9:** persistent social anxiety that does not abate with familiarity; assess whether suspiciousness rather than self-criticism motivates it.
- **MSE:** odd appearance, constricted/inappropriate affect, circumstantial/idiosyncratic speech, ideas of reference, magical
  thinking, illusions; describe conviction, insight, and any psychotic episodes; persistent psychosis needs independent assessment and does not automatically erase a preexisting personality pattern.
- **Rule-outs:** psychosis history and duration of any psychotic sx; ASD/language developmental history; substance and medical causes;
  cultural/religious context of beliefs (consult informants).
- Note family history of schizophrenia-spectrum illness if known.
