# Narcissistic Personality Disorder (Cluster B)

> **Spark note:** Pervasive grandiosity (in fantasy or behavior), need for admiration, and lack of empathy, beneath
> which self-esteem is typically fragile. The TR text stresses that presentations can be "vulnerable" — shame, envy,
> withdrawal, depressed mood, apparent humility — not only overtly arrogant. The single best discriminator from other
> Cluster B PDs is grandiosity.

## Criteria at a glance (p. 1013)
Pervasive grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy; by early adulthood; multiple contexts; **≥5 of 9**:
1. Grandiose self-importance (exaggerates achievements/talents; expects superiority recognition without matching achievement)
2. Preoccupied with fantasies of unlimited success, power, brilliance, beauty, ideal love
3. Believes self "special"/unique; understood only by or should associate with special/high-status people or institutions
4. Requires excessive admiration
5. Entitlement (unreasonable expectation of favorable treatment or automatic compliance)
6. Interpersonally exploitative
7. Lacks empathy — unwilling to recognize/identify with others' feelings and needs
8. Often envious of others or believes others envy them
9. Arrogant, haughty behaviors or attitudes

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls (p. 1013–1014)
- Overestimates abilities, boastful/pretentious; surprised when expected praise is absent; implicitly devalues others' contributions.
- Ruminates on "long overdue" admiration; compares self to famous people.
- Insists on "top" professionals and "best" institutions; devalues those who disappoint; self-esteem "mirrored" by idealized associates.
- **Self-esteem almost invariably fragile**; internal self-doubt, self-criticism, emptiness drive admiration seeking; fishes for compliments with charm.
- Entitlement: expects not to wait in line; irritated when others don't help in their "important work"; overworks others.
- Exploitation may be unwitting or deliberate (emotional, social, intellectual, financial); relationships formed if they advance purposes.
- **Empathy:** some **cognitive** empathy but lacks **emotional** empathy; oblivious to hurt; views others' needs as weakness;
  others experience coldness, lack of reciprocal interest.
- Envy/begrudging others' success; harsh devaluation of others who receive praise; snobbish, disdainful, patronizing.

## Associated features (p. 1015)
- Very sensitive to criticism/defeat → shame, humiliation, hollowness; may respond with disdain, rage, defiant counterattack
  **or** social withdrawal / apparent humility masking grandiosity.
- Interpersonal impairment from self-preoccupation, entitlement, need for admiration.
- Wide functioning range: some highly competent and successful (with self-control, stoicism, distancing, minimal self-disclosure);
  others very low vocational functioning from avoidance of competitive risk.
- Fluctuating confidence and intolerance of criticism disrupt performance.
- Low self-esteem, inferiority, shame, envy, humiliation, insecurity → withdrawal, emptiness, depressed mood.
- High perfectionist standards with fear of exposure of imperfection or failure.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- NCS-R 0.0%; NESARC 6.2%; median of 5 studies 1.6% (p. 1015).
- Traits common in adolescents; not predictive by themselves.
- May first present or worsen with crises — bankruptcy, demotion, job loss, divorce; difficulty adjusting to aging limits.
- **Corrective experiences** (durable relationships, real achievements, tolerable setbacks) can lead to improvement (p. 1015).

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues (p. 1016)
- Traits may be elevated in individualistic contexts; there they may draw less clinical attention or cause less impairment than in collectivistic contexts.
- **50–75% of adults diagnosed are men.** Men: stronger stress reactivity, compromised empathic processing; women: self-focus
  and withdrawal. Cultural gender expectations contribute.

## Suicide risk (p. 1016)
- Under severe stress, perfectionism plus exposure to imperfection/failure/overwhelming emotion can evoke suicidal ideation.
- Attempts tend to be **less impulsive and higher lethality** than in other PDs.

## Functional consequences
- Impaired relationships; variable occupational functioning from high achievement to low vocational engagement (see above).

## Differential diagnosis (p. 1016–1017)
- **Histrionic, antisocial, borderline:** interactive styles coquettish, callous, needy respectively; NPD distinguished by **grandiosity**.
- **Borderline:** NPD has more stable self-image and self-control; less self-destructiveness, impulsivity, separation insecurity,
  emotional hyperreactivity.
- **Histrionic:** NPD shows excessive pride, less emotional display, disdain for others' sensitivities; NPD needs attention to be **admiring**.
- **Antisocial:** shared toughness, glibness, exploitation, low empathy; NPD does not necessarily entail impulsive aggression/deceit and usually lacks the conduct-disorder history characteristic of ASPD; assess each independently. ASPD may involve less sensitivity to criticism.
- **OCPD:** both perfectionistic; OCPD perfectionism about order/rigidity; NPD about appearance/performance with critical
  concern about measuring up.
- **Schizotypal, avoidant, paranoid:** suspiciousness and withdrawal usually distinguish them; in NPD these derive from shame,
  fear of failure, fear of revealed flaws.
- **Successful people with narcissistic traits:** only inflexible, maladaptive, impairing traits qualify.
- **Mania/hypomania:** grandiosity tied to mood change/functional impairment.
- **Substance use disorders.**
- **Persistent depressive disorder:** self-esteem threats can produce persistent negative feelings resembling PDD; diagnose both if both met.

## Comorbidity (p. 1017)
- Depressive disorders (PDD, MDD), anorexia nervosa, SUDs (especially cocaine).
- Histrionic, borderline, antisocial, paranoid PD.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
These are assessment prompts, not observed findings. Record only assessed/reported evidence, attribute its source, and preserve uncertainty; an MSE list does not describe every person with the diagnosis.

- **1–3:** examples of inflated self-appraisal versus documented achievements; fantasies of success/fame; insistence on "the best" providers.
- **4:** compliment seeking, distress when not acknowledged (including in session).
- **5–6:** entitlement incidents (rule exemptions, demands), exploitation of colleagues/partners.
- **7:** observed inability to register others' emotional impact (cognitive understanding without emotional resonance).
- **8–9:** envy statements, devaluation of peers; condescending tone toward staff.
- **Vulnerable presentation cues:** shame, humiliation after criticism, withdrawal, perfectionism, depressed mood — document triggering events.
- **MSE:** may be charming or condescending, grandiose content without delusional conviction, reactive anger or withdrawal to
  perceived slights, affect consistent with self-esteem fluctuations.
- **Rule-outs:** hypomanic/manic episode (episodic grandiosity with mood/energy change), substance use (esp. stimulants), PDD.
- **Risk:** screen for suicidal ideation after narcissistic injury (job loss, divorce, public failure); note higher lethality potential.
