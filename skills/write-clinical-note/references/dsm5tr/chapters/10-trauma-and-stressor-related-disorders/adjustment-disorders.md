# Adjustment Disorders

> **Spark note:** A clinically significant emotional or behavioral reaction to an identifiable stressor of **any** severity. It starts **within 3 months** of the stressor, does **not** meet criteria for another disorder, and ends **within 6 months** after the stressor and its consequences end. It is a residual diagnosis. If MDD, PTSD, ASD or PGD criteria are met, use those instead. People most often go wrong on the time limits: onset more than 3 months after the stressor, or persistence more than 6 months after it ends, puts the case in **other specified trauma- and stressor-related disorder**.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** Emotional or behavioral symptoms in response to an identifiable stressor, with onset **within 3 months** of the stressor's onset.
- **B.** Clinically significant, shown by **≥1 of**:
  1. Marked distress **out of proportion** to the severity or intensity of the stressor, judged against external context and cultural factors.
  2. Significant impairment in social, occupational or other important functioning.
- **C.** Does **not** meet criteria for another mental disorder, and is **not merely an exacerbation** of a preexisting one.
- **D.** Not **normal bereavement**, and not better explained by **prolonged grief disorder**.
- **E.** Once the stressor or its consequences have ended, symptoms do not persist for **more than an additional 6 months**.

## Specifiers & severity
**Subtype, chosen by the predominant symptoms. The subtype sets the code:**
- **With depressed mood:** low mood, tearfulness, hopelessness.
- **With anxiety:** nervousness, worry, jitteriness or separation anxiety.
- **With mixed anxiety and depressed mood:** both.
- **With disturbance of conduct:** conduct disturbance predominates.
- **With mixed disturbance of emotions and conduct:** emotional symptoms plus conduct disturbance.
- **Unspecified:** a maladaptive reaction that fits none of the above.

**Duration specifier:**
- **Acute:** symptoms persist **<6 months**.
- **Persistent (chronic):** symptoms persist **≥6 months**. This is only possible when the stressor is **chronic** (e.g., a disabling medical illness) or has **enduring consequences** (e.g., the financial and emotional fallout of divorce), because symptoms still may not outlast the stressor and its consequences by more than 6 months.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- **Stressor types:**
  - A single event (end of a romantic relationship) or multiple stressors (business trouble plus marital problems).
  - Recurrent (seasonal business crises) or continuous (painful, progressive illness; living in a high-crime neighborhood).
  - Affecting one person, a family, or a whole community (natural disaster).
  - **Developmental transitions:** starting school, leaving or returning to the parental home, marriage, parenthood, failing to reach occupational goals, retirement.
- **Bereavement:** adjustment disorder can be diagnosed after a death when the intensity, quality or persistence of grief exceeds cultural, religious or age norms **and** PGD criteria are not met. This is usually in the first 12 months; see the PGD file.
- **After trauma:** use adjustment disorder when there are ASD or PTSD symptoms below threshold, or a full ASD or PTSD symptom profile after a **non**-Criterion A stressor.
- It can be diagnosed **immediately** after a stressor. No minimum symptom duration is required.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** common. Principal diagnosis in about **5–20%** of US outpatient mental health patients. Often the most common diagnosis in 1990s hospital psychiatric consultation settings, frequently reaching **50%**. May be higher in women (Danish data).
- **Course:** onset after an acute stressor is usually immediate (within days) and brief (a few months). If the stressor or its consequences continue, the disorder may persist and become the persistent form. Symptoms more than 6 months after the stressor ends rule out the diagnosis.
- **Risk:** people from **disadvantaged circumstances** face more stressors and are at higher risk.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
- Cultural context is **key** to deciding whether a reaction is maladaptive.
- Assessment is harder in **migrants and refugees** facing major contextual and cultural change.
- In some cultures suffering is considered an intrinsic part of life, so distress may not be seen as maladaptive or as needing treatment.
- **Self-immolation** is a risk associated with adjustment disorder in some cultural contexts.

## Suicide risk
- Associated with **increased risk of suicide attempts and suicide**.
- Among migrants (Turkish migrants in Western Europe; South and Southeast Asian migrants in Gulf countries), adjustment disorder was one of the most common diagnoses linked to suicide-related behavior.
- Do not treat an adjustment diagnosis as low risk. Document a risk assessment.

## Functional consequences
Lower performance at work or school, temporary changes in social relationships, and complications in medical illness (poorer adherence, longer hospital stays).

## Differential diagnosis
- **Major depressive disorder:** if MDD criteria are met in response to a stressor, adjustment disorder **does not apply**.
- **PTSD and ASD:**
  - *Stressor:* adjustment disorder allows any severity; ASD and PTSD require Criterion A.
  - *Timing:* adjustment disorder can be diagnosed immediately and lasts up to 6 months after the stressor ends. ASD covers 3 days to 1 month. PTSD needs >1 month.
  - *Profile:* use adjustment disorder for a subthreshold ASD or PTSD picture, or a full picture after a non-A stressor.
  - A persistent subthreshold PTSD-like response >6 months is an **other specified** example. Also track whether the stressor/consequences remain active: adjustment Criterion E is measured after their termination, not automatically from the trauma date.
- **Personality disorders:** some traits create vulnerability to situational distress, and stressors can worsen PD symptoms. Use the lifetime history. Diagnose adjustment disorder alongside a PD only if the stress-related disturbance **exceeds** what the PD explains (Criterion C).
- **Bereavement and PGD:** acute grief distress that is out of proportion or significantly impairs self-care and relationships may be adjustment disorder. If symptoms persist **>12 months after the death**, diagnose **PGD** if its criteria are met, otherwise **other specified** trauma- and stressor-related disorder.
- **Psychological factors affecting other medical conditions:** there, psychological factors worsen the medical condition. Adjustment disorder is a **reaction to** a stressor, which may be the illness itself.
- **Normative stress reactions:** most people get upset when bad things happen. Diagnose only when the distress exceeds what is expected (culturally variable) or causes impairment.

## Comorbidity
- Can accompany most mental disorders and any medical condition. It can be added to another mental disorder **only if that disorder does not explain the stress-reaction symptoms**. Examples: adjustment disorder with depressed mood after a job loss in someone with OCD; a depressive or bipolar disorder plus adjustment disorder if criteria for both are met.
- A common psychological response to medical illness, and may be the main one.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Criterion A:** name the stressor(s), the stressor onset date and the symptom onset date. Document that onset was **≤3 months** after the stressor. Note whether the stressor is acute, recurrent, continuous or ongoing.
- **Criterion B:** say why the distress is out of proportion **in the client's cultural context**, and/or the specific impairment (missed work, falling grades, withdrawal from relationships, poor medical adherence).
- **Criterion C:** list the disorders considered and why each is not met (e.g., "4/9 MDD symptoms; does not meet MDD", "no Criterion A trauma; PTSD n/a", "symptoms exceed baseline personality functioning").
- **Criterion D:** if the stressor was a death, document that the reaction exceeds normal grief and that PGD criteria are not met (or that the age-appropriate waiting interval has not passed: <12 months in adults, <6 months in children/adolescents).
- **Criterion E and specifier:** record whether the stressor is ongoing or ended, the date it ended, and the symptom duration. Choose acute (<6 mo) or persistent (≥6 mo, chronic stressor or enduring consequences).
- **Subtype:** name the predominant features to justify the code. Depressed mood: tearful, hopeless. Anxiety: worry, jitteriness, separation fears. Conduct: truancy, fighting, reckless driving, rule violation.
- **MSE phrases:** "mood 'overwhelmed since the layoff'; affect anxious, congruent"; "thought content focused on the stressor; no psychosis"; "no neurovegetative syndrome meeting MDD threshold".
- **Safety:** document a suicide and self-harm risk assessment, since adjustment disorder carries elevated suicide risk.
- **Reassessment:** plan a follow-up once the stressor resolves. If symptoms continue more than 6 months after it ends, re-diagnose (consider other specified, MDD, etc.).
