# Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder

> **Spark note:** After pathogenic early care, the child is culturally inappropriately and overly familiar with relative strangers: no reticence, wanders off without checking back, will leave with an unfamiliar adult. It is an externalizing disorder of disinhibition. Unlike RAD, it can occur in a child who **has** attachments, even secure ones, and it often persists after caregiving improves. The behavior must extend beyond general impulsivity to social disinhibition. ADHD may coexist; inattention or hyperactivity is not an exclusion.

## Criteria at a glance
- **A.** The child actively approaches and interacts with unfamiliar adults, with **≥2 of 4**:
  1. Reduced or absent reticence in approaching or interacting with unfamiliar adults.
  2. Overly familiar verbal or physical behavior beyond cultural and age-appropriate boundaries.
  3. Diminished or absent checking back with the caregiver after venturing away, even in unfamiliar settings.
  4. Goes off with an unfamiliar adult with minimal or no hesitation.
- **B.** The behavior is **not limited to impulsivity** (as in ADHD). It includes social disinhibition.
- **C. Extremes of insufficient care, ≥1 of:** social neglect or deprivation of emotional needs, repeated changes of primary caregiver, or rearing in unusual settings such as high-ratio institutions. These are the same three options as in RAD.
- **D.** The care in C is presumed responsible for the behavior in A.
- **E.** Developmental age is **≥9 months**.
- There is **no** ASD exclusion and **no** age-of-onset ceiling in the criteria (contrast RAD).

## Specifiers & severity
- **Persistent:** present for **>12 months**.
- **Severe:** **all** symptoms are present, each at relatively high levels.

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Essential feature: culturally inappropriate, overly familiar behavior with relative strangers that violates the culture's social boundaries.
- Requires a developmental age of ≥9 months, since the child must be able to form selective attachments.
- Signs often **persist after the other signs of neglect resolve**, so a child may present with no current evidence of neglect. Take a full early history.
- DSED can occur in children whose attachments range from absent to disturbed to **secure**.

## Associated features
Cognitive and language delays, stereotypies, and other signs of severe neglect such as malnutrition. These come from the shared etiology but often resolve while the DSED signs remain.

## Prevalence · Development & course · Risk & prognostic factors
- **Prevalence:** unknown and rare. Occurs in a minority even of severely deprived children. Up to **2%** in low-income UK community samples.
- **Course:** neglect is usually present from the first months of life. If neglect is early and signs appear, they are **moderately stable**, especially if the neglect continues. Signs have been described from the **second year of life through adolescence and into young adulthood**.
  - Toddlers: lack of the normal reticence with strangers. The child engages with, and may go off with, unfamiliar adults.
  - Preschool: verbal and social intrusiveness, attention-seeking, and physical contact with strangers.
  - Middle childhood: verbal and physical overfamiliarity, sometimes with inauthentic expressions of emotion.
  - Adolescence: indiscriminate behavior extends to peers, with superficial friendships and more peer conflict.
  - Adulthood: excessive self-disclosure and reduced awareness of strangers.
- **Temperamental risk:** blunted reward sensitivity and decreased inhibitory control (international adoptee research).
- **Environmental risk:** serious social neglect is required. Other contributors are multiple placement disruptions, maternal borderline personality disorder, aberrant caregiving and low quality of care. Most severely neglected children do **not** develop DSED. It has **not been identified when neglect occurs only after age 2**.
- **Prognosis is only modestly** tied to the quality of later caregiving. It often persists even after care markedly improves, which contrasts with RAD. Caregiving quality does seem to moderate course in young children.
- **Genetic and physiological:** neurobiological findings are preliminary.

## Culture- & sex/gender-related issues
Cross-cultural data are limited. The lack of reticence must **exceed culturally accepted norms** for how children behave with strangers.

## Suicide risk
Not specifically addressed.

## Functional consequences
Impaired relationships with adults and peers, impaired social functioning and competence, and increased risk of **peer conflict and victimization**.

## Differential diagnosis
- **ADHD:** social impulsivity in ADHD can resemble DSED. DSED does not require inattention or hyperactivity, and Criterion B requires more than impulsivity alone. Because ADHD often co-occurs (p. 455), assess both domains instead of excluding DSED when ADHD symptoms are present.
- **Reactive attachment disorder:** RAD is withdrawn and does not seek comfort. DSED approaches strangers indiscriminately.

## Comorbidity
- Neglect-related cognitive and language delays, and stereotypies.
- **ASD may co-occur**, since there is no exclusion.
- In young and middle childhood it often co-occurs with **ADHD and externalizing disorders**, possibly through a shared deficit in inhibitory control.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- **Criterion A, ≥2:** concrete observations, e.g., "approached examiner immediately and climbed into lap", "hugged unfamiliar staff", "left waiting room without looking back at foster parent", "per foster mother, would go with strangers at the park". Name the informant and setting.
- **Criterion B:** document that the behavior is social disinhibition with strangers and not general impulsivity. Record attention and activity level separately.
- **Criteria C and D:** early care history (institution, placement count and dates, neglect findings) and the timeline linking it to the behavior. Note if the neglect began only after age 2, which argues against DSED.
- **Criterion E:** developmental age ≥9 months, from screening or testing.
- **Rule-outs to document:** ADHD (inattention/hyperactivity and general impulsivity assessed separately), whether the behavior exceeds cultural norms, and RAD.
- Phrases: "overly familiar with examiner, no stranger wariness"; "physical boundary violations with unfamiliar adults"; "no secure-base checking"; "superficial, indiscriminate engagement" (older children and adolescents).
- Specifiers: persistent (>12 mo) and severe (all symptoms, high levels).
