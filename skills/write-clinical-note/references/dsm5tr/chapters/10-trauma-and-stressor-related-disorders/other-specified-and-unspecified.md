# Other Specified and Unspecified Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorder

> **Spark note:** Residual categories for presentations dominated by trauma- or stressor-related symptoms that cause distress or impairment but miss the full criteria for any disorder in this chapter. Use **other specified** when you state why (e.g., "persistent response to trauma with PTSD-like symptoms"). Use **unspecified** when you do not or cannot, e.g., in the ER with too little information. In practice these categories catch adjustment reactions that break the 3-month onset or 6-month offset rules, subthreshold PTSD lasting >6 months, and grief persisting >12 months that does not meet PGD.

## Criteria at a glance
Both categories require that:
- Symptoms characteristic of a trauma- and stressor-related disorder **predominate**.
- They cause clinically significant distress or impairment.
- They do **not** meet full criteria for any disorder in the class.

**Other specified:** the clinician records the specific reason.
**Unspecified:** the clinician chooses not to give a reason, or there is not enough information.

## Example presentations (other specified)
1. **Adjustment-like disorder with delayed onset:** symptoms start **>3 months** after the stressor.
2. **Adjustment-like disorder with prolonged duration:** lasts **>6 months** without a prolonged stressor (i.e., >6 months after the stressor and its consequences ended).
3. **Persistent response to trauma with PTSD-like symptoms** ("subthreshold/partial PTSD"): a response to a traumatic event that falls short of the PTSD threshold and lasts **>6 months**.
4. **Ataque de nervios** (see "Culture and Psychiatric Diagnosis", Section III).
5. **Other cultural syndromes** (Section III).

Other routes into this category described elsewhere in the chapter:
- Bereavement-related distress persisting **>12 months** after the death that does **not** meet PGD criteria (adjustment disorder differential, p. 481).

Recording example: "F43.8 other specified trauma- and stressor-related disorder, persistent response to trauma with PTSD-like symptoms."

## Diagnostic features & clinical pearls
- Adjustment disorder has two hard time limits (onset ≤3 months; offset ≤6 months after the stressor ends). A case that breaks either one is **not** adjustment disorder. Use other specified with "adjustment-like" wording.
- A persistent subthreshold PTSD-like response >6 months is an other-specified example (p. 488). Assess the actual syndrome and adjustment criteria; for adjustment disorder the 6-month offset limit begins only when the stressor and its consequences end.
- Unspecified is appropriate in emergency or brief settings. Revise it once more history is available.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

Use examples only when supported by the actual assessment. Attribute client/collateral reports, distinguish observations from interpretations, and leave unassessed findings unknown.
- Document the stressor or trauma, its dates, and which trauma- or stressor-related symptoms predominate.
- State **which criterion is not met** and why, e.g.:
  - "Onset 5 months after divorce (exceeds 3-month adjustment window)."
  - "Post-assault sx: B1, B2, C2, D4, E3, E6. Only 1 of 7 D sx (needs 2); present 9 months; subthreshold PTSD."
  - "Grief 18 months post-loss, yearning present but only 2 C symptoms."
- Record impairment or distress, and rule-outs (MDD, anxiety disorders, substance or medical causes).
- For cultural syndromes (ataque de nervios), describe the episode (e.g., shouting, crying, trembling, sense of loss of control) and its cultural context, and screen for comorbid PTSD, panic and depression.
- For **unspecified**, note why the presentation could not be specified (e.g., "limited history in ED") and plan a reassessment.
