# Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders: Chapter Overview

> **Spark note:** Every disorder here requires an **explicit stressor or exposure criterion**. The disorders differ in four ways: the kind of exposure (neglect, Criterion A trauma, any stressor, or a death), timing (3 days–1 month, >1 month, onset ≤3 months, ≥12 months post-death), symptom profile, and age. Post-trauma distress is often not fear-based. It can be anhedonic or dysphoric, angry and externalizing, or dissociative, which is why this class sits apart from the anxiety disorders, between the anxiety/OCD and dissociative chapters.

## What unites the chapter
- An explicit etiological event in the criteria.
- Heterogeneous phenotypes: fear-based symptoms, anhedonia and dysphoria, anger and aggression, dissociation, or mixtures. Adjustment disorders have long been recognized as heterogeneous.
- **RAD and DSED** share the same cause (**social neglect** or insufficient care in childhood). RAD is **internalizing** (withdrawn, depressive). DSED is **externalizing** (disinhibited).
- **Prolonged grief disorder** was added for grief that is excessive in duration or intensity.

## Timing and exposure cheat-sheet
| Disorder | Required exposure | Timing rule | Core threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAD | Extremes of insufficient care (neglect, caregiver changes, institutions) | Evident before age 5; developmental age ≥9 mo | Both A items + ≥2 of 3 B |
| DSED | Same as RAD | Developmental age ≥9 mo; source reports no identified cases when neglect began only after age 2 (course observation, not an added formal criterion) | ≥2 of 4 A |
| ASD | Criterion A trauma | **3 days–1 month** after exposure | ≥9 of 14, any category |
| PTSD | Criterion A trauma | **>1 month**; delayed expression if full criteria met ≥6 mo after | B≥1, C≥1, D≥2, E≥2 (≤6 y: B≥1, C≥1 of 6, D≥2) |
| Adjustment | Any identifiable stressor | Onset **≤3 mo** after stressor; ends **≤6 mo** after stressor/consequences end | Out-of-proportion distress or impairment; reaction not better explained by another disorder |
| PGD | Death of a close person | **≥12 mo** after death (≥6 mo youth); sx nearly daily for the last month | B≥1 of 2 + C≥3 of 8 |
| Other specified | Any trauma or stressor | e.g., adjustment-like onset >3 mo or duration >6 mo; subthreshold PTSD >6 mo | Reason stated |

## TR changes and notable updates in the text
- **Prolonged grief disorder** became an official diagnosis. In DSM-5 it was "persistent complex bereavement disorder", a Section III condition for further study. Threshold: 12 months for adults, 6 months for children and adolescents.
- Adjustment disorder **Criterion D** now says the symptoms are not normal bereavement **and not better explained by PGD**.
- Adjustment disorder: bereavement distress persisting >12 months goes to PGD or other specified.
- PTSD prevalence is updated with DSM-5-based US figures (lifetime 6.1–8.3%, 12-month 4.7%), and the text uses expanded culture, racism and discrimination framing.
- PTSD Criterion A narrative is expanded: noncontact sexual trauma, bullying with a credible threat, qualifying medical events, drone operators, and psychotherapists under A4.
- "Delayed onset" from DSM-IV is now **"delayed expression"**.
- **Coding:** the supplied edition prints **F43.8** for both PGD and other specified trauma- and stressor-related disorder. Notes retain those source codes; later coding revisions are outside this source-bound summary.

## Differential logic: telling neighbours apart
1. **Was there a Criterion A event** (actual or threatened death, serious injury, sexual violence; direct, witnessed, learned (violent or accidental), or occupational)?
   - No: PTSD and ASD are out. Consider adjustment disorder, even if the full PTSD symptom pattern is present (e.g., job loss, a spouse leaving).
2. **How long since the event?**
   - <3 days: a normal or transient reaction, not ASD.
   - 3 days–1 month: ASD if ≥9/14 and remaining criteria are met. Otherwise assess adjustment disorder or another fitting diagnosis; distress alone does not establish either.
   - >1 month: PTSD if full criteria. Subthreshold: assess adjustment criteria, including whether the stressor or its consequences remain active; persistent PTSD-like symptoms >6 months are a source example for **other specified**. The adjustment offset clock runs from termination of stressor/consequences, not simply the trauma date.
3. **Was it a death?**
   - <12 months (6 in youth): normal grief, or adjustment disorder if out of proportion or impairing.
   - ≥12 months (≥6 in youth): PGD if yearning or preoccupation + ≥3 C symptoms and all remaining requirements are met. Clinically significant nonqualifying presentations may fit other specified; normal grief remains possible.
   - Violent death: screen for PTSD as well. PTSD intrusions are about the event; PGD intrusions are about the relationship. Yearning occurs only in PGD.
4. **Is another disorder met?** MDD, an anxiety disorder, OCD or a dissociative disorder takes precedence over adjustment disorder (Criterion C), and may be diagnosed **with** PTSD.
5. **Child with a history of neglect:**
   - Withdrawn, does not seek or accept comfort: RAD. Rule out ASD (restricted and repetitive behavior, selective social-communication deficit, normal attachment).
   - Indiscriminately friendly with strangers: DSED. Assess ADHD separately; DSED requires social disinhibition beyond general impulsivity, and the two may coexist.

Other chapter-wide pitfalls:
- Depressive rumination is **not** an intrusion. Intrusions are involuntary, vivid and sensory.
- Media exposure counts toward Criterion A only if it is **work related**.
- Hearing the voice of, or sensing, the deceased is common in normal grief. It is not psychosis without other psychotic symptoms.
- Postconcussive symptoms overlap with ASD and PTSD. Reexperiencing and avoidance point to a trauma disorder. Persistent disorientation and confusion point to TBI.
- Adjustment disorder is **not** low risk. It is associated with suicide attempts and suicide.
- Culture: self-blame may be normative (karma, destiny), somatic presentations are common, and cultural syndromes (ataque de nervios, khyâl) may present. Judge "out of proportion" and "beyond norms" against the person's culture.

## Shared specifiers and coding conventions
- **RAD and DSED:** *persistent* (>12 months) and *severe* (all symptoms at relatively high levels).
- **PTSD** (both age sets): *with dissociative symptoms* (depersonalization or derealization, not due to a substance or seizures) and *with delayed expression* (full criteria ≥6 months post-event). One code, **F43.10**. Specifiers are written out.
- **ASD:** no specifiers. If it persists >1 month and meets PTSD criteria, recode as PTSD.
- **Adjustment disorders:** the subtype determines the code (F43.20–F43.25). Duration specifier *acute* (<6 mo) or *persistent (chronic)* (≥6 mo, only with a chronic stressor or enduring consequences).
- **PGD:** no specifiers.
- **Other specified:** record the name plus the reason. **Unspecified:** no reason given, or insufficient information (e.g., the ER).
