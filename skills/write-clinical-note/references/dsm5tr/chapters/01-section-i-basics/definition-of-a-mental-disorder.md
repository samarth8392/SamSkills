# Definition of a Mental Disorder

> **Spark note:** Every Section II disorder (except the medication-induced and "Other Conditions" chapters) must satisfy this definition: a clinically significant syndrome reflecting an *internal dysfunction*, usually with distress or disability. The two exclusions do the most work in practice: an expectable/culturally sanctioned reaction to a stressor or loss is **not** a disorder, and social deviance or person-vs-society conflict is **not** a disorder unless it stems from an individual dysfunction. A diagnosis also does **not** equal a need for treatment, and missing a diagnosis does not bar care (p. 104).

## Scope (p. 104)
- Applies to every disorder in Section II **except** the chapters on medication-induced movement disorders/other adverse effects of medication and "Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention" (Z codes). Those are coded but are *not* mental disorders.

## Required elements (structural paraphrase) (p. 104)
| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. **Syndrome** | A recognizable pattern of signs and symptoms, not a single isolated behaviour |
| 2. **Clinically significant disturbance** | Affects cognition, emotion regulation, **or** behaviour |
| 3. **Reflects a dysfunction** | In the psychological, biological or developmental processes that underlie mental functioning |
| 4. **Usually** distress or disability | In social, occupational or other important activities (usual, not absolute, in the definition; most criteria sets make it explicit via the clinical significance criterion) |
| Exclusion A | An **expectable or culturally approved** response to a common stressor or loss (e.g., death of a loved one) is not a disorder |
| Exclusion B | **Socially deviant** behaviour (political, religious, sexual) and conflicts mainly between the person and society are not disorders **unless** they result from an individual dysfunction as above |

## Clinical utility vs need for treatment (p. 104)
- A diagnosis should be **clinically useful**: it should inform prognosis, treatment planning and likely outcomes.
- **Diagnosis ≠ need for treatment.** Treatment need is a separate, multifactor judgment:
  - symptom severity
  - symptom **salience** (e.g., suicidal thoughts)
  - the person's distress ("mental pain")
  - symptom-related disability
  - risks and benefits of available treatments
  - other factors (e.g., psychiatric sx complicating another medical illness)
- People who fall **short of full criteria** may still clearly need care. Not having every symptom should **not** be used to restrict access to appropriate treatment.

## Purpose limits (p. 104)
- Built for **clinical, public health and research** purposes.
- Legal questions (criminal responsibility, disability eligibility, competency) usually need information **beyond** the DSM criteria; see [forensic-caution.md](forensic-caution.md).

## Related conceptual points
- DSM-5 is mainly **categorical with dimensional elements**; categories are not assumed to be discrete or to have absolute boundaries; see [categorical-dimensional-and-assessment-tools.md](categorical-dimensional-and-assessment-tools.md) (p. 105).
- Thresholds are set in practice by the **clinical significance criterion**; see [clinical-significance-criterion.md](clinical-significance-criterion.md).
- Cultural norms determine what counts as "expectable" or "culturally approved"; see [cultural-considerations.md](cultural-considerations.md).

## Pitfalls
- Diagnosing normal grief, a culturally normative reaction or a political/religious/sexual nonconformity as a disorder.
- Treating "meets criteria" as automatically "needs treatment", or "subthreshold" as "no care needed".
- Reading a diagnosis as a legal finding.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Show the dysfunction, not only the behaviour.** Record the internal process that is disturbed (e.g., mood regulation, perception, cognition, impulse control) and how it shows up, not just the conduct that others object to.
- **Address the expectable-response exclusion when there is a stressor or loss.** State why the reaction goes beyond what is expectable or culturally sanctioned in intensity, duration, pervasiveness or impairment, given the person's cultural context (e.g., "Grief response exceeds family/community norms as described by client and spouse; persists at X months with Y impairment").
- **Address social deviance when relevant.** If the referral is driven by conflict with others, the law or society, note whether there is evidence of an individual dysfunction behind it.
- **Document need for treatment on its own grounds.** Severity, salience (SI/HI), subjective distress, disability, risk/benefit of options. Example: "Subthreshold presentation; treatment indicated given persistent passive SI and work impairment."
- If symptoms are subthreshold but care is indicated, use an **other specified/unspecified** diagnosis or a **Z code** rather than inflating criteria; see [other-specified-and-unspecified.md](other-specified-and-unspecified.md).
