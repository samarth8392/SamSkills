# Other Specified vs Unspecified Disorders: General Rules

> **Spark note:** If a presentation causes clinically significant distress or impairment but does not meet full criteria for any specific disorder, use the **other specified** or **unspecified** category of the chapter that matches the **predominant symptoms**. The **only** difference is whether the clinician chooses to **record the reason** criteria are not met: other specified records it, unspecified does not. Unspecified works as a **placeholder** where information is limited (e.g., the ED). This is entirely a matter of clinical judgment (pp. 114–115).

## When to use (pp. 114–115)
Both conditions must be met:
1. Presentation does **not meet full criteria** for any specific disorder in the relevant class.
2. Symptoms cause **clinically significant distress or impairment** (social, occupational or other important areas).
- Pick the chapter from the **predominant symptoms** (psychotic, depressive, anxiety, trauma, etc.).
- Rationale: categorical criteria cannot cover the full range of real-world presentations.

## Other specified vs unspecified (pp. 114–115)
| | Other specified | Unspecified |
|---|---|---|
| Records **why** criteria are not met? | **Yes**, as the category name followed by the specific reason | **No** |
| Typical use | Enough information to describe the atypical/subthreshold presentation | Not enough information, or clinician chooses not to specify (e.g., ED, early assessment) |
| Text example | "other specified schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder, with persistent auditory hallucinations" (hallucinations with no other psychotic symptoms) | "unspecified schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder" |
| Placeholder role | No | Yes, until a fuller differential is possible |

- In settings such as the **emergency department**, it may only be possible to identify the most prominent symptom cluster (delusions, hallucinations, mania, depression, anxiety, substance intoxication, neurocognitive symptoms). The corresponding **unspecified** disorder can then be used as a **placeholder** (p. 114).
- **Conditions for Further Study** (Section III) are traditionally listed as **examples** under other specified. Listing them there does **not** mean APA endorses them as valid diagnoses (p. 115).
- Codes: each chapter has its own OS/US codes, which are listed in that chapter's `other-specified-and-unspecified.md`.

## TR note
- DSM-5-TR adds **unspecified mood disorder** (F39) for mood presentations that cannot yet be placed in the bipolar vs depressive chapters. This is described in those chapters, not in Section I. ⚠ It is not mentioned in the Section I text, so check the mood chapters.

## Pitfalls
- Using OS/US when full criteria **are** met; give the specific disorder instead.
- Using OS/US with **no** clinically significant distress or impairment. Then there is no diagnosis, though a Z code may apply.
- Writing "other specified" **without** stating the reason, which in effect makes it an unspecified diagnosis.
- Leaving an "unspecified" placeholder in the chart once enough data have come in. Update it to a specific or other specified diagnosis.
- Confusing **unspecified** with **provisional**. Provisional = criteria **expected** to be met once information arrives. Unspecified = criteria not met, or not able to specify. See [provisional-diagnosis.md](provisional-diagnosis.md).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Other specified format:** `<code> Other specified <chapter> disorder, <specific reason/presentation>`, e.g., "F__.__ Other specified depressive disorder, depressive episode with insufficient symptoms (4 of 9 sx for 3 wk)".
- **Unspecified format:** `<code> Unspecified <chapter> disorder`, plus a note sentence giving the reason (e.g., "insufficient history in ED; reassess after collateral").
- **Document both gates:** (a) which full criteria are **not** met and why (e.g., duration, symptom count, missing feature), and (b) the specific distress or impairment.
- **Plan the follow-up**: say what information would allow a specific diagnosis (collateral, records, tox screen, observation over time).
- Pick the chapter by the **predominant** symptom cluster and say so in the note.
