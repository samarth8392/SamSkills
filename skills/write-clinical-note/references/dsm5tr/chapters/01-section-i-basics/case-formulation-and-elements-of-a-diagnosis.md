# Case Formulation and Elements of a Diagnosis

> **Spark note:** DSM-5's main purpose is to support diagnosis **within a case formulation** that leads to a treatment plan. Checking off symptoms is explicitly **not enough**. The diagnostic sequence is: assess the criteria with clinical judgment, add subtypes/specifiers, fall back to other specified/unspecified if criteria are not met, then record with the correct code, order and modifiers (pp. 113–114).

## Case formulation (p. 113)
- Requires a **careful clinical history** and a concise summary of **social, psychological and biological** factors that may have contributed.
- Simply ticking off criteria is **insufficient**. A thorough criteria review improves reliability (dimensional severity tools can help), but weighing the **severity and salience** of each sign and symptom takes clinical judgment.
- Training is needed to recognise when the **predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective** factors have combined into a condition whose signs exceed normal ranges.
- Goal: a **comprehensive treatment plan** informed by the individual's **cultural and social context**. (DSM does not choose treatments; that is outside its scope.)

## Elements of a diagnosis: the workflow (pp. 113–114)
1. **Apply the criteria as guidelines**, informed by clinical judgment. Use the text (Diagnostic Features, Differential Diagnosis, chapter intros) to support the decision.
2. **Add subtypes and/or specifiers** as appropriate; see [subtypes-and-specifiers.md](subtypes-and-specifiers.md).
   - **Current-presentation specifiers** (most of them) can change over the course, and **may be given only if full criteria are currently met**. Examples: *with good or fair insight*, *predominantly inattentive presentation*, *in a controlled environment*.
   - **Lifetime-course specifiers** may be assigned **regardless of current status**. Examples: *with seasonal pattern*, *bipolar type* (schizoaffective disorder).
3. **If full criteria are not met but there is clinically significant distress or impairment**, consider the **other specified** or **unspecified** category that matches the predominant symptoms; see [other-specified-and-unspecified.md](other-specified-and-unspecified.md).
4. **Code and record:** ICD-10-CM code, then name, then subtype/specifiers. See [coding-and-recording-procedures.md](coding-and-recording-procedures.md).
5. **Order multiple diagnoses:** principal diagnosis or reason for visit first; see [principal-diagnosis-and-reason-for-visit.md](principal-diagnosis-and-reason-for-visit.md).
6. **Mark uncertainty** with "(provisional)" when appropriate; see [provisional-diagnosis.md](provisional-diagnosis.md).
7. **Add Z codes or other conditions** (psychosocial/contextual factors, medication adverse effects) that affect care; see [terminology-and-section-ii-conventions.md](terminology-and-section-ii-conventions.md).

## Pitfalls
- Giving a current-state specifier (e.g., insight level, severity) when full criteria are **not** currently met. Use a remission or course specifier instead, where one exists.
- Leaving out a lifetime-course specifier because the person is currently well. These may be applied regardless of current status.
- Treating the criteria checklist as the formulation.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Write a short formulation paragraph** before the diagnosis list. Cover the biopsychosocial and cultural context, plus predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors.
- **Link each criterion to evidence:** what was reported, what was observed (MSE), and what collateral or records showed.
- **Record rule-outs** the text requires (substance/medication, another medical condition, better-explained-by-another-disorder).
- **Diagnosis block template:**
  ```
  Diagnoses (in order of clinical focus):
  1. F__.__ <Disorder name>, <subtype>, <specifier(s)>, <severity> (principal diagnosis | reason for visit) [(provisional) if applicable]
  2. F__.__ <Comorbid disorder>, <specifiers>
  3. Z__.__ <Psychosocial/contextual factor>
  ```
- State the **basis for each specifier** (e.g., "with anxious distress: reports keyed-up feeling, restlessness and fear something awful will happen, most days").
