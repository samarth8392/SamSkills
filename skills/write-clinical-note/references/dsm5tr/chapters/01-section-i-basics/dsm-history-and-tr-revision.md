# DSM History, DSM-5 Development, and DSM-5-TR Revision Process

> **Spark note:** DSM-5-TR (work began in 2019) updated the **text** using the previous 10 years of literature. It added review by **Culture, Sex and Gender, Suicide and Forensic** cross-cutting groups and an **Ethnoracial Equity and Inclusion** work group. It did **not** make conceptual criteria changes, but some criteria and specifier clarifications went through the **iterative revision** process. Every change approved since DSM-5 (2013) is included. Structure is harmonized with ICD-11, but U.S. coding stays **ICD-10-CM** (pp. 100–104).

## Brief history (pp. 94–95)
| Edition | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| DSM-I | 1952 | First official manual with a glossary; "reaction" terminology (Adolf Meyer) |
| DSM-II | 1968 | Based on ICD-8 |
| DSM-III | 1980 | **Explicit diagnostic criteria**; descriptive, etiology-neutral approach (Spitzer); coordinated with ICD-9 |
| DSM-III-R | 1987 | Fixed inconsistencies |
| DSM-IV | 1994 | Empirical literature review, more than 1,000 contributors; coordinated with ICD-10 (1992) |
| DSM-5 | 2013 (approved Dec 2012) | 12-year effort; dimensional measures; nonaxial |
| DSM-5-TR | 2022 | Text revision plus iterative updates |

## DSM-5 development (pp. 95–100)
- 1999 evaluation → 2002 *A Research Agenda for DSM-V* → 13 APA/WHO/NIH research conferences (2003–2008; 400 participants, 39 countries).
- Task Force (Kupfer, Regier; 28 members), 13 work groups (more than 130 members), more than 400 advisors; commercial income disclosure and caps.
- **Four guiding principles:** (1) clinician-oriented and feasible in routine practice; (2) evidence-guided; (3) continuity with prior editions where possible; (4) no a priori limit on the amount of change.
- New diagnoses/specifiers had to show **reliability** (agreement between two clinicians). Low-utility, weakly valid disorders were candidates for deletion. Placement in Section III depended on evidence, need and research value.
- **Field trials:** medical-academic (11 sites, Dec 2010 to Oct 2011; test-retest with blinded clinicians, intraclass kappa) and routine clinical practice (Oct 2011 to Mar 2012).
- **Public comment:** postings in 2010, 2011 and 2012; more than 13,000 signed comments.
- **Review bodies:** Scientific Review Committee (evidence by validators), Clinical and Public Health Committee, forensic review by the Council on Psychiatry and Law, Assembly, Board of Trustees.

## Iterative revision process (p. 100)
- DSM Steering Committee (2014; Appelbaum chair) handles proposals through a web portal on an ongoing basis.
- Proposals need: rationale, size of change, validator evidence, reliability, clinical utility, and possible harms.
- **Validators:** antecedent (genetics, family traits, temperament, environmental exposure), concurrent (neural substrates, biomarkers, emotional/cognitive processing, symptom similarity), predictive (course, treatment response). New criteria must improve some validators. New disorders need a substantial subset of validators, must meet the mental disorder definition, and must show clinical utility.
- Pathway: Steering Committee screen → one of **5 Review Committees** → Steering Committee → public comment → Assembly and Board → online update (psychiatryonline.org).

## DSM-5-TR revision process (pp. 100–101)
- Began **Spring 2019**. Co-chairs **Michael B. First** and **Philip Wang**; vice chairs Wilson Compton and Daniel Pine.
- **More than 200 experts** reviewed the **past 10 years** of literature to find out-of-date text. Conflict-of-interest review covered every proposed change.
- **20 disorder review groups** (each with a section editor) plus **4 cross-cutting review groups: Culture, Sex and Gender, Suicide, Forensic**.
- **Ethnoracial Equity and Inclusion Work Group**: racism/discrimination as risk factors; nonstigmatizing language.
- **Scope:** **no conceptual changes to criteria sets**, but needed **clarifications** to some criteria and specifier definitions were approved through the iterative process (Steering Committee, Assembly, Board).
- **Text changes named in Section I:** new "**Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior**" section where evidence is available (p. 111); expanded culture/racism content and terminology (pp. 108–110); prevalence statements tied to the populations studied (p. 110).
- ⚠ Section I does **not** list specific new diagnoses. The TR additions known from elsewhere in the manual (e.g., **prolonged grief disorder**, **unspecified mood disorder**, new suicidal behaviour/NSSI codes, updated ICD-10-CM codes) are covered in the relevant chapter files.

## ICD-11 harmonization (pp. 103–104)
- Reasons: two systems hinder national statistics, clinical trials, global regulatory use and replication; DSM-IV and ICD-10 often disagreed even on the same patients.
- Harmonization succeeded mainly in **organizational structure**. Criteria-level harmonization was limited because DSM-5 was finished several years before ICD-11. Even so, DSM and ICD are now **closer than at any time since DSM-II/ICD-8**.
- ICD-11 endorsed May 2019, effective Jan 1, 2022. The **2022 text** reports no U.S. adoption timeline and continued ICD-10-CM use at publication.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming the TR changed criteria in a major way. It changed mainly text, with clarifications and a few additions through the iterative process.
- Using ICD-11 codes or terms for U.S. billing.
- Citing printed DSM-5 (2013) text for disorders whose text or codes were updated in the TR.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Cite the classification as "**DSM-5-TR**" with **ICD-10-CM** codes (e.g., "Diagnoses per DSM-5-TR; ICD-10-CM codes").
- When a code has changed since DSM-5 (2013), distinguish the **TR edition code** from the applicable current ICD-10-CM code and verify the latter externally, and update older chart diagnoses at the next review.
- Use the TR's **updated language** in notes (see [cultural-considerations.md](cultural-considerations.md)).
