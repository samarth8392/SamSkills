# Cultural, Social Structural, and Sex/Gender Considerations

> **Spark note:** Culture shapes how symptoms are experienced and expressed, **where the threshold of pathology lies**, help-seeking and acceptance of a diagnosis. Assessment must judge how the person's experience differs from **their own** sociocultural norms, and must account for social position, racism and discrimination. The TR adds a strong emphasis on **racism as a social determinant of health** and on **clinician bias** (e.g., misdiagnosing schizophrenia in African Americans who present with mood disorders). It also changed its language (*racialized*, *ethnoracial*, *Latinx*; no *Caucasian*, *minority* or *non-White*) (pp. 106–111).

## Cultural and social structural issues (pp. 106–107)
- Disorders are defined and recognized against **local sociocultural/community norms**. Culture shapes symptoms, signs, behaviours and **severity thresholds**.
- Sociocultural context also shapes **identity** (ethnicity, race), which sets social position and differential exposure to **social determinants of health**.
- Assessment should cover: (a) how experiences differ from **relevant sociocultural norms** and cause adaptation difficulties; (b) how **position in social structures/hierarchies** affects exposure to adversity and access to resources.

## Impact of cultural norms and practices (p. 107)
- The normal/pathological boundary and **tolerance thresholds** vary by cultural context, social setting and family.
- Routinely consider cultural meanings, identities and practices as they affect:
  - **vulnerability and disorder mechanisms** (e.g., amplifying the fears that maintain panic disorder or health anxiety)
  - **stigma and support** from family/community
  - **coping** and **help-seeking** pathways (including alternative/complementary care)
  - **acceptance or rejection of the diagnosis** and **adherence**
- Cultural differences between clinician/team and patient affect **diagnostic accuracy and acceptance**, treatment, prognosis and outcomes.

## Cultural concepts of distress (pp. 107–108)
These replaced "culture-bound syndrome" (since DSM-5):
| Concept | Definition (paraphrase) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultural idiom of distress** | Shared way of talking about or expressing suffering; need not map to a syndrome or cause; may express everyday or subclinical distress | Saying "I feel so depressed" for sub-MDD low mood; bodily idioms |
| **Cultural explanation / perceived cause** | Label or attribution in an explanatory model that gives a culturally coherent cause | Attributing illness to "stress", spirits, or not following prescribed practices |
| **Cultural syndrome** | Cluster of co-occurring distinctive sx in specific cultural groups/contexts; may or may not be locally seen as illness | *Ataque de nervios* |
- One term can serve **several functions** and change over time (e.g., "depression" is both a syndrome name and an idiom).
- They affect symptomatology, help-seeking, presentation, treatment expectations, adaptation and response. Details are in Section III, "Culture and Psychiatric Diagnosis".

## Racism and discrimination (pp. 108–109)
- **Race is a social, not biological, construct.** **Racialization** is the social process of building identity categories from racial ideologies. Other identities (ethnicity, gender, language, religion, sexual orientation) can also attract bias that affects assessment.
- **Levels of racism:** personal (internalized stereotypes, threat, devaluation) · interpersonal (explicit acts plus **microaggressions**) · systemic/institutional (embedded practices, including in health care; can run on **implicit bias**) · social structural (inequities in resources, power and policy).
- **Health effects:** linked to hypertension, suicidal behaviour and PTSD; predisposes to substance use, mood disorders and psychosis.
- **Clinical consequences:** unequal access, **clinician bias**, e.g., **misdiagnosis of schizophrenia in African Americans** presenting with mood and other disorders, more coercive pathways to care, less outpatient time, more restraint, suboptimal treatment.
- Clinicians should **actively recognize and address** racism, bias and stereotyping in assessment, diagnosis and treatment.

## TR review process and language (pp. 109–110)
- Cross-Cutting Review Committee on Cultural Issues (**19** experts) reviewed the culture-related diagnostic issues. **Ethnoracial Equity and Inclusion Work Group** (**10** clinicians) reviewed race/ethnicity references to avoid stereotyping.
- **Terminology:** *racialized* (not "racial"); *ethnoracial* = U.S. Census combined categories (Hispanic, White, African American); *Latinx*; **avoid** *Caucasian*, *minority*, *non-White*; use "cultural contexts/backgrounds" rather than "cultures" as discrete groups. Study labels are kept when reporting specific data.
- **Prevalence** statements name the population studied (e.g., "in the U.S. general population"). Ethnoracial data are given only from representative samples, which is why data on e.g. **Native Americans** are limited. Read the culture sections alongside the prevalence figures.

## Sex and gender differences (pp. 110–111)
- **Sex** = reproductive organs and XX/XY complement. **Gender** = reproductive organs plus self-representation and the psychological, behavioural and social effects of perceived gender. The text mostly reports **gender** ("women and men") because data are self-identified. It uses **sex** ("male and female") for e.g. metabolism or for pregnancy/menopause.
- **Ways sex/gender influence illness:** (1) may **solely determine risk** (PMDD); (2) **moderate risk/prevalence**; (3) shape **which symptoms** appear (ADHD in boys vs girls); (4) indirect effects such as differential **symptom endorsement** and service use (e.g., women may endorse more depressive/bipolar/anxiety sx).
- **Reproductive events** (menstrual cycle, pregnancy, menopause) can affect risk. **With peripartum onset** applies to brief psychotic disorder and to manic, hypomanic or major depressive episodes. Postpartum sleep/energy changes are often normative, so they have **lower diagnostic reliability** in postpartum women.
- Where sex/gender information appears: gender-specific sx in the criteria; sex-related specifiers; prevalence by sex/gender; the "Sex- and Gender-Related Diagnostic Issues" text section.

## Tools
- **Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI)**, informant version and supplementary modules (Section III; online at psychiatry.org/dsm5). Consider it whenever cultural dynamics matter for the diagnosis (p. 112).

## Pitfalls
- Pathologizing a cultural idiom (e.g., spirit attribution or a bodily complaint) as a delusion or somatic disorder without checking cultural norms.
- Taking a standard idiom ("I'm depressed") as meeting MDD criteria.
- Applying prevalence data to groups that were not studied.
- Over-diagnosing psychosis in racialized patients with mood disorders.
- Calling normative postpartum sleep or energy change a symptom.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Cultural identity and context:** record the person's **self-described** identity, language preference, interpreter use, and relevant community/religious affiliations. Do not record clinician-assigned race labels.
- **Explanatory model:** quote or paraphrase the person's own explanation of the problem (perceived cause) and their idioms of distress. Note whether family/community see it as illness.
- **Norm comparison:** state how the experience differs from the person's **reference group norms**, using collateral when possible (e.g., "Family confirms hearing deceased relative's voice is accepted in their tradition during mourning, but current command content and duration are outside that norm").
- **Structural factors:** document discrimination, racism-related stress, access barriers and adversity exposure. Code relevant **Z codes** where they meet listing criteria.
- **Bias check:** when diagnosing a psychotic disorder in a racialized individual, explicitly document that mood disorder (and trauma) were considered and why they were ruled out.
- **Peripartum:** record pregnancy/postpartum status and onset timing to support *with peripartum onset*. Note when sleep/energy changes are judged normative.
- Note **CFI** use (or the reason it was not used) where cultural factors affect the diagnosis.
- Use the TR's **nonstigmatizing language** in the note (e.g., "racialized", "person with schizophrenia").
