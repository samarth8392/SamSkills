# Section Introductions and Clinical Status of Manual Content

> **Spark note:** Section II combines approved diagnostic criteria with explanatory text and two clinically relevant chapters whose conditions are not mental disorders. Section III provides assessment tools, cultural formulation, an alternative personality model and proposed research conditions. The placement of an entry determines how its information should support a clinical note.

## Section I: orientation and use (pp. 92–93)

- Introduction: revision history, organization, the mental-disorder definition, categorical/dimensional approaches and cultural/social and sex/gender considerations.
- Use of the Manual: diagnostic workflow, subtypes/specifiers, residual categories, judgment, coding, terminology and online resources.
- Forensic caution: limits of applying clinical diagnoses to legal questions.
- These introductory pages map the section; detailed topic notes begin with [the Section I overview](_overview.md).

## Section II: approved criteria and clinical companions (pp. 126–129)

- Diagnostic criteria approved for routine clinical use appear with ICD-10-CM codes and descriptive text. Coding notes and recording procedures are provided where necessary.
- Descriptive text assists diagnostic decisions; the criteria, code and relevant recording instructions need to be considered together.
- Two chapters deliberately include conditions **outside the mental-disorder classification**:
  - [Medication-induced movement disorders and other adverse effects](../24-medication-induced-movement-disorders-and-other-adverse-effe/_overview.md): medication-management concerns and differential diagnosis with psychiatric presentations.
  - [Other conditions that may be a focus of clinical attention](../25-other-conditions-that-may-be-a-focus-of-clinical-attention/_overview.md): contextual problems affecting evaluation, course, prognosis or treatment.
- Their inclusion alongside diagnostic criteria reflects their clinical relevance; it does not turn adverse effects or psychosocial circumstances into mental disorders.

## Section III: emerging measures and models (pp. 1104–1106)

- [Assessment measures](../26-assessment-measures/_overview.md): Level 1 cross-cutting assessment, psychosis severity dimensions and WHODAS. Follow significant Level 1 findings with further assessment; Level 2 measures are described as online resources.
- [Culture and psychiatric diagnosis](../27-culture-and-psychiatric-diagnosis/_overview.md): cultural context, the Cultural Formulation Interview, its informant version and examples of cultural concepts of distress.
- [Alternative personality model](../28-alternative-dsm-5-model-for-personality-disorders/_overview.md): a hybrid dimensional/categorical framework based on personality functioning and pathological traits, presented as an alternative to Section II's classification.
- [Conditions for further study](../29-conditions-for-further-study/_overview.md): proposed criteria and explanatory text intended to support research. The opening lists attenuated psychosis syndrome, depressive episodes with short-duration hypomania, caffeine use disorder, internet gaming disorder, neurobehavioral disorder associated with prenatal alcohol exposure, suicidal behavior disorder and nonsuicidal self-injury disorder.
- Section III is heterogeneous: an assessment instrument, an alternative model and a research criteria set have different roles. A research condition should not be presented as an independently established Section II diagnosis merely because its proposed criteria are printed here.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

- Separate the mental-disorder diagnosis, medication adverse effects and contextual conditions in the formulation.
- Name assessment instruments and relevant findings, then document the interview evidence supporting the diagnosis.
- Label use of an alternative model or research construct explicitly, and select any official diagnostic category using its own criteria and coding instructions.
- Consult the local PDF for printed forms when an extracted page contains only an image; the text extraction is not a substitute for the instrument.
