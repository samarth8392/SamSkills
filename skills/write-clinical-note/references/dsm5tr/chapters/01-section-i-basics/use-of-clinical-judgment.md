# Use of Clinical Judgment

> **Spark note:** The criteria are **guidelines informed by clinical judgment**, not a cookbook, and they are meant for trained clinicians. Judgment can justify giving a diagnosis when a presentation falls **just short** of full criteria, provided the symptoms present are **persistent and severe**. Too much flexibility or idiosyncratic use, however, destroys DSM's value as a common language (p. 115).

## Key points (paraphrase)
- DSM-5 was built for **clinical, educational and research** use by people with **appropriate clinical training** in diagnosis (p. 115).
- It should **not be applied mechanically** by untrained individuals (p. 115; see also the forensic caution, p. 124).
- Criteria are **guidelines**, and their use should be informed by judgment (pp. 113, 115).
- **Near-threshold allowance:** judgment may justify a diagnosis that falls just short of full criteria **if** the symptoms present are **persistent and severe** (p. 115).
- **Counterweight:** unfamiliarity with DSM or overly flexible, idiosyncratic use **greatly reduces** its usefulness as a shared language (p. 115).
- Judgment is also needed for: the **severity and salience** of signs within a case formulation (p. 113); whether the **clinical significance criterion** is met (p. 116); choosing **other specified vs unspecified** (p. 115); picking the **principal diagnosis** when several conditions contributed equally (p. 117); and individual **suicide risk**, which should not rest on the diagnosis alone (p. 111).

## Pitfalls
- Using "clinical judgment" as cover for a diagnosis with mild, transient symptoms. The allowance requires **persistent and severe** symptoms.
- Rigid checklist use that ignores context, culture or collateral.
- Not explaining the deviation from the criteria in the record.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **When diagnosing just short of full criteria**, say so openly and justify it: name the unmet element, and record evidence of **persistence** (duration, frequency) and **severity** (impairment, risk). This is a clinician judgment described by the manual, not permission for an agent to waive thresholds. Mark unmet criteria explicitly and request clinician review; do not manufacture evidence or assign the diagnosis automatically.
- Consider whether an **other specified** diagnosis (with the reason stated) would communicate the presentation more accurately than a stretched specific diagnosis. Document which you chose and why.
- Note the **sources** your judgment rests on: interview, MSE, collateral, records, standardized measures.
- For risk, write an **individualized risk formulation** (risk and protective factors). Do not cite the diagnosis alone.
