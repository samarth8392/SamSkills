# Prefaces and DSM-5-TR Revision Scope

> **Spark note:** The TR preface explains that this edition combines the original DSM-5, intervening approved updates and a comprehensive text revision. Criteria clarification affected **over 70 disorders**; the volume also adds prolonged grief disorder and symptom codes for suicidal and nonsuicidal self-injury behavior. The retained DSM-5 preface describes changes made for **2013**, so its historical predictions and coding transition dates must be read in that context.

## What the TR revision includes (pp. 35–36)

- Three overlapping editorial processes contributed: the original DSM-5 Task Force; the DSM Steering Committee's continuing revisions after 2013; and the Revision Subcommittee's new descriptive text.
- The book incorporates earlier online revisions, scientific developments and ICD-10-CM adjustments. Its publication does not freeze later coding updates.
- More than 70 disorders received criteria modifications, mainly to clarify wording. The text-revision project's scope excluded conceptual redesign of existing criteria; this does not mean every criterion remained word-for-word unchanged.
- **Prolonged grief disorder** is a new diagnosis in this volume. New symptom codes permit recording suicidal and nonsuicidal self-injury behavior. The preface does not provide those codes or operational criteria; use their detailed chapter entries.
- The most extensively revised text areas include prevalence, risk/prognosis, cultural considerations, sex/gender considerations, suicidal thoughts/behavior and comorbidity.
- The Ethnoracial Equity and Inclusion review covered the entire text, including racism/discrimination as risks and nonstigmatizing wording.
- Terminology: **DSM** can refer to the system generally; **DSM-5** refers to the 2013 approved content; **DSM-5-TR** identifies this revised volume. Criteria originating in DSM-5 retain that attribution; prolonged grief disorder originates in the TR.

## Why the retained DSM-5 preface matters (pp. 37–40)

- The manual aims to support clinical communication, diagnosis, education, research and public-health statistics across disciplines and treatment settings. Available criteria describe recognizable presentations even where underlying pathology is incompletely understood.
- The 2013 reorganization recognizes shared symptom domains and vulnerabilities across categories and puts lifespan considerations into both chapter order and criteria where relevant.
- Historical changes highlighted here include consolidation into autism spectrum disorder; embedding episode criteria within mood-disorder presentations; and replacing abuse/dependence categories with substance use disorders. Physiological adaptation to prescribed medication does not by itself establish addiction.
- More specific neurocognitive diagnoses reflect clinical and biological evidence. Section II retained categorical personality disorders, while Section III introduced an alternative hybrid model focused on functioning and pathological traits.
- Section III also supplies measures and research proposals. WHODAS replaces the former global-functioning scale as a disability-assessment resource; symptom and disability measures add dimensions to categorical descriptions.
- **Historical context:** statements about forthcoming ICD-10-CM adoption or anticipated ICD-11 release in the 2012 preface describe expectations at that time. For this volume's coding framework, see [coding and recording procedures](coding-and-recording-procedures.md).
- The prefaces include acknowledgments and editorial history; these do not add diagnostic thresholds.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)

- Identify the edition supporting the diagnosis and use the detailed disorder entry for thresholds and code selection.
- Check revisions at the level of criteria, descriptive text and coding rather than assuming that a text revision only changed explanatory prose.
- Keep historical nomenclature separate from the current clinical formulation. Cite a historical label only when it explains prior records.
