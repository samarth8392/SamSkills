# Section I: DSM-5 Basics (Overview)

> **Spark note:** Section I has no diagnoses. It sets the **rules for the others**: what counts as a mental disorder, how thresholds are set (clinical significance), how criteria are applied (guidelines plus judgment within a case formulation), and **how a diagnosis is recorded**. Recording means ICD-10-CM code, then name, then subtype/specifiers. List the principal diagnosis or reason for visit first (medical condition first for due-to-AMC). Use "(provisional)" only when the source's strong-presumption or upper-duration-limit rule applies. Use other specified (reason stated) or unspecified (no reason) for subthreshold presentations. No axes, no GAF, Z codes for context. It ends with a warning that diagnosis ≠ legal status.

## What unites the section
- **Source files:** Introduction (pp. 94–112), Use of the Manual (pp. 113–122), Cautionary Statement for Forensic Use (pp. 123–125).
- Introduction = **why/what** (history, revision process, conceptual frameworks, culture, sex/gender, suicide). Use of the Manual = **how** (formulation, diagnostic elements, coding, recording, terminology). Forensic statement = **limits**.

## TR changes and notable updates in Section I
- TR process (from 2019): 10-year literature update; **20 disorder review groups plus 4 cross-cutting groups (Culture, Sex and Gender, Suicide, Forensic)**; **Ethnoracial Equity and Inclusion** work group; no conceptual criteria changes, but criteria/specifier **clarifications** approved iteratively (pp. 100–101).
- New **"Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior"** text section where relevant literature is available (p. 111).
- Expanded **racism and discrimination** content; terminology changes (*racialized*, *ethnoracial*, *Latinx*; avoid *Caucasian*, *minority*, *non-White*) (pp. 108–110).
- Prevalence findings tied to the **populations studied** (p. 110).
- The 2022 source says ICD-11 came into effect January 1, 2022, while adoption is country-specific; it describes continued U.S. ICD-10-CM use at publication (pp. 103–104).
- Specific new TR diagnoses (prolonged grief disorder, unspecified mood disorder, suicidal behaviour/NSSI codes) are **not** described in Section I; see the disorder chapters.

## Section-level pitfalls and decision logic
- **Disorder vs no disorder:** does it meet the definition (clinically significant disturbance reflecting dysfunction; not an expectable/cultural response or mere social deviance) and the particular disorder's required criteria? Evaluate distress/function where required by that criteria set; do not add an impairment criterion to diagnoses whose criteria do not contain it. Contextual problems may warrant a Z code.
- **Specific vs OS vs US vs provisional:**
  - Full criteria met → specific disorder.
  - Insufficient information with a strong presumption that full criteria will be supported, or an unresolved upper-duration-limit diagnosis → specific disorder **(provisional)**.
  - Not met, clinically significant, reason can be stated → **other specified**, with the reason.
  - Not met or cannot specify (ED, limited info) → **unspecified** (placeholder).
  - Just short of criteria but persistent and severe → specific disorder by **clinical judgment** (document why), or other specified.
- **Current vs lifetime specifiers:** current-presentation specifiers need full criteria **now**; lifetime-course specifiers do not.
- **Order:** principal/reason for visit first. AMC exception: medical condition first, mental disorder second, labelled "(principal diagnosis)".
- **Etiology wording:** substance/medication-induced vs due to AMC vs **independent**.
- **Culture/bias:** compare against the person's own reference norms. Guard against misdiagnosing psychosis in racialized individuals with mood disorders.
- **Risk:** never infer suicide or violence risk from the diagnosis alone.
- **Legal:** diagnosis ≠ legal mental illness, incapacity or lack of control.

## Master recording template (for client notes)
```
DIAGNOSES (DSM-5-TR; ICD-10-CM)  - nonaxial, in order of clinical focus
1. <code> <Disorder>, <subtype>, <specifiers>, <severity>[, (provisional)] (principal diagnosis | reason for visit)
2. <code> <Comorbid disorder>, <specifiers>
3. <code> Other specified <class> disorder, <reason criteria not met>
4. <code> <Medication-induced condition, if any>
5. Z<code> <Psychosocial/contextual factor>  - listed because: reason for visit | explains tx | initiates/exacerbates | part of plan
[Due-to-AMC case: <medical condition code/name> listed immediately before "<mental disorder> due to <AMC> (principal diagnosis)"]
Function: WHODAS 2.0 <score/domains> (no GAF)
Measures: Level 1 CCSM positives; disorder-specific severity scores (baseline/current)
```
Recording rules:
- Code first, then the **exact DSM name**, then subtype, then specifiers in the order the disorder's Recording Procedures give.
- Specifiers not built into the code are **written out**. Use the coding note where specifiers or comorbidity **change** the code (NCD, substance/medication-induced).
- "(provisional)": say what data are pending and when it will be reviewed. Remove it, or revise the diagnosis (e.g., schizophreniform → schizophrenia after 6 mo), at follow-up.
- Other specified: always state the **reason**. Unspecified: say in the narrative why it could not be specified, and the plan.
- Support every diagnosis with **criterion-mapped evidence**, a **clinical significance** statement (domain plus example), **rule-outs** (substance/medication, AMC, other disorder), and **cultural context**.

## Shared conventions (apply to all chapter files)
- Each disorder's **Recording Procedures** text section overrides general habits. Check it for combined codes.
- Text section headings are standard across disorders (see [terminology-and-section-ii-conventions.md](terminology-and-section-ii-conventions.md)).
- DSM convention lists conditions for further study as examples under **other specified** categories; this does not endorse them as validated diagnostic categories (p. 115). Check each condition's placement; this rule does not apply to every Section III topic.
