# Provisional Diagnosis

> **Spark note:** Add "**(provisional)**" after a diagnosis when there is **not yet enough information** to confirm the criteria are met, but there is a **strong presumption** the information will become available. It has two uses: (1) missing history, e.g., the person cannot give an adequate history and collateral or records are expected; (2) **duration-limited** diagnoses where the outcome depends on whether the illness stays under an **upper time limit**, e.g., schizophreniform disorder (≥1 mo but <6 mo). Remove the modifier once confirmed, or change the diagnosis (p. 117).

## Rules (paraphrase) (p. 117)
- **When:** current information is insufficient to show criteria are met **and** there is a **strong presumption** that it will be.
- **How:** write "(provisional)" **after** the diagnosis.
- **Use 1, incomplete history:** e.g., presentation looks like current MDD but the person cannot give an adequate history; information is expected from an **informant interview or medical records**. Once it confirms the criteria, **remove** "(provisional)".
- **Use 2, upper duration limit:** when the differential rests **only** on whether the illness duration stays under a maximum. Example: **schizophreniform disorder** needs ≥1 month and <6 months. With symptoms ongoing and final duration unknown, mark it "(provisional)":
  - remits within 6 months → remove "(provisional)"
  - does not remit → change the diagnosis to **schizophrenia**
- Other upper-limit cases follow the same logic (e.g., brief psychotic disorder, <1 month). This is an inference from the rule, not stated in Section I.

## Provisional vs other options
| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Criteria **probably met**, data pending | **Diagnosis (provisional)** |
| Criteria **not met** but clinically significant; reason known | **Other specified ___, <reason>** |
| Criteria not met / cannot specify (e.g., ED) | **Unspecified ___** (placeholder) |
| Competing possibilities still being evaluated | List under differential/rule-outs in the assessment narrative; code only what is supported |

## Pitfalls
- Using "(provisional)" when there is **no** strong presumption, i.e., as a hedge. Use unspecified instead.
- Never removing the modifier after confirmation, or never converting schizophreniform to schizophrenia after 6 months.
- Writing "r/o X" as a coded diagnosis. "Rule-out" is not a DSM modifier; DSM's term for expected-but-unconfirmed is "(provisional)".

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Format:** `<code> <Disorder name>, <specifiers> (provisional)`, e.g., "F32.2 Major depressive disorder, single episode, severe (provisional)".
- **State the missing information and the plan to get it:** "Provisional pending collateral from sister and prior hospital records; limited history due to catatonic features."
- **For duration-limited diagnoses, record the onset date and the review date:** "Schizophreniform disorder (provisional). Psychotic sx onset 2026-08-01; reassess by 2027-02-01. If sx persist ≥6 months total, revise to schizophrenia."
- **At follow-up, document the resolution:** "Collateral confirms ≥2-week duration and impairment; 'provisional' removed."
- Use the term exactly: "(provisional)", in parentheses, after the diagnosis and its specifiers.
