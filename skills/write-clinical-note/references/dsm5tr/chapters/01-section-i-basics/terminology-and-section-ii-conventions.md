# Terminology Notes and Section II Conventions

> **Spark note:** Key DSM-5-TR terms: **substance/medication-induced** covers intoxicants, prescribed medications and toxins, including withdrawal effects. **Independent** mental disorder (formerly "primary") means not caused by a substance or medical condition; it does **not** mean free of psychosocial causes. **Another medical condition** replaces "general medical condition"; mental disorders are medical conditions. Section II also contains non-disorder chapters (medication-induced movement/adverse effects, and Z-code "Other Conditions") that can be listed **with or instead of** a mental disorder (pp. 117–121).

## Substance/medication-induced mental disorder (p. 117)
- Symptoms caused by the **physiological effects of an exogenous substance on the CNS**, including **withdrawal** from a substance that can cause physiological dependence.
- Substances include intoxicants (alcohol, inhalants, hallucinogens, cocaine), **psychotropic medications** (stimulants; sedatives, hypnotics, anxiolytics), **other medications** (e.g., steroids) and **environmental toxins** (e.g., organophosphate insecticides).
- Called "substance-induced" from DSM-III to DSM-IV. It was renamed in DSM-5 to stress that **medications** cause psychiatric symptoms too.

## Independent mental disorder (p. 118)
- History: "organic" vs "nonorganic/functional/psychogenic" (through DSM-III-R) → DSM-IV split organic into substance-induced and due-to-medical-condition, and called the rest "**primary**" → DSM-5 uses "**independent**".
- Reason: "primary" also meant "first to occur" among comorbid disorders, which caused confusion.
- Used in substance/medication-induced criteria (e.g., criterion C of substance/medication-induced anxiety disorder: evidence of an independent anxiety disorder).
- **"Independent" ≠ independent of psychosocial or environmental stressors.**

## Another medical condition (pp. 118–119)
- DSM-IV: "mental disorder" vs "**general medical condition**" (ICD chapter location: mental = Chapter 5; others = the remaining 16 of 17 chapters).
- DSM-5: "**another medical condition**", which stresses that mental disorders **are** medical conditions and can be brought on by other medical conditions.
- These terms are for **convenience only**. They do not imply a fundamental mind/body divide.

## Standard disorder text headings (pp. 119–120)
Recording Procedures · Subtypes · Specifiers · Diagnostic Features · Associated Features · Prevalence (usually 12-month; by age, ethnoracial group, sex ratio, geography) · Development and Course · Risk and Prognostic Factors (temperamental, environmental, genetic/physiological, course modifiers) · Culture-Related Diagnostic Issues · Sex- and Gender-Related Diagnostic Issues · Diagnostic Markers (exam, lab, imaging: e.g., low CSF hypocretin-1 in narcolepsy; FDG-PET hypometabolism in AD) · Association With Suicidal Thoughts or Behavior · Functional Consequences · Differential Diagnosis · Comorbidity. A section is omitted when data are limited.
- Diagnostic Features example: apparent negative symptoms in schizophrenia may be **medication side effects**. Associated Features example: somatic sx in GAD.

## Non-disorder chapters in Section II (pp. 120–121)
**Medication-Induced Movement Disorders and Other Adverse Effects of Medication** (not mental disorders): medication-induced parkinsonism, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, medication-induced acute dystonia, medication-induced acute akathisia, tardive dyskinesia, tardive dystonia/tardive akathisia, medication-induced postural tremor, antidepressant discontinuation syndrome, other adverse effect of medication.
- Included because they matter in **medication management** and **differential diagnosis** (e.g., anxiety disorder vs medication-induced acute akathisia).

**Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention** (mostly **Z codes**; not mental disorders). These may be coded **with or without** a mental disorder if **any** of the following apply:
1. it is a **reason for the current visit**;
2. it helps **explain the need** for a test, procedure or treatment;
3. it plays a role in the **initiation or exacerbation** of a mental disorder;
4. it is a problem to consider in the **overall management plan**.
- Categories: suicidal behaviour and NSSI; abuse and neglect; relational problems (e.g., Relationship Distress With Spouse or Intimate Partner); educational, occupational, housing, economic; social environment; legal system; other psychosocial/personal/environmental circumstances (e.g., unwanted pregnancy, crime/terrorism victim); access to care; personal history (e.g., Personal History of Psychological Trauma); counselling encounters (e.g., sex counselling); other (e.g., wandering associated with a mental disorder, uncomplicated bereavement, phase of life problem).

## Pitfalls
- Diagnosing an "independent" disorder without documenting the time course relative to substance use or medication changes.
- Forgetting that **prescribed** medications (e.g., steroids) and **toxins** count as "substance/medication".
- Using "organic", "functional" or "primary" in notes.
- Coding a Z code without one of the four listing reasons, or leaving out a relevant psychosocial Z code.
- Calling akathisia an anxiety disorder.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Substance/medication timeline:** record onset of sx relative to substance use, medication starts/stops/dose changes and withdrawal. Note any symptom-free periods during sustained abstinence (≥ about 1 month is the usual criterion anchor in the induced-disorder criteria sets; check each one), since these support "independent".
- **Medical rule-out:** list relevant medical conditions, labs and imaging, and say whether symptoms are judged a **direct physiological consequence** of another medical condition.
- **Terms:** use "independent", "another medical condition" and "substance/medication-induced", not "organic", "functional", "primary" or "general medical condition".
- **Medication adverse effects:** record movement findings (AIMS/BARS if used), the medication, dose and timing. Code the medication-induced condition separately from the mental disorder.
- **Z codes:** give each one its own line after the mental disorders and state which reason applies (e.g., "Z59.__ housing problem: exacerbating depressive sx; included in plan").
