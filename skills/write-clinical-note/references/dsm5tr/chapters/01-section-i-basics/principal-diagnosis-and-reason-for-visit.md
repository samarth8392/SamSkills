# Principal Diagnosis and Reason for Visit

> **Spark note:** DSM-5 allows **multiple diagnoses**. **Inpatient**: the **principal diagnosis** is the condition found, after study, to be chiefly responsible for the admission. **Outpatient**: the **reason for visit** is the condition chiefly responsible for the services at that visit. List it **first** and order the rest by focus of attention and treatment. **Exception:** for a mental disorder **due to another medical condition**, ICD rules put the medical condition first, so the principal diagnosis is listed **second**. Add "(principal diagnosis)" or "(reason for visit)" for clarity (pp. 116–117).

## Rules (paraphrase) (pp. 116–117)
| Setting | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Inpatient | **Principal diagnosis** | Condition established **after study** to be chiefly responsible for the admission |
| Outpatient | **Reason for visit** | Condition chiefly responsible for the ambulatory services received **during that visit** |

- In most cases it is also the **main focus of attention/treatment**.
- Choosing it can be **difficult and somewhat arbitrary**. Text example: a person hospitalized with **schizophrenia and alcohol use disorder**, where each may have contributed equally.
- **Order:** principal/reason for visit **first**, the others **in order of focus of attention and treatment**.
- **Due-to-AMC exception:** when the principal diagnosis or reason for visit is a mental disorder due to another medical condition (e.g., major NCD due to Alzheimer's disease; psychotic disorder due to malignant lung neoplasm), the **etiological medical condition is listed first** and the mental disorder (the true principal diagnosis) **second**.
- **Clarity qualifier:** follow the principal diagnosis with "**(principal diagnosis)**" or "**(reason for visit)**". This is especially useful in the AMC case, where list position no longer shows which is principal.

## Pitfalls
- Listing diagnoses in historical or alphabetical order instead of order of clinical focus.
- Leaving the inpatient admission diagnosis unchanged after study has shown another condition was chiefly responsible.
- Breaking the AMC sequencing rule, or following it without adding the qualifier, which leaves it unclear what the principal diagnosis is.
- In outpatient care, the reason for visit can **change from visit to visit** (e.g., a medication visit for ADHD vs a therapy visit for MDD).

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- **Outpatient progress note:**
  ```
  1. F33.1 Major depressive disorder, recurrent episode, moderate, with anxious distress (reason for visit)
  2. F90.0 Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, predominantly inattentive presentation, mild
  3. Z56.0 Unemployment, unspecified
  ```
- **Inpatient admission/discharge:** mark "(principal diagnosis)". At discharge, update it to the condition **established after study** to have caused the admission.
- **AMC case:**
  ```
  1. C34.90 Malignant neoplasm of unspecified part of unspecified bronchus or lung
  2. F06.2 Psychotic disorder due to malignant lung neoplasm, with delusions (principal diagnosis)
  ```
- When it is hard to choose, give a one-line rationale (e.g., "AUD listed as principal: withdrawal management drove admission; schizophrenia stable on LAI").
- Keep the order consistent with the treatment plan's problem list.
