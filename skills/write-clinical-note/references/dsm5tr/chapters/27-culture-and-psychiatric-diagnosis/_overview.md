# Culture and Psychiatric Diagnosis

Culture changes symptom expression, meaning, impairment judgments, help seeking, and care relationships. This chapter supplies contextual assessment tools and ten examples, not new mental-disorder diagnoses. Read the person's own account alongside medical/psychiatric syndrome evidence.

## Chapter-level distinctions and pitfalls
- Formulation is the organizing framework; CFI is an interview method; cultural concepts are meanings/patterns elicited during assessment.
- Idiom, causal explanation, and syndrome overlap, and none maps one-to-one onto DSM diagnoses.
- Neither shared ethnicity nor unfamiliar spiritual beliefs establish the meaning of a client's experience. Assess racism, actual threat, language, structural barriers, strengths, and developmental context.
- No shared diagnostic specifiers, severity scores, or independent diagnostic codes are introduced. Section II focus-of-attention codes may be relevant after their own definitions are assessed.

## TR changes emphasized in source
The expanded cultural formulation explicitly attends to intersectionality, structural disadvantage, discrimination, and institutional relationships. The text updates cultural examples, including hikikomori, changing use of shenjing shuairuo, and prolonged grief among relevant differential directions.
