# Cultural Formulation Interview

> **Spark note:** The CFI is a flexible, semistructured assessment, not a score or diagnostic test. Its 16 core questions elicit the person's views; an informant version adds relationship assessment and has 17 questions.

## Administration
Use with demographic and clinical information for any person, age, or setting. Ask naturally, adapt wording, and follow up; no response is a correct cultural answer. It is particularly useful with uncertainty about symptom meaning or impairment, discordant care expectations, mistrust, poor engagement, or clinician–client background differences. Information must be integrated with the rest of the evaluation. (pp. 1128–1130)

## Core domains and question map
| Questions | What to elicit and record |
|---|---|
| 1–3: problem definition | Person's description; how they explain it to others; what concerns them most |
| 4–5: causes | Their explanations and explanations attributed to family/community; allow multiple causes |
| 6–7: supports and stressors | What improves or worsens the problem; concrete people, resources, conflicts, and pressures |
| 8–10: identity | Personally salient identities; effects on the problem; other identity-linked difficulties |
| 11: self-coping | Actions the person has used independently |
| 12: previous help | Professional, peer, spiritual, traditional, alternative, and other help; what helped or failed |
| 13: barriers | Cost, logistics, work/care responsibilities, stigma, discrimination, services or language mismatch |
| 14–15: current preferences | Desired help and suggestions from others; areas of agreement or conflict |
| 16: care relationship | Concerns about differences, misunderstanding, racism, language, or clinic processes; changes that could help |

This is a paraphrased topic map, not a reproduction of the interview instrument. Core instrument: pp. 1130–1132.

## Informant version
A knowledgeable relative, friend, or caregiver may supplement the core interview or provide information when the individual cannot. Question 1 establishes relationship and contact; questions 2–17 parallel core topics. The informant's account is a perspective, not a substitute for automatically inferring the person's beliefs. Keep disagreements visible and attributed. (pp. 1132–1134)

## Supplementary modules
The chapter refers to online modules for explanatory model, functioning, social network, psychosocial stressors, cultural identity, coping/help seeking, spirituality, care relationships, and populations including children/adolescents, older adults, caregivers, immigrants/refugees. Their full instruments are not reproduced in this extracted chapter. (pp. 1129–1132)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Mark whether the full core, selected domains, or informant version was used; identify informant and relationship.
- Separate elicited problem definition, causal beliefs, social stressors/supports, coping, help history, barriers, and agreed care goals.
- Report the client's degree of trust or concerns only if elicited. Do not turn willingness to attend into presumed agreement with a diagnosis.
- Record useful implications: interpreter access, appointment feasibility, desired family involvement, or negotiated treatment expectations.
- No numeric cutoff, duration requirement, ICD code, or disorder specifier is supplied by the CFI.
