# Nonsuicidal Self-Injury Disorder

## Status and coding boundary
This is a **Section III research proposal**, not an officially recognized Section II disorder or a criteria set intended for clinical diagnostic use. No independent billable diagnosis is established by meeting these proposed thresholds. Assess and document actual symptoms, impairment, safety, and any established Section II condition under its own criteria. The chapter's introductory boundary applies even when its narrative uses diagnostic language. (p. 1168)


> **Spark note:** The proposal requires injury on ≥5 days in the past year, associated functions/antecedents, and meaningful distress or interference. Nonsuicidal intent for a particular act does not imply absence of future or concurrent suicide risk.

## Proposed criteria at a glance
- **A:** on ≥5 days in the past year, deliberate injury to the body's surface expected to cause minor/moderate harm, with no suicidal intent. Absence of intent may be stated or inferred from repeated behavior known to be nonfatal.
- **B:** ≥1 expected function: relief from aversive feelings/thoughts, resolution of interpersonal difficulty, or production of a positive feeling. Relief occurs during/soon after injury.
- **C:** ≥1 associated feature: interpersonal difficulty/negative affect or cognition immediately beforehand; hard-to-control prior preoccupation; frequent thoughts of self-injury even without acting.
- **D:** not socially/culturally sanctioned, and not limited to scab picking or nail biting.
- **E:** behavior/consequences produce clinically significant distress or interference in relationships, school, or other important functioning.
- **F:** not exclusively during psychosis, delirium, intoxication, or withdrawal; not neurodevelopmental repetitive stereotypy; not better explained by another mental/medical condition. (pp. 1192–1193)

## Features and maintaining processes
Repeated minor/moderate injury may bring immediate relief, self-punishment, reduced dissociation, interpersonal response, or positive feeling; these functions must be elicited rather than assumed. Shame/stigma and experienced benefit may reduce disclosure or treatment seeking. Negative reinforcement is prominent, but other reinforcement can operate. Experimentation without relief may stop. Multiple methods are associated with greater psychopathology; avoid treating superficial injury as globally low risk. (pp. 1193–1194)

## Prevalence · Course · Culture/sex/gender
Often begins early/mid adolescence, may peak in late adolescence/early adulthood, then decline; earlier onset is associated with severity. Exposure through peers or media may contribute to initiation. The text supplies no single overall prevalence estimate. Sex ratios vary culturally and by clinical versus community samples; sexual minorities, particularly bisexual people, are overrepresented in cited research. Accepted ritual practices and purely group-belonging expressions do not establish this proposed disorder. (pp. 1194–1195)

## Suicide risk
NSSI often precedes attempts by 1–2 years in cited studies. Greater frequency, earlier onset, multiple methods, and relief/self-punishment functions predict higher suicidal thoughts/attempts in groups. Assess each act's intent and current safety directly, including collateral on changed mood or stress where appropriate. (p. 1195)

## Functional consequences · Differential · Comorbidity
Physical sequelae include scarring, infection, and other injury complications. Borderline personality disorder may coexist but is not required; depression, eating, and substance disorders also co-occur. Differentiate suicidal acts by intent, hair-pulling by its confined behavior, skin-picking by its focus/pattern, and neurodevelopmental stereotypic injury by its repetitive motor phenotype. Assess psychotic, medical, and cultural explanations before applying a research algorithm. (pp. 1195–1196)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Count **days**, not individual wounds/acts, over the preceding year; record timing, expected harm, stated intent, and corroboration.
- Elicit antecedents, expected/experienced relief or interpersonal effect, preoccupation, functional interference, medical consequences, and cultural context.
- Distinguish observed injury from inferred motive. Assess current/past suicidality independently; uncertain or mixed intent requires explicit documentation.
- Record medical care and safety actions actually taken and the established clinical formulation, while labeling any research-threshold assessment separately.
