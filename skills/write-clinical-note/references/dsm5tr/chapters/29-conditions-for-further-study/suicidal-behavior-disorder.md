# Suicidal Behavior Disorder

## Status and coding boundary
This is a **Section III research proposal**, not an officially recognized Section II disorder or a criteria set intended for clinical diagnostic use. No independent billable diagnosis is established by meeting these proposed thresholds. Assess and document actual symptoms, impairment, safety, and any established Section II condition under its own criteria. The chapter's introductory boundary applies even when its narrative uses diagnostic language. (p. 1168)


> **Spark note:** This proposal concerns an actual initiated attempt within 24 months, not ideation or preparation. Intent at the time of starting the act matters; injury severity and present denial do not settle intent.

## Proposed criteria at a glance
- **A:** ≥1 suicide attempt within the past 24 months: self-initiated behavior with expectation, when the method was applied, that the actions would cause one's death.
- **B:** act is not nonsuicidal self-injury directed at the body's surface for emotional relief or a positive state.
- **C:** ideation and preparatory actions alone do not qualify.
- **D:** act was not initiated in delirium/confusion.
- **E:** act was not undertaken solely for a political/religious objective. (p. 1189)

## Specifiers & clinical characterization
- **Current:** ≤12 months since last attempt.
- **Early remission:** 12–24 months since last attempt. Source wording overlaps at exactly 12 months; retain exact date rather than silently resolving that boundary.
- Describe medical consequences, planning/impulsiveness, and method as clinically relevant; they are not required severity thresholds. Source's “high lethality” descriptor refers to medical hospitalization beyond an emergency visit. (p. 1189)

## Diagnostic features
An attempt need not produce injury. Changed intent or outside interruption **after initiation** can still constitute an attempt; stopping **before initiation** does not meet this proposal. Deliberate intoxication to facilitate an act is not automatically exclusion D. Ambivalence, chance rescue, or limited knowledge of lethality do not negate an initiated act with intent to die. Future plans or agreement to a safety contract are unreliable reassurance. No single instrument, biomarker, or risk factor sufficiently predicts an individual's suicidal behavior. (p. 1190)

## Course · Risk · Culture/sex/gender
Rare under age 5; about 25%–30% of attempters later attempt again. Genetic liability partly overlaps depression and partly may be distinct. Risk and meaning vary with social stress, discrimination, acculturation, religion, access, and local methods. Men have higher suicide mortality on average, while nonfatal suicidal behavior is more common in women; ratios vary across settings. Transgender populations show increased risk in the cited research. These group patterns do not determine individual assessment. (pp. 1190–1191)

## Comorbidity
Reported across mood, psychotic, anxiety/trauma, substance, eating, adjustment, borderline, and antisocial disorders; a separate psychiatric diagnosis is not necessary to document suicidal behavior. Physiological consequences can produce laboratory abnormalities but are consequences rather than intent tests. (p. 1192)

## Clinical behavior codes are separate from this proposal
| Source-listed code | Clinical meaning in the source |
|---|---|
| T14.91A | Suicidal behavior in current presentation, initial encounter |
| T14.91D | Current suicidal behavior, subsequent encounter |
| Z91.51 | Personal history of suicidal behavior |

These are clinically usable Section II focus-of-attention codes, with or without another DSM diagnosis. They **do not code a recognized “suicidal behavior disorder.”** Consult the Section II suicidal-behavior note for complete recording conventions. (p. 1189)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Record dates, whether behavior was initiated, intent/expectation at that time, reported ambivalence, interruption, and actual medical consequences; avoid unnecessary procedural detail.
- Separate historical attempt, present ideation/intent/plan, current access, preparation, nonsuicidal injury, and protective supports.
- Attribute client/collateral accounts and discrepancies; assess delirium, intoxication, agitation, recent discharge/treatment change, losses, and acute stressors when relevant.
- State actual current risk formulation and actions taken; neither a research remission label nor denial of current thoughts establishes safety.
