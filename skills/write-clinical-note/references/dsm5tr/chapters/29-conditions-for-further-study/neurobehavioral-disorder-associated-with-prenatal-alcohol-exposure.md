# Neurobehavioral Disorder Associated With Prenatal Alcohol Exposure

## Status and coding boundary
This is a **Section III research proposal**, not an officially recognized Section II disorder or a criteria set intended for clinical diagnostic use. No independent billable diagnosis is established by meeting these proposed thresholds. Assess and document actual symptoms, impairment, safety, and any established Section II condition under its own criteria. The chapter's introductory boundary applies even when its narrative uses diagnostic language. (p. 1168)


> **Spark note:** The proposal requires documented prenatal alcohol exposure plus deficits across neurocognitive, self-regulatory, and adaptive functioning. Facial features or low IQ are not mandatory; exposure alone is insufficient.

## Proposed criteria at a glance
- **A:** more than minimal gestational alcohol exposure, including before pregnancy recognition; establish through maternal report, records, or clinical observation.
- **B:** ≥1 neurocognitive impairment: global intellectual ability (IQ/developmental standard score ≤70), executive functioning, learning, memory, or visuospatial reasoning.
- **C:** ≥1 self-regulatory impairment: mood/behavior regulation, attention, or impulse control.
- **D:** ≥2 adaptive deficits, including communication **or** social communication/interaction. Other options are daily living and motor skills.
- **E:** B–D symptoms begin in childhood.
- **F:** clinically significant distress or social, school, occupational, or other impairment.
- **G:** not better accounted for by postnatal substances/medications, medical illness, another teratogen, genetic condition, or environmental neglect. (pp. 1184–1185)

## Exposure and assessment details
Text operationalizes more-than-minimal exposure as **>13 drinks/month during pregnancy OR >2 drinks on any occasion**; this is a research threshold, not a safety threshold. Effects depend on exposure timing, smoking, genetics, maternal health, and other factors. Fetal alcohol syndrome's characteristic physical findings may establish significant exposure, but the neurobehavioral presentation may occur without them. Deficits can be supported by psychological/educational testing, medical records, report, and observation. The text advises deferring this proposed assessment-based diagnosis at age ≤3 when cognition is difficult to assess. (pp. 1185–1186)

## Prevalence · Course · Risk
Source estimates encompassing FASD vary substantially by method and population: U.S. 15.2/1,000 (range 11.3–50.0); 2012 global FASD estimate 7.7/1,000. These do not cleanly estimate this exact proposed algorithm. Early delay may be evident, but executive, learning, language, and social deficits often become clearer with school demands. Adverse caregiving, violence, disrupted placements, and care discontinuity may accompany exposure but are not assumed in every case. Structural disadvantage can increase exposure and worsen effects; the presentation occurs across backgrounds. (pp. 1186–1187)

## Suicide risk · Functional consequences
FASD studies report elevated suicidality, especially later adolescence/early adulthood; family factors may contribute alongside neurodevelopmental effects. Adaptive deficits can persist and affect education, employment, independent living, and legal involvement; multiple organ-system abnormalities may coexist. Do not treat adverse outcomes as inevitable. (pp. 1187–1188)

## Differential diagnosis · Comorbidity
Review other prenatal exposures, poor prenatal care, postnatal substances, head injury/neurocognitive conditions, and neglect. Genetic syndromes (including Williams, Down, Cornelia de Lange), other teratogens, and maternal phenylketonuria can resemble the picture; genetics evaluation may help. ADHD is common, but neuropsychological profiles and medication responses may differ. Weigh oppositional/conduct labels against cognitive/executive limitations. Mood and later substance use disorders also occur. (p. 1188)

## Coding
No independent diagnosis/code is authorized by the proposal. Section II includes applicable other-specified neurodevelopmental presentations; consult that chapter's full criteria/coding rather than using this research label as a billable category.

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Attribute exposure history, timing, quantity, certainty, and records; do not infer maternal drinking from a child's appearance or social background.
- Provide evidence for ≥1 B, ≥1 C, and ≥2 D deficits including communication/social interaction, with age-adjusted expectations and actual functional effects.
- Record childhood onset, testing limitations, alternative etiologies, genetic/medical evaluation, and relevant strengths/supports.
