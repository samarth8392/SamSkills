# Depressive Episodes With Short-Duration Hypomania

## Status and coding boundary
This is a **Section III research proposal**, not an officially recognized Section II disorder or a criteria set intended for clinical diagnostic use. No independent billable diagnosis is established by meeting these proposed thresholds. Assess and document actual symptoms, impairment, safety, and any established Section II condition under its own criteria. The chapter's introductory boundary applies even when its narrative uses diagnostic language. (p. 1168)


> **Spark note:** This proposal combines a lifetime major depressive episode with at least two separate hypomanic periods lasting 2–3 days. Brief mood improvement alone is insufficient: intensity, symptom count, changed functioning, and observation requirements remain.

## Proposed criteria at a glance
**Depressive component: ≥1 lifetime major depressive episode.**
- **A:** ≥5 symptoms over the same 2-week period, changed from baseline; ≥1 depressed mood (irritability may substitute in youth) or anhedonia. Other options: appetite/weight change (illustrative >5% weight/month; consider absent expected growth in children), insomnia/hypersomnia, observable psychomotor change, low energy, worthlessness/excessive guilt, impaired concentration/indecision, death/suicidal thoughts or behavior. Mood/anhedonia and most other symptoms occur most of the day or nearly daily as applicable; suicidality is recurrent rather than required daily. Do not count symptoms clearly due to medical illness.
- **B–C:** meaningful distress/impairment; not due to substances/medical illness.
- **D:** not better explained by schizoaffective disorder or superimposed on schizophrenia, schizophreniform, delusional, other specified, or unspecified psychotic disorders.

**Hypomanic component: ≥2 lifetime periods, each ≥2 but <4 consecutive days.**
- **A:** persistent abnormal elevation, expansiveness, or irritability **and** increased activity/energy.
- **B:** ≥3 of 7 associated symptoms (≥4 if mood only irritable): inflated esteem/grandiosity, reduced sleep need, increased/pressured speech, racing thoughts/flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal activity or agitation, risky pleasurable pursuits. Symptoms represent a noticeable significant change.
- **C–D:** unequivocal uncharacteristic functional change, observable to others.
- **E:** no marked functional impairment or hospitalization; psychosis means mania rather than hypomania.
- **F:** not due to substance/medication/treatment effects. (pp. 1173–1175)

## Features · Epidemiology · Risk
Compared with MDD groups, studied cases show earlier/more recurrent depression, greater comorbidity and bipolar family history, and more suicide attempts. Family bipolar history is 3–4 times as common as in MDD and similar to bipolar II cohorts. True prevalence under this exact proposal is unknown: the often-cited 6.7% uses a broader subthreshold definition. Clinical samples put these episodes at about one-quarter the frequency of full-duration-hypomania depressive presentations; women may be more often represented with atypical depressive features. (p. 1175)

## Suicide risk · Functional consequences
Attempts are reported more often than in MDD and similarly to bipolar II comparison groups. Specific functional consequences remain incompletely characterized, though global functioning can resemble bipolar II. (p. 1176)

## Differential diagnosis
- **Bipolar II:** any full-duration qualifying hypomanic episode plus MDE establishes that diagnosis regardless of later shorter periods.
- **Bipolar I:** any qualifying lifetime mania changes the diagnostic pathway; assess psychosis, marked impairment, hospitalization, and full episode requirements.
- **MDD:** distinguish truly syndromal brief activation from ordinary fluctuations; official clinical classification must use Section II, including applicable other-specified bipolar presentations.
- **MDD with mixed features:** opposing-pole features occur during depression rather than in distinct brief hypomanic periods.
- **Cyclothymic disorder:** the proposal requires a full MDE; assess the timing of any MDE against Section II cyclothymia rules rather than using chronic mood swings alone. (pp. 1175–1176)

## Documentation cues (for client notes)
- Build an episode timeline with actual dates/durations, interepisode baseline, full MDE evidence, and each activation symptom.
- Record collateral observation and functional change; distinguish less need for sleep from insomnia/fatigue.
- Document lifetime mania/full hypomania, psychosis, hospitalization, medication/substance effects, and current suicide risk.
- Record the clinical Section II formulation separately; this research label is not a substitute for it.
